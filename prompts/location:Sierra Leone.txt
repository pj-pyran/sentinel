Crisis / situation: Sierra Leone

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-05-24) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS

African Day of School Feeding celebrations highlighted the importance of school meals for nutrition, clean water, hygiene, and supporting vulnerable communities.
WFP strengthened community-led environmental governance and livelihoods through new Community Based Organizations under the EU Nature Nourishes project.
Through assessments, WFP identified communities to implement nutrition and agriculture support.

SITUATION OVERVIEW

Sierra Leone’s humanitarian needs are driven by chronic poverty (54–56 percent poor), economic fragility, and high food prices amid persistent inflation.
Climate shocks affecting rain-fed agriculture deepen vulnerability, especially in rural areas.
Despite political stability, limited fiscal space constrains response. As a result, 78 percent of people are food insecure, 13 percent severely, while malnutrition persists with 26 percent of children under 5 suffering from stunting, disproportionately affecting poor households.
However, the July 2025 Food Security Monitoring System survey reported a decline in the proportion of severely food insecure households by more than half—from 28 percent in 2023 to 13 percent in July 2025—representing a 15 percentage point reduction.
Women, girls, children, persons with disabilities, and rural households in Sierra Leone face heightened risks from gender-based violence, child marriage, poverty, food insecurity, limited services, climate shocks, and unequal access to education, health, and livelihoods.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-05-24) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS

Technical inception meeting convened for the EU-funded Strengthened Agricultural Value Chain through Infant Nutrition for Growth project.
Term three food was dispatched to more than 1,200 schools across five districts while construction of 264 school kitchens was completed ahead of installation of energy efficient stoves.
Three Forest-Edge Development Organizations formed for biodiversity conservation.

Sierra Leone’s humanitarian needs are driven by chronic poverty (54–56 percent poor), economic fragility, and high food prices amid persistent inflation. Climate shocks affecting rain-fed agriculture deepen vulnerability, especially in rural areas.
Despite political stability, limited fiscal space constrains response. As a result, 78 percent of people are food insecure, 13 percent severely, while malnutrition persists with 26 percent of children under 5 suffering from stunting, disproportionately affecting poor households.
However, the July 2025 Food Security Monitoring System survey reported a decline in the proportion of severely food insecure households by more than half—from 28 percent in 2023 to 13 percent in July 2025—representing a 15-percentage point reduction.
During the first quarter of 2026, the price of the staple rice in Sierra Leone showed mixed trends. The price of local rice increased in several districts, especially in Kailahun, due to seasonal supply constraints and higher fuel prices. Whereas the price of Imported rice recorded slight increases (5 percent) in March 2026 compared to February 2026 because of external market pressures.
