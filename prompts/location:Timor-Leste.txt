Crisis / situation: Timor-Leste

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-05-18) ---
Risk Analysis
Moderate to heavy rainfall occurred over a three-day period from 10 to 13 May 2026 in Dotic Village, Manufahi Municipality, as well as in Lautem, Viqueque, and Covalima municipalities. The rainfall caused rivers to overflow, resulting in flooding in Dotic Village (Manufahi) and in Tazhilin and Raimea areas of Covalima Municipality. The CVTL has activated the Early Action Protocol (EAP) in Manufahi Municipality to mitigate the impact of the overflowing Weberek River in Dotic Village. As rainfall continues in Manufahi, the number of evacuated people is expected to increase. Additionally, the Timor-Leste Meteorology Office forecast continued rainfall in Manufahi Municipality through 15 May 2026. Based on the latest updates and the actual situation in Dotic Village, the moderate rainfall forecasted by the Meteorology Office has not caused any significant impact on Dotic Village or the surrounding communities. In light of this, the CVTL team proceeded with the initial plan to deactivate the anticipatory action at 01:00 a.m. local time, officially ceasing early anticipatory activities and concluding its services at the evacuation center.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Timor-Leste, a least-developed small island state of 1.3 million people, has made notable progress in reconstruction, human rights, and development since independence in 2002, yet it still faces major nutrition and food security challenges, exacerbated by natural hazards.
• Stunting among children under five remains among the world’s highest. The 2024 IPC analysis shows 27 percent of households are acutely food-insecure, while the 2023 cost-of-diet study found that 75 percent cannot afford a nutritious diet, with food prices up 78 percent since 2019. Low agricultural productivity, poor maternal and child nutrition practices, high poverty rates, limited safety nets, and frequent climatic shocks further weaken food systems and household resilience.
• WFP’s country strategic plan (CSP 2026-2030) supports the Government of Timor-Leste's vision for zero hunger and contributes to the National Strategic Development Plan 2011-2030. The overall goal of the CSP is to improve food security and nutrition by strengthening existing social protection programmes (Bolsa da Mãe Kondisionál, Merenda Eskolár), the food value chain, supply chain management, and emergency shock preparedness and response.
