Crisis / situation: Panama: Earthquake - Oct 2026

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-10-29) ---
KEY POINTS

Some 4,500 people remain in shelters after the magnitude 7.5 earthquake on 9 October.
The priority is helping affected people return home, while temporary shelter solutions and home reconstruction continue.
Education activities are gradually resuming, but schools in Los Santos, Herrera, Coclé and Veraguas remain closed.
Rain and seismic activity continue to pose risks in affected areas.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-10-27) ---
Highlights


Two weeks after the 9 October earthquake, humanitarian needs remain significant in the provinces of Los Santos, Veraguas, Coclé and Herrera. Although response actions have addressed the most immediate needs of affected populations, thousands of people continue to face challenges related to temporary displacement, loss or damage of housing, disruptions to basic services, and impacts on educational infrastructure.


According to the latest official assessments, 12.365 homes were assessed and 40.880 people registered through the Damage Assessment and Needs Analysis (EDAN). Damage to housing remains one of the main impacts of the earthquake, affecting 2,896 families, of whom 908 have reported the total loss of their homes. In addition, 160 shelters continue to provide temporary accommodation to 5.779 displaced people. The provinces of Los Santos, Veraguas, Coclé and Herrera account for the majority of reported impacts.


During the reporting period, UNICEF maintained its support to the Government-led response through coordination, monitoring and assistance actions in water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), education, and child protection. Monitoring missions conducted in the Azuero Peninsula showed that, although the emergency has evolved into an early recovery phase, significant humanitarian gaps persist, particularly for displaced families, children and persons with disabilities.


UNICEF expanded its response through the distribution of 133 mosquito nets funded by DG ECHO, support to water and sanitation interventions implemented together with its implementing partner at the temporary site of La Pericada, and the coordination of actions aimed at educational recovery and psychosocial support for children and adolescents affected by the earthquake.


Despite progress in the emergency response, significant humanitarian needs remain among displaced families, children and adolescents affected by educational disruption, and communities with limited access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services. Likewise, psychosocial support and protection needs remain for the most vulnerable populations affected by the emergency.
