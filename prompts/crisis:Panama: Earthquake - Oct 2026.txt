Crisis / situation: Panama: Earthquake - Oct 2026

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-10-20) ---
Highlights

According to the National Civil Protection System (SINAPROC), as of October 15, 5,821 homes have been evaluated, corresponding to 19,309 people, of which 3,696 remain in temporary accommodation. Among the population identified by SINAPROC, 475 newborns, 2,174 children and 1,030 adolescents were reported. The authorities continue damage assessments and assistance to affected families.
The evaluations continue to identify impacts on water, sanitation, education and protection services in rural and hard-to-reach communities in the provinces of Herrera, Los Santos and Veraguas, where risk factors associated with displacement, interruption of basic services and access limitations persist.
UNICEF contributes to the government-led response, in coordination with UN agencies, funds and programmes, deploying efforts with donors and implementing partners to reach the most affected communities with psychosocial support, water, sanitation, health and learning spaces in the context of the emergency.
As part of the initial response, UNICEF distributed 415 hygiene kits to displaced people from Isla Cañas, including 325 kits for children aged 0-12 years and 90 kits for adolescents and adults, helping to reduce health risks and strengthen hygiene conditions in temporary shelters.
Needs continue to evolve as damage and impact assessments progress. UNICEF requires US$2.57 million to sustain and scale up the multisectoral response targeting children and their families in affected provinces, with an emphasis on protection, education, health and access to essential WASH services.

Humanitarian Situation and Needs
The 7.5 magnitude earthquake that struck Panama on October 9 continues to have significant impacts on families and essential services. Following the declaration of a state of emergency on October 10, national authorities continue damage assessments and humanitarian assistance to displaced families, while there are still needs for safe shelter and access to basic services in the affected areas. As of the date of the report, and according to official figures from SINAPROC, 19,309 people are affected by the earthquake. According to the preliminary breakdown by age and sex, 475 newborns, 2,174 children and 1,030 adolescents are identified. In turn, SINAPROC reports 3,696 people housed in temporary shelters, of which 2,569 are in Veraguas, followed by 644 people in Los Santos and 483 in other areas. That shows priority needs for protection, access to basic services and continuity of essential services for children. At the time of this report, two people have died as a result of the earthquake.
Infrastructure damage and access limitations persist in several of the impacted areas. In the province of Herrera, one community is reported to be cut off from communication and 32 landslides, while in areas of Veraguas there are still interruptions in drinking water, electricity and connectivity services.³ These conditions may limit the timely access of affected communities to humanitarian assistance and essential services, in addition to delaying assessment, rehabilitation and recovery efforts.
As government-led sectoral assessments progress, needs related to the restoration of water, sanitation and hygiene services, the recovery of safe learning spaces and the expansion of child protection services continue to be identified. The information remains subject to updating as authorities complete damage checks and early recovery and response efforts advance.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-10-19) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
09-10-2026
What happened, where and when?
On October 9, 2026, at 12:56 p.m. (local time), a magnitude 7.7 Mw earthquake affected Panama. According to the United States Geological Survey (USGS), the epicenter was located approximately 12 km west-southwest of Pitaloza Arriba, Herrera province, at a depth of 12.6 km, generating strong seismic movements perceived in much of the national territory and in areas of neighboring countries (1). The Institute of Geosciences of the University of Panama initially reported the event with a magnitude of 7.2 and a depth of 20 km, approximately 45 km west of Tonosí, Los Santos province (8).
After the main earthquake, an intense seismic sequence was recorded, with numerous events of magnitude greater than 5.0. According to the records of the Institute of Geosciences, on October 9 there were movements of magnitude 6.9 east of Coiba and 6.6 northwest of Tonosí. On October 12, another movement of magnitude 6.4 was recorded northeast of Coiba, while seismic activity continued on October 13 (8). The persistence of the aftershocks has increased the concern of the population, generated uncertainty about the safety of the affected structures and hindered the work of assessing damage and the continuity of some essential services. Panamanian Red Cross teams reported episodes of fainting, emotional crises and convulsions in students from Bocas del Toro, as well as families who remained outdoors and in need of psychological first aid in different locations (4).
According to SINAPROC's Official Statement No. 12, dated October 14, 2026, damage assessments and needs analysis identified 4,045 affected families, equivalent to 13,623 people. Of these, 879 families reported total loss of their homes, 926 moderate damage, 1,577 partial damage, and 663 minor or minor damage. The damage also includes educational and health infrastructure, public buildings, roads, bridges, and water supply systems. Assessments continue in the affected provinces and the figures remain subject to verification.
The earthquake had a particular impact on coastal communities on the Azuero Peninsula. In Isla Cañas, Los Santos province, approximately 300 people were evacuated preventively during the first hours after the earthquake, according to initial reports from the Panamanian Red Cross (4). As of October 12, OCHA reported that 193 displaced people from Isla Cañas remained in three makeshift accommodation points in Cañas (5). Subsequently, SINAPROC's balance sheet of October 14 identified 13 shelters set up in Los Santos, with 644 people housed. At the national level, 45 shelters were reported with 3,696 people housed, evidencing the need for temporary accommodation and humanitarian assistance for families who cannot safely return to their homes (9).
The impact on health infrastructure has led to interruptions in the provision of services and preventive evacuations of patients. According to SINAPROC's Official Statement No. 12, as of October 14, 22 health centers in Veraguas, four hospitals in Los Santos, three hospitals in Herrera, and two hospitals in Coclé were affected, in addition to damage to health facilities in other provinces (9). At the Santo Tomás Hospital, more than 40 patients were preventively evacuated from the old maternity ward due to structural damage (3, 5). The Panamanian Red Cross reports also document hospital evacuations, patient transfers, and the provision of temporary care spaces (4). These effects represent risks for the continuity of health services, particularly for the elderly, patients with chronic diseases, and people with mobility difficulties or access to health facilities.
Access to drinking water has also been affected by damage to supply systems. SINAPROC reported the persistence of interruptions in the supply of water, electricity, and telecommunications in Veraguas, as well as damage to storage tanks and fissures in a water treatment plant in Chiriquí (9). These conditions can temporarily compromise families' access to safe water, especially in communities with access restrictions and interrupted services. As part of the response, as of October 12, authorities had reported the transfer of 240 tons of water to supply communities and hospitals (2).
As a response measure, the national authorities activated emergency coordination and response mechanisms to support damage and needs assessments in the affected areas. On October 10, 2026, the Government of Panama declared a State of National Emergency through Cabinet Resolution No. 108, with the purpose of facilitating resource mobilization and response actions (7). The Panamanian Red Cross mobilized its local committees and response teams to provide first aid, pre-hospital care, psychological first aid, support for hospital evacuations, and humanitarian assistance, in coordination with SINAPROC, the Ministry of Health, and other competent institutions (4).
Due to the magnitude of the event, its relatively shallow depth, and the persistence of aftershocks, many communities remain under observation while sectoral assessments are completed. Uncertainty about the stability of some structures, coupled with the risk of new seismic movements, has led to preventive evacuations and the temporary stay of families outside their homes. The damage to homes, health facilities, water supply systems, educational centers, and road infrastructure shows humanitarian needs related to temporary housing, access to basic services, assistance to cover essential needs, continuity of health care, and mental health and psychosocial support. The figures remain preliminary and may be modified as the assessments of the authorities and the Panamanian Red Cross progress (5, 9).
