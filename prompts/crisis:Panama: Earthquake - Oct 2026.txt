Crisis / situation: Panama: Earthquake - Oct 2026

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-10-15) ---
KEY POINTS
• The 7.5-magnitude earthquake that struck on 9 October affected several provinces across the country, particularly Veraguas, Coclé, Herrera and Los Santos. The first death has been reported of a person who was hospitalised as a result of the earthquake.
• The earthquake caused extensive damage to homes, educational centres, health facilities and essential drinking water and transportation infrastructure, affecting access to basic services.
• The Government estimates more than 13,623 affected people. Several communities remain cut off due to the rains and landslides.
• On 10 October, the Government declared a state of emergency and deployed personnel and equipment to access affected communities by land, air and river.
• UN agencies are providing complementary support to the authority-led response. On 13 and 14 October, an interagency mission was conducted in Herrera and Los Santos.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-10-14) ---
Situation
On 9 October, Panama experienced one of the strongest earthquakes in its history. With a magnitude of 7.5, an epicentre on the Azuero Peninsula, and more than 400 aftershocks recorded to date, the earthquake affected several provinces, causing severe damage to homes and critical infrastructure in communities across Panama's countryside.
As of 12 October, authorities reported over 3,000 affected homes, damage to more than 650 schools, at least 27 people injured, and hundreds displaced, evacuated, and nearly 600 staying in 14 temporary shelters. Among those affected are refugee families, as well as other vulnerable populations. The full extent of the losses has not yet been quantified, but the Government declared a state of emergency to mobilize up to USD 100 million for response efforts. President Mulino reported that more than 6,000 people have been affected and that the government is implementing recovery measures, including a housing reconstruction programme.
Heavy rainfall, rising river levels and landslides in affected areas have hindered the delivery of humanitarian assistance and the assessment of the most urgent needs. Several communities remain inaccessible, with no access roads, safe drinking water or electricity.
UNHCR joined the Government of Panama-led response and has mobilized over 33 metric tons of core relief items (CRIs) to date, including family tents, blankets, mosquito nets, kitchen sets, a rub hall and other essential supplies. The Agency remains in contact with authorities and community leaders, supporting efforts to address the most urgent needs, particularly for the most vulnerable.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-10-13) ---
KEY POINTS

As of 12 October, authorities report some 1,250 affected homes, more than 800 people in shelters and 27 people injured following the magnitude 7.6 earthquake on 9 October. There are no reports of deaths or missing people.
Fearing further aftershocks, many families are sleeping outdoors or staying with relatives. More than 350 aftershocks have been recorded so far. In Herrera, some families lack regular access to water and a safe place to sleep.
Several hospitals are still treating patients in tents or car parks, and at least three health centres are out of service. In Tonosí, 11 townships remain without water or power.
The bridge over the Oria River remains closed, but aid is reaching communities in Tonosí by air and road. Heavy rain is forecast for the worst-hit areas and could hamper access.
As assessments continue to pin down needs, UN agencies and their partners have delivered tents, shelter items and health supplies as part of their ongoing support for the Government’s response.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
On 9 October at 12:56 p.m., a magnitude 7.6 earthquake struck Panama, according to the Institute of Geosciences of the University of Panama (IGC-UP). The United States Geological Survey puts it at 7.7. The epicentre was in Veraguas, near the border with Los Santos, 39 km north-west of Tonosí, at a depth of 4 km. By the morning of 12 October, IGC-UP had recorded more than 350 aftershocks. They are getting weaker, but IGC-UP says they could continue for weeks, possibly for close to a month. The last major earthquake on this fault system, a magnitude 6.9, struck in 1913. Soil type partly explains why Chitré and Penonomé suffered more damage than areas closer to the epicentre.
Impact: According to the Government’s preliminary count as of 12 October, some 1,250 homes are affected, mostly in Los Santos, Veraguas, Coclé and Herrera. Not all of them will need rebuilding, and the Ministry of Housing is assessing which ones need repairs. The earthquake also damaged 16 bridges, churches and public buildings, and affected 29 health facilities and 619 schools. Twenty-seven people are injured, and there are no reports of deaths or missing people.
Some 478,000 people live in areas that felt very strong shaking or stronger, and around 41,000 live where moderate to heavy damage is expected, according to the Pan American Health Organization/World Health Organization (PAHO/WHO).
More than 800 people remain in shelters, mostly in Veraguas, Colón and Los Santos. Many others are staying with relatives or friends or sleeping outdoors for fear of aftershocks. In Búcaro, Los Santos, residents have left their homes, many of which were destroyed, and are staying at the entrance to the village. Rain has already disrupted care in tents outside Anita Moreno Hospital, and swollen rivers and landslides are delaying access to some communities.
