Crisis / situation: Panama: Earthquake - Oct 2026

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-10-15) ---
KEY POINTS
• The 7.5-magnitude earthquake that struck on 9 October affected several provinces across the country, particularly Veraguas, Coclé, Herrera and Los Santos. The first death has been reported of a person who was hospitalised as a result of the earthquake.
• The earthquake caused extensive damage to homes, educational centres, health facilities and essential drinking water and transportation infrastructure, affecting access to basic services.
• The Government estimates more than 13,623 affected people. Several communities remain cut off due to the rains and landslides.
• On 10 October, the Government declared a state of emergency and deployed personnel and equipment to access affected communities by land, air and river.
• UN agencies are providing complementary support to the authority-led response. On 13 and 14 October, an interagency mission was conducted in Herrera and Los Santos.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-10-14) ---
Situation
On 9 October, Panama experienced one of the strongest earthquakes in its history. With a magnitude of 7.5, an epicentre on the Azuero Peninsula, and more than 400 aftershocks recorded to date, the earthquake affected several provinces, causing severe damage to homes and critical infrastructure in communities across Panama's countryside.
As of 12 October, authorities reported over 3,000 affected homes, damage to more than 650 schools, at least 27 people injured, and hundreds displaced, evacuated, and nearly 600 staying in 14 temporary shelters. Among those affected are refugee families, as well as other vulnerable populations. The full extent of the losses has not yet been quantified, but the Government declared a state of emergency to mobilize up to USD 100 million for response efforts. President Mulino reported that more than 6,000 people have been affected and that the government is implementing recovery measures, including a housing reconstruction programme.
Heavy rainfall, rising river levels and landslides in affected areas have hindered the delivery of humanitarian assistance and the assessment of the most urgent needs. Several communities remain inaccessible, with no access roads, safe drinking water or electricity.
UNHCR joined the Government of Panama-led response and has mobilized over 33 metric tons of core relief items (CRIs) to date, including family tents, blankets, mosquito nets, kitchen sets, a rub hall and other essential supplies. The Agency remains in contact with authorities and community leaders, supporting efforts to address the most urgent needs, particularly for the most vulnerable.
