Crisis / situation: Panama: Earthquake - Oct 2026

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-10-12) ---
Highlights


The 7.5-magnitude earthquake on 9 October damaged homes, educational facilities, health facilities, and water and transport infrastructure. Damage and needs assessments are ongoing, with significant impacts confirmed in Veraguas, Los Santos and Herrera provinces. No fatalities have been reported.


Children’s access to education has been significantly affected, with 419 educational facilities reported damaged nationwide and classes suspended on 12 October while safety inspections continue.


UNICEF deployed emergency personnel together with implementing partners to Coclé, Veraguas, Herrera and Los Santos provinces to conduct initial needs assessments. Initial field reports identify needs related to access to safe water, shelter, health and psychosocial support, as well as visible infrastructure damage.


UNICEF is supporting the response led by national authorities and coordinating with other United Nations agencies. Work is focused on assessing children’s needs in water, sanitation and hygiene, education, health and protection, and on preparing operational support and emergency supplies based on verified needs.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-10-12) ---
Highlights

On 9 October 2026, a magnitude 7.5 earthquake struck Panama, causing damage to homes, schools, health facilities, roads, bridges, and water supply infrastructure, particularly in Veraguas, Coclé, Los Santos and Herrera provinces. On 10 October, the government declared a state of emergency following the earthquake.
According to the National Emergency Operations Centre (COE), 27 people sustained minor injuries, with no fatalities or missing persons reported as of 10 October. The number of affected children and families remains unknown.
The Ministry of Education (MEDUCA) reported 419 affected educational facilities nationwide, raising concerns about school safety, access to water and sanitation, and continuity of learning.
The Ministry of Health (MINSA) reported 20 affected health facilities. Evacuations and temporary adjustments to patient care were reported, potentially affecting access to essential health services for children and families.
Damage to roads and bridges restricted access to several rural communities. Although water production has reportedly been restored at major treatment facilities, the situation in affected rural areas remains under assessment. Approximately 300 people were preventively evacuated from Isla Caña, while eight families were accommodated in a temporary shelter in Penonomé.
UNICEF is supporting the Government-led response and assessing priority needs affecting children and families. Initial efforts are focused on WASH, education, health and child protection, including assessing water and sanitation services in affected communities, schools, health facilities and temporary shelters. UNICEF is preparing to provide emergency supplies and support the rapid restoration of essential services, prioritizing isolated and displaced communities as needs are confirmed.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-10-11) ---
KEY POINTS

The University of Panama’s Institute of Geosciences (IGC-UP) has revised the magnitude of the 9 October earthquake to 7.6. The epicentre was 39 km north-west of Tonosí, at a depth of 4 km. The institute has since recorded more than 200 earthquakes, five of them magnitude 6.0 or above.
As of 6:30 p.m. on 10 October, the National Civil Protection System (SINAPROC) reported more than 820 damaged homes, 27 people with minor injuries and 29 affected health facilities across ten provinces and two Indigenous territories. The Ministry of Education (MEDUCA) reports 419 affected schools. No one has been reported missing.
Some 255 people are staying in two shelters in Mariato (Veraguas) and 12 in one in Penonomé (Coclé), and two more shelters are open in Boca de Quema (Los Santos). Authorities evacuated about 300 people from Isla de Cañas.
Tonosí has no water or electricity. The Pedasí–Tonosí road remains closed and the Ministry of Public Works (MOP) has shut the bridge over the Oria River, so only light vehicles can reach the town, via Macaracas and Llano de Piedra. Six communities in Los Santos and Herrera are still cut off.
MEDUCA has cancelled classes at all public and private schools on Monday 12 October. SINAPROC has called off the third national emergency drill, and a rain warning is in effect until 1:00 p.m. that day.

--- Report 4: OCHA (2026-10-10) ---
KEY POINTS

On 9 October at 12:56 p.m., a magnitude 7.2 earthquake struck 39 km north-west of Tonosí, in the south-central province of Los Santos, at a depth of 4 km, according to the University of Panama’s Institute of Geosciences (IGC-UP). Aftershocks are ongoing, with 56 aftershocks recorded up to 8:11 p.m. No deaths or missing people have been reported.
On 10 October, the Cabinet Council, convened by the President of Panama, declared a state of emergency following the earthquake.
The National Civil Protection System (SINAPROC) reported at least 134 damaged homes, 27 people injured and 188 damaged schools nationwide as of 8:00 p.m.
Veraguas, Coclé, Los Santos and Herrera provinces account for most of the damage. In Veraguas, the townships (corregimientos) of Arena, Cacao and Quebro remain cut off, with power, water and Internet outages.
Some 300 people were evacuated from Isla Cañas (Los Santos) due to rising sea levels, and eight families are staying in a shelter set up in Penonomé (Coclé).
The Ministry of Health (MINSA) reported non-structural damage to 17 health facilities: 6 hospitals, 8 health centres and 3 MINSA-CAPSI (Innovative Primary Health Care Centres). Hospitals in Los Santos, Chiriquí and Herrera provinces and the Panama City metropolitan area were evacuated, and the Hospital del Oriente Chiricano was fully evacuated (209 people).
The Ministry of Education (MEDUCA) suspended classes nationwide and reported 188 affected schools. Those with infrastructure damage include 22 in the Ngäbe-Buglé Indigenous comarca (a semi-autonomous territory), 15 in Chiriquí, 5 in Panamá Centro, 4 in Los Santos and 3 in Panamá Este. At the Alberto Zúñiga School (Coclé), 15 students suffered minor injuries.
The National Institute of Aqueducts and Sewers (IDAAN) reported that the Chilibre water treatment plant is operating at full capacity and that supply is gradually recovering.
