Crisis / situation: Afghanistan: Flash Floods - Jul 2026

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-08-13) ---
Highlights

On 20 July, heavy rainfall triggered flash floods across several provinces in central, northern and eastern Afghanistan, resulting in casualties, damage to homes and critical infrastructure, and disruption to essential services and livelihoods.
In the Eastern Region (ER), Parun district of Nuristan Province was among the most severely affected areas. Preliminary reports indicate 24 fatalities (26 per cent women, 74 per cent men), and 125 injuries (7 per cent women, 93 per cent men), with additional individuals reported missing. The floods destroyed or damaged 120 houses and affected health facilities, local markets, bridges, roads, flood protection walls, agricultural land and water supply networks, disrupting livelihoods and access to safe drinking water.
On 3 August, a second flash flood struck Barg-e-Matal district in eastern Nuristan, affecting 191 families through destruction or damage to their houses. This flood similarly disrupted livelihoods and damaged critical infrastructure, including bridges, roads, flood protection walls, agricultural land and water supply networks, further constraining access to safe drinking water and essential services.
In the Central Region (CR), flash floods across nine provinces resulted in two fatalities and six injuries. A total of 316 houses were affected, impacting 2,057 people – including 688 girls, 610 boys, 395 women and 364 men. Approximately 1.6 million square metres of agricultural land were destroyed, some 300 livestock were killed, and 17 schools sustained damage, further affecting livelihoods and access to basic services.
A separate flooding incident in Paktia province destroyed 11 houses and damaged agricultural land. A joint needs assessment team has been deployed to assess humanitarian needs, with distribution of assistance planned for 12 August.
The recent flash floods may also have displaced explosive ordnance (EO) or exposed previously buried hazards, increasing protection risks for affected communities and humanitarian personnel operating in flood-affected areas.

--- Report 2: UNFPA (2026-08-12) ---
Since 20 July 2026, heavy rainfall and flash floods have affected Laghman, Nangarhar, Nuristan, Logar, Kapisa, Parwan, Ghazni, Paktia, and Paktika provinces, resulting in deaths and injuries, displacement, and widespread damage to homes, livelihoods, and public infrastructure.
Flooding has severely constrained access to essential health and protection services, affecting an estimated 1,994 women and girls of reproductive age. Pregnant women, adolescent girls, older women, and women-headed households face heightened protection and reproductive health risks as a result of disrupted services and limited access to care.
UNFPA continues to scale up its response by providing emergency sexual and reproductive health services and psychosocial support. As of 30 July, UNFPA’s mobile sexual and reproductive health and psychosocial support team had reached more than 1,400 people, providing antenatal and postnatal care, reproductive health and family planning services, general health consultations, and psychosocial support. In addition, 227 dignity kits and 50 mama and baby kits have been distributed to flood-affected women.
UNFPA is conducting joint needs assessments to identify emerging priority needs in sexual and reproductive health, gender-based violence, and psychosocial support among affected populations, alongside dedicated community feedback mechanisms. The findings continue to inform the scale, targeting, and prioritization of UNFPA’s response, in close coordination with humanitarian partners.
