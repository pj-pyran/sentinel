Crisis / situation: Afghanistan: Flash Floods - Jul 2026

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-07-29) ---
HIGHIGHTS

Heavy rainfall, flash floods and related natural hazards continued to affect communities in central, eastern, northeastern and southeastern regions of Afghanistan.
Significant impacts were reported in Ghazni, Kapisa, Laghman, Logar, Nangarhar, Paktia, Paktika and Parwan, among other provinces. In Laghman, three people were killed and four others were injured, with 187 families affected. A total of 183 houses were damaged or destroyed, while approximately 800 acres of seasonal crops and around 310 livestock were also reportedly lost or damaged.
Nuristan Province remains the most severely affected area. The completed inter-agency needs assessment in Parun district identified 120 families eligible for humanitarian assistance, while humanitarian assistance has already been provided to the first 92 identified families.
Joint needs assessments have now been completed in Logar, Kabul, Kapisa and Parwan. Humanitarian assistance is scheduled for 79 families in Logar, 28 families in Kabul, 65 families in Kapisa, and 45 families in Parwan. Assessments in Maidan Wardak and Panjshir are expected to commence shortly.
Humanitarian partners continue to scale up the response through shelter, health, WASH, protection and education interventions. Water network rehabilitation is underway in Parun, emergency health services continue to be provided, and cluster partners are expanding protection, psychosocial support and education responses.
Recent flooding and landslides may have displaced or exposed explosive ordnance (EO), increasing risks for affected communities and humanitarian personnel.

--- Report 2: UNFPA (2026-07-28) ---
Heavy rainfall and flash flooding have affected communities across Nangarhar, Nuristan and Laghman provinces in eastern Afghanistan, causing deaths, injuries, displacement risks, damage to homes and livelihoods, and disruption to essential services. In Nangarhar, flooding affected the Torkham border area, disrupting returnee movement at the border crossing. In Nuristan, the impact has been severe, with reported deaths, injuries and more than 100 people missing, alongside extensive damage to homes, markets, roads, bridges, agricultural land and water systems. Around 1,000 families reportedly face disrupted access to safe drinking water. In Laghman, flooding damaged agricultural land and crops, threatening livelihoods and household food security.
UNFPA is working with partners to monitor the situation and to identify emerging needs, particularly for women and girls, including pregnant women, adolescent girls and women-headed households. Ensuring continued access to essential sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence services, dignity supplies, safe referrals, and protection support will be critical as assessments continue.
