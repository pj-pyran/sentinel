Crisis / situation: Myanmar: Floods - Jul 2026

--- Report 1: AHA Centre (2026-08-19) ---
OVERVIEW: Heavy and continuous rainfall since 30 July 2026 triggered flooding in several areas in Myanmar. According to the Department of Disaster Management (DDM) Myanmar, severe weather resulted to flooding and riverbank erosion that caused a breach in the Ngawun River embankment in Laymyethna Township, Ayeyarwady Region, which resulted in flooding of five urban wards, major roads, and 150 surrounding villages. In Kayin State, DDM reported displaced households and a casualty due to overflowing of the Thanlwin River. Forecasts indicate that heavy rainfall may persist in the coming week (DMH).
IMPACTS: The following impacts have been reported by DDM Myanmar:

HAZARDS &amp; ANTIPICATED RISKS:

Heavy Rain Warning: According to DMH Myanmar, as of 07000H UTC+7, due to the strong monsoon winds in the Andaman Sea and the southern Bay of Bengal and the strong development of clusters of rain clouds, strong winds, flash floods and landslides may occur along with heavy rains. There may be scattered showers and thunderstorms in Nay Pyi Taw, Yangon Region, Mandalay Region, Bago Region, Magway Region, Sagaing Region, Ayeyarwady Region, Tanintharyi Region, Kachin State, Kayin State, Chin State, Mon State, Rakhine State and Shan State from this morning to 23 August 2026, while scattered heavy rains may occur in Nay Pyi Taw, Yangon Region, Mandalay Region, Bago Region, Upper Sagaing Region, Ayeyarwady Region, Tanintharyi Region, Kachin State, Kayin State, Mon State, Rakhine State and Shan State for this forecast period.

PREPAREDNESS AND RESPONSE:

DDM Myanmar, together with relevant ministries, technical experts, military personnel, firefighters, civil society organisations (CSOs), and charitable organisations, is working around the clock to repair the breached sections of the embankment and control the flooding.
Search and rescue operations are also underway, alongside the provision of food, drinking water, and essential healthcare services to affected communities.
The Government of Myanmar has provided approximately USD 85.2K in assistance to affected communities.
DMH continues to monitor the prevailing weather systems and provides regular updates and advisories.

The AHA Centre will continue to monitor for further developments and issue necessary updates. The AHA Centre stands ready to support DDM Myanmar should the need arises.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-08-18) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
05-08-2026
What happened, where and when?
Heavy and continuous monsoon rainfall caused rising water levels and flooding across low-lying and flood-prone areas of Ayeyarwady Region. The situation escalated on 30 July 2026, when an embankment near Myit Kyo Village in Laymyatna Township breached, and floodwaters rapidly entered Laymyatna town and surrounding communities. The flooding has affected residential areas, agricultural land, roads, and public infrastructure, with communities along the Ngawun River being particularly affected. Water levels in the Ngawun River have exceeded the danger level and remain elevated, with further rainfall potentially worsening the situation.
The Myanmar Red Cross Society (MRCS) Regional Branch started the response with its own resources on 1 August 2026, and the information indicates impacts across 5 wards, 23 village tracts, and 122 villages in Laymyatna Township; 2 wards, 16 village tracts, and 14 villages in Yegyi Township; and 10 village tracts and 33 villages in Hinthada Township. The most significant impacts are in Laymyethna, Yegyi, and Hinthada Townships, among other townships in Ayeyarwady, particularly communities located along the Ngawun River and other low-lying areas. In August 2026, the request was submitted to IFRC by MRCS Headquarters, following six days after the trigger date, as the situation had escalated and required more support from the Headquarters level.
The same preliminary information estimated that approximately 8,907 households, representing 28,455 people, had been affected and displaced to 28 evacuation camps, including community centres, schools, temporary shelters, and monasteries. The situation remains ongoing, with water levels continuing to pose a risk to additional communities. Rescue and evacuation operations are underway, but access to affected areas is severely constrained by inundated roads, making motorboats the primary means of rescue and emergency transportation. The number of affected people and the overall extent of damage remain provisional and subject to further assessment, particularly as additional rainfall during the monsoon may further expand the impact.
