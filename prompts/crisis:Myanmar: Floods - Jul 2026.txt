Crisis / situation: Myanmar: Floods - Jul 2026

--- Report 1: CGIAR (2026-10-14) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS:

Rice prices increased substantially in August 2026, with the national average rising by 5.2 percent monthon-month and 14.9 percent year-on-year. Regional price disparities persisted, reflecting differences in production conditions, market access, and transportation costs. The country-wide increase in retail rice prices was reportedly associated with flood-related concerns, supply disruptions, and transportation difficulties.
Export crop prices showed mixed movements. Maize prices declined month-on-month but remained substantially above year-earlier levels, while black gram and pigeon pea prices also remained elevated year-on-year. Green gram prices declined while chickpea prices increased. Vegetable prices generally increased in August. Chili and onion prices rose sharply, supported by constrained supplies and continued export demand, while garlic prices increased following a seasonal pattern. Potato prices rose more moderately.
Most animal-sourced food prices remained substantially above year-earlier levels. Chicken, eggs, mutton, beef, and fish prices increased year-on-year, with mutton prices remaining at record highs. In contrast, pig prices declined both month-on-month and year-on-year amid reports of swine disease and weaker pork demand in Yangon. Reports of livestock disease affecting poultry, pigs, and cattle also raised broader animal health concerns.
The conflict in Iran has led to a partial closure of the Strait of Hormuz — a major transit route for global oil and fertilizer trade — while fighting has also escalated in the Red Sea, another key shipping corridor. Together, these disruptions are raising concerns for Myanmar's agrifood system, which depends heavily on fuel for transportation, irrigation, mechanization, and processing. Fertilizer prices also remain substantially above last year’s levels, possibly reducing the profitability of fertilizer use and leading to lower application rates and agricultural production.
Flooding intensified during August, particularly in major rice-producing areas of Ayeyarwady and Sagaing. Flooding reportedly inundated approximately 97,000 acres in Ayeyarwady and affected more than 10,000 acres of paddy and other cropland in Sagaing. Meanwhile, strengthening El Niño conditions could bring additional risks from irregular rainfall, higher temperatures, and drought, potentially affecting agricultural production and food supply.
The outlook for Myanmar food prices will remain influenced by export demand, border trade conditions, input costs, and weather-related risks. Concerns over pulses production in India could support demand for Myanmar’s black gram and pigeon pea, while softer Chinese demand for green gram remains a concern. Changes in Thailand’s maize import arrangements and continued volatility in border trade stations could also affect agricultural market flows and will be important to monitor in the coming months.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-10-12) ---
Description of the Event
Crisis Category Supporting Document 
Date of event
30-09-2026
What happened, where and when?
In late September 2026, a low-pressure system over the North Andaman Sea and Mon-Tanintharyi coastal areas intensified into a depression, resulting in strong monsoon conditions across southern Myanmar. The system brought heavy rainfall, strong winds, flooding and landslides, with significant impacts reported in Tanintharyi Region, particularly in Launglon, Dawei, Thayetchaung and Tanintharyi Townships.
By 30 September, the system had moved north-northwestwards across Myanmar, bringing additional rainfall to areas already affected by several days of heavy precipitation and increasing the risk of flooding, landslides and damage to houses and infrastructure. On 6 October 2026, the Department of Meteorology and Hydrology (DMH) officially announced the withdrawal of the southwest monsoon from the entire country.
The hazard is expected to continue affecting low-lying flood-prone communities, riverine areas, coastal settlements and landslide-prone hilly terrain, particularly across southern and western parts of Tanintharyi Region. Launglon Township remains the area of greatest concern due to the severity of flooding and landslide impacts. Given the saturated ground conditions and damaged infrastructure, there remains a risk of additional localized flooding, further slope instability and secondary landslides during the immediate response period.
The humanitarian impact has increased significantly since the onset of the event. A Rapid Needs Assessment conducted by the Myanmar Red Cross Society (MRCS), in coordination with local authorities, indicates that approximately 49,371 people and 7,629 houses have been affected across four townships: Dawei, Launglon, Thayetchaung and Tanintharyi. An estimated 9,032 people were temporarily displaced and accommodated in 24 evacuation centres. The most severe impacts were reported in Launglon Township, where more than 400 households (approximately 2,560 people) were affected and over 400 houses were destroyed. A total of 25 deaths and 11 injuries were also reported.
Floods and landslides have damaged roads, bridges, water sources and other critical infrastructure, limiting access to essential services and humanitarian assistance. Rapid assessments conducted by MRCS identified food, emergency shelter, essential household items, water-source rehabilitation, emergency sanitation support and access to healthcare as priority needs. While food and drinking water are currently available in many accessible locations through support from local authorities, community-based organizations and private donors, shortages persist in isolated villages where access remains restricted.
According to weather forecasts issued by DMH, isolated heavy rainfall was forecast for Tanintharyi Region, with warnings issued for communities living in flood-prone and landslide-risk areas. Although DMH announced the withdrawal of the southwest monsoon from Myanmar on 6 October 2026, the combination of saturated soil conditions, damaged drainage systems and ongoing recovery challenges is expected to maintain an elevated risk of recurrent flooding and landslides throughout the immediate response period.
