--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-11-19) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
01-11-2026
What happened, where and when?
On 1 November 2026, heavy and persistent rainfall associated with the ongoing El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) phenomenon triggered flooding, river overflows, landslides and mudslides across several areas of Ecuador, causing damage to housing, livelihoods, road infrastructure, water systems, health facilities, educational establishments and other essential services. The impacts were particularly severe in the provinces of Esmeraldas, Guayas, El Oro, Galápagos, Santa Elena, Los Ríos, Manabí, Bolívar and Santo Domingo de los Tsáchilas. As of 3 November 2026, more than 55,000 people and over 12,000 homes had been affected, while damage to transportation networks, water systems and basic services further increased humanitarian needs and access constraints in affected communities.
The emergency occurred within the context of a strengthening ENSO event. Oceanic and atmospheric monitoring conducted by the National ERFEN Committee identified elevated ocean heat content extending toward the eastern Pacific, positive sea-level anomalies of up to 45 cm in the central-eastern Pacific, 40 cm around the Galápagos Islands and 35 cm along the Ecuadorian coast, the propagation of warm Kelvin waves and a deepened thermocline. Sea surface temperature anomalies in the Niño 1+2 region reached up to +5.2°C above average, creating favourable conditions for above-normal rainfall and increasing the likelihood of severe hydrometeorological impacts across the country.
Anticipating these conditions, the Government of Ecuador progressively strengthened preparedness measures and, on 29 August 2026, the National Secretariat for Risk Management (SNGR) declared a nationwide Red Alert through Resolution No. SNGR-238-2026 due to the risk of flooding, landslides and other hydrometeorological hazards. Based on the forecasts and available lead time, the Ecuadorian Red Cross activated its ENSO National Contingency Plan and launched an Imminent DREF operation focused on anticipatory actions in Esmeraldas, Guayas, Los Ríos and Santo Domingo de los Tsáchilas, including community preparedness, strengthening of Early Warning Systems, temporary protection of homes, safeguarding of water systems, risk communication and institutional readiness measures.
During October and early November, forecasted impacts progressively materialized and expanded geographically, resulting in recurrent flooding, river overflows, landslides and disruptions to essential services across multiple provinces. Assessments and official reports indicate that the scale, severity and duration of impacts now exceed the scope originally envisaged under the 45-day Imminent DREF operation, requiring a transition to a Response DREF. The operation will support affected populations through emergency response activities while maintaining anticipatory actions in at-risk communities where forecast impacts have not yet materialized.
According to forecasts issued by INAMHI, the ERFEN Committee and NOAA, which estimates a probability of more than 90 per cent that the current ENSO event could reach a very strong intensity, above-average rainfall is expected to persist during the coming months and into the 2026-2027 rainy season. Additional flooding, river overflows, landslides and damage to critical infrastructure and essential services remain likely, particularly in coastal provinces, low-lying areas and communities exposed to recurrent flooding and territorial isolation.
