Crisis / situation: Mozambique: Floods - Dec 2025

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-08-24) ---
Highlights

In May, insecurity in Cabo Delgado intensified, particularly in southern districts, displacing over 17,000 people, including more than 8,800 children (51%).
UNICEF-supported health interventions reached 20,972 children through measles vaccination and 9,032 people through Integrated Mobile Brigades.
UNICEF nutrition services reached 5,946 children under five with screening, treatment and preventive interventions, while 3,558 caregivers received nutrition support and counselling.
More than 16,000 children benefited from UNICEF-supported education interventions, including 9,371 flood-affected children in Gaza and over 6,700 children in Cabo Delgado.
UNICEF provided child protection services to over 32,000 children, including psychosocial support, case management services and birth registration.
UNICEF supported WASH interventions reached more than 50,000 people affected by conflict, floods and cholera across four provinces.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-08-23) ---
Highlights

Insecurity and displacement dynamics in northern Mozambique remained volatile but localized, with approximately 3,259 people, 51,7 percent children on the move.
UNICEF-supported health and nutrition interventions in northern Mozambique resulted in 443,013 people vaccinated against cholera, 118,136 children under five vaccinated against polio, and more than 6,500 people receiving primary health care, nutrition screening and treatment services, including 185 children admitted for acute malnutrition treatment.
UNICEF supported continued access to learning for conflict-affected children in Memba district through the distribution of education supplies, benefiting more than 15,800 children.
UNICEF child protection interventions provided psychosocial support and case management services to more than 12,000 children in Nampula province.
UNICEF provided safe water, hygiene kits and cholera prevention services to conflict-, flood-and cholera-affected populations, reaching more than 35,000 people.
