KEY HIGHLIGHTS

WFP Iraq, in partnership with the Ministry of Education, launched the &quot;Healthy Kitchen&quot; pilot to enhance the National School Feeding Programme. Financed by the Ministry and supported by WFP technical assistance, centrally managed kitchens will distribute nutritious, locally sourced meals to school clusters. supporting local agriculture and vulnerable livelihoods and generating evidence for a sustainable, government-led scale-up of school feeding in Iraq.
Humanitarian funding shortfalls are impacting WFP’s ability to maintain assistance. Continued donor engagement is critical to safeguard support for displaced populations and sustain resilience‑building initiatives.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-09-02) ---
Event development
Events impact
The escalation of regional hostilities during February 2026 had significant humanitarian repercussions across Iraq. A series of airstrikes, missile incidents, and drone attacks occurred within Iraqi territory, including strikes in Jurf Al-Sakhar south of Baghdad and repeated attacks targeting strategic locations in the Kurdistan Region. These incidents heightened insecurity among civilians and increased concerns over the potential expansion of hostilities into populated areas.
The impact on communities extended beyond the immediate effects of the attacks. Fear of further escalation, coupled with uncertainty regarding the security situation, prompted precautionary movements among families residing near locations perceived to be at risk.
Households in Baghdad, Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Diyala, Wasit, Maysan, and Basra reported concerns over personal safety, disruptions to transportation routes, and potential interruptions to essential services. Many families temporarily relocated to stay with relatives or within host communities, while others restricted their movements and adopted precautionary measures to reduce exposure to potential risks.
According to the International Organization for Migration (IOM), at least 90 households had been identified as displaced in relation to the crisis by the end of March 2026. In addition, increased cross-border mobility was observed as the conflict affected movement patterns throughout the region. By the end of March, IOM recorded 843 arrivals from Iran, 551 departures to Iran by Iraqi citizens, and 181 returnees from Iran.
The humanitarian consequences extended beyond displacement. Airspace restrictions, security measures, and concerns over the safety of critical infrastructure disrupted livelihoods, access to services, and freedom of movement. Vulnerable groups, including women, children, older persons, persons with disabilities, and those already displaced by previous crises, faced heightened protection concerns and increased barriers to accessing essential services. The escalation also placed additional pressure on host communities and local service providers, particularly in areas already supporting internally displaced persons, returnees, and refugee populations.
While the scale of displacement remained localized compared to previous crises in Iraq, the events demonstrated the vulnerability of communities to regional instability and underscored the importance of preparedness, protection, and humanitarian assistance for populations affected by sudden security shocks.
