Crisis / situation: Iraq

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-09-29) ---
CONTEXT
As of the end of August 2026, Iraq generously hosts over 343,000 refugees and asylum-seekers, the majority (88%) of whom are Syrians. Close to 80% of this population resides in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. While most refugees and asylumseekers live in urban and peri-urban settings, 30% remain in nine refugee camps across the country. Additionally, around 97,000 Internally Displaced People (IDPs) live in 18 IDP camps.1 UNHCR works with the Government of Iraq and partners to support refugee and asylumseeker access to protection and inclusion in public services, such as education and healthcare, as well as access to economic opportunities and in advancing solutions.

KEY HIGHLIGHTS

WFP is scaling climate-smart agriculture and water resilience across southern Iraq, supporting 120 smallholder farmers with drip irrigation and pastureland rehabilitation in Najaf and Anbar, and advancing an 80-farmer aquaponics initiative with the Basra Gas Company, demonstrating tangible progress in helping drought-affected communities adapt their livelihoods.
Green-skills and livelihood diversification programmes are reaching growing numbers of rural women, with 1,500 women engaged in green-skills training across Basra and Thi-Qar, complemented by mangrove restoration, wetland rehabilitation and market showcases such as the Baghdad Agricultural Week Fair, reflecting sustainability of WFP-supported livelihoods.
