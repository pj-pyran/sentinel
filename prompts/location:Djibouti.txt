Crisis / situation: Djibouti

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-10-15) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS

More than 3,700 refugees have arrived from Yemen since 10 September, increasing pressure on an already underfunded response. Food assistance has been suspended since August, and WFP requires USD 6.7 million to resume support and assist new arrivals.
WFP handed over the Humanitarian Logistics Base (HLB) to the Government of Djibouti, a milestone in the transition to national leadership of the country’s strategic logistics infrastructure. WFP will provide six months of technical support and capacity strengthening to ensure a seamless transition and continued operational readiness.
A delegation from the U.S. Bureau of Disaster and Humanitarian Response (DHR) visited Djibouti, including Markazi Refugee Village, to meet refugees, local authorities and partners. The visit coincided with a new U.S. contribution received in mid-September, which has enabled WFP to launch procurement and prepare the resumption of assistance to refugees.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-10-14) ---
Activation Overview
Djibouti Red Crescent National Society has activated it’s Simplified Early Action Protocol for Flood.
Djibouti seasonal forecast for October-December shows a rainfall impact related to the El Nino event. In line with this outlook ICPAC has released a weekly forecast that indicates a potential rainfall in the coastal zones including Djibouti, Tadjourah, obock, Arta and few areas of Ali Sabieh will be affected.
National Society has been monitoring the threshold and for the period of 13-20 October 2026 the agreed trigger which is total rainfall 50mm and above in Djibouti and its Suburbs is reached (as per the forecast it is in the range of 50- 100mm). 13 - 20 October 2026 - ICPAC
DRCS will target 2,500 people living in flood-prone areas who are expected to be severely affected, prioritizing low-income households; female- or child-headed households; large households with three or more children; households with children under five; and households with elderly members.
Anticipatory actions will be implemented in Djibouti among the communities living in Bouloas area within seven days to raise awareness of the forecasted flood event through early warning messages including dissemination through National media houses, sandbagging to protect shelters, strengthen community resilience by distributing and replacing roofing materials, promotion of hygiene to prevent health risks associated with stagnant water.
