Crisis / situation: Djibouti

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-09-14) ---
The UN Emergency Relief Coordinator allocated USD 2 million to support anticipatory actions ahead of the expected El Niño drought, enabling UN agencies to deliver coordinated multi-sector assistance to more than 53,000 vulnerable people, including refugees, before conditions deteriorate further.
A delegation from U.S. Foreign Assistance visited the Ali Addeh refugee settlements on 16 August to observe WFP’s food and cash assistance activities firsthand, reinforcing donor engagement and confidence in the refugee response.
WFP and the Government of Djibouti are preparing to sign a Memorandum of Agreement that formalizes the transfer of the Humanitarian Logistics Base (HLB) to national ownership, marking a key milestone towards greater government leadership and long-term sustainability.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-09-14) ---
Description
Since 10 September 2026, the Obock region has been experiencing a significant influx of people arriving from Yemen, amid a deteriorating security and humanitarian situation in the country. According to estimates shared by ONARS, approximately 1,600 Yemeni refugees, mainly women and children, have arrived in the Obock region to date.
Some individuals are reported to have been rescued at sea by the Djiboutian authorities after their boats ran out of fuel. The main arrival points identified are Obock Port, as well as the coastal areas of Khor Angar, Moulhoul and Siâm, where groups of refugees have reportedly landed and are currently awaiting arrangements for their transfer to Obock town.

Summary
The Djibouti Red Crescent Obock Branch, through its Restoring Family Links (RFL) services, facilitated telephone calls for 28 refugee families who had recently arrived from Yemen. To help separated family members re-establish contact with their relatives, provide reassurance about their safety and whereabouts, and reduce the distress and uncertainty caused by the displacement. The calls were facilitated by CRD staff and volunteers as part of the immediate humanitarian response, particularly for families who had limited or no access to communication services.
Actions taken by others
The Government of Djibouti has mobilized relevant authorities to respond to the influx of refugees arriving from Yemen. ONARS is leading the registration of newly arrived refugees and their referral to appropriate services, while the Ministry of Social Affairs and Solidarity (MASS) is providing targeted food assistance, particularly for children. The Djiboutian Coast Guard has also conducted search and rescue operations at sea and provided assistance to people in distress, including those whose boats ran out of fuel. In parallel, IOM has provided food assistance in the form of ready-to-eat meals to newly arrived refugees.
