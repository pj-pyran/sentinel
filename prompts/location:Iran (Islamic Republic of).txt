Crisis / situation: Iran (Islamic Republic of)

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-10-19) ---
This Humanitarian Update is produced by OCHA Regional Office for the Middle East and North Africa (ROMENA) on behalf of and in collaboration with UN agencies and humanitarian organisations. It covers the period from 1 to 30 September 2026.
HIGHLIGHTS

In early September, strikes in four southern provinces reportedly killed 18 people and injured 142.
Household purchasing power deteriorated further amid rising food prices and currency depreciation.
School year started in person nationwide and temporarily online in Hormozgan Province.
The Ministry of Health reported supply shortages of 32 essential medicines, due to procurement challenges, import delays and transport disruptions.
Flooding reportedly damaged homes, infrastructure and farmland across Mazandaran, Gilan and Golestan provinces, killing one person, and leaving two missing. The Iranian Red Crescent Society (IRCS) provided assistance to 1,027 people.
Cash assistance reached 24,175 people in 6,166 households across five provinces, while an additional 618 highly vulnerable refugee households and 1,719 women and girls received targeted cash support to meet essential needs and protection-related expenses.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
The humanitarian response continued throughout September amid renewed hostilities early in the month in the southern provinces as well as flooding on 8-9 and 27-28 September in the northern provinces. On 1 September, damages to civilian buildings and critical infrastructure were reported in Sistan and Baluchestan, Hormozgan, Bushehr, Khuzestan and Kerman provinces, including Sirik Port, Jiroft Airport and the energy infrastructure in Assaluyeh. The Ministry of Health and Medical Education (MoHME) reported that 18 people were killed (including two women and one child) and 142 injured (including 61 women and 44 children), between 30 August and 2 September. Reports additionally indicated that a commercial vessel was impacted near Qeshm Island on 13 September and two fishing boats near Larak Island and Bandar Kargan on 14 September.
In northern Iran, heavy rainfall, storms and flooding reportedly damaged housing, public infrastructure and agricultural assets in Mazandaran and Gilan provinces on 8 and 9 September and in Golestan Province on 27 and 28 September. One person reportedly died and two went missing, while the Iranian Red Crescent Society (IRCS) provided emergency assistance to 1,027 people affected by these floods.
HUMANITARIAN IMPACT AND NEEDS
Health
Damage to health facilities and electricity infrastructure continued to disrupt essential health services and threaten cold-chain operations. Southern and southeastern provinces remained at risk of vector-borne and other communicable disease outbreaks, requiring strengthened surveillance and response capacity. The demand for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) among healthcare workers continued to be high. On 22 September, MoHME reported supply shortages of 32 essential medicines, due to procurement challenges, import delays and transport disruptions. This has raised concerns over potential impacts on the availability of other medicines, diagnostic supplies and medical equipment, and the continuity of health service delivery.
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH)
Communities in Hormozgan, Isfahan and Sistan and Baluchestan provinces continued to face challenges in reliable access to safe drinking water due to damage sustained by water infrastructure and essential services during the July escalation. On 22 September, the Minister of Energy reported that since 28 February airstrikes had affected more than 2,000 water and electricity infrastructure sites nationwide. Priority needs include restoring access to safe water and expanding emergency water storage and treatment capacity.
Food Security
Food affordability deteriorated further amid high inflation, exchange-rate pressures and disruptions affecting the import of essential goods. Year-on-year food inflation reached 128.6 per cent in August (increasing by 0.5 per cent from July). The Iranian rial (IRR) continued to depreciate, with the exchange rate exceeding IRR 2.5 million per US dollar by 29 September, an 18 per cent decline in value relative to the previous month. The World Food Programme (WFP) reported in September that the costs for the standard food basket rose by 5.8 per cent in one month, reaching IRR 310.2 million (approximately US $173). Rising prices of nutrient-dense foods are increasing the risks of reduced dietary diversity. Priority needs include affordable, nutritious food and the protection of household purchasing power.
Protection
Protection risks continued to affect vulnerable groups, particularly women and girls facing challenges related to access to dignity items, menstrual hygiene supplies, and MHPSS. Refugees and other foreign nationals faced heightened protection risks as economic hardship intensified and documentation barriers, including the costs and administrative requirements associated with renewing residence documents, continued to limit access to food, housing, healthcare, education and legal stay. Priority needs included dignity items, MHPSS, regularization support, legal counselling, financial assistance, access to education and strengthened protection referral pathways.
Education
The 2026/27 academic year started on 23 September with in-person learning, except in Hormozgan Province, where all schools temporarily shifted to virtual and other non-face-to-face modalities for two weeks starting on 26 September. Several schools remained non-operational due to sustained damages or being used as collective shelters for temporarily relocated families. This has reduced the available learning space and increased pressure on functioning schools. Priority needs include rehabilitation of damaged and non-operational schools, expanding classroom capacity, supporting safe transport, and ensuring inclusive access to education among affected children.
Shelter and Non-Food Items
Low-income and vulnerable households continued to face difficulties meeting housing and basic needs. In Bandar Abbas (Hormozgan Province), 200 households remained temporarily sheltered in schools, according to the International Organization for Migration (IOM). Priority needs include hygiene kits, food assistance, cash assistance and support for households in temporary accommodation.
Early Recovery
Conflict-related restrictions and damages continue to constrain fisheries in Hormozgan and Bushehr provinces. Affected fishing communities urgently need the restoration of fishing assets, facilities and post-harvest services to restart production, reduce losses and restore livelihoods.
RESPONSE
Health
The World Health Organization (WHO) delivered MHPSS services to more than 15,000 healthcare workers and donated 10,000 cholera rapid diagnostic tests to the Iranian Centre for Communicable Disease Control for use in Hormozgan, Khuzestan, Kerman and Sistan and Baluchestan provinces. The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) provided sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and MHPSS awareness-raising activities to 2,396 people (1,846 females and 550 males). Furthermore, 1,600 pregnant and lactating women received antenatal, postnatal, nutrition, and mental health awareness sessions through health providers. Among these beneficiary women, a total of 1,325 received cash assistance through the State Welfare Organization. UNFPA and MoHME also trained 248 social workers on cash and voucher assistance and basic SRH. The United Nations Refugee Agency (UNHCR) continued supporting inclusive public health services by financing health personnel through MoHME and advancing the procurement and delivery of essential medicines and medical equipment for facilities serving refugees and host communities. The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) delivered five additional generators to MoHME as part of its ongoing support to essential health services and medical cold chains in 142 priority health facilities, bringing the total number of generators delivered to 46. In Kerman City, INTERSOS began rehabilitating two primary health facilities.
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH)
The United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF), in partnership with MoHME, installed 12 water tanks with a combined capacity of 105,000 litres, improving safe-water storage for more than 3,150 people and health facilities affected by intermittent or inadequate supply in Chabahar and Iranshahr counties (Sistan and Baluchestan Province). UNDP delivered 50 towable water tanks and 75 chlorination and disinfection units across Sistan and Baluchestan, Hormozgan and Isfahan provinces to strengthen emergency water-supply and treatment capacity. UNDP installed pumps and electrical cables at an affected pumping station in Chabahar, restoring the water-transmission system. UNHCR distributed more than 13,000 family hygiene kits to refugees and host communities across 10 provinces. Gaps in needs data, rising procurement costs, supply constraints and delays in the delivery of internationally sourced equipment continued to slow the implementation and assessment of WASH activities.
Food Security
WFP continued to deliver cash and food assistance to 35,611 highly vulnerable refugees across 20 settlements, including the delivery of bread and wheat flour for household baking.
Protection
UNFPA, through Relief International and local civil society organizations, provided integrated protection and MHPSS services in Hormozgan, Khuzestan and Tehran provinces. UNFPA reached 1,719 women and girls with cash assistance to purchase essential dignity and menstrual hygiene items and provided individual or group MHPSS services to 1,682 women and girls. Of those receiving MHPSS services, 267 also received cash assistance to help cover the costs of accessing five individual counselling sessions. A further 4,974 women and girls with disabilities across 11 provinces received menstrual-health awareness and cash assistance to address menstrual hygiene needs. UNHCR continued registration and verification of refugees and other foreign nationals and provided counselling, legal assistance, case management, MHPSS support and referrals.
Education
The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) continued the rehabilitation and replacement of equipment for 42 schools across Kerman, Hormozgan and Tehran provinces, and delivered catch-up classes to 2,000 conflict-affected children in Tehran and Hormozgan provinces. NRC launched a learning and MHPSS programme at two school-based collective shelters for temporarily relocated families in Hormozgan Province targeting 200 children.
Early Recovery
The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) in coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture Jihad and the Iran Fisheries Organization started a project to support 300 affected fishing households in Hormozgan Province.
Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA)
WFP provided emergency cash assistance to 24,175 people in 6,166 households across Sistan and Baluchestan, Bushehr, Tehran, Hormozgan and Alborz provinces, targeting those affected by displacement, housing or livelihood losses, and conflict-related injury or death of a family member. UNHCR provided MPCA to 618 highly vulnerable refugee households to meet essential needs amid declining purchasing power.
