Crisis / situation: Iran (Islamic Republic of)

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-10-06) ---
This Humanitarian Update is produced by OCHA Regional Office for the Middle East and North Africa (ROMENA) on behalf of and in collaboration with UN agencies and humanitarian organisations. It covers the period from 1 to 31 August 2026.
HIGHLIGHTS

In southern and south-eastern areas, an estimated 200,000 people require humanitarian assistance following the re-escalation of hostilities in July. Up to 60 per cent of the fishing fleet reportedly remains inactive, disrupting the livelihoods of approximately 37,000 fishers. Damage to infrastructure and the presence of debris, coupled with forecast of above-average rainfall and potential flooding, continue to pose significant public health risks.
UN agencies are providing test kits for water-borne diseases and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) emergency supplies to support the restoration of safe drinking water. They are also assessing livelihood support needs in the fisheries sector.
An oil spill in August reportedly affected approximately 32,000 m² of Qeshm’s coastline and 90,000 m² of surrounding waters, raising concerns about its impact on marine and coastal ecosystems.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
The humanitarian situation in Iran continues to be shaped by recurring escalations and their economic and operational consequences. Disruptions to basic services and infrastructure persist, while inflation and market uncertainty continue to erode household purchasing power. Restrictions on maritime and air transport routes, along with trade disruptions, have also affected the import and distribution of pharmaceutical supplies.
The July escalation increased humanitarian needs, particularly in the south. According to a local partner reporting to IOM, around 300,000 people were affected across Hormozgan, Bushehr, Khuzestan and Sistan and Baluchestan provinces, including an estimated 200,000 people in need of humanitarian assistance. Damage to water and electricity infrastructure has disrupted access to safe drinking water, while forecasts of above-average rainfall and potential flooding could further worsen humanitarian and public health risks.
An oil spill reported between 10 and 13 August from vessels damaged in the Strait of Hormuz affected an estimated 32,000 m² of coastline and 90,000 m² of surrounding waters off Qeshm Island, raising concerns over impacts on marine and coastal ecosystems. Cleanup operations were reported to be largely completed by 24 August.
HUMANITARIAN IMPACT AND NEEDS
Health
In southern and south-eastern areas, infrastructure damage and debris from the July escalation, combined with forecast of above-average rainfall and potential flooding linked to El Niño conditions, may increase the risk of dengue, malaria and other vector-borne diseases. Nationwide, out-of-pocket payments account for an estimated 43 per cent of total health expenditure, placing additional strain on households amid declining purchasing power. Continued support is needed to sustain essential health services, including cold chains, lifesaving medicines, diagnostic capacity, primary healthcare, and maternal and reproductive health services.
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH)
In August, the National Water and Wastewater Engineering Company reported that 410 water and wastewater networks and 2,000 electricity network assets had been damaged since March 2026, affecting power generation and water services. In Hormozgan, Isfahan and Sistan and Baluchestan provinces, damage caused by the July escalation has disrupted access to safe drinking water. Priority needs include repairing damaged infrastructure and restoring reliable water services for vulnerable populations in affected areas.
Food Security
Despite well-functioning markets, purchasing power continues to decline. Food inflation reached 128.1 per cent in July 2026, compared with headline year-on-year inflation of 87.9 per cent. Although the minimum wage increased by 60 per cent in March-April 2026, real food purchasing power remained around 30 per cent lower than a year earlier, while the gap between income and minimum expenditure widened by 137 per cent. Food assistance needs remain significant among conflict-affected households, particularly those reliant on fisheries and coastal livelihoods in Hormozgan, Sistan and Baluchestan, and Bushehr provinces.
Protection
The reported disruptions to health services, water and sanitation systems, and livelihoods disproportionately affect children under five, pregnant and lactating women, women-headed households, persons with disabilities, Afghan refugees, and other vulnerable groups. Access to mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), protection services, and essential dignity and menstrual hygiene supplies remains a priority, particularly for vulnerable women and girls.
Education
Education needs remain significant. Humanitarian partners have identified 28 schools in Hormozgan, Alborz and Tehran provinces that still require rehabilitation, with funding shortfalls risking further disruptions to learning for vulnerable children. While Afghan refugee children with valid documentation continue to access national education services, conflict-related population movements and documentation barriers may increase the risk of exclusion and interrupted schooling.
Shelter and Non-Food Items
Approximately 200 households displaced from the southern islands were temporarily accommodated in schools in Bandar Abbas and require support to meet their basic needs. In Sistan and Baluchestan Province, an estimated 20 per cent of the population in the southern district of Chabahar, or around 50,000 people, has been temporarily relocated to rural areas elsewhere in the province. Reduced household incomes and declining purchasing power are limiting access to shelter repairs and essential household items.
Early Recovery
Approximately US$105.9 million in direct and indirect losses to the fisheries sector have reportedly been recorded in Hormozgan and Bushehr provinces. Up to 60 per cent of the fishing fleet in affected areas remains inactive, disrupting the incomes of 37,000 fishers and threatening the livelihoods of an estimated 300,000 people. Damage to a major shrimp hatchery in Bushehr has further disrupted production and affected local workers and their families. Debris clearance operations have been carried out nationwide, particularly in southern provinces and Tehran.
Cultural Heritage
Since 28 February 2026, a total of 1,341 cultural heritage sites and museum objects have reportedly been damaged, including seven UNESCO World Heritage sites and 57 museums. In addition, 16 protected areas, including four national parks, two wildlife refuges and two UNESCO biosphere reserves, were reportedly affected. UNESCO has so far confirmed damage to two World Heritage sites and impacts on 628 museums, cultural institutions and heritage assets.
RESPONSE
Health
The World Health Organization (WHO) delivered 10,000 cholera rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs) and is procuring an additional 150,000 malaria and dengue RDTs for Hormozgan, Kerman, Khuzestan and Sistan and Baluchestan provinces. To help maintain essential health services, WHO supplied five backup power units, including three uninterruptible power supplies and two generators, to hospitals in Lorestan, Hormozgan and Khuzestan provinces, benefiting more than 84,000 patients annually.
The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) delivered 41 generators to the Ministry of Health and Medical Education across 18 provinces to support backup power for health facilities and vaccine storage, against an identified need for 101 generators. The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) is providing cash assistance to 1,600 women and girls in Tehran, Khuzestan and West Azerbaijan provinces, as well as around 5,000 women with disabilities across 11 provinces, while strengthening social work capacity, reproductive health services and support for Afghan refugees. The United Nations Agency for Children (UNICEF) integrated risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) into its emergency health response through 293 frontline health backpacks targeting 5,300 vulnerable people. The United Nations Refugee Agency (UNHCR) supported primary healthcare in 15 provinces by financing 208 health personnel and supplying essential medicines and medical equipment through two consignments to health facilities.
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH)
UNICEF provided pre-positioned WASH emergency supplies to Hormozgan, Khuzestan and Bushehr provinces, strengthening water safety and sanitation services for more than one million people in affected areas. UNDP is supporting the restoration of safe drinking water in conflict-affected communities in Hormozgan, Isfahan and Sistan and Baluchestan provinces, with a focus on water-stressed areas in Chabahar, Sirik and Jask to reduce reliance on unsafe water sources and mitigate the risk of waterborne diseases.
Food Security
WFP is supporting 195,000 people with food and cash assistance across Tehran, Alborz, Bushehr, Hormozgan and Sistan and Baluchestan provinces. It also provided bread and wheat flour assistance to 35,611 refugees through supported bakeries and flour distributions. The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) delivered multipurpose cash assistance to an additional 524 affected families in Hormozgan, complementing support already provided to 8,922 households.
Protection
UNFPA is supporting 4,800 women and girls in Tehran, Khuzestan and Hormozgan provinces through cash assistance for dignity items, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and protection awareness activities. UNICEF provided a second round of financial support to 58 residential care centres, benefiting 661 children without parental care across East Azerbaijan, Khuzestan, Qazvin, Kurdistan and Lorestan provinces. In Hormozgan Province, NRC is preparing to launch protection services for 72 temporarily relocated households, including psychosocial support, referrals and cash assistance. IOM has provided one month of multipurpose cash assistance to 6,281 people (1,525 households) in Tehran, Yazd, Isfahan, Fars, Alborz and Khorasan Razavi provinces to help meet urgent needs.
Education
UNHCR is constructing four schools in Yazd, Qom, Isfahan and Tehran provinces, which are expected to benefit around 3,000 refugee and host-community students, with three additional schools planned in Fars, Tehran and Hormozgan. NRC is rehabilitating 16 conflict-damaged schools for approximately 4,000 refugee and Iranian students, while also providing education support to 200 displaced children in Hormozgan and catch-up classes for 2,000 conflict-affected children in Tehran and Hormozgan.
Shelter and Non-Food Items
IOM provided cash-for-shelter assistance to 627 people (173 households) for essential repairs in Tehran, Fars, Isfahan and Alborz provinces. It also donated 200 shelter kits and 200 kitchen sets to the Iranian Red Crescent Society for distribution to affected households nationwide, with each batch expected to benefit around 1,000 people.
Early Recovery
FAO is assessing the needs of conflict-affected fisheries in southern Iran to inform potential livelihood support. UNDP has provided debris-removal machinery to support clearance operations in Tehran, enabling the removal of up to 1,500 m³ of debris per day. It also supplied 22 generators to the Tehran Mitigation and Management Organization to help maintain emergency operations and essential services during power disruptions.
Cultural Heritage
UNESCO is mobilizing resources for environmental recovery, ecosystem restoration and monitoring, biodiversity conservation, and damage monitoring and documentation of affected cultural heritage sites.
Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA)
IOM provided one month of multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to 6,281 people (1,525 households), including migrants and host communities, across Tehran, Yazd, Isfahan, Fars, Alborz and Khorasan Razavi provinces to help meet urgent needs. UNHCR also provided MPCA to 765 of the most vulnerable refugee households nationwide to support their basic needs amid rapid inflation and rising prices.

KEY HIGHLIGHTS

Despite the escalation in the region, WFP remains steadfast in its commitment to the people it serves. The operation in Iran continues without disruption, with teams on the ground ensuring uninterrupted delivery of food and cash assistance to the most vulnerable refugees residing in settlements. WFP is also in close consultation with the Government to explore opportunities to scale up its operations.
WFP completed the first round of emergency cash assistance in response to the recent escalation, reaching 1,713 affected Iranians so far.
