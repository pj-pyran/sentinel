Crisis / situation: Thailand

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-10-01) ---
Description
According to the Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM), Ministry of Interior, Thailand has experienced prolonged heavy rainfall since mid-September, resulting in widespread flooding across the country. The flooding has been driven by intense rainfall, river overflows and increased water discharge, with water levels continuing to rise in several central and eastern provinces. The situation remains dynamic, with water levels rising, stabilising or receding depending on the location.
Water levels continued to rise in several provinces, including Nakhon Sawan, Uthai Thani, Lop Buri, Chai Nat, Sing Buri, Suphan Buri, Kanchanaburi, Phra Nakhon Si Ayutthaya, Samut Prakan, Pathum Thani, Nakhon Pathom, Ratchaburi, Nakhon Nayok, Prachin Buri, Chachoengsao, Sa Kaeo, Chon Buri and parts of northeastern Thailand. In other affected areas, floodwaters remained stable or gradually receded. Bangkok was also significantly affected, with approximately 329,000 households impacted.
As of 1 October 2026, flooding remained ongoing in 30 provinces, affecting 191 districts, 1,228 sub-districts and 8,714 villages. An estimated 1,177,404 households (3,352,395 people), including those in Bangkok, were affected.

Description
Thailand has experienced widespread flooding following heavy rainfall during 16–28 September 2026. According to the Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation(DDPM) report, flooding affected 41 provinces, covering 214 districts, 1,017 sub-districts and 5,164 vilages, with approximately 927,400 households (2,561,569 people) affected. A total of 23 deaths were reported, with no injuries recorded.
As of 28 September, flooding remained ongoing in 28 provinces, covering 160 districts, 849 sub-districts and 4,685 villages, affecting approximately 921,488 households (2,547,117 people). Bangkok was also affected, with approximately 329,000 households reported affected.
The most significant impacts were reported in the Central, Eastern and Bangkok metropolitan areas, including Samut Prakan, Pathum Thani, Bangkok, Chon Buri, Nakhon Pathom, Ayutthaya, Suphan Buri and Nakhon Nayok. Several areas continued to experience rising or stagnant water levels.
Particularly significant impacts included:

Samut Prakan: 260,784 households affected; 8 deaths.
Pathum Thani: 113,323 households affected; 1 death.
Bangkok: approximately 329,000 households affected and 4 deaths.
Chon Buri: 118,723 households affected.
Ayutthaya: 28,176 households affected; 1 death.
Suphan Buri: 9,985 households affected.
Sa Kaeo: 8,719 households affected; 6 deaths.

The situation was driven by heavy and accumulated rainfall, river overflow and increased discharge from the Chao Phraya Dam. The DDPM warned that further rainfall could result in flash flooding, accumulated flooding, river overflow and landslides, particularly in low-lying areas and areas near waterways.

Actions taken by National Society
Summary
The Thai Red Cross Society (TRCS) has activated its disaster relief mechanisms through the Relief and Community Health Bureau (RCHB), Red Cross Stations, provincial Red Cross chapters and volunteers. Bangkok. On 28 September, RCHB operated the Thai Red Cross Mobile Kitchen to prepare and distribute 8,000 cooked meals to flood-affected communities and shelters in six areas: • Khlong Sam Wa shelter – 1,500 meals • Rom Klao Housing Estate, Lat Krabang – 800 meals • Suan Luang shelter – 200 meals • Riverside community, Wang Thonglang – 1,500 meals • Saphan Sung – 2,000 meals • Bang Kapi – 2,000 meals An additional 100 ready-to-eat meal sets were provided at Amornphan Condominium, Khan Na Yao. Thai Red Cross volunteers supported food preparation and distribution. On 27 September, the Thai Red Cross also distributed 1,000 cooked meals in Bangkok, including 200 meals at Ban Bang Kapi School temporary shelter and 800 meals at Khlong Chan flats. Due to high flood levels in Khlong Chan, where normal vehicles could not access affected communities, RCHB used a high-clearance Unimog vehicle to transport cooked food and essential supplies to affected residents. Provinces outside Bangkok The Thai Red Cross has mobilised provincial Red Cross chapters, the Office of Provincial Red Cross Affairs, RCHB and 12 Red Cross Stations outside Bangkok to support affected communities, particularly in: • Nakhon Nayok • Samut Prakan • Pathum Thani • Chachoengsao Where widespread flooding or access constraints made it difficult to establish mobile kitchens or transport dry food, provincial Red Cross chapters were authorised to contract local/private food providers to prepare cooked meals for affected communities. The Thai Red Cross has also authorised the use of high-clearance vehicles and larger boats to support transportation and delivery of cooked meals to areas affected by high flood levels.
Actions taken by Federation
Summary
IFRC is maintaining close coordination with TRCS and monitoring the evolving humanitarian situation.
Actions taken by others
The Government and local authorities have activated multi-agency response measures, including: • Search, rescue and immediate assistance: Provincial DDPM offices, district authorities, police, military units, local authorities, volunteers and foundations have been deployed to assist affected communities, move household belongings to higher ground and accelerate drainage. • Evacuation and temporary shelters: Temporary evacuation centres have been established in several provinces. For example, 27 shelters were opened in Sa Kaeo, while shelters were also established in Chanthaburi and Nakhon Ratchasima. • Flood drainage and emergency equipment: Authorities have deployed pumps, boats and other equipment to support drainage and rescue operations. In Nakhon Sawan, for example, the regional DDPM centre provided 5 flat-bottom boats, 2 rescue boats and 10 paddle boats. • Government monitoring and coordination: The Government established a Joint Information and Communication Center (JICC) to consolidate and communicate flood, rainfall and risk information. The Government also instructed provinces to provide daily situation and assistance updates. • Water management: As of 28 September, the Chao Phraya Dam was discharging approximately 1,900 m³/s. Authorities warned that discharge could be increased to 1,500–2,000 m³/s depending on conditions, potentially increasing downstream water levels. • Health and hospital preparedness: The Ministry of Public Health instructed hospitals in high-risk areas to maintain 24-hour preparedness, secure essential medicines, oxygen, electricity, water, communications and referral systems, with contingency stocks for at least 72 hours. • Bangkok response: Bangkok authorities have supported drainage, traffic management, emergency assistance and temporary shelters. The DDPM reported 233 temporary shelters opened in Bangkok.
