Crisis / situation: Chad: Cholera Outbreak - Jun 2026

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-09-10) ---
As the rainy season sweeps across West and Central Africa, cholera is once again compounding communities’ vulnerabilities already grappling with conflict, displacement, poverty and limited access to basic services. From the shores of Lake Chad to the banks of the Congo and Oubangui rivers, the disease is spreading in overcrowded displacement sites, refugee camps and underserved urban neighborhoods, threatening lives and livelihoods.
Behind the numbers are families like that of Aisa Maani Tela and Kabiru Mohammed in north-east Nigeria. Borno State alone has recorded more than 59,000 suspected and confirmed cases and over 300 deaths as of late August. In Monguno, where thousands of displaced families live in overcrowded camps, Aisa remembers the night her daughters Kellu and Zara suddenly fell ill.
&quot;I kept thinking about how quickly children can die from illness,&quot; she said.
Both girls were diagnosed with cholera and treated through interventions supported by the Nigeria Humanitarian Fund and a US$4 million allocation from the Central Emergency Response Fund (CERF). The family also received water-treatment supplies, hygiene awareness support, and access to newly constructed sanitation facilities. For families uprooted by years of conflict, humanitarian aid has become a vital lifeline.
Further west, Cameroon's Far North Region is facing a rapidly expanding outbreak. Since late June, more than 1,250 cases and 31 deaths have been reported across nine health districts. The outbreak has already surpassed the scale of recent epidemics recorded in 2019, 2022 and 2024 in Cameroon. Particularly concerning is the situation in Minawao refugee camp, home to over 81,000 refugees, where dozens of cases have been recorded. To bolster the response, $1 million was allocated from CERF to support cholera prevention, treatment, water and sanitation interventions targeting 230,000 people, including children and refugee populations in high-risk border areas. The funding comes as humanitarian partners struggle with severe shortfalls under the country's humanitarian response plan.
In neighboring Chad, cholera has emerged alongside a worsening displacement crisis in the Lake Province. Violence has recently forced more than 75,000 people to flee, while cholera, confirmed in early August, caused 219 cases claiming the lives of eight persons by mid-August. Recognizing the dual emergency, humanitarian partners mobilized $7 million, including $4 million from CERF and $3 million from the Regional Humanitarian Fund for West and Central Africa. CERF resources are supporting emergency health, nutrition and cholera response activities, while the regional pooled fund is financing food aid, shelter, household items and water, sanitation and hygiene interventions. The allocation also enables national non-governmental organizations to lead frontline operations in hard-to-reach areas around Lake Chad.
Across the Congo Basin, cholera remains a persistent threat. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), Africa's largest cholera hotspot, over 39,400 suspected cases and 1,173 deaths were reported between January and August 2026. The eastern provinces of North Kivu and South Kivu remain the epicentre of the outbreak, accounting for more than half of all new weekly cases. Ongoing insecurity, people’s movements and inadequate water and sanitation infrastructure continue to drive transmission, placing additional strain on a health system already stretched by multiple humanitarian crises. To strengthen preparedness and response efforts, a $750,000 CERF Rapid Response anticipatory action allocation was activated, targeting cholera-affected and high-risk communities.
In the neighboring Republic of the Congo, authorities continue to battle an outbreak first declared in July 2025. By early September 2026, the country reported 1,598 cases and 130 deaths across multiple departments. An earlier CERF allocation of $1 million proved instrumental in reducing spreading, helping halt the spread in affected districts for nine consecutive weeks and preventing any deaths in cholera treatment centres. However, the outbreak has since expanded into new riverine areas, prompting renewed efforts to secure additional emergency funding, strengthen community engagement, improve surveillance, and support cross-border coordination with the DRC.
Meanwhile, in the Central African Republic (CAR), cholera has returned for the first time in a decade. Since the Government declared an epidemic in June 2026, 650 suspected cases and 44 deaths have been reported, with infections spreading from Bimbo and Mbaïki to the capital, Bangui. To contain the outbreak, CERF allocated $1 million to scale up health, water, sanitation and hygiene interventions, reaching nearly 89,000 people in affected and high-risk areas. Humanitarian partners are expanding disease surveillance, community engagement, water treatment, and hygiene promotion to prevent a wider public health emergency.
Across these six countries, cholera is deepening vulnerabilities amidst chronic gaps in access to safe water and sanitation. Yet timely funding from CERF and country and regional humanitarian pooled funds is helping partners act quickly, contain the outbreak and save lives. Sustained funding is critical to prevent further disease upsurge and protect the most vulnerable communities.
