For each of these events, a brief description, followed by public health measures implemented and an interpretation of the situation, is provided.
A table is provided at the end of the bulletin with information on all new and ongoing public health events currently being monitored in the region, as well as recent events that have been controlled and closed.
Major issues and challenges include
Mpox in Madagascar: Mpox transmission in Madagascar eased in the latest reporting week, with confirmed cases and test positivity falling for a second consecutive week. However, the outbreak remains widespread, with cases across districts nationwide. Household and close-contact exposure still drive transmission, highlighting the need for sustained community-based prevention, early detection and contact follow-up. Surveillance reporting completeness recovered after a previous dip, though logistics reporting still lags, with several regions unreported. Challenges include harmonising and integrating surveillance, laboratory and case-management data, and the ongoing interruption of the national toll-free hotline, hampering community alerts and information access. Operational gaps persist in supply distribution, activating new treatment centres and maintaining diagnostics and response commodities.

--- Report 2: UNFPA (2026-09-21) ---
On August 20, 2026, a vehicle convoy inside Chadian territory (Ennedi-Est) was targeted by Sudanese Armed Forces-linked strikes, prompting Chadian forces to raise defense alerts over sovereignty violations.
As of August 30, 2026, some 944,933 new Sudanese refugees have been registered since the conflict began in April 2023, including approximately 6,000 during August. Women and children continue to represent over 85% of the Sudanese refugee population in Chad.
The cholera outbreak expanded beyond its initial epicenter in Karal, spreading to N'Djamena and Lac Province (early August). By August 25, 2026, cumulative national figures reached 599 cases and 28 deaths across 11 health districts, with a case fatality rate of 4.7%. Lac Province is the most affected, accounting for 311 cumulative cases and 15 of the 28 deaths, while N'Djamena has recorded 120 cases.
