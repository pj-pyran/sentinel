--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-08-05) ---
Highlights

Humanitarian context: Approximately 290 million children in East Asia and the Pacific are exposed to multiple extreme climate hazards, increasing their risk of climate-related disasters and emergencies. Between January and June, Tropical Cyclone Maila affected nearly 300,000 people in the Solomon Islands and Papua New Guinea and a major earthquake in the Philippines affected around 1.9 million people. Disease outbreaks strained essential services, while 3.75 million people remained displaced in Myanmar.
Coordinated response: Across the region, UNICEF supported governments and partners to deliver life-saving, child-focused, inclusive and accountable humanitarian action across health, nutrition, WASH, child protection, education and shock-responsive social protection in Cambodia, China, Fiji, Federated States of Micronesia, Kiribati, Lao PDR, Marshall Islands, Mongolia, Myanmar, Papua New Guinea, Philippines, Solomon Islands, Thailand, Vanuatu and Viet Nam.
Results for children: With UNICEF support, 3.4 million children were vaccinated against polio and 1.3 million against measles, and 20,231 children aged 6–59 months were treated for severe wasting. Critical WASH supplies reached more than 113,000 people and over 96,000 children received individual learning materials. Mental health and psychosocial support reached over 171,000 children, adolescents and caregivers. 7,610 households received humanitarian cash assistance to support their immediate response and recovery needs; and over 35 million people received life-saving information.
Funding: US$9.8 million in new funding has been received so far in 2026. In addition to addressing gaps in funding for ongoing emergency responses, further investment in preparedness and anticipatory action ahead of the strong El Niño impact anticipated across the region is urgently needed.
