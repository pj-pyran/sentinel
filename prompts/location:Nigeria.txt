Crisis / situation: Nigeria

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-09-25) ---
Situation Update
Coordinated armed attacks on the communities of Ayaba, Ukpen, Bom, Aboajio and Yina in Mbazun Ward, Ukum LGA, killed 58 people in August, in the deadliest single episode recorded in Benue this year. A joint assessment by the Benue State Emergency Management Agency, the Nigerian Red Cross Society and UNHCR profiled more than 440 displaced families, with total household displacement estimated at around 500 and verification continuing. four more residents were killed in a night attack on Abaushwa community in Guma, and further deaths were recorded in Logo, Okpokwu and Otukpo. Civilians remained at the centre of the crisis in August, with 92% of reported security incidents involving direct protection violations. Benue is facing a widening protection crisis. Kidnappings reached their highest level of 2026 in August, with 14 incidents recorded across nine LGAs, while escalating attacks continue to kill, displace and terrorize rural communities. The impact extends far beyond immediate casualties: families are being cut off from farmland during a critical agricultural period, displacement into host communities is increasing pressure on already scarce resources, and women and children face heightened protection risks. Despite the growing severity of the crisis, some of the most affected LGAs remain outside structured community-based protection monitoring systems, limiting early warning and timely humanitarian response.

Diphtheria in Nigeria:
Diphtheria remains a significant public health concern in Nigeria, with transmission highly concentrated in northern states, particularly Kano, Bauchi, Katsina, Borno and Sokoto, which account for most cumulative cases. Persistent immunity gaps, including large zero-dose and undervaccinated populations, continue to drive transmission. Inadequate primaryseries and booster vaccination coverage leaves susceptible populations among older children, adolescents and adults. Surveillance and reporting backlogs, alongside data-quality limitations, hinder accurate assessment and targeted response efforts. The situation is further exacerbated by insecurity, population displacement, disruptions in vaccine and essential supply availability, and substantial subnational inequities in immunization coverage and access to health services, complicating outbreak control efforts across affected communities.
