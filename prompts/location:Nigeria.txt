Crisis / situation: Nigeria

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-11-10) ---
Situation Update
• October harvests are expected to improve food access for households with farmland across northern Nigeria. However, insecurity, repeated displacement and access constraints continue to restrict cultivation and the movement of produce to markets.
• Dry spells and a delayed peak in rainfall have increased agricultural production costs and damaged crop development across the region, further limiting food access and raising the risk of local production shortfalls.
• The latest report indicates that food inflation rose slightly in September 2026 compared with August but remained slightly below September 2025 levels.
• Persistently high food prices continue to erode purchasing power and limit food access despite seasonal improvements in food availability.
• Displaced and vulnerable households in conflict-affected northern Nigeria continue to face barriers to food and livelihoods, keeping humanitarian needs high despite the harvest.

--- Report 2: GBV AoR (2026-11-09) ---
In September 2026, Gender-Based Violence (GBV) response efforts across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states faced
significant operational challenges, including severe funding cuts, camp closures, and relocations. Borno retained the highest
protection severity in the region, particularly across key affected Local Government Areas (LGAs) such as Dikwa, Gubio, Jere,
Konduga, Magumeri, Mobbar, and Monguno.
The BAY States continued to experience overlapping humanitarian challenges, including severe flooding, attacks on
healthcare facilities by non-state armed groups in Geidam LGA (specifically Gumsa Community) Yobe State, and
displacement across Maiduguri, Jere, Bama, Gwoza, and Monguno. These crises heightened vulnerabilities among IDPs,
women, and adolescent girls, disrupting access to essential services. Flooding and insecurity further increased gender-based
violence risks and worsened living conditions.
Significant service gaps remain, including LGAs lacking case management and GBV reach—such as Gubio in Borno, Demsa
in Adamawa, and Bursari in Yobe. Furthermore, safe shelters and security services are highly inadequate, driven by severe
shortages relative to demand, poor structural quality, and restricted humanitarian access due to insecurity, threatening the
protection of vulnerable women and girls.
