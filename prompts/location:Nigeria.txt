Crisis / situation: Nigeria

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-05-22) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHT
• WFP has resumed food assistance in Borno and Yobe states after funding constraints compelled it to pause food distributions in March. Using evouchers, WFP assisted 420,163 people with emergency food assistance in April.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Intersecting security, climatic and economic challenges are driving high food needs and a widescale humanitarian crisis in Nigeria.
• Over 3.6 million people are internally displaced across the northeast, northwest and northcentral states due to the protracted conflict in the region.
• According to the latest Cadre Harmonisé 34.8 million people will likely face crisis or worse (IPC3+) levels of food insecurity between July and September 2026—the period between cultivation and harvest.
• WFP has collaborated with the Government since 2016 to provide emergency food and nutrition assistance. Currently, WFP is implementing a five-year Country Strategic Plan (2023-2027) that aims to deliver emergency food assistance and support recovery and resiliencestrengthening efforts for vulnerable people and communities. WFP’s strategy for lasting solutions is aligned with national priorities while enhancing the domestic capacity for Government-led solutions.
• Despite funding shortfalls which compelled WFP to reduce its coverage in 2025, WFP continues to deliver integrated support to national efforts towards stabilization.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-05-22) ---
Situation Update
• Insecurity continues to drive food insecurity across the northern states. In April, increased attacks on communities across northern Nigeria continued to disrupt livelihoods and sustainable food production.
• In April, food inflation continued its steady increase for the third consecutive month and reached 16.06 percent driven by increasing prices of essential staple foods. The current food inflation also outpaces headline inflation rate with sustained increase in food prices.
• Humanitarian food assistance remains critical for conflict affected communities across northern Nigeria, but coverage has declined significantly at the start of 2026 compared to previous years due to funding and access constraints, leaving gaps in support for vulnerable populations.
• Consequently, poor households are increasingly dependent on humanitarian assistance amid depleted food stocks, limited income opportunities, and high prices of fuel and food, which continue to erode their purchasing power and affect food security.
