Crisis / situation: Nigeria

--- Report 1: UNFPA (2026-07-28) ---
In June 2026, Nigeria faced a multifaceted humanitarian crisis characterized by protracted conflict, macroeconomic inflation, and disease outbreaks, leaving an estimated 36.3 million people food insecure. The north-east epicenter is suffering from non-state armed violence, severe malnutrition, and a massive cholera outbreak, while the forced closure of internally displaced person camps in Borno State has triggered mass displacement and relocation. Simultaneously, banditry and intercommunal violence have destabilized the north-west and north-central regions, exemplified by a mid-June attack in Zamfara State that caused numerous casualties and widespread displacement. These compounding factors, along with early seasonal flooding, have severely disrupted health infrastructure and heightened protection risks for women and girls, including increased exposure to gender-based violence (GBV) and limited access to essential sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services.
UNFPA's humanitarian response in Nigeria focused on delivering integrated SRH, GBV, and youth empowerment services through a network of health facilities, safe spaces, youth spaces, and one-stop centres. SRH interventions provided critical care, including 359 managed deliveries, antenatal and postnatal services for thousands of women, contraception access for nearly 3,000 individuals, and comprehensive medical treatment for survivors of sexual violence. GBV response efforts reached over 12,600 people with mental health and psychosocial support, referral linkages, and specialized protection services, while youth programming engaged over 7,300 young people through life-skills training, vocational programmes, and civic engagement initiatives.
To deliver the required SRH and GBV humanitarian response, a total of US $11,900,000 is required. As of June 2026, UNFPA’s appeal was 11.6% funded, leaving a significant gap in support for essential SRH and GBV services.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-07-27) ---
Nigeria is entering the peak ood season (July–September 2026), with forecasts from the Nigeria Hydrological Services Agency (NIHSA) indicating a high likelihood of widespread riverine and ash ooding across several parts of the country. The 2026 Annual Flood Outlook projects that approximately 250,788 km² of the country could be inundated during the season, of which 138,227 km² (55.1%) are classied as high-risk areas expected to experience severe to extreme ooding.
The Annual Flood Outlook further identies more than 14,000 communities across 266 Local Government Areas (LGAs) in 33 states and the Federal Capital Territory (FCT) as being at high ood risk, while an additional 15,500 communities across 35 states fall within the moderate ood risk category. July to September is projected to be the period of highest ood likelihood, with rising water levels expected across major river basins, including the Benue, Kaduna, Gongola, Hadejia–Jama'are, Cross River, and Niger river systems.
On 14 July 2026, NIHSA issued National Flood Advisory No. NFA-2026-195, placing 13 states - Adamawa, Akwa Ibom, Anambra, Bauchi,
Benue, Borno, Cross River, Edo, Enugu, Imo, Kaduna, Niger and Plateau, under a Medium Flood Risk alert. The advisory identied 16 hydrological stations with river levels approaching critical thresholds and forecast localized ooding along the Karam, Waya and Amber Rivers in Adamawa and Bauchi States. Communities, schools, health facilities, markets and places of worship located within the projected ood footprint are expected to be aected should the forecast rainfall materialize.
Preliminary exposure mapping undertaken by NIHSA indicates that the projected ood footprint includes at least 151 communities, 49 schools, 46 health facilities, 11 markets and 38 places of worship, highlighting the potential for signicant humanitarian impacts should river levels continue to rise.
The current forecasts are consistent with the 2026 Seasonal Climate Prediction issued by the Nigerian Meteorological Agency (NiMet) and the IFRC West and Central Africa Risk Analysis, which classies Nigeria as &quot;High Risk – Prepare&quot;, with an estimated 6.51 million people exposed to imminent ood risk, particularly in vulnerable riverine and coastal communities. The anticipated humanitarian impacts include loss of life and injuries, displacement of households, damage to homes and essential household assets, disruption of livelihoods, contamination of water sources, interruption of basic services, and an increased risk of waterborne diseases, particularly in communities with limited preparedness and coping capacity.
Based on the latest hydrological and meteorological forecasts, signicant ood impacts are expected between late July and September 2026, with the highest likelihood occurring during periods of sustained heavy rainfall and peak river discharge. Although the exact timing and location of ood impacts may vary across river basins, current forecasts provide sucient lead time to implement anticipatory actions before oodwaters reach critical levels in the targeted communities. In addition to scientic forecasts, reports from state authorities indicate that localized ooding has already occurred in parts of Lagos, Akwa Ibom, Bauchi and Plateau States, reecting saturated ground conditions and reduced drainage capacity in several locations.
The available forecast information provides an operational lead time of approximately four to eight weeks before the anticipated peak ood impacts during August and September, although localized ash ooding may occur earlier following intense rainfall events. This window is sucient to implement time-critical anticipatory actions, including early warning dissemination, volunteer deployment, household preparedness, evacuation planning, community clean-up campaigns and protection of livelihoods before river levels reach their seasonal peaks.
In response to these anticipated impacts, this Imminent DREF has been designed to complement existing Movement interventions and avoid duplication of assistance. Following consultations with Movement partners, the operation will target 16 priority LGAs across eight states - Adamawa, Akwa Ibom, Bauchi, Benue, Cross River, Edo, Lagos and Plateau, where there are identied gaps in existing anticipatory action support. Given the available funding, the operation will focus on two of the most at-risk LGAs in each state, prioritized based on the latest ood forecasts, exposure, vulnerability and anticipated humanitarian impact. The proposed interventions will focus on reducing preventable loss of life through early warning and evacuation preparedness, protecting essential household assets and livelihoods, reducing public health risks through water, sanitation and hygiene measures, and strengthening community preparedness and local response capacity before the anticipated oods occur.
