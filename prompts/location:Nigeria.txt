Crisis / situation: Nigeria

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-08-18) ---
Description
Sustained and heavy rainfall across Ondo State beginning in early August 2026 has triggered widespread riverine and urban flooding across at least seven Local Government Areas in the state, inundating homes, submerging roads and bridges, destroying property and displacing large numbers of residents. The flooding is ongoing and worsening, with multiple communities in the riverine south of the state particularly severely affected as water levels continue to rise.
The situation in Ilaje Local Government Area is the most critical. Igbokoda community in Ilaje LGA, including the Naval Base in Igbokoda and the council headquarters, has been submerged. Displaced residents have been observed sleeping under improvised shelters by the roadside, while others have sought refuge with friends and relatives in other LGAs. The Nigerian Red Cross Society Ondo State Branch has formally recommended to the Ondo State Government that an urgent IDP camp be established in a safe LGA to accommodate the displaced population. In Okitipupa LGA, communities including Ayeka, Ikoya, Igbodigo, Igbotako, Erinje, Kuneyin and Apata have been severely affected, with homes and commercial premises submerged, bridges destroyed and movement restricted. Properties worth millions of naira have been lost.
The NRCS Ondo State Branch conducted a rapid assessment of three of the seven affected LGAs; Ondo West, Ilaje and Okitipupa through the branch volunteers. Assessment of the remaining four LGAs (Irele, Ese-odo, Ile-oluji/Okeigbo and Odigbo) is ongoing in coordination with the Ondo State Emergency Management Agency (ODSEMA) and NEMA. From the three assessed LGAs alone, 2,527 households have been confirmed as affected. The true scale of impact across all seven LGAs is significantly higher and will be confirmed upon completion of the full assessment.
The Ondo State Government has deployed amphibious machines to clear waterways and drainage channels in Ilaje LGA under the direction of Governor Lucky Aiyedatiwa, targeting the removal of obstructions from canals and river channels to reduce the risk of further flooding. NEMA has also conducted an assessment in the affected LGAs, attributing the severity of the flooding to blocked waterways, indiscriminate refuse disposal and illegal structures erected along river channels. Ondo State is explicitly listed among 21 states identified by the Federal Government on critical flood alert for 2 to 15 August 2026, which covers 944 communities at highest risk nationally.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-08-17) ---
Description
In the early hours of Wednesday, 13 August 2026, between approximately 4:42am and 8:39am, a heavy downpour struck Lafia, the capital of Nasarawa State, triggering severe urban flooding across four communities in Lafia Local Government Area: Bukansidi, Millionaires Quarters, Sadaji Junction/Police B Division and the Makurdi Road/Local Government Secretariat area. The intense and sustained rainfall overwhelmed the area’s inadequate drainage infrastructure, causing floodwaters to inundate residential buildings, damage essential public facilities and disrupt livelihoods.
In total, 37 houses were destroyed across the four affected communities, with further damage reported to household property, non-food items and furniture workshops in the affected areas. Many furniture workshops and businesses along the flooded streets sustained significant losses. Floodwaters also caused heavy traffic disruption across Lafia metropolis. A total of 307 persons were displaced by the flooding and are currently sheltering in host communities within Lafia LGA.
No deaths or injuries were recorded. The displaced population now faces pressing needs for shelter support, food, non-food items and psychosocial assistance, with particular attention required for the most vulnerable among them including women, children and older persons. The Nigerian Red Cross Society Nasarawa Branch conducted a rapid assessment of the affected communities, and the figures in this report reflect confirmed assessment findings.
Nasarawa State and Lafia LGA sit within a wider flood-risk context: the NiMet 2026 Seasonal Climate Prediction specifically identifies Lafia and Doma LGAs among the areas in Nasarawa most likely to experience severe flooding, and the NIHSA 2026 Annual Flood Outlook lists Nasarawa among 23 high-risk states, with the state’s vulnerability closely linked to proximity to the River Benue, rapid urban expansion and inadequate drainage infrastructure. The flooding on 13 August 2026 is consistent with this forecast pattern, and further flooding is anticipated as the rainy season peaks through September.

Actions taken by others
The Nasarawa State Commissioner for Environment and Natural Resources, Princess Dr Margaret Elayo, inspected flood-affected areas in Lafia. She cautioned residents against constructing buildings on water channels, urged those in flood-prone areas to temporarily relocate to safer locations, and attributed the flooding to depleted underground water sources, obstructed drainage channels and poor urban infrastructure. The government ordered the temporary closure of Sandaji Hotel for non-compliance with environmental standards. Residents are appealing for urgent government intervention.
