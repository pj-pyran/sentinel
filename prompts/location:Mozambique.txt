Crisis / situation: Mozambique

--- Report 1: Health Cluster (2026-09-30) ---
1 Context Overview
Mozambique continues to face a complex humanitarian situation driven by the combined impacts of climate-related shocks and protracted conflict, with significant consequences for the health and well-being of vulnerable populations. In 2026, severe flooding in the central and southern provinces caused widespread displacement, damaged health infrastructure, and heightened the risk of waterborne disease outbreaks, disrupting access to and continuity of essential health services.
At the same time, insecurity in Cabo Delgado continues to trigger displacement and place additional pressure on already fragile health systems. Humanitarian needs remain acute, particularly among displaced populations, returnees, and host communities, requiring sustained support for primary health care, emergency medical services, mental health and psychosocial support, and outbreak prevention and response.
In response, Health Cluster partners have maintained a coordinated humanitarian effort to ensure the delivery of life-saving and essential health services. Interventions have focused on integrated approaches, including mobile health services, disease surveillance and outbreak response, sexual and reproductive health care, mental health support, and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) activities. Despite these efforts, considerable challenges remain, particularly related to humanitarian access, funding constraints, shortages of critical resources, and the need to further strengthen health information management, monitoring, and reporting systems.

--- Report 2: Health Cluster (2026-09-29) ---
Este documento analisa a situação atual da cadeia de abastecimento de produtos médicos de Moçambique, com contributos dos parceiros do Cluster de Saúde, e identifica onde a continuidade do investimento poderá melhor proteger os doentes.
As organizações parceiras que trabalham diretamente com os doentes descrevem um quotidiano sob pressão: 79% destas organizações referiram ruturas frequentes ou constantes de artigos essenciais, e algumas indicaram que as faltas duravam habitualmente mais de um mês. Luvas, antibióticos, compressas para tratamento de feridas, analgésicos e contracetivos foram os artigos mais frequentemente afetados. Os parceiros humanitários continuam a complementar os esforços do Governo com donativos próprios para ajudar a colmatar estas lacunas; como muitas organizações de saúde também são afetadas pela crise de financiamento humanitário, várias salientaram que um financiamento mais previsível ajudaria a manter este apoio ao longo do tempo.
