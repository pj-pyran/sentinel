Crisis / situation: Indonesia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: MPBI (2026-08-26) ---
Situation Report
• BNPB reported the following IDP figures as of August 24 at 09:00 WIB:
• Damage was recorded to 502 educational facilities, 396 houses of worship, 504 office buildings, one Bulog warehouse, 10 irrigation facilities, 39 public facilities, 78 road locations, one market, and 13 bridges.
• The Ministry of Health's situation report as of August 24 at 07:30 WIB recorded 5,931 cases of acute respiratory infection (ARI/ISPA) across Manggarai, Sikka, Nagekeo, West Manggarai and East Manggarai, as well as 27 cases of rabies-risk animal bites in Nagekeo, Ngada and East Manggarai. The report also noted that stocks of medicines and medical consumables (BMHP) in East Manggarai were beginning to run low.
• At its first coordination meeting on August 22, the Sub-Cluster for the Protection of Older People, Persons with Disabilities and Other At-Risk Groups (LDR) discussed accessibility gaps at centralized displacement sites. These barriers had led many persons with disabilities to stay in self-managed tents near their homes or with host families. The LDR Sub-Cluster also emphasized the importance of rapid inclusion assessments led by organizations of persons with disabilities (OPDs) to identify specific needs, including assistive devices and adapted hygiene items.
• The LDR Sub-Cluster also highlighted reports of deaths in Nagekeo that were not directly caused by earthquake injuries but were associated with shock and acute diarrhoea, underscoring the importance of post-disaster health management. It also raised concern that some people may have acquired disabilities as a result of earthquake-related injuries but remain unrecorded because of limited road access or because injured people have not yet reached health services.
• The Health Services Sub-Cluster in Sikka Regency reported on August 24 a need for weighing scales, thermometers, privacy screens (sampiran), and nebulizers.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-08-25) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
15-08-2026
What happened, where and when?
Magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck Flores Island in East Nusa Tenggara Province. According to the Meteorology, Climatology and Geophysics Agency (BMKG), the earthquake was centred offshore at 8.41°S and 121.39°E, approximately 30 kilometres north-east of Mbay, Nagekeo District, at a depth of 15 kilometres. The earthquake was associated with activity along the Flores Back-Arc Thrust.
Following the earthquake, BMKG issued a tsunami warning for several coastal areas. Sea-level changes were observed at several monitoring locations, including approximately 0.94 metres at Maurole in Ende District. The tsunami warning was lifted at approximately 07:30 WIB after BMKG confirmed that the threat had ended. However, frequent aftershocks continued to affect the area. As of 16 August 2026 at 15:00 WIB, BMKG had recorded 995 aftershocks ranging from magnitude 1.9 to 6.2, of which 46 were felt by communities.
The earthquake affected seven districts across Flores Island: West Manggarai, Manggarai, East Manggarai, Ngada, Nagekeo, Ende and Sikka. Earthquake impacts and population evacuations were also reported in Bima, West Nusa Tenggara Province, and the Selayar Islands, South Sulawesi Province.
Landslides, damaged roads and debris continued to restrict access to several affected communities. The Larantuka–Maumere route had reopened, while the Ende–Nagekeo route remained reportedly cut off. Efforts to clear affected roads and establish alternative access routes were ongoing. Electricity and telecommunications services had largely begun to recover, although repairs were still required in several locations. Continued aftershocks were also affecting assessment, evacuation and emergency service delivery.

--- Report 3: IFRC (2026-08-25) ---
Description
On 15 August 2026 at 04:58 WIB, a magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck Flores Island, East Nusa Tenggara Province. The epicentre was located offshore, approximately 30 kilometres northeast of Mbay, Nagekeo District, at a depth of 15 kilometres. The earthquake affected seven districts: Manggarai Barat, Manggarai, Manggarai Timur, Ngada, Nagekeo, Ende and Sikka. Thousands of aftershocks have continued, increasing anxiety among communities and discouraging many families from returning to damaged homes.
As of 24 August 2026 at 09:00 WIB, the National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB) reported 100 deaths, 1,603 people injured or ill, and 180,327 people displaced. A total of 73,818 houses were damaged, together with 1,378 educational facilities, 118 health facilities, 504 government offices, and 396 places of worship. The figures remain preliminary and may change as assessment and verification continue.
Displaced people are staying in communal sites, temporary shelters, open areas, with relatives or in small self-established sites near their homes. Many independently displaced families remain difficult to identify and reach, particularly in remote, mountainous and island communities. Priority concerns include emergency shelter, safe water and sanitation, healthcare, essential household items, protection and psychosocial support. Children, pregnant and breastfeeding women, older people, persons with disabilities, people with chronic health conditions and families whose homes were severely damaged face increased risks. Although access along major routes has improved, several remote villages and island communities continue to experience logistical constraints. Existing water shortages linked to the dry season and El Niño, together with ongoing volcanic activity in parts of Flores, are further increasing humanitarian needs.

Summary
As of 24 August 2026, Palang Merah Indonesia (PMI) had recorded 76 response activities across the seven affected districts, reaching 16,192 people. These included evacuation and first aid services for 2,493 people, health services for 735 people, emergency shelter support for 9,570 people, water and sanitation services for 2,248 people, relief distribution support for 997 people, and psychosocial support for 149 people. PMI has mobilised 55 personnel and continues to conduct rapid assessments, operate mobile health services, support displacement sites, distribute safe water and essential relief items, and establish emergency tents for health facilities and displaced families. Two operational coordination hubs have been established in Nagekeo and Manggarai to support coordination, information management and the delivery of assistance across the affected districts. PMI is working in coordination with government authorities, IFRC and other humanitarian partners.

Summary
IFRC is supporting PMI with needs assessment, information management, and the development of a DREF application targeting 20,000 people across the seven affected districts. IFRC has provided an Emergency Advance DREF allocation of CHF 100,000 to support the initial response while the full DREF application is being finalised. Technical coordination between PMI and IFRC continues to refine the operational strategy, targeting, sectoral priorities and budget based on updated assessment findings.
Actions taken by others
The Government of Indonesia, through BNPB, BPBD, Basarnas, the Indonesian Armed Forces, the National Police and local authorities, continues to conduct search and rescue, evacuation, damage and needs assessments, emergency health services, management of displacement sites and relief distribution. Emergency command posts have been activated in several affected regencies. A field hospital has been established in Reok Sub-District, while efforts continue to reopen affected roads and restore electricity and communication networks.
