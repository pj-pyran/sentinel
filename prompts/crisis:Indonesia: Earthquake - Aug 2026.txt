• NTT BPBD reported on August 18 that, based on a rapid assessment, more than 30% of people in centralised evacuation sites are women, children, children under five, and pregnant women who require specific protection interventions, not only general logistics assistance.
• As of August 19, reported damage included 217 education facilities, 96 health facilities, 46 government offices, and 77 places of worship.
• BMKG's weather forecast issued on August 19 at 09:26 WIB for the period August 19-21 indicated the potential for strong winds across East Nusa Tenggara.

• ASB also reported that basic household items and shelter facilities remain a priority, particularly for people whose homes have been damaged and who remain anxious because aftershocks are still occurring frequently. Gaps remain in meeting the specific needs of groups at heightened risk, including persons with disabilities, older people, children, and women. These needs should be prioritised across WASH, food and nutrition, household items, mobility support in temporary shelters, and access to health-facility services.
• The earthquake triggered tidal surges and sea-level recession at several coastal locations. Assessment is needed to determine possible damage to coral reefs from the strong earthquake and to coastal ecosystems affected by the tsunami, even though the tsunami was relatively small, with heights of up to 1 metre. Dry-season climate risks in Flores, compounded by El Nino conditions, may prolong drought and reduce supplies of safe water and water for agriculture. These risks may be further aggravated by infrastructure damage and possible earthquake impacts on springs and other water sources. Areas with a high potential for landslides should also be mapped and assessed, particularly because continued aftershocks may increase landslide risk in already susceptible locations.
• The earthquake also triggered landslides that blocked river channels and disrupted drinkingwater supply systems. As a result, water supplied through the SPAM system became turbid because soil material entered the system.
• Wahana Visi Indonesia reported on August 19 that preliminary assessment findings indicate that children continue to experience psychological distress due to repeated aftershocks, prolonged displacement, and uncertainty about their future housing. Assessments in Ende, Ngada, and Manggarai also identified limited or unavailable active Infant and Young Child Feeding (PMBA/IYCF) services in affected areas.

--- Report 2: MPBI (2026-08-19) ---
Situation Report
• BMKG noted that on Monday (August 17) there had been 1,576 aftershocks. BMKG appeals to people on Flores Island and its surroundings to remain calm, evacuate independently away from the beach area to the gathering point.
• Damage occurred to 264 educational facilities, 83 health facilities, 1 warehouse of the Logictics Affairs Agency (BULOG), 116 public facilities, 1 market, 157 houses of worship, 171 office units, 7 irrigation facilities, 34 road points, and 5 bridges.
• The Ministry of Health reported on August 17 at 07:30 WIB of the impact on 21 hospitals and 190 health centers. Most hospitals and health centers are still operating even though many services are carried out outside the building.
• The Coordination Meeting of the Reproductive Health Subcluster (Kespro) on August 17, 2026 in Flores, NTT, discussed the implementation of the Minimum Initial Service Package (PPAM) of Kespro as part of the earthquake emergency response. This package includes nine main components, ranging from the appointment of coordinators, prevention of sexual violence, handling HIV and STIs, to efforts to reduce maternal and neonatal mortality rates. In addition, PPAM also highlights the prevention of unwanted pregnancies, adolescent health, minimum services for toddlers and the elderly, and the integration of reproductive health services into basic services.
