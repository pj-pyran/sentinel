Crisis / situation: Indonesia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: UN Women (2026-09-02) ---
The Gender Alert highlights critical protection and gender-specific needs following the 7.7-magnitude earthquake that struck Flores Island, East Nusa Tenggara, Indonesia, on 15 August 2026. As of 30 August, the earthquake had caused 111 deaths, injured 1,629 people and displaced 188,161 people across eight districts. Women, pregnant and lactating women, children, adolescents, older people and persons with disabilities account for a significant proportion of those affected.
The alert identifies heightened protection risks for women and girls in displacement settings, including gender-based violence, limited access to maternal and reproductive health services, and gaps in safe and inclusive evacuation facilities. Damage to nine hospitals and 68 community health centres has further disrupted access to essential services.
It calls for gender-responsive humanitarian assistance, improved collection of sex-, age- and disability-disaggregated data, strengthened GBV coordination and survivor-centred services, and gender- and disability-inclusive WASH, shelter and evacuation facilities. The alert also emphasizes the importance of safe spaces, dignity supplies, psychosocial support and meaningful engagement of women’s organizations and persons with disabilities in the response.
This is jointly developed by the Ministry of Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection, Republic of Indonesia; Indonesia Humanitarian Coordination Platform (IHCP); UNFPA; and UN Women.

• The IHCP multi-stakeholder coordination meeting held on August 25, 2026 noted the following main problems:
o The high number of self-settled IDPs scattered in self-displacement areas makes it difficult to collect data, monitor the condition of IDPs, and distribute logistical assistance and health services. This condition has had an impact on delays in health treatment, even causing cases of death among self-settled IDPss. In West Manggarai, all IDPs are still independent, while the distribution of logistics has only reached the sub-district level and has not been supported by a clear call center or complaint mechanism. Although the logistics stock is actually sufficient, the distribution is not optimal due to limited human resources, access constraints, and a lack of a strong information connection with the post. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen coordination and mapping the role and scope of partner support through the 5W mechanism to ensure that interventions are directed, do not overlap, and reach all areas in need.
o Displacement and Protection Clusters: although coordination has begun and involves many organizations, the compilation of 5W data is still in process, so the available information has not been fully consolidated into one integrated system. In addition, the harmonization of response planning and implementation across sectors and regions still needs to be strengthened to prevent overlap or service gaps. Multi-hazard risk analysis is still in the development stage, so a comprehensive risk picture is not yet available to support anticipatory steps. On the other hand, the ongoing implementation of the Joint Needs Assessment (JNA) and Joint Market Assessment (JMA) means that the information base on the needs of affected communities and market conditions is not fully available as a reference for decision-making and the prioritization of interventions.
o Sub-cluster for the Protection of the Elderly, Disability, and Other Vulnerable Groups (LDR): complete disaggregated data on vulnerable groups is not available, thus hindering the identification of needs and the targeted distribution of assistance. In addition, limited access to clean water, especially for people with disabilities who are internally displaced, is still a major challenge in meeting basic needs. This condition is exacerbated by the still high demand for hygiene kits and clothing and limited access for people with disabilities to humanitarian services and assistance, thereby increasing the vulnerability of this group to health, protection, and welfare risks. In addition, limited access to assistance and accessibility of evacuation posts is also a problem.
o Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) Sub-cluster: there is a risk of health problems due to water quality that has not been monitored evenly, as shown by the increase in diarrhea cases in Nagekeo Regency and the lack of optimal water quality checks in all affected areas. At the same time, the need for emergency sanitation facilities remains high, as reflected in the mobilization of portable latrines across regions and the lack of comprehensive SOPs for fecal sludge management (which will be discussed at the AMPL Sub-cluster coordination meeting on Saturday). The fulfillment of AMPL needs also still depends on cross-regional support and coordination with various partners, so it has the potential to create service gaps if coordination is ineffective. Post-disaster conditions also increase the risk of environmentbased diseases related to water, sanitation, waste, and disease vectors, requiring sustainable interventions such as water quality monitoring, waste management, and vector control.
o Child Protection Sub-cluster: there is still a need to strengthen the child protection system in the affected areas, both in terms of service capacity and coordination between related agencies. The mechanism for reporting and referral cases of violence against children is also still inadequate, so it needs to be strengthened so that each case can be identified, reported, and handled quickly and appropriately. In addition, the IDPs and post-disaster situation increase the vulnerability of children to various protection risks, including violence, exploitation, neglect, and other forms of violations of children's rights. Although various partners have provided psychosocial support services, the needs of affected children are still high and require wider and sustainable coverage of services. On the other hand, the availability and quality of child protection services are not even in all affected areas, so it is necessary to expand and standardize services, including strengthening the SAPA Post and coordination mechanisms in each district.
o Health Cluster: maintaining the continuity of health services in the midst of limited service capacity (which is prioritized for pregnant women, maternal mothers, newborns, the elderly, women with disabilities), continued handling of trauma cases, especially orthopedic injuries that have the potential to not be reported optimally due to weak surveillance systems, as well as the increased risk of ISPA due to the dry season and evacuation conditions. In addition, there is still a need for hygiene kits and waste management to prevent environmental health problems, special attention to the health of pregnant women as a vulnerable group, and limited availability of drugs in several health centers. Other challenges include coordination between district and provincial HEOCs, the extent of the affected areas, and the low utilization of formal health services because some people prefer traditional medicine; mobile health services that reach evacuation points are needed. All of these efforts are also constrained by limited operational support, particularly transportation facilities for mobilizing health workers, logistics, and field services.
There was a shift in the disease pattern in disaster-affected areas, from acute trauma cases to infectious cases, accompanied by an increase in dog-bite cases. Although trauma cases had decreased in the second week, the cases were found again. The most commonly reported diseases at evacuation points include ISPA, gastrointestinal disorders, skin diseases, non-communicable diseases such as hypertension and diabetes, maternal and child health problems, and reproductive health, including the tendency of pregnant women to be reluctant to access health center services. In addition, psychosocial impacts are also experienced by survivors and health workers. The latest surveillance data shows a significant increase in diarrhea cases, although some of them are accumulated reports since day one. Meanwhile, a report from the Reok Health Post, Manggarai shows that many children and toddlers are dehydrated and have Acute Respiratory Infection (ISPA), while the most common complaints in adults are headaches and fever.
o Reproductive Health Sub-cluster: disruption of puskesmas operations and high demand for emergency reproductive health service facilities, which threaten access to services for pregnant women, mothers, newborns, and other vulnerable groups. The main priorities are increasing the capacity of health tents, restoring the function of health facilities, and ensuring the sustainability of reproductive health services during the emergency response period and recovery transition.
o Nutrition Sub-cluster: There are still high cases of underweight, and several cases of malnutrition have been found, although there are no toddlers with severe malnutrition. The most vulnerable groups include infants, toddlers, pregnant women, and adolescent girls who require continuous monitoring and nutrition interventions. The implementation of nutrition services also faces obstacles because not all targets are concentrated at the posts, so some vulnerable groups may be unreachable for screening, supplementary feeding (PMT), and supplementation activities. In addition, the limited number of nutrition workers due to the impact of disasters reduces the capacity to conduct monitoring, education, and case follow-up in the field. In terms of assistance, the donation of formula milk requires strict supervision because suboptimal water quality and hygiene can increase health risks for infants and children. On the other hand, although services through PMBA kitchens and various nutrition interventions have been running, the coverage is still limited to some areas so it is necessary to expand and strengthen services to reach more vulnerable groups in the affected areas.
