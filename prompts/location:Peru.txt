Crisis / situation: Peru

--- Report 1: Cruz Roja Peruana (2026-11-04) ---
Description
According to Complementary Report No. 15400, updated on 3 November 2026 at 06:57, the department of Tumbes has reported 324 people severely impacted and 625 people affected, along with 2 destroyed homes, 110 uninhabitable homes, and 225 affected homes. The report also indicates impacts on 10 educational institutions, one health facility, and 2 km of local roads. The province of Zarumilla accounts for the vast majority of displaced people (320) and uninhabitable homes (109), with the district of Aguas Verdes being the most severely affected, reporting 316 displaced people, 108 uninhabitable homes, and three affected educational institutions. In contrast, the province of Tumbes concentrates most of the affected population (265 people) and the reported impacts on education, health, and road infrastructure. It should be noted that the Damage and Needs Assessment (EDAN) remains ongoing at the local government level; therefore, the reported figures are preliminary and subject to revision as additional information becomes available
Through Supreme Decree No. 149-2026-PCM, the Government of Peru declared a 60-day State of Emergency in selected districts across 24 departments and the Constitutional Province of Callao, including Tumbes. During the emergency period, regional and local authorities will implement immediate risk reduction, response, and rehabilitation measures. The decree establishes that response activities will be financed through the institutional budgets of the respective government entities, without requiring additional allocations from the National Treasury.
The event has been linked to anomalous weather conditions associated with the Coastal El Niño phenomenon. According to specialists from the National Meteorology and Hydrology Service of Peru (SENAMHI), rainfall accumulations exceeding 100 mm have been recorded in areas where seasonal precipitation is typically limited to only 2 to 3 mm, highlighting the exceptional nature of the event.
Response operations are ongoing. The Ministry of Housing, Construction and Sanitation conducted a drone overflight over the Puerto Perú sector in Aguas Verdes to assess damage and activated the El Beso Early Warning and Response System (SARE), operating two pumps that evacuated approximately 84 m³ of accumulated water resulting from the rise of the Tumbes River. In the district of Tumbes, operational teams and two hydrojet units were deployed to remove standing water and restore drainage system functionality.
The Ministry of Education has temporarily shifted educational services from in-person to virtual modalities in affected areas as a preventive measure. In addition, personnel from the Rapid Disaster Intervention Company of the Armored Infantry Battalion No. 211 were deployed to the Urcus sector in the district of Corrales to support drainage and canal-cleaning activities in coordination with local authorities.
Looking ahead, SENAMHI forecasts the continuation of heavy to extremely heavy rainfall between 4 and 6 November, particularly in Tumbes, while moderate to heavy rainfall is expected in the inland coastal areas of Piura.

--- Report 2: Cruz Roja Peruana (2026-11-03) ---
Description
According to COEN-INDECI Preliminary Report No. 1106, as of 07:50 on 2 November, intense rainfall caused material damage in the districts of Matapalo, Aguas Verdes, and Zarumilla in Zarumilla Province, as well as in the districts of Tumbes, Corrales, Pampas de Hospital, and San Jacinto in Tumbes Province. The heaviest precipitation primarily affected localities such as San Jacinto and Matapalo, located near the Ecuadorian border, where water accumulation generated the most critical conditions.
In the city of Tumbes, the heavy rainfall significantly disrupted normal nighttime activities. The city’s main square (Plaza de Armas), which typically attracts large numbers of residents during the evening, remained largely deserted as many families returned early to their homes to seek shelter from the rains.
The same report indicates that the Damage and Needs Assessment (EDAN) is still being conducted by local authorities. As a result, no fatalities, injuries, or health impacts have been reported to date.
One of the most affected localities is Puerto Perú settlement, located in the district of Aguas Verdes, where flooding has had significant impacts on both the population and housing. This event occurred in the context of intense rainfall and water accumulation in vulnerable urban areas.
As part of the response efforts, the Peruvian Army has deployed personnel from the Rapid Disaster Response Company (CIRD) to the most affected locations. In addition, the Regional Government of Tumbes is coordinating the deployment of water pumps and specialized equipment to flooded areas. At the national level, the Ministry of Health (MINSA) has announced the installation of a field hospital to address potential emergencies resulting from the heavy rains. The facility is expected to have a capacity of between 80 and 120 beds and will provide care for patients affected by dengue, leptospirosis, and other emergency-related health conditions. Similarly, the response capacity of the Regional Hospital II-2 JAMO will be strengthened.
Furthermore, according to the National Meteorology and Hydrology Service of Peru (SENAMHI), the Tumbes River reached the red alert threshold after recording a flow rate of 1,021.33 m³/s at 05:00 on 2 November, exceeding the established red alert threshold of 1,000 m³/s. However, by 08:00 the river flow had decreased to 910.43 m³/s, returning to the orange alert level.
Finally, according to SENAMHI Report No. 306-2026-SENAMHI/DMA/SPM, moderate to extremely heavy rainfall is forecast for Tumbes and the inland coastal areas of Piura, while light to moderate rainfall is expected along the coastal regions of Lambayeque and La Libertad. In addition, the Short-Term Weather Advisory indicates that red-level rainfall conditions may persist in several provinces of the Piura and Tumbes departments until 3 November. According to the report, accumulated rainfall across the coastal region is expected to range between 0.1 mm and 80 mm within a 24-hour period, with a high probability of occurrence exceeding 60 per cent.
