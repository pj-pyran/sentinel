Crisis / situation: Global

--- Report 1: FEWS NET (2026-07-01) ---
Global Overview: El Niño is present. Drought emerges in parts of Eastern Africa, Central America, and Northern South America. Flooding persists in portion of West Africa, Central America, and Central Asia.
Africa Weather Hazards
Hot and abnormally dry conditions have been persisting over several weeks for a large swath of East Africa; Localized heavy rains and flooding have been occurring along the Gulf of Guinea Coast.
1) Inundation continues in the Sudd wetlands of South Sudan.
2) Over the last 30 days or more, poor rainfall caused significant deficits and abnormal dryness in portions of central, southwestern and northwestern Ethiopia, South Sudan, eastern CAR, Uganda, western Kenya, central and northeastern DRC. Longer term rainfall deficits, lasting at least 8 weeks, result in the placement of drought in southern South Sudan.
3) Below-average rainfall since the beginning of the rainfall season has led to negative SPI values and abnormal dryness in western Sierra Leone, western Guinea, and Liberia.
4) Observed and forecasted above-average rainfall is likely to lead to flooding along West Africa’s Gulf of Guinea coast as well as parts of western Guinea and Sierra Leone.
5) Forecasted much above-average temperatures greater than 35°C will lead to abnormal heat in parts of eastern CAR, South Sudan, northeastern DRC, Uganda, western Kenya, Sudan, western and central Ethiopia, as well as Morocco during the outlook period.
6) Heavy rain last week, continuing into this week, is leading to high runoff and the threat for flash floods in parts of western Ethiopia.

--- Report 2: FEWS NET (2026-06-30) ---
Key Messages

In West Africa, cereal prices were stable or increased across the Sahel in May, with the sharpest increases observed in Niger and Chad. In Nigeria, staple food prices increased seasonally due to tightening supplies and higher fuel and transportation costs. Coastal countries recorded stable or declining prices, supported by adequate supplies and subdued regional demand, while conflict, poor access to trade routes, and high transportation costs kept prices elevated in parts of Cameroon and the Central African Republic (CAR). Prices are expected to rise seasonally across most regional markets (Page 3).
In East Africa, prices of staple foods, including maize, sorghum, and wheat flour, remained stable or declined marginally in Burundi, Kenya, Uganda, South Sudan, and Tanzania, supported by improved availability from the first bimodal harvests. Prices continued increasing in Djibouti, Ethiopia, Somalia, and Sudan, reflecting lean season demand, elevated fuel and transport costs, currency depreciation, and localized market disruptions. Livestock prices continued increasing due to improved productivity and seasonally reduced market supply (Page 5).
In Southern Africa, maize and maize product prices were broadly stable between April and May, reflecting ample supplies from large carryover stocks and bumper harvests across much of the subregion. Physical stock availability is expected to moderate extreme price volatility. The large regional surplus will keep maize prices structurally depressed for most of MY 2026/27, with markets gravitating toward export parity due to persistent excess supply (Page 7).
In Central America, maize and bean prices were generally stable across the region, with ample imports supporting lean season supplies. Maize prices persisted above average in Honduras and El Salvador, and close to average in Guatemala. Bean prices were below average in El Salvador and Guatemala and close to average in Honduras, reflecting differences in import flows. In Haiti and Colombia food prices remained stable, while in Venezuela, food prices increased, driven by currency depreciation and increased demand (Page 9).
In the Middle East and Europe, staple food price trends remained mixed. In Syria, most prices increased driven by exchange-rate depreciation, rising fuel prices, and higher import costs. In Yemen, wheat flour, sunflower oil, and sugar remained stable in Aden due to exchange rate stability, while rice, fuel, and livestock prices increased. In Lebanon, staple food prices remained broadly stable, supported by adequate imports. Food prices showed improvement following increased inflows in Gaza but remained above pre-crisis levels, while prices in the West Bank eased. Conflict-related pressures continued in Ukraine, with mixed food price trends (Page 11).
In Asia, staple food prices were generally stable but elevated across much of the region. In Central Asia, stable domestic supplies and wheat exports from Kazakhstan supported market stability, although imports remained under upward pressure in Afghanistan due to border and trade disruptions. Across South and Southeast Asia, prices were generally stable, supported by harvests and imports in Nepal, Sri Lanka, and India. Localized price increases persisted in Pakistan and Burma due to elevated fuel and transport costs and conflict-related supply disruptions (Page 13).
International markets showed mixed trends in May. Maize prices declined due to stable global supply and record production. Rice prices increased amid El Niño-related weather concerns and higher crude oil and fertilizer costs, although production and stocks remain ample. Wheat prices rose due to adverse weather and higher input costs despite record output. Crude oil and fertilizer prices decreased, following expectations that supply will improve after the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz (Page 2).
