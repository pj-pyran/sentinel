Crisis / situation: Global

--- Report 1: FEWS NET (2026-09-30) ---
Global Overview: El Niño is strengthening. Abnormal heat continues to threaten parts of northern and eastern Africa, Hispaniola, and northern South America. Previously dry parts of Central America received heavy rain. Southern Africa sees flooding.
Africa Weather Hazards
Abnormal heat is forecast over northwestern Africa, the eastern Sahel, and much of Eastern Africa, while high flood risk persists in far western West Africa and parts of Southern Africa.
1) Inundation in the Sudd wetlands of South Sudan persists.
2) Following 90 days of severe rainfall deficits across Chad and East Africa, abnormal dryness has intensified into drought over western Sudan, southern and southeastern South Sudan, northern Uganda, and portions of northern, eastern, and central Ethiopia.
3) Abnormal dryness persists over southeastern Mali and central and eastern Liberia due to consistently below-average rainfall.
4) Recent heavy rainfall has triggered flooding across Nigeria's Anambra and Delta States, Ghana's North East Gonja District, and northern Libya.
5) Forecast heavy rainfall during the next week raises flood risk across parts of West Africa (southern Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea-Conakry, Sierra Leone, and western Liberia) and Southern Africa (central Angola, southern Zambia, much of Zimbabwe, southern Mozambique, South Africa’s northern, eastern, and southern regions, and southern Madagascar).
6) In the coming week, abnormal heat is expected in southern Morocco, Western Sahara, eastern Chad, Sudan, South Sudan, northern Uganda, northwestern Kenya, and northeastern Ethiopia.

--- Report 2: FEWS NET (2026-09-30) ---
Key Messages

In West Africa, cereal prices were below last year and the five-year average across the Sahel and coastal countries, except for conflict-affected and refugee-hosting areas. In Nigeria, local cereal prices were mostly declining or stable, while rice and cowpea prices remained high. Prices in Cameroon were mixed, with atypical declines in the north, and elevated prices for imported rice in urban areas. In the Central African Republic (CAR), fuel shortages and supply constraints kept prices high. Regional livestock prices broadly remained above the average (Page 3).
In East Africa, staple food prices remained elevated but broadly stable. Prices declined seasonally in Somalia with increasing gu harvest availability, while stronger increases persisted in Sudan, South Sudan, and parts of Uganda amid tight supplies, high transport costs, currency depreciation, and conflict-related disruptions. In Kenya and Tanzania, domestic and cross-border supplies continued to moderate maize prices despite below-average production in some areas. Livestock prices generally remained stable at above-average levels (Page 5).
In Southern Africa, August maize prices were mixed. Prices continued to decline in Zambia and Lesotho and were stable or decreasing across most of Tanzania and Mozambique. Malawi and South Africa recorded increasing prices due to concerns over El Niño-related weather risks. Prices trended upward in Madagascar and Angola, driven by an early lean season and production constraints. Maize prices were lower than last year in Zambia, Mozambique, and South Africa, but remained under upward pressure in several deficit markets across the region (Page 7).
In Central America, staple prices remained stable across the region with the start of the primera harvest but showed mixed trends compared to average levels. Black bean prices were below average in Guatemala, driven by expanded import quotas, and above average in Honduras due to tighter domestic supply and low imports. In Haiti, local food prices were stable due to post-harvest supply. In Venezuela, monthly inflation eased, and food prices increased in USD and VED. In Colombia, staple food supply was near average, and cereal prices remained stable (Page 9).
In the Middle East and Europe, staple price trends remained mixed. In Lebanon, wheat flour prices were stable, and prices increased for animal-source foods and produce. In Syria, staple prices remained high, while high fuel costs and shortages disrupted milling and bakeries. In Gaza, markets remained functional with continued inflows; in the West Bank, staple prices were broadly stable, but higher fuel, gas, egg, and chicken prices increased the CPI. In Yemen, staples were mostly stable, but intensified conflict, LPG shortages, and liquidity constraints continued to affect market functioning. In Ukraine, bread prices declined, but conflict and input costs continued to shape markets (Page 11).
In Asia, staple food prices were mixed but largely stable. Stable wheat and rice prices in Afghanistan and Sri Lanka reflected adequate domestic and imported supplies, while wheat flour remained elevated in Pakistan despite little monthly change. Stronger pressures were concentrated in Burma, where palm oil and diesel prices rose amid fuel and foreign exchange constraints, and Kathmandu, where coarse rice and black bean prices increased sharply due to monsoon-related road disruptions and higher transport costs (Page 13).
International markets: Maize prices rose 5.3 percent driven by decreased U.S. yields and the smallest EU crop since 2007/08. Wheat prices increased 4.6 percent, and Black Sea shipping remained at a standstill. Rice prices were mixed. Brent averaged 91 USD/bbl with 6.7 million barrels per day of Middle East production shut in, and the fertilizer index fell 1.9 percent as nitrogen eased while phosphate rose. No grain faces a global shortfall, but importers face grain freight costs 18.5 percent above a year ago (Page 2).
