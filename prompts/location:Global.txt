Crisis / situation: Global

--- Report 1: FAO (2026-11-20) ---
Synopsis (short abstract)
The triannual Crop Prospects and Food Situation report provides a forward-looking global analysis of cereal production, market trends and acute food security conditions, with a particular attention on low-income food-deficit countries.
FAO estimates that 41 countries/territories require external assistance for food: 31 in Africa, eight in Asia, one in Latin America and the Caribbean, and one in Europe. Persisting conflicts and insecurity remain the primary drivers of the most severe levels of acute food insecurity, while weather shocks associated with the strong El Niño event are worsening conditions in several subregions. Looking ahead, El Niño-related impacts on production are expected to remain severe into 2027 in several subregions, likely aggravating acute food insecurity further.
The report also includes FAO's latest forecast for 2026 cereal production among the Low-Income Food-Deficit Countries (LIFDCs), pegged at 126.5 million tonnes, about 2 percent above both the 2025 level and the five-year average. The increase largely reflects a strong production recovery in Asia, particularly in Near East Asia, while production among African LIFDCs is expected to remain near-average.

--- Report 2: FEWS NET (2026-11-18) ---
Global Overview: El Niño is present. Abnormal Dryness and Drought conditions persist in East Africa, northwestern and southeastern Kazakhstan, and parts of Central America, Hispaniola, and Northern South America.
Africa Weather Hazards
Erratic rainfall has led to dryness in eastern Horn of Africa; high flood risks remain in many areas of Eastern Africa and Southern Africa.
1) Inundation persists in the Sudd Wetlands of South Sudan.
2) A large part of western Eastern Africa faces dryness due to persisting, below-average rainfall over the past 90 days. Southern and southeastern South Sudan and northern Uganda have already experienced deteriorated vegetation conditions, which have resulted in droughts in the region.
3) Flooding is likely to continue in Gemena of northwestern DRC, parts of Gabon, Congo, western DRC, along the Juba River in southern Somalia, the KwaZulu-Natal of eastern South Africa, and Eswatini as additional rainfall is forecast in each region during the next week.
4) High flooding risks also exist in southern Ethiopia, southern Kenya, northern and western Tanzania, westcentral Angola, north-central Mozambique, and central Madagascar due to the forecast, additional rainfall during the next week.
5) Southeastern Ethiopia and central Somalia face dryness due to poor rainfall over the past four weeks.
6) Hot conditions are predicted over parts of far western West Africa, Eastern Africa, and a wide area in central and eastern Southern Africa.
