Crisis / situation: Afghanistan

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-07-08) ---
UNHCR Afghanistan's Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) operates at both Country Office and field office levels through a range of accessible channels. The operation currently manages a protection mailbox, Six hotlines operated by Country Office Kabul, and 33 complaint boxes located across the country. All queries and feedback received through these channels are recorded in the centralized Feedback and Response System (FRS). The CFM also receives referrals from the inter-agency humanitarian call centre, Awaaz.Community-based outreach remains a key component of the mechanism. As of 2026, 140 Community Outreach Volunteers (COVs) and Community Outreach Focal Points (COFPs) deployed across multiple provinces are able to receive and record queries directly into the FRS, helping to expand access to the CFM and strengthen communication with affected communities.In the context of returns, returnees can use the CFM to raise concerns, submit complaints, provide suggestions, and share feedback during registration and assistance processes at UNHCR-supported locations.In addition, Community-Based Protection Monitoring (CBPM) and Household Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (HMNA) monitors collect questions, feedback, suggestions, and complaints received during their activities and channel them through the CFM, further expanding the mechanism's outreach and supporting consistent responses and follow-up.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-07-08) ---
BACKGROUND AND METHODOLOGY
▪ In line with UNHCR’s protection monitoring objectives, UNHCR maintains a strong presence through border monitoring partners at official and unofficial crossing points connecting Afghanistan with Iran, Pakistan,
Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan. The monitoring exercise is protection-focused and aims to assess the triggers, intentions, and motives behind Afghan cross-border movements, including protection needs, movement trends, and related protection risks and human rights concerns.
▪ UNHCR conducts inflow and outflow monitoring interviews at individual and household levels across ten official crossing points, while Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) are conducted at around 70 unofficial crossing points.
▪ Outflow monitoring at official borders covers passport holders, whereas inflow monitoring covers Afghan returnees and deportees upon arrival, regardless of documentation status. At unofficial borders, KIs include transport drivers, nearby business owners, community elders, and local health providers.
▪ Respondents are selected using a systematic sampling approach, based on a fixed skipping interval determined by the average number of arrivals/departures during the previous week.
▪ During the first quarter of 2026, UNHCR border monitoring partners conducted 44,335 interviews, including 455 KIIs, at 9 official and 65 unofficial crossing points.
▪ Among respondents, 65 percent were aged 18–34, 32 percent were aged 35–59, 1 percent were aged 12–17, and 2 percent were aged 60 and above — see charts #1 and #2.
▪ The lower proportion of female respondents reflects the profiles of individuals crossing the borders and operational constraints (see page 2).
UNHCR and partners have continued efforts to increase female participation, including recruiting female staff and providing separate interview spaces. These measures enabled the inclusion of 13,008 female respondents, representing 29 percent of 44,335 respondents during January to April 2026. While slightly below the 32 percent female sample size recorded in 2025, reaching 29 percent within the first four months of 2026 indicates positive progress, with continued mitigation efforts expected to further improve female participation in the coming months.
