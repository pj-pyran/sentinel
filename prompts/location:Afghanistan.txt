Crisis / situation: Afghanistan

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-10-14) ---
This monthly protection update for Afghanistan provides a comprehensive overview of UNHCR’s different protection programs, aiming to prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks faced by vulnerable populations in the context of the current humanitarian crisis and in the absence of durable solutions. The data and insights presented are derived from an extensive protection analysis, collected through a variety of robust tools and systems. The activities include protection monitoring, household assessments, voluntary repatriation, returns monitoring, specialized support for persons with specific needs, community engagement, complaints and feedback systems, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), child protection, women protection, and civil documentation. This update aims to highlight key achievements, ongoing challenges, and the critical needs of the affected communities, ensuring a transparent and effective protection response.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-10-12) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• Rising food prices, weak incomes, and persistent malnutrition continue to leave vulnerable Afghan families struggling to meet their basic needs.
• Ahead of winter, WFP is prepositioning assistance for 1 million people and expanding nutrition services nationwide temporarily thanks to an influx of commodities.
• Despite ongoing border closures and regional security tensions, WFP maintained uninterrupted operations by establishing alternative supply corridors to Afghanistan.

--- Report 3: WFP (2026-10-12) ---
Key Highlights
Market Overview
During the 1st week of October, national average prices recorded generally modest week-on-week movements, while several key food commodities remained elevated compared with last year and their longer-term averages. Rice, wheat, cooking oil, sugar and salt prices remained above their corresponding annual levels, while elevated fuel and fertilizer prices continued to add pressure to transportation, production and supply-chain costs. Meanwhile weak labour availability and subdued purchasing power continued to constrain household access to food.
Food Categories:
National average food prices recorded limited week-on-week movements, although notable annual and longer-term price disparities persisted across commodities.
• Wheat and Wheat Flour: Wheat grain prices increased marginally by 0.6% during the week, while high-price wheat flour remained unchanged and low-price wheat flour declined by 0.2%. Compared with last year, wheat grain and low-price wheat flour were both 10% higher, while high-price wheat flour was 6% higher.
• Rice: Rice prices declined during the week, with Palawi and Sholae falling by 1.9% and 1.7%, respectively. Despite these weekly declines, Palawi remained 17% and Sholae 31% above last year's levels, while both varieties remained substantially above their three-year averages, by 19% and 20%, respectively.
• Other Staples: Weekly movements in cooking oil, pulses, sugar, salt and bread remained limited. Cooking oil and sugar prices were 12% and 10% higher than last year, while salt was 4% higher. In contrast, pulses and bread remained 12% and 3% below their respective annual levels.
• Vegetables: Potato prices declined by 4.1% during the week, while onion fell by 3.1% and tomato by 0.2%. Despite the weekly decline, potato remained 25% above last year's level and 23% above its three-year average. In contrast, tomato and onion prices were 55% and 6% lower year-on-year, respectively.
Non-Food Categories:
Terms of Trade (ToT): Purchasing power remained constraint for both labour-dependent households and pastoralists, as declining income and higher prices limit their access to food.
• Unskilled Labour ToT: The nominal unskilled labour ToT stood at 9.9 kg of low-price wheat flour, representing a 13% decline from last year and 12% below the three-year average. The continued deterioration indicates sustained pressure on labour income relative to food prices.
• Labour Availability and Wages: Unskilled labour availability remained at 2.0 days per week, 14% below last year and 21% below the three-year average. Unskilled labour wages also declined by 5% year-on-year, contributing to a 25% decline in the real unskilled labour ToT compared with last year and 30% below the three-year average.
• Pastoralist ToT: A pastoralist could exchange a one-year-old female sheep for 260 kg of low-price wheat flour, 12% below last year's level and 7% below the three-year average, indicating continued pressure on the purchasing power of livestock-dependent households.
• Fertilizers and Agricultural Inputs: DAP prices increased marginally by 0.3% during the week, while urea prices declined by 1.8%. Despite these limited weekly movements, DAP and urea remained 10% and 24% higher than last year, respectively. Improved seed and animal feed prices remained below last year, by 8% and 5%, respectively.
