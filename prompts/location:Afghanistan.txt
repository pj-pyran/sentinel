Crisis / situation: Afghanistan

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-08-27) ---
Key Highlights
Market Overview
Market conditions remained broadly stable during the fourth week of August, although price movements varied across food commodities, vegetables, fuel, and agricultural inputs. Most staple food prices changed only marginally, while vegetable prices declined. At the same time, diesel and fertilizer prices remained elevated, sustaining pressure on transportation and agricultural production costs. Overall commodity availability remained adequate, although regional trade conditions and higher fuel and input costs continued to weigh on market stability.
Food Items:
• National food prices remained relatively stable during the fourth week of August, with most staple commodities recording only modest weekly changes. However, several food items continued to trade above last year's levels, particularly rice, wheat, cooking oil, and potato.
• Wheat and Wheat Flour: Wheat grain and flour prices experienced negligible changes during the week. Compared with last year, wheat grain and low-price wheat flour remained 14% and 12% higher, respectively.
• Rice: The prices of rice categories showed mixed movements, with high-price Palawi rice declining slightly by 0.3%, while low-price Sholae rice increased by 1.2%. However, both varieties remained substantially above last year's levels, at 25% and 36% higher, respectively, and well above their three-year averages.
• Other Staples: Cooking oil and sugar prices increased by 0.6% and 0.8%, respectively, while pulses and salt declined slightly and bread prices remained unchanged. Year-on-year trends remained mixed, with cooking oil and sugar prices 11% and 8% higher, respectively, while pulses and bread were 14% and 3% lower than last year.
• Vegetables: Vegetable prices continued to show divergent trends. Tomato, onion and potato prices fell by 4.6%, 2.2% and 0.7%, respectively. Despite the weekly declines, potato prices remained 50% higher than last year, while tomato and onion prices remain near to last year’s levels.
Non-Food Items:
• Exchange Rate: The Afghani strengthened slightly to AFN 64.8/USD, appreciating by 0.6% week-on-week. It remained 5% stronger than last year and 13% stronger than the three-year average, continuing to provide some support in containing imported commodity costs.
• Fuel: Diesel prices continued its upward trend and increased by 1.2% during the week to AFN 78.0 per litre. Prices are 17% higher than the same time last year and 20% above the three-year average, maintaining upward pressure on transportation and supply-chain costs.
• Fertilizers: Fertilizer prices showed mix movements during the week, with DAP increasing by 0.4% while urea declined by 1.2%. However, fertilizer prices stand considerably above last year's levels, with DAP and urea prices 15% and 30% higher, respectively, continuing to raise production costs for farmers.
• Terms of Trade (ToT): The ToT, which reflects the purchasing power, remained under pressure for both labourdependent households and pastoralists during the fourth week of August. Nominal casual labour ToT declined by 0.8% week-on-week to 10.4 kg of wheat flour and remained 14% below last year, while unskilled labour availability fell by 2.8% to 2.0 days per week, remaining 16% below both last year and the three-year average. Similarly, pastoralist ToT declined by 0.7% week-on-week and 16% year-on-year, reflecting continued weakness in the purchasing power of livestock keepers.

--- Report 2: WHO (2026-08-27) ---
Highlights
Over 155 900 returnees entered Afghanistan in July, with 62% of whom arrived from Pakistan.
Three hundred eighty health workers were trained, and 240 medical kits were distributed to strengthen the health system.
CCHF cases decreased by 37.5% compared to June levels; however, monitoring needs to continue to ascertain seasonality of this trend.
