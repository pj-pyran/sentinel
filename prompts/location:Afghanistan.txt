Crisis / situation: Afghanistan

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-08-24) ---
Key Highlights
Market Overview
Market trends were mixed during the third week of August, with declines in several food and agricultural input prices partly offset by increases in vegetables, rice, and fuel. Commodity availability remained broadly sufficient, and weekly price movements were generally moderate. However, elevated diesel prices, ongoing regional trade disruptions, and supply constraints continued to keep transportation and import costs under pressure. Despite these factors, higher fuel costs have so far translated only moderately into retail food prices.
Food Items:
• National food prices showed mixed movements during the third week of August, with several staples recording small declines while others remained firm. Overall, price movements were limited, and market supplies continued to be adequate, although several commodities remained well above last year's levels.
• Wheat and Wheat Flour: Wheat prices edged down during the week, with wheat grain and low-price wheat flour both declining by 0.5%, while high-price wheat flour increased marginally by 0.1%. Compared to last year, prices remained higher, particularly wheat grain and low-price wheat flour, which were 13% and 12% higher, respectively.
• Rice: Rice prices moved slightly upward, with high-price Palawi and low-price Sholae rice increasing by 0.4% and 0.5%, respectively. Nevertheless, rice prices remained substantially above last year and the three-year average.
• Other Staples: Cooking oil, pulses, sugar, and salt prices registered small weekly declines, while bread prices were unchanged. Year-on-year comparisons remained mixed: cooking oil and sugar prices are still higher than last year, whereas pulses are 13% lower, supported by relatively favourable availability and domestic production.
• Vegetables: Vegetable prices experienced mixed movements during the week. Tomato prices fell by 6.0%, while potato and onion prices increased by 3.7% and 5.2%, respectively. Tomato and potato prices remained notably higher than last year, while onion prices were 4% lower, continuing its comparatively favourable price trend.
Non-Food Items:
• Exchange Rate: The Afghani strengthened slightly to AFN 65.2/USD, appreciating by 0.7% week-on-week. It remained 5% stronger than last year and 13% above the three-year average, continuing to provide some relief against imported cost pressures.
• Fuel: Diesel prices edged up by 0.8% during the third week of August to AFN 77.0 per litre. Prices were 15% higher than last year and 18% above the three-year average, maintaining pressure on transportation and supply-chain expenses amid continued regional supply constraints.
• Fertilizers: Fertilizer prices eased during the week, with DAP declining by 0.5% and urea by 2.2%. Despite these reductions, both remained considerably more expensive than last year, with DAP 14% and urea 32% higher, continuing to increase input costs for farmers.
• Livestock: The price of a 1-year-old live female sheep increased by 0.6% week-on-week to AFN 7,784. Nevertheless, the price remained 5% below last year and 1% below the three-year average, suggesting continued weakness in livestock purchasing power. (Pastoralist ToT showing a 15% decline year-on-year).
• Labour Market: Labour market conditions remained subdued during the week, with unskilled labour availability holding at 2.1 days per week. Availability remained 14% below both last year and the three-year average, while unskilled and skilled daily wages declined marginally during the week.

--- Report 2: WHO (2026-08-23) ---
Wild poliovirus type 1 (WPV1)
Cases: No new WPV1 positive cases reported this week. In total, 52 WPV1-positive cases have been confirmed in 2025 (21 from Afghanistan and 31 from Pakistan in 2025) and 18 WPV1 cases reported in 2026 (15 from Afghanistan and 3 from Pakistan).
Environment: Fifteen new WPV1 positive environmental samples in 2026 – 5 from Afghanistan and 10 from Pakistan were reported this week. The new WPV1 environmental samples from Afghanistan are: 4 from Nangarhar province (2 from Jalalabad and 1 each from Behsud and Batikot districts) and 1 from Kandahar province/district, with collection dates between 22nd to 24th June 2026. While the new WPV1 environmental samples from Pakistan are: 5 from Balochistan province (3 from Quetta, and 1 each from Chaman and DeraBugti districts), 3 from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province (2 from Bannu and 1 from Peshawar districts), and 1 each from Punjab province (Rawalpindi district) and Sindh province (Karachi Malir town) with collection dates between 6th to 16th July 2026. In total, 745 WPV1-positive environmental samples have been reported in 2025 (94 from Afghanistan and 651 from Pakistan) and 176 WPV1-positive environmental samples reported in 2026 (58 from Afghanistan and 118 from Pakistan).
