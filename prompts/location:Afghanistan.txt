Crisis / situation: Afghanistan

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-07-10) ---
Key Highlights
Exchange Rate and Trade Dynamics:
• Exchange Rate: During the first week of July, the Afghani depreciated to AFN 65.7/USD, weakening by 1.7% compared to the previous week. Despite this decline, the currency remained 5% stronger than last year and 13% stronger than the three-year average, continuing to provide some support in containing imported inflation.
• Trade: Market supply remained generally adequate, supported by domestic harvests and ongoing regional trade. However, Afghanistan's dependence on imported goods continues to expose domestic markets to fluctuations in the exchange rate, fuel costs, and regional trade conditions, which contribute to price volatility across the country and expose the economy to external vulnerabilities.
Food Items:
• National average food prices showed divergent trends during the first week of July, with modest weekly changes across most staples.
• Wheat and Flour: Wheat grain prices rose marginally this week, while high-price and low-price flour also recorded moderate increases. On an annual basis, wheat grain (+13%), high-price flour (+7%), and low-price flour (+11%) remained elevated, mainly due to higher transportation costs associated with renewed import routes.
• Rice: Both rice categories declined slightly this week. Despite these weekly decreases, rice prices remained significantly higher than last year (high-quality +27%; low-quality +34%) and above the three-year average level.
• Other Staples: Cooking oil, pulses, sugar and salt prices edged down this week. Sugar posted a notable weekly drop, though it remained above last year's levels (+9%). Cooking oil prices held steady, staying negligibly above last year (+2%). Salt declined weekly but remained higher year-on-year (+8%).
• Vegetables: Seasonal harvests continued to support domestic vegetable markets with slight weekly fluctuations. Compared to the previous week, tomato (+2.3%) and onion (+2.5%) prices rose slightly, while potato prices remained broadly stable (-0.6%). Compared to last year and the three-year average, potato and tomato prices remains significantly higher, while onion prices have declined.
Non-Food Items:
• Fuel: Diesel prices rose by 7.2% during the week, mainly reflecting reduced fuel imports, particularly from Russia. Compared to last year and the three-year average, diesel prices remained 9% and 12% higher, respectively.
• Fertilizers: Fertilizer prices changed only marginally, with DAP declining by 0.2% and Urea by 1.4% over the week. Compared to last year, DAP and Urea prices remained considerably higher (+17% and +49%, respectively), while improved seed and animal feed prices continued to remain below both last year and the three-year average.
• Livestock: The price of a one-year-old live female sheep declined marginally (-0.4%) during the week and was 5% below last year's level and 2% below the three-year average, contributing to slightly weaker pastoralist ToT.
Labour Market:
• Labour market conditions remained subdued. The unskilled labour wage declined marginally (-0.5%), while labour availability remains low at 1.9 days per week. Labour availability remained 18% below both last year's level and the three-year average, underscoring continued pressure on household income opportunities for casual labourers

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-07-09) ---
INTRODUCTION
In Afghanistan’s complex protection context, characterized by continued returns, ongoing displacement, economic hardship, and sustained protection risks, Community-Based Protection Monitoring (CBPM) plays a central role in informing protection analysis and response. CBPM enables UNHCR and partners to systematically collect community-reported information on protection risks, vulnerabilities, and coping mechanisms affecting returnees, internally displaced persons, and host communities. By generating regular, area-specific evidence, CBPM supports the identification of priority protection concerns, facilitates the monitoring of protection trends over time, and informs UNHCR programming, coordination, and advocacy efforts. The analysis presented in this report is based on data collected through UNHCR’s CBPM tools in the first quarter of 2026, including household-level surveys, key informant interviews (KIIs), and focus group discussions (FGDs). These tools are designed to generate evidence-based information on the protection needs and risks faced by returnee, displaced, and host communities, and to inform UNHCR programming, strategic decision-making, and advocacy, while also feeding into the Afghanistan Protection Cluster's analysis and information products. Survey locations are selected based on the concentration of returnees and displaced populations. The CBPM covers UNHCR population groups of concern, including returnees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), IDP returnees, refugees and asylum seekers, as well as members of host communities. This report presents the findings and analysis of the CBPM exercise for the first quarter of 2026, providing an overview of key protection trends and highlighting the main findings during the period from January to March 2026.

--- Report 3: UNHCR (2026-07-08) ---
UNHCR Afghanistan's Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) operates at both Country Office and field office levels through a range of accessible channels. The operation currently manages a protection mailbox, Six hotlines operated by Country Office Kabul, and 33 complaint boxes located across the country. All queries and feedback received through these channels are recorded in the centralized Feedback and Response System (FRS). The CFM also receives referrals from the inter-agency humanitarian call centre, Awaaz.Community-based outreach remains a key component of the mechanism. As of 2026, 140 Community Outreach Volunteers (COVs) and Community Outreach Focal Points (COFPs) deployed across multiple provinces are able to receive and record queries directly into the FRS, helping to expand access to the CFM and strengthen communication with affected communities.In the context of returns, returnees can use the CFM to raise concerns, submit complaints, provide suggestions, and share feedback during registration and assistance processes at UNHCR-supported locations.In addition, Community-Based Protection Monitoring (CBPM) and Household Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (HMNA) monitors collect questions, feedback, suggestions, and complaints received during their activities and channel them through the CFM, further expanding the mechanism's outreach and supporting consistent responses and follow-up.

--- Report 4: UNHCR (2026-07-08) ---
BACKGROUND AND METHODOLOGY
▪ In line with UNHCR’s protection monitoring objectives, UNHCR maintains a strong presence through border monitoring partners at official and unofficial crossing points connecting Afghanistan with Iran, Pakistan,
Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan. The monitoring exercise is protection-focused and aims to assess the triggers, intentions, and motives behind Afghan cross-border movements, including protection needs, movement trends, and related protection risks and human rights concerns.
▪ UNHCR conducts inflow and outflow monitoring interviews at individual and household levels across ten official crossing points, while Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) are conducted at around 70 unofficial crossing points.
▪ Outflow monitoring at official borders covers passport holders, whereas inflow monitoring covers Afghan returnees and deportees upon arrival, regardless of documentation status. At unofficial borders, KIs include transport drivers, nearby business owners, community elders, and local health providers.
▪ Respondents are selected using a systematic sampling approach, based on a fixed skipping interval determined by the average number of arrivals/departures during the previous week.
▪ During the first quarter of 2026, UNHCR border monitoring partners conducted 44,335 interviews, including 455 KIIs, at 9 official and 65 unofficial crossing points.
▪ Among respondents, 65 percent were aged 18–34, 32 percent were aged 35–59, 1 percent were aged 12–17, and 2 percent were aged 60 and above — see charts #1 and #2.
▪ The lower proportion of female respondents reflects the profiles of individuals crossing the borders and operational constraints (see page 2).
UNHCR and partners have continued efforts to increase female participation, including recruiting female staff and providing separate interview spaces. These measures enabled the inclusion of 13,008 female respondents, representing 29 percent of 44,335 respondents during January to April 2026. While slightly below the 32 percent female sample size recorded in 2025, reaching 29 percent within the first four months of 2026 indicates positive progress, with continued mitigation efforts expected to further improve female participation in the coming months.
