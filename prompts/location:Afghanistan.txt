Crisis / situation: Afghanistan

--- Report 1: DACAAR (2026-08-17) ---
Performance Overview
During the first half of 2026, Afghanistan’s WASH response continued to operate in a complex humanitarian environment characterized by prolonged drought and water scarcity, localized flooding, earthquakes, Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD), displacement, and increasing return movements from Iran and Pakistan. These overlapping shocks placed additional pressure on already limited water resources and essential WASH infrastructure, particularly in underserved and high-risk communities. Against an estimated 15.9M PiN and an annual WASH Cluster target of 7.84M people, 25 operational partners reached 908,176 beneficiaries between January and June 2026, without double counting across WASH interventions. Partners delivered an integrated package of lifesaving and resilience-building assistance, including safe drinking water, gender and disability sensitive sanitation, hygiene promotion, critical WASH supplies, and basic WASH services in schools, health facilities and nutrition centers. At intervention level, partners reported reaching over 1.01 million people with safe water, 1.48 million through hygiene promotion, 987,447 with critical WASH supplies, and 118,633 with sanitation services. In addition, 608,954 people benefited from WASH services in institutions. These intervention level achievements may overlap and should therefore be distinguished from the Cluster’s unduplicated overall reach of 908,176 people. Emergency WASH assistance remained particularly important at key border reception and transit locations in response to continued return movements from Iran and Pakistan. Partners sustained water supply, sanitation, hygiene promotion and essential WASH supplies while the Cluster supported preparedness, operational coordination and continuity of services. AWD preparedness and response also remained a priority as rising temperatures, deteriorating water availability and water-quality risks increased the threat of waterborne diseases. Partners strengthened water-quality monitoring, chlorination, hygiene promotion and coordination with health actors, complemented by continued engagement through AWD coordination mechanisms. Alongside emergency interventions, partners continued rehabilitating and constructing climate-resilient water systems to improve sustainable access to safe water and strengthen the resilience of communities exposed to recurrent drought and climate-related shocks. However, response coverage remained geographically uneven, with substantial differences in achievement and operational partner presence across provinces. The Cluster continued using response monitoring and partner-presence analysis to identify underserved areas, support geographic prioritization and promote more effective use of available operational capacity. The WASH Cluster maintained national and sub-national coordination throughout the reporting period, bringing together partners, government counterparts and inter-sector actors to review response progress, identify gaps and address implementation bottlenecks. Engagement focused on preparedness for population movements and AWD, border response coordination, administrative and MoU-related challenges, continuity of essential services, and strengthening partner capacity in priority locations. The Cluster also maintained donor engagement and advocacy while contributing to efforts to strengthen localization and the participation of national organizations in humanitarian coordination. Despite these efforts, the response remained critically underfunded. By mid-year, only US$43.06M of the US$163.3M required had been received approximately 26% of requirements, leaving a funding gap of around US$120.2M. Funding shortfalls, rising operational costs, administrative delays, limited emergency supplies and uneven partner capacity continued to restrict response scale and coverage. With return movements, drought and water scarcity, seasonal AWD risks and potential climate-related shocks expected to continue during the second half of 2026, additional donor support and strengthened operational partnerships will be critical to sustain life-saving WASH services, close geographic and sectoral gaps, and strengthen preparedness and resilience.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-08-17) ---
Key Highlights
Market Overview
Market conditions remained generally stable at the national level during the second week of August, with moderate price movements across most monitored commodities. Several food prices eased during the week, although key staples remained elevated compared to last year and the three-year average. Persistent trade disruptions, regional supply constraints, and high transportation costs continued to influence market prices. Diesel prices increased during the week and remained significantly above historical levels, adding to supply-chain and delivery costs. However, the pass-through of elevated fuel costs to retail food prices remained moderate.
Food Items:
• National average food prices showed a modest easing during the second week of August, with several commodities recording week-on-week declines. Despite this moderation, prices for several key staples remained elevated compared to historical levels, while market availability remained generally adequate.
• Wheat and Wheat Flour: Wheat grain and wheat flour prices remained broadly stable week-on-week, with only marginal increases recorded across the three commodities. However, prices remained significantly higher than last year, particularly wheat grain and low-price wheat flour, which were 14% and 13% higher, respectively.
• Rice: Rice prices declined during the week, with high-price Palawi and low-price Sholae rice falling by 1.5% and 0.7%, respectively. Nevertheless, rice prices remained substantially above last year and the three-year average.
• Other Staples: Prices of cooking oil, pulses, sugar, and salt showed negligible movements during the week. Meanwhile, compared to last year, cooking oil and sugar remained elevated, while pulse prices were 14% lower, reflecting relatively favourable availability and domestic production.
• Vegetables: Vegetable prices eased during the second week of August, led by a 7.1% decline in tomato prices. Potato and onion prices also decreased by 2.1% and 2.0%, respectively. Compared to last year, tomato and potato prices remained significantly higher, while onion prices continued to remain below last year's level.
Non-Food Items:
• Exchange Rate: The Afghani remained broadly stable at AFN 65.6/USD, appreciating by 0.6% week-on-week. The currency remained 4% stronger than last year and 12% stronger than the three-year average, helping to partially offset imported inflationary pressures.
• Fuel: Diesel prices increased by 1.2% during the second week of August and remained significantly elevated, at 15% above last year and 17% above the three-year average. Continued regional supply constraints and elevated international fuel prices are sustaining pressure on transportation and supply-chain costs.
• Fertilizers: Fertilizer prices declined during the week, with DAP falling by 1.4% and urea by 4.7%. Despite the weekly declines, prices remained substantially above last year's levels, with DAP 15% and urea 35% higher.
• Livestock: The price of a 1-year-old live female sheep remained broadly stable, with negligible week-on-week fluctuations. However, it remained 5% below last year and 2% below the three-year average, indicating a slight decline in pastoralists' terms of trade.
• Labour Market: Labour market conditions remained subdued, with labour availability unchanged at an average of 2.1 days per week. Availability remained 14% below both last year and the three-year average, while unskilled and skilled daily wages remained unchanged during the week.
