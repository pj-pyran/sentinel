Crisis / situation: Afghanistan

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-07-24) ---
Key Highlights
Fuel Price Shock and Exchange Rate Dynamics:
A sharp spike in fuel prices is emerging as a primary driver of inflationary pressure, with the cost of transportation cascading into other food and non-food items. This increase is directly impacting supply chains and delivery costs, driven by rising diesel prices. The main catalysts are deepening supply shortages from Russia, exacerbated by the export ban and damage to critical refining infrastructure. Over the past two weeks, limited alternative supplies from Central Asian countries, Iran, and Iraq have contributed to the price surge. Beyond diesel, consumers in parts of the country are reporting shortage of gas used for cooking appliances, with prices rising sharply. Similarly, petrol prices have surged from around AFN 66 to AFN 82 per litre. Concurrently, while the Afghani remained broadly stable at AFN 65.9/USD, it has exhibited an upward trend in recent weeks, depreciating by 2.5% compared to the third week of June. However, it remains 4% stronger than last year and 13% stronger than the three-year average, which continues to partially moderate imported inflation.
Food Items:
• National average food prices showed broad-based upward pressure during the third week of July, with rising transportation costs increasingly contributing to price movements across several commodities.
• Wheat and Flour: All wheat categories rose by around 1% week-on-week, remaining elevated compared to last year—wheat grain (+14%), high-price flour (+8%), and low-price flour (+12%)—reflecting sustained production and transportation costs.
• Rice: Both rice categories edged up modestly and remained substantially above last year's levels, with high-price rice 27% higher and low-price rice 34% higher, standing significantly above their three-year average levels.
• Other Staples: Cooking oil surged by 4.6%, the sharpest weekly increase among staples, while sugar rose modestly (+0.5%) and pulses and salt remained largely stable. Year-on-year, prices of cooking oil (+8%), sugar (+10%), and salt (+8%) remains elevated, whereas pulses show a 14% decline.
• Vegetables: Potato prices continued to climb (+2.2%), while tomato and onion saw marginal increases. Year-on-year, tomato and potato remained significantly elevated by 35% and 55%, respectively, while onion prices eased by 9%, reflecting improved seasonal availability.
Non-Food Items:
• Fuel: Diesel prices continued their sharp upward trajectory, increasing by 3.1% during the week, mainly due to higher international oil prices and continued supply constraints from Russia. Compared to last year and the three-year average, diesel prices remained 19% and 21% higher, respectively.
• Fertilizers: Fertilizer prices rose during the week, with DAP increasing by 1.7% and Urea by 1.1%. However, compared to last year, DAP and Urea remained considerably higher by 19% and 51%, respectively. Improved seed and animal feed prices stayed below last year and the three-year average, reflecting weak consumer demand.
• Livestock: The price of a one-year-old live female sheep increased marginally by 0.4% during the week but remained 6% below last year's level and 2% below the three-year average, contributing to weaker pastoralist ToT.
• Labour Market: Daily wage unskilled labour availability remains limited at 2.0 days/week which is 17% lower than last year and the three-year average, indicating continued pressure on household income opportunities

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-07-24) ---
Situation Overview
• On 20 July, heavy rainfall triggered flash floods across three provinces in eastern Afghanistan. The most severe impact was reported in Parun City, Nuristan Province, where extensive flooding caused deaths, injuries, significant damages with disruption of services to the affected communities.
• Preliminary reports1 indicate 23 fatalities and 125 injuries, with additional individuals still missing. The floods destroyed and damaged 92 houses, while also impacting a health clinic, markets, and disrupting local livelihoods.
• Critical infrastructure and services sustained damages, including bridges, road sections, flood protection walls, agricultural land and damaged water supply networks, disrupting access to safe drinking water.
• Within 24 hours, UNICEF with partners were on the ground, providing immediate lifesaving emergency health services to cover over 600 people including children.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-07-23) ---
Highlights

Heavy rainfall and flash floods have affected communities across several provinces in central, eastern and southeastern Afghanistan. The impacts range from loss of life and extensive damage to homes, public infrastructure and livelihoods to localized impacts affecting individual families.
Parun City in Nuristan Province remains the most severely affected location reported to date. According to figures shared by the local authorities with the inter-agency assessment teams, 23 people were killed, five remain missing and 125 were injured, including 38 with severe injuries and 87 with minor injuries. The completed inter-agency needs assessment identified 92 families eligible for humanitarian assistance.
In Paktika Province, flash floods affected Sara Qala village in Sharan City on 21 July. Initial reports indicate that three people were injured, 14 houses were destroyed, 37 livestock were killed, 10 vehicles were damaged and approximately 122 hectares of agricultural land were washed away.
In Paktia Province, flooding on 21 July affected 46 families in Ahmad Aba and Zurmat districts. The Provincial Disaster Management Committee (PDMC) is discussing the deployment of an assessment team.
In Ghazni Province, flooding on 20 July affected 66 households in Ghazni City. Joint needs assessment teams, comprising the International Organization for Migration (IOM), the World Food Programme (WFP), the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) and the Danish Refugee Council (DRC), have been deployed and the assessment is ongoing.
In Maidan Wardak Province, flooding in Sayedabad District affected two families, partially damaged their houses and resulted in one death. In Kapisa Province, the shelter of nine families in Tagab District was reportedly slightly affected.
Humanitarian partners are responding through emergency health, shelter, food, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), protection and other assistance, while assessments and early recovery planning continue across affected areas.

Regional Impact
As of 23 July, heavy rainfall and flooding have affected parts of the central, central highlands, eastern and southeastern regions, with significant variation in the scale of impact between locations.
Eastern Region
Nuristan Province
Parun City remains the most severely affected location reported to date. According to figures shared by the local authorities, the flooding resulted in 23 fatalities, while five people remain missing. A total of 125 people sustained injuries, including 38 with severe injuries and 87 with minor injuries.
The completed inter-agency needs assessment identified 92 families eligible for humanitarian assistance. Beyond the impact on affected households, the flooding caused extensive damage to community infrastructure, public facilities and livelihoods.
Six markets and approximately 70 shops were destroyed, significantly disrupting local economic activity. Approximately 400 metres of protection wall were damaged, increasing the risk of further flooding and erosion. One water-supply network, including approximately 500 metres of pipeline, was damaged, disrupting access to safe drinking water for an estimated 1,000 families. One of the two springs feeding the network was destroyed. The floods also damaged approximately 280 orchard trees and resulted in the loss of 30 livestock. Approximately 30 vehicles were damaged, four bridges were destroyed or severely damaged, and livelihood assets including carpentry workshops and two saw machines were affected. Public infrastructure damage included one mosque, one private clinic, approximately six kilometres of road and one micro-hydropower plant.
Nangarhar Province
Flooding affected Torkham and surrounding areas, including a returnee transit camp on the Pakistan side of the border. According to IOM and local sources, three Afghan refugees were reportedly killed and nine injured at the transit camp.
Flooding temporarily disrupted movement at the Torkham crossing, delaying the movement of returnees for approximately two hours. The floods also damaged the protection wall of the Directorate of Refugees and Repatriation compound at Zero Point, Torkham, where humanitarian partners are providing basic services to returnees. Private shops and livelihood-related installations at Torkham Zero Point were also reportedly damaged or washed away.
Laghman Province
Flash flooding was reported in Alingar District. According to the Afghanistan National Disaster Management Authority (ANDMA), no damage to residential houses had been reported at the time of the latest update. Agricultural land and irrigation systems were affected.
Ghazni Province
Flooding on 20 July affected 66 households in Ghazni City. Joint needs assessment teams have been deployed and the assessment is ongoing.
Southeastern Region
Paktika Province
Flash floods affected Sara Qala village in Sharan City on 21 July. Initial reports indicate that three people were injured, 14 houses were destroyed, 37 livestock were killed, 10 vehicles were damaged and approximately 122 hectares of agricultural land were washed away. Joint needs assessment teams were deployed on 22 July and the assessment is ongoing.
Paktia Province
Flooding on 21 July affected 46 families in Ahmad Aba and Zurmat districts. The Paktia Provincial Disaster Management Committee is discussing the deployment of an assessment team.
Central Region
Maidan Wardak Province
Flooding in Sayedabad District reportedly affected two families, partially damaged their houses and resulted in one person killed.
Kapisa Province
The shelter of nine families in Tagab District was reportedly slightly affected. No casualties or destroyed houses have been reported.
No reports related to the recent rainfall and flooding have been received from Kabul, Logar, Panjshir or Parwan provinces.
Central Highlands Region
No casualties, damage or destruction related to the recent rainfall and flooding have been reported from Bamyan or Daikundi provinces.
Humanitarian Needs and Response
The response has moved from immediate search and rescue and emergency life-saving assistance towards a broader, multi-sector response addressing shelter, health, food, WASH, protection, livelihoods and planning to address early recovery needs.
In Nuristan, the completed inter-agency needs assessment identified 92 families eligible for humanitarian assistance. Humanitarian partners are providing emergency assistance while coordinating the rehabilitation of damaged infrastructure and planning early recovery interventions.
In Ghazni and Paktika, joint needs assessment teams have been deployed and assessments are ongoing. In Paktia, coordination is underway to determine the deployment of an assessment team.
Across affected areas, humanitarian partners continue to monitor the situation and prioritize assistance based on verified needs and assessment findings.
Food Security &amp; Agriculture (FSAC): The floods in Parun damaged approximately 280 orchard trees and resulted in the loss of 30 livestock. WFP has distributed High Energy Biscuits (HEBs) to 250 families, with additional supplies dispatched and further food assistance planned for 24 July. Shanti Volunteer Association is providing food assistance to 30 families in coordination with WFP.
Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) is reviewing the assessment findings and plans to support rehabilitation of irrigation canals, provision of livestock feed and other agriculture and livestock recovery interventions.
In Paktika, approximately 122 hectares of agricultural land were reportedly washed away and 37 livestock killed. Further impacts on agriculture and livelihoods in other affected provinces remain under assessment.
Shelter, Land and Site Coordination (SLSC): In Parun, 92 families identified as eligible for humanitarian assistance are receiving coordinated support. IOM is distributing 92 tents, 92 winter kits, 92 blankets and solar kits. UNICEF will distribute 92 non-food item (NFI) kits and hygiene kits each, while Save the Children (SCI) will provide 92 shelter repair kits. Distributions are planned for 23 July.
The extent of shelter damage in Ghazni and Paktia remains under assessment.
Health: The World Health Organization (WHO), in coordination with the Provincial Public Health Directorate (PPHD), the Basic Package of Health Services (BPHS) implementing partner ARSDO and other health partners, has dispatched additional medical supplies, including Emergency Health Kits (EHKs), Essential Health Kits (EH Kits), Trauma Kits and Cholera Response Kits.
Two Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) teams have been deployed, while two Mobile Health Teams (MHTs) remain on standby for rapid deployment. A specialized trauma expert team is scheduled to visit Parun on 27 July.
The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA), through its implementing partners MOVE and AFGA, has deployed a mobile psychosocial support team and two ambulances to facilitate referrals.
Première Urgence Internationale (PU-AMI) has dispatched medicines and medical consumables to the BPHS implementing partner. Handicap International (HI) and the Norwegian Committee for Afghanistan (NCA) have expressed readiness to provide physical rehabilitation services for injured people.
Further information on health needs in other newly affected provinces is expected through ongoing assessments.
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH): In Parun, one water-supply network, including approximately 500 metres of pipeline, was damaged, disrupting access to safe drinking water for an estimated 1,000 families. One of the two springs feeding the network was destroyed.
Pending rehabilitation, UNICEF and WASH Cluster partners are exploring interim solutions, including water trucking and other emergency water-supply options. UNICEF is also distributing hygiene kits, while hygiene promoters will conduct hygiene awareness and promotion sessions. Humanitarian partners have raised concerns over the heightened risk of waterborne disease outbreaks following the flooding.
Protection: The Protection Coordination Group is coordinating critical protection services, including MHPSS, child- and women-friendly spaces, family tracing and reunification for separated children, and psychosocial counselling for families who have lost loved ones or have missing family members.
UNFPA has initiated prevention and response to gender-based violence (GBV) and MHPSS services and will distribute dignity kits to affected families. UNICEF’s implementing partner HYSO is conducting a child protection-focused needs assessment.
Cash Assistance: The Cash Working Group is coordinating potential Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA). To date, Community World Service Asia has confirmed its readiness to provide MPCA to up to 200 affected families.
Coordination
OCHA, together with humanitarian partners and ANDMA, continues to coordinate the humanitarian response in collaboration with the United Nations and national and international non-governmental organization partners. Joint needs assessments have been completed in affected areas, while additional assessments are ongoing or planned to identify needs and inform response planning.
With inter-agency coordination mechanisms activated across affected regions, Operational Coordination Teams (OCTs) continue to meet and coordinate the response across affected provinces. Efforts are ongoing to scale up assistance in the most affected areas while supporting early recovery planning and the restoration of essential services and livelihoods.
This is a flash update based on information available as of 23 July 2026. Figures and other information remain subject to verification and may change as response activities continue.

--- Report 4: WHO (2026-07-22) ---
HIGHLIGHTS
On 20 July 2026, intense rainfall triggered severe flash floods across Nuristan Province in eastern Afghanistan, with the most significant impacts reported in Parun City, the provincial capital of Nuristan Province. Floodwaters rapidly inundated populated areas, causing widespread damage to homes, public infrastructure, agricultural land and commercial properties. Preliminary reports indicate significant loss of life, injuries and extensive disruption to livelihoods, essential services and local markets.
To date, 23 deaths and 15 injuries have been reported, while additional individuals remain missing across the affected areas. Search and rescue operations are ongoing. Casualty figures are expected to rise as assessment teams gain access to affected locations and rescue efforts continue.
WHO and partners are closely coordinating with local health authorities to support rapid assessments, deploy emergency health supplies and ensure the continuity of essential health services, including care for injured and affected populations.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
Intense rainfall triggered severe flash floods in eastern Afghanistan near Parun City, the provincial capital of Nuristan Province, at approximately 15:00 local time on 20 July 2026. The floods caused significant loss of life, injuries, and widespread damage to homes, public infrastructure, markets, roads and livelihoods in Parun City and surrounding communities.
The situation remains fluid, with search and rescue operations ongoing in Parun City and surrounding areas. Humanitarian partners and de facto authorities are providing emergency health assistance and other life-saving support to affected communities. Mobile Health Teams have been deployed, health facilities placed on heightened alert, and additional medical, nutrition and food supplies mobilized to address immediate needs.
Emergency response operations are ongoing. Health workers, search and rescue teams, and humanitarian partners have been mobilized to provide lifesaving medical care, emergency supplies and mental health and psychosocial support to affected communities.
WHO will continue to coordinate closely with national and provincial health authorities and partners to address the most urgent health needs of affected populations, support ongoing response efforts, and ensure the continuity of essential health services.

--- Report 5: ARCS (2026-07-22) ---
Description
On Monday, 20 July 2026, at approximately 3:00 p.m. local time, a sudden flash flood struck the central areas of Nuristan Province following heavy and continuous rainfall.
The low-lying areas and communities located along rivers and seasonal watercourses were the most severely affected. In Nuristan Province, the flash floods resulted in fatalities and injuries, while causing extensive damage to the central market, residential houses, public infrastructure, livelihoods and income sources, as well as agricultural land
This event is part of seasonal flooding currently affecting parts of Nuristan Province, with the most severe impacts reported in Parun District, the provincial capital.

Summary
Following the flooding, the Afghan Red Crescent Society (ARCS) immediately mobilized its emergency response teams, deploying more than 70 volunteers and 16 staff members from the Nuristan Provincial Branch. Search and Rescue (SAR) and First Aid teams were rapidly deployed to the affected areas. Coordination was established with the relevant government authorities and health service providers. ARCS has been actively engaged in search and rescue operations, which remain ongoing. Two ambulances have been deployed since yesterday to support the transportation of injured people.
Actions taken by others
The Government has continued its response efforts by deploying personnel and equipment to support search and rescue operations, evacuate injured individuals, clear access roads, recover bodies from the debris of collapsed structures, and carry out other emergency response activities.
