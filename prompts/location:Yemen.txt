Crisis / situation: Yemen

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-10-26) ---
IOM Yemen DTM’s Rapid Displacement Tracking (RDT) tool collects data on estimated numbers of households forced to flee on a daily basis from their locations of origin or displacement, allowing for regular reporting of new displacements in terms of estimated numbers, geography, and needs. It also tracks returnees who returned to their location of origin.
From 1 January to 24 October 2026, IOM Yemen DTM tracked 49,388 households (HH) (296,328 Individuals) who experienced displacement at least once.
Between 18 and 24 October 2026, IOM Yemen DTM tracked 2269 households (13614 individuals) displaced at least once. The majority of people moved into/within the following governorates and districts:
• Lahj-(900 HHs) – Al Madaribah Wa Al Aarah (534 HHs), Tuban (179 HHs), Al Maqatirah (92 HHs) districts. Most displacements in the governorate were internal.
• Taiz-(600 HHs) – Al Mudhaffar (162 HHs), Mashrah Wa Hadnan (143 HHs), Ash Shamayatayn (132 HHs) districts. All displacements in the governorate were internal.
• Aden-(553 HHs) – Al Burayqah (409 HHs), Dar Sad (93 HHs), At Tawahi (22 HHs) districts. Most displacements in the governorate originated from Lahj.
The majority of people moved from the following governorates and districts:
• Lahj- (1248 HHs) – Al Madaribah Wa Al Aarah (1240 HHs), Al Maqatirah (4 HHs), Al Musaymir (3 HHs) districts.
• Taiz- (897 HHs) – Al Misrakh (209 HHs), Al Mudhaffar (125 HHs), Ash Shamayatayn (117 HHs) districts.
• Al Hodeidah (75 HHs) – Al Khukhah (21 HHs), Hays (19 HHs), Al Jarrahi (10 HHs) districts.
IOM identified 2626 additional households displaced in earlier reporting periods, based on Kobo submissions received during the current reporting period in the governorates: of Aden (2005 HHs), Lahj (300 HHs), Abyan (250 HHs), Marib (26 HHs), Hadramawt (16 HHs), Ad Dali (15 HHs), Al Maharah (9 HHs), and Taiz (5 HHs). These figures have been added to the cumulative displacement total recorded since the beginning of the year.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-10-26) ---
IN NUMBERS
1.4 m people assisted in September 2026
12,220 mt food distributed
US$ 5.8 m cash transferred
US$ 294 m 6-month net funding requirement (November 2026 – April 2027)
HIGHLIGHTS

WFP welcomes the release of one of its staff members arbitrarily detained in Yemen and stands in solidarity with the 71 UN personnel, including 37 WFP staff and five former UN personnel who remain detained, continuing efforts to secure their immediate and safe release.
The food security situation continues to deteriorate in GoY controlled areas. The crisis is even more acute in Ta’iz, with disrupted supply routes, soaring prices and collapsing essential services exhausting vulnerable households’ coping capacity.
Amid blocked humanitarian access, WFP has launched calibrated emergency cash-based assistance in Ta’iz cut-off districts, providing a vital food lifeline for displaced people.
WFP requires USD 294 million to sustain its regular activities through April 2027 and support up to 1.5 million people affected by the ongoing conflict, should hostilities escalate further across all fronts.
Restoring safe, sustained and unimpeded humanitarian and commercial access to Ta’iz is critical to prevent vulnerable families from slipping deeper into hunger and meet surging needs.
WFP signed a strategic agreement with the Ministry of Agriculture, Irrigation and Fish Wealth to strengthen food security information and climate resilience while advancing locally-led recovery and institutional capacity.
