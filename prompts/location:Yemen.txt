Crisis / situation: Yemen

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-09-23) ---
IOM Yemen DTM continues to record the displacement caused by escalation in the west coast. On 21 September 2026, IOM Yemen DTM recorded the increase of 2,892 individual IDPs compared to the previous report, making the displacement between 1 August to 20 September reach an approximate of 131,274 individuals (approximately 21,879 households).
This brings the cumulative number recorded to 132,330 individuals (22,055 households) since the escalation began in June 2026. The largest concentration of displaced people was reported in Ta’iz Governorate, hosting 74,790 individuals (57%), followed by Lahj Governorate, with 34,428 individuals (26%) and Aden Governorate, with 9,942 individuals (8%). Additional displacement was recorded in Al Hodeidah, with 8,244 individuals (6%); Abyan, with 3,324 individuals (3%); Hadramawt, with 1,008 individuals (less than 1%); and Ma’rib, with 594 individuals (less than 1%).

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-09-22) ---
Highlights
§ Escalating hostilities in Yemen have driven significant displacement within the country and cross-border movements across the Gulf of Aden, with close to 130,000 people internally displaced inside Yemen1 and over 3,000 people crossing to Djibouti2 . Arrivals in Somalia remain smaller but are gradually increasing, including Yemeni refugees and Somali refugee returnees.
§ In response to the evolving situation, UNHCR has declared a Level 1 emergency for Yemen, Djibouti and Somalia for three months and is scaling up preparedness and response across the affected operations.
§ In Yemen, UNHCR is taking part to the inter-agency response under the Yemen Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP). In light of the recent escalation, UNHCR requires USD 25 million to support an estimated 231,000 people with protection services including monitoring, legal assistance and support for people at heightened risk, cash assistance, shelter and core relief items (CRIs) inside Yemen.
§ In Djibouti, UNHCR with the Government of Djibouti, developed an interagency appeal for a total amount of USD 13,27 million. The plan reflects a coordinated inter-agency approach covering activities among agencies (WFP, UNICEF, UNFPA, IOM and UNHCR) in support of 10,000 individuals (2,500 households) over a six-month period.

--- Report 3: Health Cluster (2026-09-22) ---
Key Highlights
• Casualties continue to be reported daily on every front. Between 13 and 18 September the MoPHP Health EOC recorded 680 new casualties (516 injuries, 164 deaths), with daily totals ranging from 78 to 195. Cumulative casualties since 6 August stand at 3,672 (2,998 injuries, 674 deaths), 789 more than published in Sitrep #3 once the 109 cases added retrospectively to the register on 13 September are included. Reported daily totals this period are below those of 3–12 September, but the deaths-to-injuries ratio has risen: 164 deaths against 516 injuries (24%) compared with 17% previously, and on 15 September deaths (35) exceeded injuries (43). Since the rise is not explained by casualty volume, it could indicate limitations in access to timely trauma and surgical care and in referral mechanisms, though the nature of the incidents and possible under-reporting of injuries where access and communications are degraded may also contribute. The HEOC is reviewing the underlying causes, including time to reach care and surgical capacity in the receiving areas.
• Lahj has become the most active front. The Al-Madharibah wa Ras Al-Arah district – the coastal approach from Bab el-Mandeb towards Aden – reported 284 casualties in six days (226 injuries, 58 deaths), including 123 on 16 September, the highest daily governorate total of the period. Lahj's cumulative toll has almost tripled, from 149 to 433. Marib (Al-Jubah, Harib, Raghwan and Marib Al-Wadi) reported 277 casualties and remains the largest cumulative burden (1,690, 46%); Taiz (Al-Shamayatayn/At-Turbah, Al-Ma'afer, Maqbanah, Sabir Al-Mawadim and Al-Misrakh) reported 103. Al-Jawf (Al-Hazm, eight deaths on 15 September), Shabwah (Usaylan) and Al-Dhale'e (Muris) also recorded incidents.
• Displacement has passed 120,000 people across eight governorates. The Executive Unit for IDP Camp Management's consolidated report through 15 September records 17,852 displaced families, an estimated 122,297 people, in Taiz (9,562 families), Lahj (4,511), Al-Hodeidah (1,471), Aden (1,180), Abyan (1,003), Hadramout (66), Al-Mahra (40) and Shabwah (19) – an increase of about 44,500 people on the 77,789 reported by the Health Cluster through 11 September, and the first registrations in Hadramout and Al-Mahra. Of particular concern, 3,034 families are hosted in Al-Madharibah wa Ras Al-Arah, the same Lahj district that is now producing the highest casualty loads, and the 1,471 families previously in Hays and Al-Khokha have moved on to Aden. The inter-agency picture is consistent: OCHA's countrywide preliminary estimate is about 18,000 households (≈126,000 people) since the beginning of September, still under registration and verification, with 13,644 RRM displacement alerts across nine governorates of which 8,312 households had been verified and assisted by 12 September. IOM DTM tracking shows most movement is short-distance – from Jabal Habashi, Maqbanah, Al-Wazi'iyah, Al-Ma'afer and Mawza into neighbouring districts of Taiz, southern Al-Hodeidah and western Lahj – and that at least 908 households in the Taiz hub were already displaced before this wave. The Executive Unit states that its figures are not final and that the Aden field team faces constraints in reaching displaced households.
• Attacks on health care and on displaced civilians. On 18 September ambulances were targeted at the Kahbub front in Lahj, killing an ambulance driver and an emergency medical responder. Health authorities in Marib reported shelling of Al-Hamah IDP site in Marib City on 9 September (three children killed, six people injured) and of a residence in Raghwan on 14 September (eight children injured), in addition to the Asdas camp attack reported in Sitrep #3. Nine health facilities or assets have now been damaged, put out of service, closed or directly attacked in Taiz, Lahj and Al-Hodeidah; on the West Coast, Al-Jurrah Health Unit (Hays) and Dhubab Hospital have reopened while Jisr Al-Hamli Centre (Mawza) remains closed.
• Civilian harm is shifting with the front lines. Open-source monitoring by the Civilian Impact Monitoring Project (CIMP) recorded 53 civilian casualties (14 killed, 39 injured) in 45 incidents between 10 and 16 September, 70% fewer than the previous week (178), as the most active front lines moved away from populated areas of southern Al-Hodeidah and south-western Taiz towards less densely populated north-western Lahj. Lahj nonetheless accounted for over half of front-line civilian casualties (18), including nine when a mosque on the Al-Bayda border was shelled and four on the Hayjat Al-Abd road – the main paved Taiz–Aden route, struck twice this month. Airstrikes on 14 September hit a bridge in Maqbanah and houses in Dhubab (five casualties); 65 houses, two bridges, two airports, a seaport, two water facilities and a school were among the civilian structures affected. CIMP's 2026 year-to-date civilian casualty count stands at 1,256.
• WHO's third dispatch has been delivered to all four hospitals. Surgical supplies, commodities and antibiotics were offloaded at Al-Gamhoriah Teaching Hospital and 22 May Hospital in Aden on 13 September, at Kara Hospital in Marib on 16 September and at Marib General Hospital Authority on 17 September, following MoPHP confirmation of road access. Signed proof of delivery is being collected. Procurement of 17,000 adults and 3,000 paediatric blood bags (≈US$ 32,000) continues. The Aden warehouse remains at stock-out for normal saline, sterile surgical gloves, atropine and potassium chloride, and without TESK/trauma kits and anaesthetic drugs; replenishment is now the binding constraint on any further dispatch. WHO is engaging its Dubai Hub for rapid procurement and shipment of supplies using ongoing and pipeline resources.
• Coordination has moved to a daily rhythm. The MoPHP HEOC now issues a daily consolidated report covering casualties, displacement and 24-hour operational priorities, with Lahj, Taiz, Marib and Aden designated as the geographic priority. At the ad hoc MoPHP–partners meeting of 15 September, MoPHP shared the list of essential surgical supplies and medicines, and the list of frontline hospitals and stabilization centres was circulated for partner mapping by 17 September. Health Cluster partners are adding capacity: Pure Hands is dispatching Interagency Emergency Health Kits and equipment to Al-Nashmah and Khalifah hospitals in Taiz and to hospitals in Marib, Lahj and Al-Dhale'e; INTERSOS has a mobile team at Al-Saab camps; IRC has two mobile teams ready for Aden and Lahj; 15 mobile medical teams are supported across Taiz, Aden, Lahj, the West Coast and Marib.
• Current response under Scenario 2, with a funding gap: All triggers for activation of the second scenario of the joint response plan have been met, and MoPHP, WHO and Health Cluster partners are now operating under Scenario 2 interventions. Of the US$ 9 million required for Scenario 2, US$ 2 million has been secured or pledged to date, leaving US$ 7 million (78%) unfunded. As a result, the trauma stabilization points, specialized care teams, ICU expansion, referral pathways, mobile medical teams and essential services for displaced communities, outbreak prevention and response, and trauma supplies that Scenario 2 specifies cannot be completed. Triggers for the third scenario are monitored daily by the HEOC, with contingency planning for Scenario 3 under way.

--- Report 7: UNFPA (2026-09-21) ---
Rapidly escalating hostilities along Yemen’s western coast and southern Taiz frontlines have displaced nearly 94,000 people, a 135 percent increase in displacement since 8 September. The number continues to rise by the hour, with many families experiencing repeated displacement. Women and girls are among the most vulnerable and urgently need health and protection support. Continued military escalations, insecurity and impassable roads are severely constraining the humanitarian response.
Intensifying violence has disrupted reproductive health and women’s protection services in the most affected Governorates, with several health facilities and women and girls safe spaces (WGSS) closed. Limited safe shelter, privacy, and sanitation along with shortages of dignity kits, are increasing protection risks, while an estimated 400 displaced pregnant women require urgent comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care.
The UNFPA-led UN Inter-Agency Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) remains a first line of humanitarian response and has delivered emergency relief to nearly 59,000 individuals as of 14 September. Lack of funding is severely straining UNFPA’s ability to respond; US$22.7 million is urgently needed to scale up the response to December 2026.
