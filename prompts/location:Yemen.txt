Crisis / situation: Yemen

--- Report 1: IMC (2026-10-08) ---
FAST FACTS

197,906 people from 29,519 families have been displaced across 10 governorates, approximately a 10% increase from the last report.
Eighteen of 19 International Medical Corps-supported health facilities remain operational across the west coast despite continuing access constraints.

OUR FOOTPRINT

Across Yemen, International Medical Corps supports 63 health facilities, with an estimated coverage of 3.3 million people across seven governorates: Aden, Al Dhale’e, Al Hodeidah, Ibb, Lahj, Sana’a and Taiz.
Our lifesaving multi-sectoral assistance prioritizes active conflict zones and high-risk environments. In 2025, we helped more than 400,000 people.

OUR RESPONSE

A mobile medical team that we support has provided 475 medical consultations, screened 106 children under 5 and 38 pregnant and lactating women for acute malnutrition, provided immunization services to 31 women and 36 children, and conducted 50 antenatal care consultations.
Our supported facilities have provided 11,835 medical consultations to people on the west coast.
We have provided 26 metric tons of essential medicines and medical supplies to six health facilities in Aden and Lahj that will help sustain critical health, emergency obstetric and referral services amid increasing needs and ongoing supply constraints.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-10-07) ---
Yemen: Escalating Protection Risks
Displacement, access constraints and mounting pressure on internally displaced people, refugees and host communities
Situation at a glance
The latest military escalation in Yemen has displaced growing numbers of people at an alarming rate, worsening an already severe humanitarian crisis and intensifying existing protection concerns. Families uprooted from the West Coast, Taiz, Lahj and Marib are fleeing their homes carrying limited belongings while facing insecurity, high transportation costs, and shortages of food, water and medicines. Host communities continue to provide support; however, reduced assistance, increasing pressure on already overstretched services, and competition over limited resources risk weakening social cohesion. Many of the newly displaced families have already experienced multiple waves of displacement, further exhausting their coping capacities, depleting their resources, and increasing their exposure to protection risks. As the conflict and insecurity intensify, displacement patterns are expanding beyond traditional hotspots, with growing movements from Taiz towards Lahj, Aden and Abyan, placing additional pressure on host communities, public services and humanitarian response capacities.
