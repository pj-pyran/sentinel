Crisis / situation: Yemen

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-11-19) ---
IOM Yemen DTM continues to record displacement caused by the escalation in western Yemen. As of 17 November 2026, IOM Yemen DTM recorded an increase of 9,281 individual IDPs compared to the figure presented in the RDT report distributed on 16 November 2026, bringing displacement between 1 August and 17 November to approximately 356,602 individuals (59,426 households)1. This brings the cumulative number recorded since the escalation began in June 2026 to 357,658 individuals (59,602 households).
The largest concentration of displaced people was reported in Ta’iz Governorate, hosting 145,344 individuals (41%), followed by Lahj Governorate with 95,580 individuals (27%), and Aden Governorate with 71,706 individuals (20%). Additional displacement was recorded in Abyan with 17,208 individuals (5%); Al Hodeidah with 8,244 individuals (2%)2; Shabwah with 6,183 individuals (2%); Ma’rib with 4,104 individuals (1%); Hadramawt with 3,576 individuals (1%); Al Maharah with 3,096 individuals (less than 1%); and Ad Dali’ with 2,617 individuals (less than 1%).

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-11-18) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
Yemen’s security and humanitarian situation worsened significantly throughout September, driven by the continued spread of ground fighting following August’s violent escalations. Ground hostilities expanded rapidly across southwestern fronts, with Ansar Allah (AA) forces intensifying offensives in Ta’iz and Lahj as they attempted to consolidate gains on the West Coast by securing the overlooking mountains. The fighting triggered a massive surge in displacement, forcing over 160,140 individuals to flee their homes, particularly from the Maqbanah, Jabal Habashi, and Al Ma’afer districts in Ta’iz. Vital supply lines, including the crucial road linking Mokha to Ta’iz City, were severely disrupted and cut off, stranding communities and impacting humanitarian access. The AA forces claimed to have targeted several sites in Riyadh and the Aramco facilities in Yanbu, a Saudi Red Sea port, with missiles and drones. Saudi Arabia requested air defenses for critical facilities from Egypt, France, Pakistan, and the UK. French President Emmanuel Macron said France would send military assets to help protect Yanbu following the attack, as the concerns over maritime security and global oil prices grew. Saudi Arabia also reportedly urged the Government of Yemen (GoY) to call for a general mobilization, which was announced in a speech marking the anniversary of the September 26 Revolution. On the other side, in a move to capitalize on its capture of the Bab al-Mandeb and the West Coast, the AA-affiliated Red Sea Ports and Tax Customs Authorities announced reductions in vessel and container fees and port taxes, effective 1 October. The sharp escalation in violence severely strained Yemen’s already compromised health infrastructure, with hospitals in Ta’iz, Ma’rib, and Hadramaut overwhelmed by casualties. Major health centers were forced to suspend non-emergency care—such as maternity and obstetrics services—to reallocate scarce beds and medical personnel toward trauma management. The cholera epidemic maintained a strong hold across southern and western governorates, with transmission exacerbated by population displacement and damaged water and sanitation infrastructure. Abyan and Lahj continued to report the highest concentration of suspected cases, while newly displaced clusters in Ta’iz and Aden saw rising incidence rates. According to the IPC, nearly five million people faced crisis levels of hunger or worse in government-controlled areas between March and May, including 1.4 million in emergency conditions, and it has been observed that food security has significantly deteriorated since the escalation began. Wheat flour prices increased by 10 per cent in parts of Ta’iz as of late September, while traders estimate wheat stocks will cover only about two months of demand. At the UN General Assembly, 80 countries supported a statement condemning “the Houthis’ decision to resume the conflict in Yemen, their continuous strikes on Saudi Arabia, and their reckless attacks on the Red Sea.” UN Special Envoy Hans Grundberg met with AA negotiators in Muscat to avoid further escalation and held talks in Cairo with the regional powers, including Egypt and the League of Arab States.

--- Report 3: IOM (2026-11-18) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
Yemen’s security and humanitarian crisis worsened in October with the expanding ground offensives, intensifying cross-border air strikes, and severe strains on already fragile structures for essential services such as healthcare. Ansar Allah (AA) forces sustained their multi-axis offensives across southwestern fronts, continuing heavy assaults on the key frontlines in Taiz while launching multiple strikes at strategic structures including the Presidential Palace and International Airport in Aden. This led to large-scale movements, driving the cumulative number of newly displaced individuals to over 300,000 since June. Taiz became the main frontline and humanitarian concern when AA forces captured Al-Turbah and AsSafiyah, cutting the main Taiz–Aden Road and effectively encircling Taiz City. Key commercial and humanitarian transport corridors remained blocked or compromised, with severe access restrictions critically isolating the population in Taiz. AA forces expanded long-range ballistic missile and drone strikes against the International Airports, oil infrastructures, and military installations across Saudi Arabia, and Saudi-led coalition forces alongside the Government of Yemen (GoY) also intensified air strikes against key AA positions in Yemen. As of 27 October, OHCHR had documented 263 civilian casualties, including 67 people killed. Children accounted for 97 of the casualties and about half of those killed. With the surge in conflict casualties, major referral hospitals across Taiz, Lahj, Marib, and Hadramawt faced severe shortages of basic medical supplies, fuel, and medical oxygen, and were forced to suspend routine and elective care to prioritize trauma operations. The health emergency was compounded by damaged water treatment systems and overcrowded, unsanitary conditions in displacement sites across the top concentrated governorates such as Lahj, Abyan, Taiz, and Aden. Food security exacerbated as staple commodities and fuel prices increased steeply with the continuing supply chain blockades and worsening access situation. Depleted humanitarian stockpiles and severe underfunding raise concerns of acute malnutrition cases increasing, as estimated 5.4 million people in GoY-controlled areas are expected to be in IPC Phase 3 or worse food insecurity in the final quarter of the year. Both GoY and AA raised fuel prices in their respective areas of control in the last week of October, with official price of gasoline ranging between YR 570 per liter (AA-controlled area, 20% increase since midSeptember), YR 1,700 per liter (GoY-controlled area), and over YR 2,500 per liter in Taiz. The citizens are forced to purchase fuel in the black market, where petrol prices skyrocketed to over YR 150,000 for a 20-liter can, as most gas stations have shut down. Water production in Taiz city has fallen by about half due to the critical shortage of fuel.
