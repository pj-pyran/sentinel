Crisis / situation: Yemen

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-10-30) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS

Displacement continues to grow, with more than 281,000 people displaced. Most have fled fighting in Ta’iz and along the West Coast, and a new surge from Lahj in mid-October shows that the crisis is spreading rather than stabilizing.
People in and around Ta’iz City face growing hardship. With access to several city districts severely restricted, health facilities face shortages of fuel, water and medicines, some supplies for the city are still awaiting delivery, and fewer displaced families are able to leave the governorate.
Civilians continue to bear the brunt of the fighting. As of 27 October, OHCHR had documented 263 civilian casualties, including 67 people killed. Children account for 97 of the casualties and about half of those killed.
207,480 IDPs (29,640 households) have been reached with first-line RRM assistance, and most have received their kits; almost 9,000 registered families are still waiting as supply pipelines, clearances and insecurity slow delivery.
Second-line assistance lags far behind needs. Emergency cash has reached most verified families and food assistance just over half of verified households, but shelter and WASH support has reached only a minority of households in need, and nutrition treatment a small share of those targeted, leaving families in crowded sites exposed as cholera spreads.
Protection risks are rising. Partners report more family separation, explosive ordnance risks, eviction threats and a growing risk of gender-based violence, while five safe spaces for women and girls have closed.
Partners continue to deliver in hard-to-reach areas. A small number of national and international partners keep health, water, shelter, cash and mine action services running in the West Coast.
Access getting more constrained. Taiz continues to be encircled with limited access for humanitarian and commercial supplies. Humanitarian movements are being restricted at checkpoints despite approved permits, and damaged roads are slowing supplies between Ta’iz and the south.
Much of the displacement in areas under the de facto authorities goes unrecorded. Unverified estimates point to thousands of newly displaced families in Ta’iz, Ibb, Al Bayda and Dhamar.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-10-29) ---
IOM Yemen DTM continues to record displacement caused by the escalation in western Yemen. On 27 October 2026, IOM Yemen DTM recorded an increase of 12,522 individual IDPs compared to the figure presented in the RDT report distributed on 26 October 2026, bringing displacement between 1 August and 27 October to approximately 286,711 individuals (47,779 households). This brings the cumulative number recorded since the escalation began in June 2026 to 287,767 individuals (47,955 households).
The largest concentration of displaced people was reported in Ta’iz Governorate, hosting 128,484 individuals (45%), followed by Lahj Governorate with 71,100 individuals (25%), and Aden Governorate with 53,016 individuals (18%). Additional displacement was recorded in Abyan with 15,102 individuals (5%); Al Hodeidah with 8,244 individuals (3%); Shabwah with 3,420 individuals (1%); Hadramawt with 2,820 individuals (1%); Ma’rib with 2,280 individuals (less than 1%); Al Maharah with 1,968 individuals (less than 1%); and Ad Dali’ with 1,333 individuals (less than 1%)
