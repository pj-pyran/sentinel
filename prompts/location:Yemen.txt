Crisis / situation: Yemen

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-10-01) ---
Highlights
▪ Around 142,000 people have been newly displaced inside Yemen since 1 August, while almost 3,793 refugees have crossed by sea to Djibouti and some 150 people, including Yemeni refugees, Ethiopian nationals and Somali returnees, have arrived in Somaliland and Puntland. Arrivals to Somalia remain low but are expected to increase from mid-October, when sea conditions improve.
▪ In Yemen, UNHCR is part of the inter-agency response under the Yemen Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP). In light of the recent escalation, UNHCR requires US$ 25 million to support an estimated 231,000 people with protection services, including monitoring, legal assistance and support for people at heightened risk, cash assistance, shelter and core relief items (CRIs).
▪ In addition, UNHCR requires some US$ 7.4 million to lead and scale up the refugee response in Djibouti and strengthen preparedness for new population movements towards Somalia.

--- Report 2: IMC (2026-10-01) ---
FAST FACTS

180,513 individuals from 27,413 households have been displaced.
Eighteen of 19 International Medical Corps-supported health facilities are now functional across the west coast, up from 15 in the last two weeks.

OUR FOOTPRINT

Across Yemen, International Medical Corps supports 63 health facilities with an estimated coverage of 3.3 million people across seven governorates: Aden, Al Dhale’e, Al Hodeidah, Ibb, Lahj, Sana’a and Taiz.
Our lifesaving multi-sectoral assistance prioritizes active conflict zones and high-risk environments. In 2025, we reached more than 400,000 people with integrated humanitarian programming.

OUR RESPONSE

International Medical Corps-supported mobile medical teams provided 271 medical consultations, screened 54 children under 5 and 28 pregnant and lactating women for acute malnutrition, provided immunization services to 31 women and 20 children, and conducted 32 antenatal care consultations.
We provided 5 metric tons of essential medicines and medical supplies to Al Thawra Hospital, a key referral facility serving cases from the west coast and displaced families in Taiz. This will help sustain critical health services for approximately two months, amid increasing needs and ongoing supply constraints. Continued availability of medicines and medical supplies remains essential to ensuring uninterrupted access to lifesaving care for displaced populations and vulnerable host communities.
