Crisis / situation: Myanmar

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-07-27) ---
KEY Highlight June 2026
Rising Food Basket Cost
By the first week of July (3 July), the national average food basket cost was 20 percent higher than in the last week of February. In June, it increased by 2 percent month-on-month to MMK 51,187, reflecting rising prices of key food commodities. Elevated transportation costs, driven by continued fuel shortages, remained a key factor contributing to higher household food costs.
Impact of Fuel Price Surge
Despite a gradual decline in diesel prices during the reporting period, prices remained substantially above pre-crisis levels. Nationwide, diesel prices were still 45 percent higher on average, with Yangon recording the largest increase at around 1.5 percent above the pre-crisis benchmark. In conflict-affected areas, fuel costs remained particularly elevated, with diesel selling at approximately MMK 11,000 per liter in Sittwe and MMK 12,500 per liter in Myitkyina—nearly five times the benchmark price previously observed in Yangon.
Significant Price Increases
Tomato prices increased sharply in Central Rakhine and recorded modest increases in several other areas, reflecting seasonal declines in market supply. At the same time, key basket commodities, including rice, chickpeas, and salt, also rose in some areas. Prices of other commonly consumed items, such as Tomato and onions, increased as well. According to traders, these price increases were driven by supply chain disruptions, high transportation costs, seasonal fluctuations in production areas, and continued market instability resulting from ongoing conflict in several parts of the country.
Notable Price Decreases
Egg prices decreased across most markets in Myanmar in June 2026 compared with the previous month, driven primarily by improved supply. Reduced consumer purchasing power also weakened demand, contributing to the price decline. In contrast, prices remained elevated in conflict-affected areas of Northern Shan, where transport disruptions continued to limit the flow of supplies into local markets.

--- Report 2: DFS (2026-07-27) ---
Executive Summary
Myanmar faces a protracted humanitarian emergency characterized by widespread displacement, ongoing armed conflict, severe economic deterioration, and compounding natural disasters. The crisis demands sustained international attention and coordinated humanitarian response across multiple sectors. The Myanmar crisis requires sustained engagement across diplomatic, humanitarian, and development channels. Immediate priorities include scaling humanitarian assistance, protecting civilian infrastructure, and maintaining pressure for political resolution while preparing for long-term recovery and resilience-building efforts.
