Crisis / situation: Myanmar

--- Report 1: Insecurity Insight (2026-09-10) ---
Using data collected by Insecurity Insight, the Safeguarding Health in Conflict Coalition (SHCC) identified 259 incidents of violence against or obstruction of health care in Myanmar in 2025. The use of explosive weapons in attacks on health care increased in 2025 compared to previous years, highlighting a rise in more lethal and indiscriminate violence with wide-area effects in the reporting year. Aircraft-delivered strikes by the MAF rose by nearly 25%, from 66 incidents in 2024 to 81 in 2025, with its share increasing from 52% of all reported incidents involving explosive weapons to 67% over the same period, increasing the lethality of MAF strikes on health care in opposition-held areas. Members of the PDF and LDFs also conducted air strikes that impacted health care, but using much smaller delivery platforms, underlying the asymmetry of their weapons systems and the associated lower lethality of their conduct of the conflict.
In Bago, Mandalay, and Sagaing, MAF paramotors and gyrocopters (light, low-flying aircraft capable of evading detection) were reportedly used to drop bombs on health facilities in villages under the control of armed groups, including the KNU, as well as hospitals operating under NUG control. At least six health workers were killed by these weapons. An MAF medic was also reported killed in Kayin by KNLA and PDF handmade electroshock rocket attacks (improvised weapons with a limited payload and low accuracy).

--- Report 2: KHRG (2026-09-10) ---
This Incident Report describes events occurring in Bu Tho Township, Mu Traw (Hpapun) District, in December 2025. On 12 December 2025, a Burma Army fighter jet dropped two 500-pound bombs onto Tz--- village, Bu Ah Der village tract, even though there was no fighting in the area or armed resistance fighters in the village. Simultaneously, the Burma Army also launched two kamikaze drones at the village. As a result, a four-year-old girl was killed and 12 villagers, including two children, were injured. The injured villagers travelled on foot and by boat to Ck--- Hospital for treatment. As a consequence of the air strikes, villagers from Tz--- village no longer dared to live in the village and sought refuge near a river, in forests, on plantations, and in farm huts.[1]

Part 3 – Complete Description of the Incident
Describe the Incident(s) in complete detail.
On 12 December 2025, at approximately 10 pm, the Burma Army Air Force carried out air strikes on Tz--- village, Bu Ah Der village tract, Bu Tho Township, Mu Traw (Hpapun) District. A fighter jet dropped two 500-pound bombs, and at the same time, the Burma Army also launched two kamikaze drones at the village. A child named Naw Q---, aged four years and ten months, was hit on the back by shrapnel from the explosion of one of the fighter jet’s bombs and was killed on the spot. In addition, the following 12 villagers were injured:

Saw G--- (64 years old) was injured on his head;
Saw I--- (39 years old) was injured on his buttocks;
Saw Y--- (26 years old) [nature of injury unknown];
Naw B--- (31 years old) [nature of injury unknown];
Naw N--- (six years old) [nature of injury unknown];
Naw J--- (24 years old) [nature of injury unknown];
Naw M--- (49 years old) [nature of injury unknown];
Naw L--- (74 years old) [nature of injury unknown];
Saw K--- (38 years old) [nature of injury unknown];
Saw R--- (four years old) was injured on his head;
Saw Z--- (49 years old) [nature of injury unknown]; and
Naw T--- (34 years old) [nature of injury unknown].

Tz--- village is a small village with approximately 25 households. The houses are simple structures built primarily from bamboo and roofed with leaves. There were no houses constructed with wood or roofed with zinc sheets in the village. [The village’s buildings were visibly residential.]
According to local villagers, there were no Kaw Thoo Lei[6] soldiers [in this context, referring to soldiers from the Karen National Liberation Army (KNLA)[7] or the Karen National Defence Organisation (KNDO)[8]] in or near Tz--- village at the time of the air strikes, and no armed clashes were taking place in the area. Fighting had only occurred near a bridge along the Hpapun Road, which was located far away from the village where the air strikes were conducted. Villagers were therefore unable to identify any military objective that could have justified the attack.
After the incident, villagers assisted one another and [those who were not injured] carried those who were injured to a hidden [safer] place. Moreover, some villagers transported injured villagers to the Ck--- Hospital located in Nk--- village, Htee Ler Poo village tract, Bu Tho Township, administered by the KDHW [Karen Department of Health and Welfare][9] for treatment, while others checked damaged houses and searched for survivors. On their journey to the hospital, they had to walk from Tz--- village to Zy--- Salween shore [on the Salween riverbank at Zy--- place], then sail by motorboat to Ck--- Hospital. Local authorities from the KNU [Karen National Union][10] and the KNDO organised the boat transportation for them. [Villagers who were treated at Ck--- Hospital recovered and returned to their families following the incident.] Meanwhile, some villagers who were not injured contacted their neighbours [villagers from other nearby villages to inform them about the incident].
After the air strikes, the Burma Army conducted air reconnaissance over the village [Tz--- village] every day. [The reconnaissance occurred during the day, night, or both, and as of January 2026 was still continuing.] Therefore, since 12 December 2025, and until now [January 2026], villagers were afraid and no longer dared to stay in their village. Villagers fled to the source of a river, and some fled to forests for safety. Also, some villagers had built farm huts and prepared [stored] food in them as a precaution before the incident in their village. [Following the air strikes, some villagers also fled to these farm huts, and some fled to their plantations for safety.]
[As of January 2026,] villagers from Tz--- village had not yet dared return to their homes and were facing livelihood challenges. Also, teachers and students did not dare to study in the school in the village, so they studied in a forest.
The incident also caused serious fear for some women, including some who were postpartum. It affected their mental health, and some were traumatised by what they had experienced [for example, they ran whenever they heard sounds similar to those of aircraft].
