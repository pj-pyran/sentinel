Crisis / situation: Chad

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-10-06) ---
UPDATE ON SITUATION IN SUDAN
Context
The security situation along Chad-Sudan borders remains a concern, although condition within the country is generally stable. The border provinces of Wadi Fira, Ennedi Est, and Ouaddaï continue to be affected by the spillover effects of the conflict in neighbouring Sudan. The ongoing crisis continues to drive people to seek safety in Chad while also impacting border communities and humanitarian operations.
Recent reports indicate continued insecurity and armed clashes in several parts of Sudan, including North Kordofan, North Darfur, and South Darfur. As hostilities persist and frontlines shift, civilian populations continue to face significant protection risks and new waves of displacement. In Nyala, South Darfur, residents reported renewed aerial strikes on 27 September following a period of relative calm.
The continued conflict exposes civilian populations to heightened protection risks, including injuries and loss of life caused by hostilities, forced displacement, disruption of livelihoods, and reduced access to essential services. Humanitarian actors remain concerned about the impact of the intensification of military operations on civilians, particularly in areas regularly affected by conflict and insecurity.
In addition to conflict-related challenges, several regions of Sudan are experiencing worsening climatic conditions. Below-average rainfall, degraded grazing land, and increasing water scarcity are undermining livelihoods, particularly for communities dependent on agriculture and livestock. The combined effects of conflict, drought, and declining access to natural resources are increasingly driving population movements towards safer areas, including eastern Chad. New arrivals interviewed at the border frequently cited insecurity, along with its consequences, including the loss of livelihoods, as key factors that prompted their departure. Insufficient rainfall and the drying up of water sources have further exacerbated the erosion of livelihoods, making already precarious living conditions even more difficult.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-10-06) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• A joint mission to Bol and Bagasola in Lac Province found that displacement, drought, and limited access to productive land continue to drive acute humanitarian needs, underscoring the need for strengthened food assistance and expanded resilience-building activities to support both displaced and host communities.
• Conducted jointly by WFP and UNHCR in coordination with the National Commission for Refugees and Returnees (CNARR), the vulnerabilitybased targeting assessment was completed and preparations for the transition to vulnerabilitybased assistance are underway, with rollout now expected in October 2026.
• Under the anticipatory action framework, 29,568 people received cash-based assistance in Lac Province.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• From 10 to 15 August, the Food Security Cluster, the Cash Working Group, and the Ministry of Social Action, Solidarity and Humanitarian Affairs conducted a joint mission to Bol and Bagasola in Lac Province to assess humanitarian needs, response gaps, and market conditions for newly displaced people. The mission confirmed that the humanitarian situation remains critical, with continued population displacement and a high dependence on humanitarian assistance among both displaced and host communities. Stakeholders highlighted the increasing pressure on host households, which are simultaneously coping with the effects of drought and declining incomes. The mission also found that belowaverage rainfall is threatening agricultural production, the main livelihood source for many households, while limited access to productive land remains a major constraint for displaced populations.
