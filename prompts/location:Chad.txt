Crisis / situation: Chad

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-06-09) ---
Returnees are Chadian nationals who were living in Sudan and have been displaced back to their home country, where they lack the resources to rebuild their lives. Returnees are living either in displacement sites or within host communities. They are in urgent need of food, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), shelter, household items (NFIs), healthcare and protection. IOM is the lead agency for the returnee response, in close coordination with the Government of Chad, local authorities, other UN agencies, and local and international NGOs.
From IOM and UNHCR data, 1,329,732 people have crossed into Chad since the onset of the crisis in Sudan, of which 400,914 are Chadian returnees as of 31 May 2026. IOM expects this number to rise to 405,000 by June 2026 due to the ongoing conflict and food insecurity in Darfur.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-06-08) ---
Key Highlights of the Week
• A total of 734 refugees (205 households), including 56 Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs), were relocated through two (02) convoys organized on 25 and 29 May 2026, from the border area of Adré to the Arkoum site (Ouaddaï).
• Overall, 9,372 refugees (2,663 households) have been relocated from Adré to the sites of Kouchaguine-Moura (3,927 individuals / 973 households), Dougui (787 individuals / 343 households), and Arkoum (4,658 individuals / 1,347 households) since the launch of the accelerated relocation operation by the authorities on 1 March 2026. Among them, 1,021 were Persons with Specific Needs.
• More than 80 m³ of safe drinking water per day is currently supplied at the sites of Kouchaguine-Moura and Arkoum (Ouaddaï).
• A total of 3,502 emergency shelters have been constructed and 70 family tents installed, out of 16,900 planned.
