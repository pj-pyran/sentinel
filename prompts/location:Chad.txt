Crisis / situation: Chad

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-08-10) ---
UPDATE ON SITUATION IN SUDAN
Context
The security situation in Chad remains generally stable, although some border areas with Sudan, particularly in the Wadi Fira and Sila provinces, continue to be closely monitored. Security incidents reported between 3 and 4 August 2026 in Birséliba and surrounding localities near the Sudanese border reportedly had a severe impact on civilian populations, causing loss of life, injuries, and new population displacements. As a result, several families were forced to flee to the Chadian localities of Korabo and Birak in search of protection and safety.
A joint mission by UNHCR, the National Commission for the Reception and Reintegration of Refugees and Returnees (CNARR), and the Chadian Red Cross (CRT) was deployed to the border area to better understand the evolving situation and assess humanitarian needs. The mission observed the arrival of new households from Sudan and initiated preregistration activities while maintaining regular monitoring of population movements. Authorities and humanitarian partners continue to monitor the area closely to guide any necessary assistance based on identified needs.
In eastern Chad, the security situation in refugee-hosting areas remains relatively calm. The ongoing disarmament operation in Sila Province continues to contribute to a secure environment, although the partial withdrawal of security forces from Goz Beïda calls for continued vigilance. Protection concerns nevertheless persist in Wadi Fira, where a refugee family relocated for security reasons continues to face threats.
In addition, several incidents of crime and delinquency, including burglaries and assaults involving minors, were reported in some refugee sites. Tensions were also observed in Métché between refugees and host communities, as well as in Adré and Goudrane, where demonstrations by local youth linked to access to land and economic opportunities were reported.
The rainy season currently represents one of the main threats to both affected populations and humanitarian actors. Flooding and rising seasonal rivers (wadis) have caused several deaths, material damage, and disruptions along major road networks, complicating access to refugee sites and the delivery of humanitarian assistance.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-08-10) ---
Weekly Highlights
• From 27 - 31 July, 2,387 refugees (679 households) were relocated from border areas to the inland sites of Arkoum (Ouaddaï Province) under the accelerated relocation programme.
• Since 15 April 2026, a cumulative 31,059 individuals (8,519 households) have been relocated under the accelerated relocation programme, bringing the total number relocated since January 2026 to 47,902 refugees (15,910 households).
• Since April 2023, UNHCR and its partners have relocated 648,797 individuals (173,847 households) from border areas to safer sites, representing 69% of the 940,612 Sudanese refugees who have arrived in Chad since April 2023.
• Relocations have taken place to Ennedi Est (79,516 individuals), Sila (81,304), Wadi Fira (198,479), and Ouaddaï (289,498). • Since 15 April 2026, a total of 50,508 refugees has been biometrically registered and 11,293 others have been preregistered, making a total of 61,801 individuals under OCHA-US funding.
• Since 15 April, a total of 23,605 refugee documentation (households and individuals) has been delivered to refugees in Eastern Chad. • Since April 2026, a total of 10,542 emergency shelters constructed across selected sites in the Ouaddai, Sila and Wadi Fira, improving living conditions for newly relocated refugee families.
