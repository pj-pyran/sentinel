Crisis / situation: Uganda

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-11-16) ---
The Uganda Red Cross Society (URCS) Regional Operations Managers, Branch Managers, RCATs are activated and have been disseminating Disaster Risk Information and Early Warning advisories based on the official Government of Uganda seasonal September - December 2026 forecast that includes predicted El Nino impacts in Uganda.
At the end of October 2026, the rains intensified. As November 2026 began, several disaster events were already being registered in disaster hotspot areas across Uganda.
In the period 7th - 15th November 2026, several disaster-prone areas in Eastern, Northern and Western Uganda experienced intensive prolonged rainfall that caused floods and other hydro-meteorological disasters affecting vulnerable communities in 11 districts. The rains continue and more disaster events are expected to be registered in various parts of the country.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-11-16) ---
Description of the Event
Date when the trigger was met
07-09-2026
What happened, where and when?
Uganda’s Karamoja region is in a severe drought after an exceptionally dry second half of the March to September 2026 rainy season. Between June and late August, cumulative rainfall was about 200 mm below average across most of the sub-region, and up to 500 mm below average in parts of Kaabong, Kotido and Nakapiripirit. By late August, vegetation greenness was only 60 to 80 percent of the seasonal average.
Prolonged dry spells since early June have caused crop losses of 50 to 75 percent across much of the sub-region, and main-season cereal and pulse production is expected to be extremely poor or to have failed in most areas. Pasture and water shortages have worsened, and livestock body conditions are declining.
The drought followed a significantly underperforming March to September rainy season, characterized by prolonged rainfall deficits, vegetation stress, crop losses and deteriorating food-security conditions. The event occurred during a period of strengthening El Niño conditions globally. The IPC update of 7 September 2026 attributes the deterioration in Karamoja to El Niño-related shocks, rising food prices and reduced humanitarian assistance, and Save the Children links the rise in child malnutrition to El Niño-linked dry spells. The available evidence therefore supports El Niño as a contributing climatic factor, rather than a confirmed sole cause of the drought.
Food insecurity has deepened. Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected across all nine Karamoja districts through January 2027, with some households in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). IPC projections to February 2027 put 432,109 people in Phase 3 and 41,259 in Phase 4, out of about 1.5 million people in the sub-region. At least 16 people were reported to have died from hunger by July 2026. An August 2026 nutrition survey found critical levels of global acute malnutrition in Kaabong (12.1 percent) and Nabilatuk (10.5 percent), and GAM is expected to remain Critical in Kotido and Kaabong through February 2027. The situation has since worsened as documented by ACT Alliance in July,
Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET) in August, and through the IPC update of September indicating about 673,000 people were in Phase 3 or worse in August to October, the highest since 2018. For the early actions, the forecast trigger is the IPC projection that Crisis outcomes continue through January 2027 and deteriorate from January as household stocks run out, together with rainfall about 200 mm below average since June and vegetation at 60 to 80 percent of normal.
Water scarcity raises the risk of poor hygiene and water-borne diseases, and puts pressure on households, livestock and the communal valley dams and earth water pans that many communities rely on during the dry season. The Government has distributed food relief, but there are still major gaps in primary information on the scale, location and characteristics of needs beyond food, especially for water, health, hygiene and livelihoods. URCS will therefore carry out a multisectoral assessment to generate primary evidence on needs and gaps, and will begin limited, no-regret early actions in the five most-affected districts to reduce the impact of the drought while the assessment is under way.
