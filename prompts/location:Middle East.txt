Crisis / situation: Middle East

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-09-25) ---
OVERVIEW
On 17 June 2026, the United States of America and the Islamic Republic of Iran reached an agreement on a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) concerning the cessation of hostilities. The escalation in the Middle East and beyond had triggered a humanitarian crisis affecting multiple regions in an already fragile context. A resurgence of hostilities in recent weeks is placing pressure on humanitarian access and threatens civilian and essential services. Mobility dynamics across the region and beyond due to renewed escalation remains uncertain. The situation continues to be highly fragile, requiring sustained monitoring and operational preparedness.1 In the Islamic Republic of Iran, some families have begun returning to their homes amid signs of de-escalation. However, thousands continue to shelter in hotels due to damage to housing and civilian infrastructure. As of 01 June, approximately 9,000 individuals remain displaced, unable to return to their homes due to the extent of the damage. Overall, population movements remain localized, temporary, and economically driven, with no signs of mass displacement. In Yemen, displacement increased significantly following the escalation of conflict along the West Coast in early September 2026. Between 07 June and 21 September 2026, IOM Yemen DTM recorded approximately 132,330 displaced individuals, the majority of whom were hosted in Ta’iz and Lahj governorates. Displacement accelerated sharply in early September, with many families experiencing repeated movements and seeking refuge in already overstretched displacement sites and host communities. In addition, 3,506 Yemeni nationals arrived in Djibouti between 10 and 21 September, reportedly fleeing the deteriorating security situation.2 In Lebanon, displacement nationwide remains significant despite continuing returns. As of 15 September 2026, 341,228 people remain internally displaced across 626 cadasters, a 2 per cent increase compared to the previous reporting period (as of 09 September 2026). Of the total IDPs in Lebanon, 16,536 individuals remained displaced in 166 collective shelters. Returns have continued, with 864,680 returned IDPs reported as of 15 September 2026, marking a 6 per cent increase from the previous round (as of 09 September 2026).3 Humanitarian and protection needs remain acute, driven by the destruction of homes and services, prolonged displacement, explosive ordnance risks, protection concerns, and declining coping capacities. Meanwhile, cross-border flows into the Syrian Arab Republic are continuing, with a total of 813,000 movements recorded as of 21 September 2026.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-09-24) ---
Lebanon remains among the countries most affected by forced displacement globally, hosting one of the largest refugee populations per capita while almost 334,353 people remained internally displaced at the end of August. Despite ceasefire agreements, repeated violations and persistent insecurity continue to drive humanitarian needs and repeated displacement, hinder safe and sustainable returns and recovery of conflict-affected areas, and place additional stress and strain on families and affected local communities.
Around 136,000 refugees who had been living in areas directly impacted by the escalating hostilities became internally displaced and faced difficulties finding safe and dignified shelter and livelihoods in displacement. Nearly 29,700 displaced Syrian refugees were directly identified as being in need of protection and assistance and referrals to services. The volatile situation, repeated displacement and uncertainties continue to affect people’s safety, dignity and psychological well-being.
