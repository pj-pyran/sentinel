Crisis / situation: Middle East

--- Report 1: ETC (2026-07-31) ---
This situation report provides updates on the Middle East escalation, with focus on Lebanon’s security situation. It outlines the telecoms area activities of the Logistics &amp; Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) in support of the humanitarian response1. The Cluster was formally activated on 16 March 2026 and is led by the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP).
Summary Points
• LTC Telecoms continued coordination with government counterparts, telecommunications providers, and humanitarian partners to ensure emergency telecommunications support remains aligned with operational requirements and ready for deployment as needed across the country.
• On 15 July, the local inter-agency ICT Working Group reviewed ongoing challenges in procuring and activating Starlink devices, with implementation awaiting licensing approvals from the Ministry of Telecommunications (MoT). The LTC is engaging the MoT to ensure prioritization of humanitarian responders in the approval process.
• From 01–05 July, LTC Telecoms successfully tested eSIM data connectivity in southern Lebanon. Where available the solution is an additional connectivity option in areas with limited or no GSM coverage.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-07-30) ---
On 17 June 2026, the United States of America and the Islamic Republic of Iran reached an agreement on a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) concerning the cessation of hostilities. The escalation in the Middle East and beyond had triggered a humanitarian crisis affecting multiple regions in an already fragile context. A resurgence of hostilities in recent weeks are placing pressure on humanitarian access and threatens civilian and essential services. Mobility dynamics across the region and beyond due to renewed escalation remains uncertain. The situation continues to be highly fragile, requiring sustained monitoring and operational preparedness.
In the Islamic Republic of Iran, population movements remain localised, temporary, and economically driven, with no signs of mass displacement.
In Lebanon, displacement nationwide remains significant despite continuing returns. As of 22 July 2026, 375, 090 people remain internally displaced across 729 cadasters, a 9 per cent decrease compared to the previous reporting period (as of 15 July 2026). Of the total IDPs in Lebanon, 30,007 individuals remained displaced in 273 collective shelters. Returns have increased, with 753,024 returned IDPs reported as of 22 July 2026, marking a 2 per cent increase from the previous round (as of 15 July 2026). Meanwhile, cross-border flows into the Syrian Arab Republic are continuing, with a total of 603,615 movements recorded as of 27 July 2026.
IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) is on the ground, actively tracking displacement and cross border mobility while scaling operations in coordination with local authorities and partners to better understand how the crisis is impacting human mobility in the region.
