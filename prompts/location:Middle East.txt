Crisis / situation: Middle East

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-07-08) ---
Since late February, hostilities in Iran and Lebanon have generated humanitarian and protection consequences across the region, including refugee returns in adverse circumstances to Afghanistan and Syria. While diplomatic efforts have contributed to a reduction in large-scale military activity, uncertainty remains high and the situation continues to unfold against an already fragile regional baseline. Prior to the escalation, there were more than 31 million forcibly displaced people and recent returnees across the region, alongside host communities facing significant economic and social pressures.
Inter-agency emergency response efforts are underway in directly affected countries. In Iran, the Flash Refugee Response Plan supports refugees and others in need of international protection alongside affected host communities. In Lebanon, the Flash Appeal complements the Lebanon Response Plan, while in Afghanistan support for returnees is reflected under the Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan.
UNHCR and partners continue to focus on the wider consequences of the situation, including returns under adverse circumstances, economic deterioration and pressure on national systems affecting growing protection risks facing refugees and vulnerable populations across the region. Preparedness arrangements remain in place, including border monitoring, reception and protection readiness, but continue to depend on flexible and predictable funding in an increasingly constrained resource environment.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-07-08) ---
Since the announcement of the Memorandum of Understanding between the United States of America and the Islamic Republic of Iran, its impact on regional mobility remains uncertain. Across neighbouring countries, movements continue to reflect largely routine and precautionary travel patterns, with no large-scale cross-border displacement observed; however, the situation remains fragile and requires sustained monitoring. Inside the Islamic Republic of Iran, mobility remains localized and temporary, with approximately 9,000 individuals still displaced due to housing damage and no signs of mass displacement. Meanwhile, Lebanon and the Syrian Arab Republic continue to face significant displacement pressures. While returns in Lebanon have increased, more than 700,000 people remain displaced and insecurity continues to hinder sustainable return. At the same time, over 464,000 movements from Lebanon into the Syrian Arab Republic have been recorded since March, with economic reintegration challenges further complicating return prospects and placing continued pressure on already fragile humanitarian systems

--- Report 3: ETC (2026-07-06) ---
This situation report provides updates on the Middle East escalation, with focus on Lebanon’s security situation. It outlines the telecoms area activities of the Logistics &amp; Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) in support of the humanitarian response1 . The Cluster was formally activated on 16 March 2026 and is led by the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP).
Summary Points
• Lebanon’s telecommunications network remained operational but fragile during June, with damaged infrastructure, restricted maintenance access, and continued insecurity in southern Lebanon increasing the risk of localized service disruptions and limiting network resilience.
• LTC Telecoms continued coordination with government authorities, telecommunications providers, and humanitarian partners to strengthen emergency telecommunications preparedness, advance discussions on Starlink access for humanitarian operations, and support the implementation of inter-agency ICT common services.
• LTC Telecoms expanded efforts to strengthen connectivity and communications for humanitarian responders, including testing eSIM-based data solutions in southern Lebanon, collecting field connectivity data to support operational planning, and enhancing emergency voice communications capacity through additional satellite communications equipment.
