Crisis / situation: Middle East

--- Report 1: ETC (2026-09-04) ---
This situation report provides updates on the Middle East escalation, with focus on Lebanon’s security situation. It outlines the telecoms area activities of the Logistics &amp; Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) in support of the humanitarian response1 . The Cluster was formally activated on 16 March 2026 and is led by the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP).
Summary Points
• The security situation in southern Lebanon remained volatile during August, with renewed hostilities and population movements continuing to affect humanitarian access and operational planning.
• Telecommunications services remained largely operational nationwide, but connectivity resilience continued to be constrained by localized disruptions in southern Lebanon, delayed fibre connectivity approvals, and ongoing restrictions affecting Starlink deployment.
• LTC Telecoms launched a nationwide assessment of humanitarian telecommunications needs and engaged partners, UNDSS, and national authorities to identify priority connectivity gaps, strengthen preparedness planning, and support future telecommunications interventions.
Situation Overview
The security situation in southern Lebanon remained volatile throughout August despite ongoing diplomatic engagement and efforts to sustain existing ceasefire arrangements. In early August, renewed Israeli strikes and a displacement order affecting Al-Mansouri prompted further population movements and highlighted the fragility of the security environment. Continued insecurity and periodic hostilities affected humanitarian access, operational planning, and the delivery of assistance in affected areas.
Humanitarian organizations continued to monitor developments closely and support affected populations, particularly in southern Lebanon where displacement and access challenges persisted. The evolving security situation underscores the importance of maintaining operational readiness and ensuring that responders can safely coordinate and deliver humanitarian assistance.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-09-03) ---
Across the Middle East and beyond, the inter-agency protection response combines a range of interventions alongside support to strengthen national protection systems, seeking to address protection risks and meet the needs of IDPs, refugees, migrants, returnees, and host communities alike. Local, national and international protection actors collaborate to provide communitybased protection, gender-based violence and child protection prevention and response, mental health and psychosocial support, legal aid and civil documentation support, protection monitoring, cash for protection, support for housing land and property rights, mine action services. The response remains severely funding-constrained.
