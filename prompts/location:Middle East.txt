Crisis / situation: Middle East

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-06-09) ---
Overview
• The humanitarian situation in Lebanon worsened amid a renewed escalation of hostilities, leading to heightened protection risks, increased displacement, and growing humanitarian needs.
• Protection monitoring and Protection needs assessments indicate a continued deterioration of the protection environment, driven by repeated displacement, insecurity, and housing instability.
• Vulnerabilities are increasing as more households face homelessness, overcrowding, and unsafe living arrangements.
• Syrian refugees and other non-Lebanese populations remain disproportionately affected, encountering barriers to accessing safe shelter and facing heightened risks of exploitation and protection incidents. Psychosocial distress is also on the rise, particularly among children, with growing reports of fear, anxiety, sleep disturbances, and emotional distress linked to ongoing insecurity and uncertainty.
• The Lebanon Flash Appeal, launched in March, was extended for a further three months (June to August 2026) to enable humanitarian partners to continue rapidly delivering effective humanitarian assistance.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-06-09) ---
The escalation of hostilities across the Middle East and beyond continues to trigger a humanitarian crisis spanning multiple regions in an already fragile context, with significant impact on population mobility. Inside the Islamic Republic of Iran, destruction of more than 149,000 civilian infrastructures and services has directly impacted over 400,000 individuals as of 29 April. Overall, population movements remain localized, temporary, and economically driven, with no signs of mass displacement; however, ongoing strain on housing, livelihoods, and basic services is likely to sustain elevated mobility levels in the near-and medium-term.
In Lebanon, continued hostilities and new displacement orders have contributed to increasing displacement, with 130,369 individuals remaining displaced across 632 collective shelters as of 31 May, overwhelming hugely overstretched services, with shelter capacities being reached in Saida and Tyre, South Governorate.
Women and girls - who account for nearly 52% of those sheltered - are affected disproportionally by ongoing displacement, facing heightened protection risks, increased care responsibilities, and greater challenges in accessing essential services. Cross-border flows into the Syrian Arab Republic are continuing, with 395,369 movements recorded as of 1 June 2026, placing additional pressure on an already overstretched humanitarian system.
In the region, Afghanistan observes the highest number of movements with substantial variations in the volume of flows.
412,008 arrivals from the Islamic Republic of Iran to Afghanistan and 162,557 departures from Afghanistan to the Islamic Republic of Iran between March and June 2026 underscore the criticality of ensuring continued humanitarian operations at the border under the IOM-led Border Consortium. Cross-border flows from Pakistan and Iraq remain moderate and variable rather than steadily increasing; with a total of 25,708 individuals having crossed from the Islamic Republic of Iran into Pakistan, and a net incoming movement of 1,609 individuals into Iraq, the two countries have witnessed smaller and more occasional movements.
The situation along the borders with Türkiye, Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Turkmenistan remains stable, but investments in contingency planning and preparedness remain critical.
