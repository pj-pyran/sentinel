Crisis / situation: Pakistan

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-09-18) ---
INTRODUCTION
In response to the Ministry of Interior's (MoI) decision of 26 September 2023 to enact an “Illegal Foreigners' Repatriation Plan”, UNHCR, the UN Refugee Agency and IOM, the UN Migration Agency, have collated their data on the arrest, detention, and deportation of Proof of Registration (PoR) holders, Afghan Citizen Card (ACC) holders, and undocumented Afghan nationals as well as on the outflows of Afghan nationals at the Torkham, Ghulam Khan,
Chaman, Badini and Bahramcha border crossing points, to better understand the protection environment and movements of Afghan nationals returning to Afghanistan from Pakistan.
In 2025, the Government of Pakistan (GoP) issued several directives impacting Afghan nationals. In January, the GoP announced that Afghan nationals should relocate from Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT) and Rawalpindi, or else face deportation. In April, further announcements indicated the implementation of the second phase of the “IFRP”, targeting ACC holders, in addition to undocumented Afghans. In July, the GoP issued a Statutory Regulation Order (SRO) directing the repatriation/deportation of PoR card holders following the expiry of PoR card validity on 30 June. The GoP later indicated a deadline of 1 September for PoR holders to leave Pakistan, before the start of the repatriation/deportation process. UNHCR issued a press briefing expressing concern regarding the decision and urging the GoP to exempt Afghans with international protection needs, as well as those with particular circumstances or vulnerabilities, from involuntary return.

--- Report 4: Islamic Relief (2026-09-18) ---
BACKGROUND
Pakistan continues to face significant and interconnected challenges stemming from economic instability and the increasing frequency of climate-related disasters. The country remains highly vulnerable to natural hazards, ranking among the world's most at-risk nations due to its exposure to disasters, underlying socioeconomic vulnerabilities, and limited coping and recovery capacities. These recurring shocks place additional strain on communities, livelihoods, and public resources, while posing ongoing challenges to sustainable development and economic resilience.
Pakistan’s 2026 monsoon season commenced on 26 June 2026 and continued until mid-September 2026. According to the latest NDMA situation reports, the monsoon resulted in 185 fatalities and 521 injuries across the country as of 17 September 2026, marking the conclusion of the season’s final rainfall spell. Throughout the monsoon period, the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) played a proactive role in disaster risk communication by regularly disseminating weather forecasts, impact-based alerts, and situation updates to government stakeholders, humanitarian partners, and the public through official channels and social media platforms. These timely warnings and information-sharing efforts contributed to enhanced preparedness, enabling communities and relevant departments to take preventive measures and strengthen response mechanisms in areas at risk of flooding and other weather-related hazards.
