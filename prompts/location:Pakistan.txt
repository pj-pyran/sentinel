Crisis / situation: Pakistan

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-07-24) ---
INTRODUCTION
In response to the Ministry of Interior's (MoI) decision of 26 September 2023 to enact an “Illegal Foreigners' Repatriation Plan”, UNHCR, the UN Refugee Agency and IOM, the UN Migration Agency, have collated their data on the arrest, detention, and deportation of Proof of Registration (PoR) holders, Afghan Citizen Card (ACC) holders, and undocumented Afghan nationals as well as on the outflows of Afghan nationals at the Torkham, Ghulam Khan, Chaman, Badini and Bahramcha border crossing points, to better understand the protection environment and movements of Afghan nationals returning to Afghanistan from Pakistan.
In 2025, the Government of Pakistan (GoP) issued several directives impacting Afghan nationals. In January, the GoP announced that Afghan nationals should relocate from Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT) and Rawalpindi, or else face deportation. In April, further announcements indicated the implementation of the second phase of the “IFRP”, targeting ACC holders, in addition to undocumented Afghans. In July, the GoP issued a Statutory Regulation Order (SRO) directing the repatriation/deportation of PoR card holders following the expiry of PoR card validity on 30 June. The GoP later indicated a deadline of 1 September for PoR holders to leave Pakistan, before the start of the repatriation/deportation process. UNHCR issued a press briefing expressing concern regarding the decision and urging the GoP to exempt Afghans with international protection needs, as well as those with particular circumstances or vulnerabilities, from involuntary return.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-07-22) ---
Description
Since the onset of the 2026 southwest monsoon on 28 June, intermittent to heavy rainfall has affected several parts of Pakistan, significantly increasing the risk of flash floods, riverine flooding, cloudbursts, landslides, urban flooding, thunderstorms, and Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF)-related incidents, particularly across northern and mountainous regions. Successive monsoon spells have impacted Gilgit-Baltistan (GB), Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), Punjab, Balochistan, and Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK), resulting in localized flooding, damage to residential houses and critical infrastructure, disruption of transport and communication networks, and humanitarian impacts in vulnerable districts. The National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), Provincial Disaster Management Authorities (PDMAs), and the Pakistan Meteorological Department (PMD) continue to closely monitor the evolving situation and issue regular weather advisories and impact-based forecasts.
Between 28 June and 21 July 2026, multiple weather-related incidents were reported from Gilgit-Baltistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Punjab, Balochistan, and Azad Jammu and Kashmir. The most affected districts included Diamer, Ghizer, Ghanche, Nagar, Astore, Upper Chitral, Lower Chitral, Shangla, Khyber, Lower Dir, Mardan, Rajanpur, Dera Ghazi Khan, Khuzdar, and Neelum. Reported impacts included flash floods, cloudbursts, landslides, damage to residential houses, schools, roads, bridges, irrigation channels, water supply schemes, electricity infrastructure, standing crops, orchards, and other public facilities, resulting in casualties, injuries, displacement, and disruption of essential services. Provincial Disaster Management Authorities (PDMAs), district administrations, rescue services, and relevant line departments continue to conduct rapid damage assessments, emergency response, and restoration activities in the affected areas.
Among the significant incidents reported during the period, a cloudburst on 19 July in Lari Mengal, Doga Top, and Katha Peeran (Union Council Salkhala, Tehsil Athmuqam, District Neelum, AJK) triggered a flash flood that damaged educational institutions, residential houses, commercial shops, and vehicles. Rescue teams and relevant government departments immediately initiated response operations, rapid damage assessments, and the relocation of affected families to safer locations. On the same day, heavy rainfall triggered flash flooding in Rupal Valley, Sub-Division Shounter, District Astore, Gilgit-Baltistan, where no casualties or damage to residential houses were reported, although minor damage to agricultural crops occurred. Subsequently, on 21 July, another flash flood in Louse Village, District Astore, inundated several residential houses, damaging household belongings and essential assets. According to the initial assessment conducted by the District Disaster Management Authority Astore, number of houses were severely affected, and the Gilgit-Baltistan Disaster Management Authority (GBDMA) initiated arrangements for the distribution of emergency relief items while detailed assessments remain ongoing.
According to the latest PMD and NDMA weather advisories, active monsoon conditions are expected to persist through 24 July 2026. Moist monsoon currents from the Arabian Sea, supported by a shallow westerly wave, are likely to generate widespread rain with isolated heavy to very heavy falls over Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Gilgit-Baltistan, Azad Jammu and Kashmir, northeastern Punjab, and adjoining hilly areas. Localized flash flooding, landslides, cloudbursts, and GLOF-related incidents remain possible, particularly across mountainous and upper catchment areas. Although the Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, and Sutlej rivers are currently flowing within normal ranges and no significant riverine flooding is anticipated, authorities continue to closely monitor river flows, rainfall patterns, and vulnerable locations due to the possibility of localized high-impact weather events.

Summary
The National aSociety remains in close coordination with Provincial and District Branches, the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), Provincial/State Disaster Management Authorities (PDMAs/SDMA), district administrations, and other relevant government departments to monitor the evolving monsoon situation, assess humanitarian needs, and support preparedness and response. The PRCS Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) continues to monitor weather forecasts, official advisories, and field updates on a 24/7 basis to ensure timely information sharing and coordination. PRCS remains on operational standby, with Emergency Operations Centres at National and Branch levels operational and District Disaster Response Teams (DDRTs), Community Disaster Response Teams (CDRTs), National Disaster Response Team (NDRT) members, First Aid volunteers, and trained staff ready for deployment. Pre-positioned emergency relief stocks remain available for rapid mobilization based on assessed needs. Following the 19 July 2026 cloudburst in District Neelum, Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK), the PRCS AJK State and District Neelum Branches immediately coordinated with the district administration, shared the initial incident report with NHQ, and supported rapid needs assessments. PRCS NHQ continues to closely coordinate with the AJK Branch, NDMA, SDMA, and district authorities while remaining ready to provide humanitarian assistance, if required. To strengthen Movement coordination and the National Society's preparedness and response, the first Movement Coordination Meeting is being convened on 22 July 2026. The meeting will provide updates on the evolving monsoon situation, review preparedness and response actions, identify operational and resource gaps, and enhance coordination among Movement partners to ensure timely and effective humanitarian support, if required.
