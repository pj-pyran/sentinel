BACKGROUND
Pakistan continues to face significant and interconnected challenges stemming from economic instability and the increasing frequency of climate-related disasters. The country remains highly vulnerable to natural hazards, ranking among the world's most at-risk nations due to its exposure to disasters, underlying socioeconomic vulnerabilities, and limited coping and recovery capacities. These recurring shocks place additional strain on communities, livelihoods, and public resources, while posing ongoing challenges to sustainable development and economic resilience.
Pakistan’s 2026 monsoon season commenced on 26 June 2026 and continued until mid-September 2026. According to the latest NDMA situation reports, the monsoon resulted in 185 fatalities and 521 injuries across the country as of 17 September 2026, marking the conclusion of the season’s final rainfall spell. Throughout the monsoon period, the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) played a proactive role in disaster risk communication by regularly disseminating weather forecasts, impact-based alerts, and situation updates to government stakeholders, humanitarian partners, and the public through official channels and social media platforms. These timely warnings and information-sharing efforts contributed to enhanced preparedness, enabling communities and relevant departments to take preventive measures and strengthen response mechanisms in areas at risk of flooding and other weather-related hazards.
