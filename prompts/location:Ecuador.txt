Crisis / situation: Ecuador

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-10-21) ---
Scenario analysis
When and where is the hazard expected to happen?
Ecuador progressively escalated its alert levels in response to the evolving El Niño threat, moving from Yellow Alert in May to Orange Alert in July, followed by the declaration of a nationwide Red Alert on 29 August and the issuance of Executive Decree No. 485 on 31 August 2026.
Since then, localized rainfall-related emergencies have been managed through existing local and national capacities. The CN-ERFEN Technical Executive Report No. 010-2026, issued on 1 October, confirms that El Niño remains active and continues to strengthen. As of 23 September, sea-surface temperature anomalies reached +4.7°C in the Niño 1+2 region and +3.1°C in Niño 3.4, reaching the maximum values established for both thermal components of Ecuador’s El Niño Index. The report also notes that international forecasts maintain a strengthening scenario during the last quarter of 2026.
The current event presents similarities with the 1997–1998 El Niño in terms of ocean heat accumulation, thermal structure and deepening of the thermocline. However, CN-ERFEN cautions that these oceanic similarities do not imply that atmospheric behaviour, rainfall or impacts will necessarily reach the same magnitude, as these will depend on the evolution of ocean-atmosphere coupling and regional weather conditions.
Evidence of a stronger atmospheric response was already observed during September. According to INAMHI, rainfall exceeded normal September levels across much of the coastal region. Guayaquil recorded its wettest September since records began in 1961, exceeding the September rainfall observed during the 1982 and 1997 El Niño events. In La Concordia, 149.5 mm of rainfall was recorded within 24 hours on 14 September, the highest value registered there for that month. Increased rainfall and river levels resulted in 49 overflows, while 131 rainfall-related events affected 13,303 people across the coastal region. While these impacts remained largely localized and were managed through existing capacities during September, conditions continued to deteriorate during early October.
According to the National Secretariat for Risk Management (SNGR), 267 rainfall-related events were recorded nationally between 29 August and 7 October, of which 101 remained active. Esmeraldas accounted for the highest number of ongoing emergencies, followed by Los Ríos and Guayas. On 8 October, authorities declared a 90-day Level III emergency in Esmeraldas due to the cumulative impacts of flooding and landslides, with 14,493 people reported as affected across six cantons.
Although current impacts remain concentrated in specific locations, particularly in Esmeraldas, forecasts indicate that the expected evolution of El Niño conditions and the onset of the rainy season may result in more widespread and recurrent impacts across multiple coastal provinces in the coming months. The operation therefore seeks to complement ongoing response efforts with anticipatory measures aimed at reducing the humanitarian consequences of further forecast escalation.
The INAMHI climate prediction for October 2026, as presented in the latest CN-ERFEN analysis, forecasts above-normal rainfall and abovenormal maximum and minimum temperatures across the coastal region. Atmospheric conditions are expected to remain favourable for enhanced rainfall activity, particularly across the northern and central coastal areas. The associated hydrological outlook indicates a high probability of river rises and overflows in several coastal river basins that have already shown sensitivity to increased rainfall during September. Recent INAMHI meteorological warnings forecast daily rainfall accumulations of between 42 and 100 mm and highlight the potential for river rises, flooding and landslides in areas already exposed to hydrometeorological hazards.
Taken together, the activation of this Imminent DREF is based on the convergence of three factors: (i) the nationwide Red Alert maintained by the Government of Ecuador (SNGR-238-2026), (ii) confirmation through CN-ERFEN Technical Executive Report No. 010-2026 that El Niño remains active and strengthening, and (iii) INAMHI forecasts indicating above-normal rainfall conditions and increased hydrological risk across the coastal region.
While above-normal rainfall conditions are forecast during October, both INAMHI and CN-ERFEN indicate that the impacts associated with the ongoing El Niño event are expected to intensify as the rainy season develops. According to INAMHI, rainfall along the Ecuadorian coast is expected to increase in both frequency and intensity during November and December 2026, while CN-ERFEN anticipates the greatest impacts of the event between November 2026 and March 2027. The transition from anomalous rainfall conditions observed in September and forecast for October toward the onset of the rainy season is therefore expected to increase the likelihood of more widespread flooding, river overflows and landslides across the coastal region. Based on these forecasts, the operation considers early November 2026 as the most likely date for the first significant escalation of humanitarian impacts.
The operation therefore prioritizes Esmeraldas, Guayas, Los Ríos and Santo Domingo de los Tsáchilas, where the area of above-normal rainfall forecast by INAMHI overlaps with recent hydrological activity, flood- and landslide-prone areas and a high concentration of exposed communities. Guayas and Los Ríos contain extensive low-lying areas and interconnected river systems susceptible to progressive flooding; Esmeraldas has recorded the highest recent population impacts along the coast; and Santo Domingo de los Tsáchilas, including La Concordia, is exposed to rapid river rises, flash flooding and rainfall-triggered landslides. Approximately 1.25 million people are exposed to varying levels of flooding and landslide risk across the four provinces.
The progression from exceptionally high rainfall and localized impacts observed during September, combined with forecasts of above normal rainfall during October and expectations of more frequent and intense rainfall from November onwards, increases the likelihood of more frequent and severe flooding, river overflows and landslides in the coming weeks while anticipatory measures can still be implemented before impacts become more widespread.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-10-20) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• The Government of Ecuador declared a nationwide Red Alert due to the anticipated impacts of El Niño, activating provincial and municipal emergency plans and triggering preparedness and response measures.
• The School Feeding Programme is expanding and currently provides fresh, nutritious meals to students in rural schools, yet the system requires external support to expand coverage and adequacy.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Ecuador continues to face severe security, socioeconomic, and environmental challenges. Violence driven by drug-related crime has escalated sharply, making Ecuador one of the most insecure countries in the region.
