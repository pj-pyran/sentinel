Crisis / situation: Cameroon: Cholera Outbreak - Jun 2026

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-10-09) ---
Highlights

The outbreak continues to expand geographically. Laboratory-confirmed cholera cases are reported in twenty-one health districts across the Far North and North regions, while four additional districts in the Far North remain under investigation for suspected cases.
UNICEF is supporting the Government-led response through Health, WASH, Social and Behaviour Change, Accountability to Affected Populations, Child Protection, Nutrition, Youth Engagement and coordination.
The response is 48 per cent funded. A US$660,800 gap remains, with particularly significant needs in Health, Nutrition and Child Protection/MHPSS.

Overview
Cholera transmission continues to expand across the Far North and North regions. Population movements and active transmission in neighbouring areas increase the risk of further spread. Immediate priorities include stronger surveillance, timely case management, targeted WASH interventions, risk communication and preparedness in affected and high-risk districts.
As of 23 September 2026, the Far North region has reported 2,753 cholera cases and 77 deaths (CFR: 2.8%), with active transmission persisting in several health districts, notably Mada, Kolofata, Mokolo, Mora and Makary. The implementation of two rounds of reactive Oral Cholera Vaccination (OCV) campaigns in seven high-risk districts (Fotokol, Kousseri, Mada, Makary, Mora, Kolofata and Mokolo) has likely contributed to reducing the spread and severity of the outbreak. However, the worsening humanitarian situation, including population displacement from Logone-et-Chari division due to insecurity, is placing additional pressure on host communities in Waza and the urban health districts of Maroua I, Maroua II and Maroua III. This influx of displaced populations is increasing vulnerability to cholera transmission through overcrowding and heightened pressure on already constrained health and WASH services.
In the North Region, 37 cases and 6 deaths have been reported (CFR: 16.22%). Guider reported the highest number of cases. Although the caseload remains low, the high case fatality rate may indicate delays in detection and referral or limited access to quality case management.
Continued surveillance, WASH interventions, risk communication and community engagement and cross-border coordination remain critical to prevent further spread and reduce mortality

--- Report 2: IMC (2026-10-07) ---
FAST FACTS

As of October 5, 459 cholera cases and four deaths had been reported in Minawao Refugee Camp, which currently is home to more than 81,000 refugees and operating at about 325% of intended capacity.
Multiple transmission chains are suspected within the camp, where heavy rainfall is increasing the risk of rapid spread among vulnerable populations.
Nationwide, according to the most recent government figures, there have been 2,903 confirmed cases and 84 deaths nationwide across 13 health districts.
The cholera outbreak in Cameroon has a case fatality rate of 2.89%, which is nearly three times higher than the internationally accepted target of less than 1%.

OUR RESPONSE

International Medical Corps has been operating in Minawao Refugee Camp since 2013, providing Nigerian refugees with primary, community-based and referred secondary healthcare.
In response to the outbreak, our team has treated all 459 patients, expanded the cholera treatment unit to 65 beds, trained 105 health workers in case management and infection prevention, and reached a daily average of 6,478 people through community information sessions on hygiene, safe water and early care-seeking.
We are coordinating with national health authorities, United Nations agencies, camp management and humanitarian partners to support a coordinated response.
