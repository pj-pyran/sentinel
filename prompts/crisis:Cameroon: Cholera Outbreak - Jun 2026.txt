Crisis / situation: Cameroon: Cholera Outbreak - Jun 2026

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-11-12) ---
EARLY ACTION PROTOCOL ACTIVATION
Activation Overview
The Cameroon Red Cross (CRC) activated its cholera Early Action Protocol (EAP) following flooding in the city of Douala, Littoral Region.
On the night of 11 September 2026, heavy rainfall caused flooding in several neighbourhoods of Douala, particularly Bonamoussadi (Sable Banya and Denver) and Makèpè Missokè. The floods affected communities and several water, sanitation and hygiene facilities, increasing the risk of contamination of water sources and exposure to waterborne diseases, particularly cholera.
Field assessments conducted by Cameroon Red Cross volunteers identified approximately 500 affected households, representing nearly 2,300 people, as well as latrines/pits and water supply sources affected by the floods. Stagnant water, high population density, poor housing conditions in some areas and the ongoing rainy season further increase health risks.
Following analysis of the data collected and monitoring of the trigger criteria defined in the cholera EAP, the threshold for the first trigger was considered to have been reached on 18 September 2026. This led the Cameroon Red Cross to activate the cholera EAP in Bangué Health District, Douala, Littoral Region.
Anticipatory actions will be implemented over three months to reduce communities’ exposure to cholera and limit the potential consequences of a possible outbreak.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-11-12) ---
Highlights

Cholera transmission continues in the Far North and North regions, although the epidemiological trend is declining. Confirmed cases have been reported in 20 health districts, with four additional districts under investigation.
UNICEF continues to support the Government-led response through Health, WASH, Nutrition, Social and Behaviour Change (SBC)/AAP, Child Protection, Youth Engagement and coordination interventions, while progressively shifting towards prevention, preparedness and resilience-building activities in high-risk areas.
The response is 78.3 per cent funded, leaving a funding gap of US$275,002. Additional resources are needed to sustain prevention, preparedness and resilience-building interventions across Nutrition, SBC, Child Protection (including MHPSS), WASH and Health sectors.
