Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: Insecurity Insight (2026-10-22) ---
Data collected by Insecurity Insight shows that at least 3,508 attacks on health care took place across the occupied Palestinian territory between 07 October 2023 and 30 September 2026. 95% of all incidents were attributed to Israeli forces. In Gaza, where 2,412 attacks on health care were recorded, health facilities were damaged 459 times, 785 health workers were killed and 399 arrested. In the West Bank, where 1,034 attacks on health care were recorded, health facilities were damaged 33 times, 13 health workers were killed and 200 arrested while ambulances and health workers were prevented from providing vital medical care to people in need over over 630 times.
In Gaza, 157 attacks on health care were recorded between the 10 October 2025 ceasefire and 30 September 2026, with 92% of these incidents attributed to Israeli forces. Health facilities including hospitals, health centres and medicine warehouses were reportedly damaged on 17 occasions. Nearly 90% of these 17 incidents were attributed to Israeli aircraft and drone strikes, artillery fire and gunfire. In one such incident, Israeli forces reportedly bombed medicine stores at Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital, destroying two of the hospital’s four medicine stores and damaging the remaining two. The strike also damaged the hospital’s outpatient clinic building and vital quantities of medicines and other supplies intended for patients. Two health facilities were deliberately set on fire by Israeli forces while in two other incidents, health facilities were damaged by gunfire from unidentified conflict parties. At least one damaged health facility was reportedly located within the so-called “Green Zone”, an area under Israeli control: Kamal Adwan Hospital was hit by an Israeli quadcopter drone strike that reportedly injured three hospital workers. In some incidents, health workers and patients were harmed. For example, Yemen Al-Saeed Hospital was reportedly hit and damaged by two Israeli missiles that struck the first floor of the facility in the evening, killing three Palestinians, including a woman, and injuring 16 others, including children. Two health facilities were reportedly damaged multiple times since the October 2025 ceasefire: both Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital and Yemen Al-Saeed Hospital were damaged twice.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-10-20) ---
Highlights

In the Gaza Strip, Israeli airstrikes, shelling and gunfire continued to result in reported civilian casualties, while incursions, demolitions and reported shifts in the “Yellow Line” heightened concerns and risks of further displacement.
About 950,000 displaced people have been assessed as living in sites at medium to high risk of flooding, including over 400,000 at high risk. Available supplies could partially assist only 18 per cent of the 294,250 families requiring shelter assistance for the rainy season.
Daily cooked-meal production has declined by 12 per cent to approximately 614,000 amid funding constraints, while shortages of fuel, engine oil and essential supplies threaten the continuity of health and water services.
In the West Bank, over the past two weeks, five Palestinians were killed and over 240 injured by Israeli forces or settlers, with 74 per cent of injuries occurring in the context of settler attacks.
Over 160 olive harvest-related settler attacks resulted in casualties, property damage or both over the past two weeks. Since September, some 200 olive harvest-related settler attacks were documented.
Demolitions displaced 96 Palestinians over two weeks, including 69 in At Tur – the largest displacement in a single demolition incident in East Jerusalem since UN records began in 2009.

Gaza Strip
The reporting period is 29 September – 12 October 2026 unless otherwise specified.
Israeli airstrikes, shelling and gunfire continued across Gaza, reportedly causing civilian casualties, including among displaced people. Israeli incursions and demolitions were also reported near “Yellow Line.” On 10 October, Israeli authorities issued four evacuation orders and two shelter-in-place orders affecting humanitarian sites across three governorates; no damage was reported. On 11 October, Israeli forces reportedly moved the “Yellow Line” markers westward near Ash Shuja'iyyeh junction in Gaza city, raising concerns about further displacement in an area hosting displaced people. The UN Human Rights Office has verified 1,076 conflict-related deaths between the ceasefire announcement on 10 October 2025 and the end of July 2026, including 331 near the “Yellow Line.”
According to the Ministry of Health in Gaza, which operates under the de-facto authorities, 57 Palestinians were reported killed and 319 injured between 29 September and 12 October, bringing the total number of casualties since the ceasefire agreement to 1,493 fatalities and 5,299 injuries.
Humanitarian conditions remained critical amid widespread displacement, damaged infrastructure and shortages of essential supplies. Daily cooked-meal production declined by approximately 12 per cent compared with late September, while shortages of fuel, engine oil, spare parts and medicines threatened essential health and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services. Efforts to prepare for the rainy season became increasingly urgent for families in deteriorating shelters and unsafe buildings, but shortages of materials, machinery, and funding – alongside Israeli restrictions on access to suitable land – continued to constrain mitigation and relocation efforts.
Humanitarian cargo movements into Gaza continued to face operational and logistical challenges, including inspection and scanning procedures, limited handling capacity, and coordination requirements across multiple stages of the supply chain. According to the Logistics Cluster, between 29 September and 12 October, 937 trucks carrying humanitarian supplies were manifested and 807 offloaded across five corridors: Ashdod, Egypt, Jordan, Israel and the West Bank. Of those offloaded, 373 were routed through Ashdod, 206 through Egypt, 198 through Jordan, 18 through Israel and 12 through the West Bank. More intensive inspections and scanning procedures contributed to slower cargo movements from Egypt, while additional crossing capacity, improved coordination and more predictable clearance and handling arrangements remained critical to facilitating the timely entry of humanitarian assistance.
