Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: ActionAid (2026-09-25) ---
Executive summary
This snapshot is based on a survey of 29 international and national NGOs conducted in the first week of September and covering the previous six months, from March through August 2026. While the results do not capture every NGO operating in the West Bank, they reflect the persistently constrained operating environment and impact on Palestinian communities.
Israeli policies in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, are pushing Palestinians deeper into humanitarian crisis. State-backed settler violence is escalating sharply, targeting Palestinians in their homes, on the roads and on their land.
Combined with military raids, settlement expansion, demolitions and movement restrictions, these policies and practices are further consolidating annexation of the occupied West Bank, forcibly displacing communities and making it increasingly difficult for Palestinians to remain on their land.
For humanitarian organisations, the consequences are immediate. Needs are rising while the space to respond is shrinking. Staff and partners face attacks and threats, routes are closed or too dangerous to use, and communities are increasingly cut off from essential assistance and services. National and international NGOs are consistently being forced to delay, relocate, scale back or suspend services, while adopting alternative ways of working to maintain operations
wherever possible. Despite reducing their visibility to mitigate risk, they continue to face attacks. At the same time, many of these organisations are facing shrinking budgets as donors reduce funding, forcing cuts to essential services for families in need.
As Israeli elections approach, the government is not restraining these dynamics, and instead is allowing them to accelerate. Political measures and increasingly extreme rhetoric are emboldening settlers and entrenching impunity, leaving already displaced and isolated communities to face even greater danger.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-09-24) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

Severe resource constraints are forcing WFP to scale back assistance despite significant humanitarian needs. Across Gaza and the West Bank, programmes are being increasingly prioritized and targeted, with reductions in both food and cash assistance.
In the West Bank, WFP halved programme coverage from 400,000 to 200,000 people from 1 September, despite food insecurity having more than doubled since 2023. Further reductions across operations are planned in October and, without urgent flexible funding, additional cuts will be unavoidable by December.
Kerem Shalom (Karam Abu Salem) Crossing, located at the southeastern edge of the Gaza Strip near the borders with Israel and Egypt, remains the sole humanitarian entry point for cross-border cargo, underscoring the critical need to reopen a northern crossing and ease restrictions on essential operational supplies.
