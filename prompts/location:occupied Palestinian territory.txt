Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: UNRWA (2026-10-01) ---
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

UNRWA started the 2026/2027 school year on 19 September across the Gaza Strip through a combination of in-person and distance learning. A launch event was held in Nuseirat Camp at an UNRWA school that has fully returned to education use, after having been used previously as an emergency shelter. During the first week of the school year, nearly 97,000 students attended UNRWA learning spaces across the Gaza Strip.
Students also returned to in-person learning at both campuses of the UNRWA Rehabilitation Centre for the Visually Impaired (RCVI), with classes resuming in Deir El Balah on 19 September and in Gaza City on 20 September.
UNRWA opened two additional carpentry workshops, bringing the total to three facilities operating in Khan Younis, Deir El Balah and Gaza City. Together, the workshops can produce approximately 450 school desks and chairs per week to support the expansion of learning spaces.
Multiple war-damaged residential buildings collapsed in the Gaza Strip during the reporting period, highlighting the risks faced by families sheltering in unsafe structures. UN Humanitarian Coordinator Ramiz Alakbarov warned that people live in “hundreds of damaged structures assessed as unsafe, for lack of better options”. These include some families sheltering in non-operational UNRWA installations, which are no longer managed by UNRWA, for safety reasons. With winter approaching, risks are expected to increase as rainfall further weakens already compromised structures. UNRWA remains engaged in inter-agency efforts to address the issue and encourage families to relocate to safer shelter options where available.
Over the reporting period, Israeli authorities issued multiple evacuation orders and shelter-in-place alerts affecting UNRWA installations across the Gaza Strip. Separately, hostilities in the vicinity of UNRWA facilities resulted in injuries to six displaced persons sheltering at UNRWA schools, including four children. One cash-for-work beneficiary supporting UNRWA operations was killed and one UNRWA staff member seriously injured in separate incidents linked to strikes by Israeli forces in their vicinity.

Key points
The Gaza Strip
Fatalities and injuries

Between 7 October 2023 and 20 September 2026, according to the Ministry of Health, as reported by the Health Cluster, 73,914 Palestinians were killed in the Gaza Strip and another 174,952 were injured. This includes 1,394 fatalities and 4,991 injuries between the announcement of a ceasefire agreement on 10 October 2025 and 20 September 2026.
UNRWA has recorded 394 colleagues killed in the Gaza Strip since the start of the war (311 UNRWA personnel and 83 persons who were supporting UNRWA[2], as of 22 September.

Health crisis

The worsening fuel and engine oil shortage is increasingly disrupting health services across the Gaza Strip. The UN warned that reduced fuel support to hospitals, driven by funding shortfalls and high procurement costs, could affect most hospitals and lead to cuts in primary and secondary healthcare services.
Over the reporting period, several UNRWA health centres also experienced temporary disruptions due to generator overload, highlighting the growing operational impact of the shortage of fuel, lubricant oil and spare parts on the delivery of healthcare.

Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs

According to the WASH Cluster’s Light Touch Monitoring Survey, completed in early July 2026, 90 per cent of the population are experiencing frequent, unpredictable disruptions in access to safe, clean, and reliable water for daily drinking, cooking, and hygiene – an increase from 84 per cent the previous month.

Shelter needs

According to the Shelter Cluster’s classification, based on surveys conducted between 14 and 28 June 2026, an estimated 1.98 million people, representing 94 per cent of the population of the Gaza Strip, require shelter and non-food item assistance (NFI). The assessment also suggests that 58 per cent of the population is facing critical or catastrophic shelter needs, meaning severe deficiencies in shelter conditions or lack of access to essential services and NFIs that expose them to serious health, safety, and dignity risks.

Protection needs

According to UN Women, the number of women-headed households in the Gaza Strip has doubled since October 2023, reaching about 86,000 households – or almost one in every four families. This includes widows and women whose husbands are missing, detained, severely injured, or forcibly disappeared, or whose fate simply remains unknown.

Operational implications and humanitarian response

Around 10,500 Palestinian UNRWA personnel continue to provide services and assistance to people in the Gaza Strip. In the occupied West Bank, UNRWA also plays a central role with over 4,000 of the Agency’s Palestinian personnel providing education, health, and other services to Palestine Refugees.
All UNRWA international staff are prevented from entering the Occupied Palestinian Territory - the Gaza Strip and the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. This follows the implementation of laws passed by the Israeli parliament on 28 October 2024[3] that purport to prohibit UNRWA’s operations in areas that Israel considers its sovereign territory, including occupied East Jerusalem, and seek to bar any contact between Israeli officials and UNRWA. Israeli authorities have not granted the Agency’s international staff visas or permits to enter the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including the Gaza Strip, since the end of January 2025.
Since March 2025, the Israeli authorities have been blocking UNRWA from directly bringing humanitarian personnel and aid into the Gaza Strip. Despite the huge needs on the ground, UNRWA was therefore unable to distribute the aid it had pre-positioned outside Gaza, which included enough food parcels, flour, and shelter supplies for hundreds of thousands of people.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-09-29) ---
Highlights

Demolitions displaced 147 Palestinians across the West Bank in one week, more than three times the 2026 weekly average, including 58 people in South ‘Anata Bedouins, near the area designated for the E1 settlement expansion plan.
Israeli forces’ operations and movement restrictions disrupted access to essential services for thousands of Palestinians in Ramallah governorate, while operations by Israeli forces in Nablus temporarily displaced families and closed schools and a health clinic.
Funding shortfalls threaten mobile health services in Area C: 20 locations are at risk of losing mobile-clinic coverage without continued funding, while 17 locations are already without services.
In Gaza, shortages of cancer medications, chemotherapy and radiotherapy supplies, dialysis materials, insulin and engine oil – caused by underfunding and lack of clearance for limited available medicines to enter Gaza – are disrupting life-saving services.
The new academic year in Gaza began on 19 September with an estimated 200,000 children remaining out of school, amid funding shortfalls. Partners had prepared by removing rubble, cleaning some classrooms and providing bags and stationery. In the Strip, approximately 495,000 preschool and school-aged children are enrolled in learning spaces supported through the UN and partners’ humanitarian response.
Funding shortfalls and restrictions are severely constraining preparations for the rainy season, with very limited available stocks covering only a fraction of the needs; hundreds of child-friendly spaces and protection services face disruptions.
Across the Gaza Strip, 451 displacement sites identified as being at high risk of flooding are hosting an estimated 464,402 people.

Overview
Violence and restrictions continue to drive humanitarian needs in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank, while shortfalls in humanitarian funding are further holding back relief efforts. Addressing Member States as part of the General Assembly High-level Week, the United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres said that &quot;we must not lose sight of the devastating situation in the Occupied Palestinian Territory.&quot;
Regarding Gaza, he said that “the resumption of assistance since the ceasefire was announced last October has brough some relief – but conditions remain intolerable... International law, including international humanitarian law, is brazenly violated. Humanitarian access remains severely restricted, and the level of human suffering is still off the charts.” He reiterated the need to allow the UN to perform its mandates in Gaza and for UN and humanitarian personnel to be protected, stressing that all parties must comply with international law and the orders of the International Court of Justice.
Regarding the West Bank, he said that &quot;record levels of settler attacks and relentless efforts to expand settlements are pushing Palestinian communities to the breaking point. Such actions are unlawful and unjust.&quot; The Secretary-General further said that violence, displacement and settlement expansion by Israel &quot;are bulldozing the path to peace and raising the spectre of ethnic cleansing.&quot;
