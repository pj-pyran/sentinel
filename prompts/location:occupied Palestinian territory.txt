Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: Protection Cluster (2026-10-09) ---
Protection Developments – Escalating Settler Violence and Displacement
Between June and August 2026, the operating environment in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, deteriorated further amid a worsening security situation and additional measures by Israeli authorities that constrained humanitarian access and operations.
During the reporting period, OHCHR verified the killing of 27 Palestinians in the West Bank, including eight boys. Of these, 20 were killed by Israeli forces (IF), two by Israeli settlers, and five by either IF or Israeli settlers. In addition, two Israelis, including one civilian and one IF member, were killed in settler attacks followed by clashes with Palestinians.
Settler violence continued to intensify in scale, geographic spread, and severity, with repeated attacks occurring amid persistent impunity and insufficient protection for affected Palestinian communities. OCHA recorded 229 settler attacks resulting in casualties and/or property damage in June 2026, 221 in July, and 198 in August, up from 148 in January 2026, reflecting a sustained upward trend. Annual incidents have exceeded 1,000 since 2023 and reached 1,836 in 2025. In many cases, attacks reportedly occurred in the presence of IF, which are increasingly seen failing to prevent, facilitating, or participating in such violence.
A related trend was the expansion of settler attacks beyond areas qualified as “C” under the Oslo Accords, alongside the growth of settler farm outposts, which reportedly controlled about 18% of West Bank land as of July 2026. According to the CSO Yesh Din, the share of attacks occurring in Areas “A” and “B” increased from 32% in 2025 to 63% in January-June 2026. This surge contributed to increased casualties, property damage and livelihood losses, movement restrictions, limited access to services, heightened displacement risks, shrinking safe spaces for Palestinians, and increasingly constrained humanitarian operations.
In one particularly grave incident, on 24 July 2026, approximately 60 settlers, several of them armed, entered the village of Tell, southwest of Nablus in Area “B”, reportedly as part of an unauthorized hike, triggering clashes involving Palestinians, armed settlers, and IF. Four Palestinian men from the same family and two Israelis, one of them an off duty IF soldier, were killed. The incident was followed by what appeared to be extensive collective punishment measures, including the temporary detention of approximately 100 Palestinian men. In the following days, settlers carried out coordinated attacks across Qalqilya and Nablus in apparent retaliation, resulting in Palestinian injuries, extensive property damage, and movement restrictions that impeded access to essential services. According to the CSO Peace Now, settlers had established five outposts near Tell in reprisal by the end of July.
A similar incident occurred on 21 August 2026 in Sa'ir, Hebron (also in Area “B”), when at least 50 settlers participating in a reported unauthorized hike entered the village armed with firearms, batons, sharp objects, slings, and Molotov cocktails. They attacked homes and residents, resulting in the shooting and killing of a 16-year-old boy and critically injuring several others, including a 71-year-old man.
A related emerging pattern involved the forcible displacement of Palestinian families from homes in residential areas on the outskirts of Palestinian villages in Area “B”, following sustained harassment and intimidation by Israeli settlers. In several cases, families reportedly endured prolonged sieges of their homes, including repeated attacks and obstruction of access to essential services and supplies, making daily life untenable and ultimately forcing them to leave. Their properties were subsequently occupied by settlers.
One such incident occurred on 22 July 2026, when two Palestinian families comprising seven people, including three children, were displaced from their home northeast of Jalud, Nablus, following months of settler violence and harassment. After a nearby outpost was established in April, the families reportedly faced repeated attacks, had their vehicle burned, were denied access to food and medicine, and experienced disruptions to water and electricity services. Settlers reportedly occupied the two homes immediately after the families left.
A similar case occurred in Area B near Qusra, Nablus Governorate. On 9 August 2026, settlers from a nearby outpost blocked access roads to three Palestinian homes and erected a tent between them, effectively trapping approximately 15 Palestinians, including children, inside. Although Israeli forces later removed the tent, deployed to the area and declared it a closed military zone, residents continued to face severe movement restrictions as of the end of August, with limited access to food, water and other essential supplies. Settler harassment and intimidation continued, while visits to the affected homes required prior coordination with the Israeli military.
Against this backdrop, protection partners faced growing challenges in addressing protection risks and maintaining services to affected communities. Reports indicated increased harassment and attacks against humanitarian actors, which, together with the designation of some affected areas as closed military zones, further restricted humanitarian space.

--- Report 2: UNRWA (2026-10-09) ---
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

Over 106,000 children attended in-person learning in UNRWA Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) during the first weeks of the 2026/2027 school year, marking an important milestone in efforts to expand access to learning across the Gaza Strip. At the same time, demand for in-person learning continues to outpace available capacity, and efforts are ongoing to identify additional learning spaces and expand existing TLS. Learning activities continue in challenging conditions in many locations, and significant investment is required to improve learning environments, undertake repairs, rehabilitate water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities, and produce essential education furniture. UNRWA estimates that several million dollars are needed to address these requirements and support safe, inclusive, and conducive learning conditions for children across UNRWA schools and temporary learning spaces.
Over the past months, with the support of partners, UNRWA teams have produced more than 6,000 pieces of furniture for UNRWA TLS and health centres, including desks, chairs, tables, cupboards, and benches, helping expand learning capacity despite severe resource constraints.
Military activity remained concentrated along and around the Israeli-controlled military zone, known as the &quot;Yellow Line&quot;, continuing to expose civilians to significant protection risks. UNRWA recorded several incidents affecting its installations and displaced persons sheltering within them. In the Middle Area, quadcopter fire prompted the precautionary relocation of displaced persons within an UNRWA school. In separate incidents in Jabalia, two displaced persons sheltering in UNRWA schools, including a seven-year-old child, were reportedly injured by gunfire while sleeping inside the shelters.
As winter approaches, as reported by the Site Management and Shelter Clusters, an estimated 235,000 people are sheltering in around 400 flood-prone displacement sites, while more than 44,000 people continue to live in unsafe buildings at heightened risk of collapse during winter. Combined, these figures represent over 13 per cent of Gaza's population exposed to heightened winter-related risks. On 28 September, the UN Humanitarian Coordinator Ramiz Alakbarov warned the UN Security Council that “restrictions on the entry of critical humanitarian items continue to impede the scale-up of the humanitarian response, including preparations” for the rainy season. As co-lead of the Site Management Cluster, UNRWA continues to coordinate and support winter preparedness and risk-reduction efforts through site-level risk assessments, prioritisation of high-risk locations, and coordination of targeted mitigation measures, including flood-risk reduction, access improvements, debris removal, site and shelter reinforcement, and community engagement.

Key points
The Gaza Strip
Fatalities and injuries

Between 7 October 2023 and 2 October 2026, according to the Ministry of Health, as reported by the Health Cluster, 74,228 Palestinians were killed in the Gaza Strip and another 175,188 were injured. This includes 1,445 fatalities and 5,227 injuries between the announcement of a ceasefire agreement on 10 October 2025 and 2 October 2026.
UNRWA has recorded 394 colleagues killed in the Gaza Strip since the start of the war (311 UNRWA personnel and 83 persons who were supporting UNRWA[2], as of 6 October.

Health crisis

The worsening fuel and engine oil shortage is increasingly disrupting health services across the Gaza Strip. The UN warned that reduced fuel support to hospitals, driven by funding shortfalls and high procurement costs, could affect most hospitals and lead to cuts in primary and secondary healthcare services.
In September, six UNRWA health centres also experienced temporary disruptions due to generator overload, highlighting the growing operational impact of the shortage of fuel, lubricant oil, and spare parts on the delivery of healthcare.

Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs

According to the WASH Cluster’s Light Touch Monitoring Survey, completed in early July, 90 per cent of the population are experiencing frequent, unpredictable disruptions in access to safe, clean, and reliable water for daily drinking, cooking, and hygiene – an increase from 84 per cent the previous month.

Shelter needs

According to the Shelter Cluster’s classification, based on surveys conducted between 14 and 28 June 2026, an estimated 1.98 million people, representing 94 per cent of the population of the Gaza Strip, require shelter and non-food item assistance (NFI). The assessment also suggests that 58 per cent of the population is facing critical or catastrophic shelter needs, meaning severe deficiencies in shelter conditions or lack of access to essential services and NFIs that expose them to serious health, safety, and dignity risks.

Operational implications and humanitarian response

Around 10,500 Palestinian UNRWA personnel continue to provide services and assistance to people in the Gaza Strip. In the occupied West Bank, UNRWA also plays a central role with over 4,000 of the Agency’s Palestinian personnel providing education, health, and other services to Palestine Refugees.
All UNRWA international staff are prevented from entering the Occupied Palestinian Territory - the Gaza Strip and the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. This follows the implementation of laws passed by the Israeli parliament on 28 October 2024[3] that purport to prohibit UNRWA’s operations in areas that Israel considers its sovereign territory, including occupied East Jerusalem, and seek to bar any contact between Israeli officials and UNRWA. Israeli authorities have not granted the Agency’s international staff visas or permits to enter the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including the Gaza Strip, since the end of January 2025.
Since March 2025, the Israeli authorities have been blocking UNRWA from directly bringing humanitarian personnel and aid into the Gaza Strip. Despite the huge needs on the ground, UNRWA was therefore unable to distribute the aid it had pre-positioned outside Gaza, which included enough food parcels, flour, and shelter supplies for hundreds of thousands of people.

The occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem

According to OCHA, between 7 October 2023 and 6 October 2026, 1,138 Palestinians – at least 250 of them children – were killed in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Ninety-one of them have been killed since the beginning of 2026.
Israeli forces again renewed the military closure orders covering Jenin, Tulkarm, and Nur Shams camps in the northern West Bank until 30 November, deepening the near-total restrictions on accessing the camps. Some 34,000 Palestine Refugees remain forcibly displaced as a result of the ongoing operation “Iron Wall”, which started in January 2025.
Movement and access restrictions imposed by Israeli forces across the occupied West Bank were tightened during this period, coinciding with the Jewish holiday of Sukkot and in anticipation of the third anniversary of the attacks of 7 October 2023. The Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in the Palestinian territory occupied since 1967 released a statement on 2 October condemning the impact of checkpoints on Palestinian life.
Tendering for the construction of the E1 Israeli settlement plan located east of Jerusalem was postponed by the office of the Israeli Attorney General until after the upcoming Israeli elections. The E1 settlement project stands to displace multiple Palestine Refugee communities and would severely undermine the territorial contiguity of the occupied West Bank.
During this period, two Palestinians were killed in connection with settler violence incidents, which also resulted in damage to Palestinian property and agricultural assets across the occupied West Bank. The official start of the annual olive harvest season is scheduled for 15 October, with an increase in settler violence and harassment anticipated.
