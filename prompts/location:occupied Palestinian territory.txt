REDUCING RODENT INFESTATION RISKS: AN INTEGRATED SITE MANAGEMENT APPROACH
At A12 site in Gaza city, Beit Lahia Development Association, with support of IOM, implemented a comprehensive package of site management activities to improve living conditions amidst a severe rodent and insect infestation driven by its proximity to heavily damaged buildings outside the site boundary and overcrowding within the site. These conditions created significant public health and protection risks, particularly for children, women, older persons, and persons with disabilities. A site safety audit identified the infestation as the site’s highest priority concern, informing an integrated response led through site management activities.
The response began with community awareness sessions where residents received information on the pesticides and rodenticides that would be used, the locations where control materials would be placed, and the precautions needed to protect children and other vulnerable groups from exposure. This was followed by a site-wide pest and rodent control campaign covering 196 shelters. Shelters, communal areas, internal pathways, and the site perimeter were treated using insecticides, repellents, and strategically placed rodent bait stations.
Following the intervention, community meetings and additional awareness activities reinforced key prevention messages and encouraged residents to maintain safer environmental practices. A subsequent cleaning campaign removed solid waste and environmental hazards from pathways and communal areas, helping reduce conditions that contribute to rodent infestation. Together, these linked interventions reduced public health and protection risks while creating a cleaner, safer, and more dignified living environment for displaced families.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-07-20) ---
Gaza Strip Situation Update

Humanitarian conditions in the Gaza Strip remain dire, with most of the population – 1.9 out of 2.1 million people – displaced and exposed to ongoing health and environmental risks.
Access restrictions and operational constraints continue to disrupt humanitarian delivery, with only 280 out of 633 UNICEF aid trucks (44 per cent) from Egypt able to offload during May and June 2026.
Essential services remain critically limited: access to healthcare, water, sanitation, and protection services continue to be disrupted by destruction or damage to critical civilian infrastructure, and movement restrictions.
Severe restrictions on the entry of fuel, spare parts, and essential materials – including engine oil required to operate generators – are driving system failures across health and WASH services.
In the WASH sector, conditions remain critical. An estimated 82 per cent of families are water insecure, and up to 70 per cent cannot access 6 litres per person per day – far below minimum emergency standards – placing children at direct risk of dehydration and waterborne disease.
Nearly 90 per cent of water and sanitation infrastructure has been damaged or destroyed. Limited latrine access, unsafe disposal practices, and disrupted sludge management compromise infection prevention and control, increase the risk of healthcare-related infections, the growing presence of rats and other disease vectors, and contribute to widespread environmental contamination.

--- Report 3: DFS (2026-07-20) ---
The Occupied Palestinian Territory is facing a severe humanitarian crisis, with both Gaza and the West Bank experiencing unprecedented levels of violence and deprivation. In Gaza, following the collapse of a brief pause in hostilities in March 2025, Israeli forces resumed intensive bombardment while maintaining a complete blockade that has now entered its eighth week, preventing all humanitarian aid and commercial supplies from entering. This has left Gaza's 2.2 million residents facing starvation as food stocks deplete, bakeries cease operations due to lack of flour and fuel, and nearly 90% of the population remains displaced with critical infrastructure almost entirely destroyed. A new U.S.-backed peace plan led to a pause in hostilities agreement coming into effect on 10 October 2025, raising cautious hopes for increased humanitarian access and a potential reduction in hostilities after months of relentless violence. However, despite the pause in hostilities, sporadic violations, limited aid entry, and continued displacement highlight the fragility of the pause in hostilities and the deep humanitarian strain that persists across Gaza.
Meanwhile, the West Bank has seen a dramatic escalation in military operations, particularly since the launch of &quot;Operation Iron Wall&quot; in January 2025, which has resulted in mass displacement of Palestinians and widespread destruction across refugee camps. Israeli forces have displaced approximately 40,000 people, with officials stating that residents will not be allowed to return for at least a year, while expanded checkpoints and military presence severely restrict movement between Palestinian cities.
International organisations and UN bodies have expressed increasing concern about systematic violations of international humanitarian law in the West Bank, including accelerating settlement expansion, land reclassification and forced displacement that multiple observers warn are driving de facto annexation dynamics. These concerns have intensified following the International Court of Justice's 2024 advisory opinion on the illegality of the prolonged occupation and the Israeli authorities' security cabinet decisions of 8 February 2026 expanding civil and administrative control over West Bank land and governance.

--- Report 6: IOM (2026-07-20) ---
BACKGROUND
As the largest provider of shelter assistance in Gaza, IOM plays a central role in supporting displaced and conflict-affected households through a network of locally based partners. IOM cocoordinates the Shelter Cluster, strengthening the coherence and effectiveness of the shelter response across the sector, despite ongoing access constraints, shortages of shelter materials, widespread infrastructure damage, and prolonged displacement. By leveraging local expertise and available resources, IOM and its partners deliver adaptable, incremental shelter solutions that respond to rapidly changing humanitarian needs.
