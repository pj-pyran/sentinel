Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: ETC (2026-07-03) ---
The WFP-led Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) was activated as the Emergency Telecommunications Cluster (ETC) on 31 October 2023 in response to the crisis in Gaza. This Situation Report provides a monthly update on LTC telecoms activities. All references to the LTC in this report relate specifically to the telecommunications area of the cluster.
Summary Points
• LTC Telecoms continued supporting critical communications in Gaza, coordinating with UN, humanitarian, and private sector partners to sustain telecommunications services, address fuel shortages, and advocate for the entry of essential ICT equipment, spare parts, and consumables.
• Inter-agency Security Communications Systems (SCS) were further strengthened, with progress on the Unified Radio Configuration Project, expanded VHF radio coverage, technical support to the Gaza Security Operations Centre (SOC), and security communications training for humanitarian partners.
• Telecommunications services remained severely constrained by conflict-related damage and supply restrictions, with an estimated 75 percent of infrastructure damaged or destroyed since October 2023. Ongoing shortages of ICT equipment, fuel, spare parts, and maintenance supplies continued to affect network reliability and limit connectivity for humanitarian responders and affected communities.

--- Report 2: ETC (2026-07-03) ---
Operation overview
Armed conflict between Palestinian militant groups and Israeli military forces began on 07 October 2023. The conflict has resulted in a dire and devastating humanitarian crisis and significant disruption to communications networks, ultimately impeding life-saving humanitarian operations. The WFP-led Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC Telecoms) was activated on 31 October 2023 to meet the urgent need to support access to reliable shared communications services for humanitarian organizations operating in Gaza.

--- Report 3: UNRWA (2026-07-03) ---
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

On 30 June, Israeli forces forcibly entered the UNRWA Kalandia Training Centre, located in occupied East Jerusalem. They conducted a tour, took photos of the premises and stated that the centre had to close. The centre serves hundreds of students from refugee camps across the West Bank, many of whom face difficult socio-economic conditions and benefit from free education services at the facility. The spokesperson for the UN Secretary-General condemned the operation and noted that it “breached the inviolability of UN premises and violated Israel’s obligations concerning the privileges and immunities of the United Nations”.
The Humanitarian Country Team in the Occupied Palestinian Territory warned that the continued expansion of areas under Israeli control in the Gaza Strip is intensifying risks to civilians and further constraining humanitarian efforts. For reference, 126 UNRWA installations are currently located in areas where access has been restricted (i.e. behind the so-called “Yellow Line” and “Orange Line” of military control implemented by the Israeli authorities)
Over the reporting period, UNRWA staff members were injured as a result of airstrikes conducted across the Gaza Strip. This includes an airstrike conducted on 29 June on tents in Al Mawasi area of Khan Younis, where scores of Palestinians were injured and hundreds of tents were damaged or destroyed.
Amid severe overcrowding and deteriorating public health conditions, UNRWA continues to conduct cleaning campaigns, community awareness activities, and targeted pesticide application by trained UNRWA personnel, alongside solid waste disposal and other activities to improve environmental conditions. In total, 352 campaigns to control the spread of disease-carrying animals and pests have already been completed across 371 displacement sites since 7 June, reaching an area where almost 684,000 people reside. UNRWA is a main contributor to the campaign, taking part directly or in collaboration with partners in 335 out of the 352 planned campaigns.
The continued shortage of engine oil and spare parts poses a major challenge for the Agency’s humanitarian operations. Generators in UNRWA schools-turned-shelters are operating for just 2-3 hours per day, leading to reduced access to water and essential services. Furthermore, an increasing number of UNRWA vehicles remain grounded, resulting in the accumulation of waste in camps and collective shelters, and limiting the ability of UNRWA teams to move across the Strip.

--- Report 4: UNRWA (2026-07-01) ---
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

UNRWA continues to participate in the pest-control campaign led by the WASH Cluster, aiming to prevent the spread of diseases and improve living conditions for displaced communities amid severe overcrowding and deteriorating public health conditions.
UNRWA is conducting cleaning campaigns, community awareness activities, and targeted pesticide use by trained UNRWA personnel, alongside solid waste disposal, and other activities to improve environmental conditions. In total, 264 campaigns to control the spread of disease-carrying animals and pests have already been completed across 371 displacement sites since 7 June, reaching an area where almost 684,000 people reside. UNRWA is a main contributor to the campaign, taking part (directly or together with partners) in 335 out of the 352 planned campaigns.
On Saturday, 20 June, UNRWA schools started registering new first-grade students for the 2026/2027 school year.
