Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-07-24) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

Funding shortfalls are impacting WFP’s ability to meet significant humanitarian needs. From July, WFP will provide monthly cash assistance to 100,000 households through a revised, vulnerability-based approach, maintaining full transfers to the most vulnerable households and reduced transfers for others, which will help extend available cash assistance through late November.
Additional funding is urgently needed to prevent further reductions in food assistance. Without new contributions, in-kind assistance, which is already being provided at a 75 percent ration, will be reduced from October, with fewer people receiving reduced rations.
Newly released IPC analysis shows improvements in food security and child nutrition across Gaza, however progress remains fragile, with the improvements driven largely by expanded humanitarian food, nutrition and health interventions rather than a recovery of livelihoods, local food systems or essential services.

--- Report 2: NRC (2026-07-23) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
GAZA
• Shelter Cluster partners continued delivering life-saving Shelter and NFI assistance across the Gaza Strip throughout June, responding to urgent needs among affected households through in-kind and voucher modalities. In June 2026, a total of 36,210 households were reached with shelter and NFI support. Distributed items included 17,626 tarpaulins, 14,814 kitchen sets, 12,670 bedding kits, 7,055 bedding items, 1,334 tents, 504 clothing voucher CVA, 50 clothing kits, and 65 sealing off kits. These interventions remained critical in addressing urgent shelter and household needs, supporting vulnerable and displaced populations, improving living conditions, and helping families cope with ongoing displacement and deteriorating humanitarian conditions across Gaza.
• Under the Rapid Joint Distribution Mechanism (RJDM), a significant increase in incident alerts was reported during the reporting period. Recorded incidents included artillery shelling, airstrikes, domestic fires, and forced displacement, affecting thousands of people across the Gaza Strip. Following rapid assessments, partners assisted 587 households with shelter and non-food item (SNFI) support. Of these, 464 households received joint response packages, while 123 households received shelter and NFI assistance tailored to their specific needs.
• Available stocks of essential household items inside Gaza have reached critically low levels, with remaining supplies sufficient to support fewer than 15,300 additional households. The facilitation of Shelter and NFI material entry remains urgently required to sustain response operations and respond to ongoing humanitarian needs, particularly considering increased seasonal requirements during the summer period.
• Shelter partners also reported additional interventions comprising the following:
◦ Emergency repairs and upgrades were completed for 1,837 makeshift shelters, bringing the cumulative number of upgraded makeshift shelters to 2,696.
◦ Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) were newly installed for 500 households, increasing the total number of installed ESKs to 867.
• In addition, partners completed emergency repairs to 1,025 partially damaged homes across Gaza’s governorates, bringing the cumulative total of rehabilitated housing units to approximately 1,299 by the end of June.
• During June, the distribution of solar-powered lamps to 7,200 households across the Gaza Strip was underway with support from IOM. The intervention targets 26 displacement sites in both northern and southern Gaza. An update from the implementing partner is still pending and has not yet been received by the Shelter Cluster (SC).
• Pipeline constraints continue to significantly limit the shelter response. The timely release of materials currently in the pipeline—particularly solar lights, timber for framing kits, ropes, and tools—remains essential to enable partners to scale up priority interventions. These include repairs of partially damaged shelters, upgrading makeshift shelters, and supporting a gradual transition toward early recovery activities.

--- Report 3: UNRWA (2026-07-22) ---
All information updated for 8 - 14 July 2026 [1]
Days 1004 - 1010 since the start of the war in the Gaza Strip
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

The second semester of the academic year 2025-2026 has been successfully completed. Final examinations took place between 27 June and 12 July. Student participation showed improvement compared to the previous scholastic year, with 261,907 children participating in final exams out of 282,838 registered students (93 per cent participation rate).
Preparations for the summer learning activities are ongoing, with the start date expected to be 18 July (for eight weeks). Education Cluster partners delivered over 1,700 School-in-a-Box kits to support this activity, while additional recreational materials are being procured locally.
Restrictions on the entry of generators, engine oil, and spare parts continue to pose a critical threat to UNRWA’s operations. Essential services such as solid waste management, water delivery, and health services are particularly affected, as facilities and equipment rely on generators that face frequent breakdowns. Currently, generators in UNRWA health centres are operating for only 3-4 hours per day. Furthermore, dozens of Agency vehicles have already been grounded due to major mechanical failures, and the Agency struggles to sustain the remaining operational fleet, which is operating far beyond its standard technical lifespan.
