Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: UNRWA (2026-10-28) ---
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

On 12 October, UNRWA opened a new health facility in Jabalia and began receiving patients. As UNRWA’s northernmost health facility in the Gaza Strip, the clinic expands access to health services for underserved communities. Together with the Saftawi Health Centre, the two facilities provide new anchor points for delivering health services to thousands of Palestine Refugees in the area.
UNRWA recorded several significant armed incidents affecting its facilities and schools-turned-shelters. At least six displaced people, including a child, were injured and one person was killed while sheltering inside UNRWA facilities, mostly as a result of stray bullets. Operations at several facilities across Rafah, Khan Younis and the Middle Area were also temporarily suspended, either due to insecurity or following evacuation orders issued by Israeli forces.
UNRWA’s winterisation response is underway, with winter emergency teams activated across all areas to clean manholes, gullies, and drainage networks, identify high-risk locations, inspect available equipment, and put preventive measures in place, including sandbags in flood-prone areas. Winterisation preparedness, however, remains seriously constrained by limited operational capacity: many pumps to address flooding are not functioning due to lack of engine oil and spare parts, and heavy machinery cannot enter Gaza.

The occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem

Marking three years since the attacks of 7 October 2023, movement and access re-strictions were tightened across the occupied West Bank.
The olive harvest season officially commenced on 15 October, accompanied by settler violence and access restrictions impacting Palestinian farmers in several areas of the occupied West Bank. During the reporting period, one Palestinian was reportedly killed in an incident related to settler violence.

Key Points
The Gaza Strip
Fatalities and injuries

Between 7 October 2023 and 9 October 2026, according to the Ministry of Health, as reported by the Health Cluster, 74,370 Palestinians were killed in the Gaza Strip and another 175,371 were injured. This includes 1,481 fatalities and 5,410 injuries between the announcement of a ceasefire agreement on 10 October 2025 and 9 October 2026.
UNRWA has recorded 394 colleagues killed in the Gaza Strip since the start of the war (311 UNRWA personnel and 83 persons who were supporting UNRWA[2], as of 20 October.

Health crisis

Hypothermia remains a serious risk, particularly for newborns and infants, with deaths reported in previous winters since the war began. UNRWA health and nutrition teams are strengthening counselling for women and caregivers on breastfeeding, skin-to-skin care and other simple measures to keep children warm. Infant warming units are being distributed across UNRWA health facilities to address emergency cases. However, supply restrictions have prevented the entry of additional warmers for community distribution, while use of existing units depends on unreliable electricity supplies.
In September, six UNRWA health centres also experienced temporary disruptions due to overloaded generators having to be switched off until new lubricant oil can be found. This highlights the growing operational impact of the shortage of fuel, lubricant oil, and spare parts on the delivery of healthcare.

Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs

According to the WASH Cluster’s Light Touch Monitoring Survey, completed in September, 84 per cent of the population are experiencing frequent, unpredictable disruptions in access to safe, clean, and reliable water for daily drinking, cooking, and hygiene. Water insecurity thus remains a significant concern, despite signs of improvement (91 per cent of the population reported water insecurity in the previous round).
Access to basic hygiene supplies also remained severely constrained, with 89 per cent of households reporting insufficient access to essential hygiene items.

Displacement, site management

UNRWA continues to monitor the movement of displaced persons and displacement sites. A total of 113 UNRWA collective emergency shelters host 65,000 internally displaced persons.

Shelter needs

According to the Shelter Cluster’s classification, based on surveys conducted between 14 and 28 June, an estimated 1.98 million people, representing 94 per cent of the population of the Gaza Strip, require shelter and non-food item assistance (NFI). The assessment also suggests that 58 per cent of the population is facing critical or catastrophic shelter needs, meaning severe deficiencies in shelter conditions or lack of access to essential services and NFIs that expose them to serious health, safety, and dignity risks.

Operational implications and humanitarian response

Around 10,500 Palestinian UNRWA personnel continue to provide services and assistance to people in the Gaza Strip. In the occupied West Bank, UNRWA also plays a central role with more than 4,000 of the Agency’s Palestinian personnel providing education, health, and other services to Palestine Refugees.
All UNRWA international staff are prevented from entering the Occupied Palestinian Territory - the Gaza Strip and the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. This follows the implementation of laws passed by the Israeli parliament on 28 October 2024[3] that purport to prohibit UNRWA’s operations in areas that Israel considers its sovereign territory, including occupied East Jerusalem, and seek to bar any contact between Israeli officials and UNRWA. Israeli authorities have not granted the Agency’s international staff visas or permits to enter the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including the Gaza Strip, since the end of January 2025.
Since March 2025, the Israeli authorities have been blocking UNRWA from directly bringing humanitarian personnel and aid into the Gaza Strip. Despite the huge needs on the ground, UNRWA was therefore unable to distribute the aid it had pre-positioned outside Gaza, which included enough food parcels, flour, and shelter supplies for hundreds of thousands of people.

The occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem

According to OCHA, between 7 October 2023 and 9 October 2026, 1,143 Palestinians – at least 250 of them children – were killed in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Ninety-six of them have been killed since the beginning of 2026.
Marking three years since the attacks of 7 October 2023, movement and access re-strictions were tightened across the occupied West Bank.
Settler violence and harassment continued to account for a significant percentage of recorded protection incidents, with 395 incidents and at least 115 related injuries reported from 7-20 October alone. In addition to widespread reports of obstruction and attacks targeting the olive harvest, sustained pressure on Palestinian communities is contributing to an increasingly coercive environment and heightened protection concerns.
A Palestinian from Burqa was reportedly killed in the context of settler violence on 7 October. Other Palestinians sustained injuries during the incident. The incident underscores the high levels of settler violence currently affecting Palestinian communities, particularly during the olive harvest season.
On 10 October, a Palestinian from Beit Ula was shot and killed by Israeli forces using live ammunition in Hebron City.
Israeli forces have continued to keep all entrances to Madama village closed since 11 October.
The olive harvest season officially commenced on 15 October, accompanied by settler violence and access restrictions impacting Palestinian farmers in several areas of the West Bank. On 16 October, in Abu Falah, a foreign journalist was among the injured. On 17 October, in At-Tayba, a European diplomatic delegation came under attack during a visit to observe the olive harvest.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-10-27) ---
Highlights

In the West Bank, 148 Palestinians were injured by Israeli forces or settlers between 13 and 19 October, including 69 in olive-harvest-related settler attacks.
Since 1 September, 272 olive-harvest-related settler attacks have been documented, exceeding the totals recorded during the entire 2024 and 2025 harvest seasons.
Israeli settlers set fire to a mosque in Jerusalem governorate, bringing to 14 the number of mosques vandalized by settlers since the beginning of 2026 in the West Bank.
According to WHO, 146 attacks on health care were recorded in the West Bank between January and September 2026, resulting in four fatalities and 43 injuries, alongside 131 instances of obstruction to health-care delivery.
In Gaza, as of 22 October, eight children had been reported killed and 20 injured in just six days. These are among hundreds of children who have been reported killed since the announcement of the October 2025 ceasefire.
According to the Gaza Market Monitoring Committee, the price of food items soared. Potatoes reportedly rose by 72 per cent, and the food basket increased by 20 per cent on average.
Due to funding shortages, Food Security Sector partners are only being able to reach less than one million people out of an estimated 2.1 million in Gaza with monthly food aid from October 2026 onwards. Meanwhile, 60 per cent of funds required by the Shelter Cluster to help people prepare for the cold and rainy season remains unmet and 11 partners might have to cease the delivery of drinking water by truck.
Across the Strip, trauma, surgical and rehabilitation supplies, as well as specialized medicines for noncommunicable diseases remain scarce due to ongoing aid entry restrictions. Oncology diagnostics and imaging are severely compromised.

West Bank
The reporting Period is 13–19 October 2026 unless otherwise specified.
The humanitarian situation in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, remained severe amid operations by Israeli forces, settler violence, demolitions, damage to homes and productive assets, and restrictions on access to land and essential services. No Palestinian fatalities were recorded during the reporting period, while 148 Palestinians, including six children, were injured: 92 by Israeli forces and 56 by settlers. Of those injured, 74 (50 per cent) were in the context of settler attacks, including 69 during olive-harvest-related incidents. On 13 October, during an eight-hour operation in Ramallah city, Israeli forces injured 59 Palestinians, including two children, mainly through tear gas inhalation, and raided and closed the offices of a Palestinian human rights organization.
Settler violence remained at high levels, with 83 attacks recorded across 48 communities during the reporting period, resulting in casualties or damage to homes, a mosque, at least 151 olive trees and other property. Since the beginning of 2026, 2,159 settler attacks resulting in Palestinian casualties, property damage or both have been documented across more than 300 communities, averaging 7.4 attacks per day, compared with five per day in 2025. On 14 October, Israeli settlers set fire to a mosque in Mikhmas village, northeast of Jerusalem, causing extensive damage to its interior. They also smashed windows, sprayed anti-Palestinian slogans on the mosque's walls and set fire to a nearby vehicle. Since the beginning of 2026, settlers have vandalized 14 mosques across Palestinian villages in Nablus, Tulkarm, Ramallah and Jerusalem governorates. On 17 October, in Ma'in (Hebron), nine Palestinians, including a woman, were injured during an incident involving Israeli settlers and forces, in which settlers reportedly stole 139 sheep from four animal pens and damaged residential and livelihood assets belonging to four families.
During the reporting period, Israeli authorities demolished or forced Palestinians to demolish 21 Palestinian-owned structures, displacing 37 people. Of those displaced, 32 were affected by demolitions carried out for lack of Israeli-issued building permits, which are impossible for Palestinians to obtain. Another five were displaced during an operation by Israeli forces; on 16 October, Israeli forces fired tear gas canisters into a residential apartment in Area B of Kafr Malik village (Ramallah), causing a fire that rendered it uninhabitable. On 13 October, three Palestinian refugee households comprising 16 people, including 10 children, were displaced in At Tur, East Jerusalem, after a residential building was partially self- demolished by its owners and the remaining demolition was carried out by Israeli authorities, citing the lack of an Israeli-issued building permit. Since the beginning of 2026, approximately 1,790 Palestinians have been displaced by demolitions for lack of Israeli-issued building permits across the West Bank, including 668 (37 per cent) in East Jerusalem.
On 22 October, the Acting Special Coordinator, Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator, Ramiz Alakbarov, visited Jenin governorate with diplomatic representatives from 13 Member States to hear directly from Palestinian communities affected by settler violence, access restrictions and prolonged displacement. In Arraba, olive farmers described how recurrent settler attacks and Israeli-imposed access restrictions are undermining their livelihoods, while humanitarian partners briefed the delegation on protective accompaniment and legal assistance. In Qabatiya, the delegation met Palestinians displaced from Jenin Camp since early 2025 who remain unable to return to their homes. Alakbarov called for the protection of Palestinian farmers, accountability for attacks against them, increased humanitarian funding and measures to enable the safe and dignified return of displaced residents.
