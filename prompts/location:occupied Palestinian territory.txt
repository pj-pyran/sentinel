Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-11-02) ---
ESCALATION IN HEBRON DISPLACES MORE THAN 480 FAMILIES, INCLUDING OVER
1,800 CHILDREN, AND DISRUPTS ACCESS TO ESSENTIAL SERVICES
Highlights

The update covers the humanitarian impact of the escalation in Hebron that began on 23 October 2026, including large-scale displacement, movement restrictions and disruptions to essential services. As of 29 October, Hebron Municipality reported 483 displaced families (4,304 people, including 1,845 children), with more than 300 families lacking access to safe shelter.
The report highlights the effects on children and families, including disruptions to education affecting more than 251,000 students across 691 schools in Hebron Governorate, priority humanitarian needs, and risks associated with prolonged displacement, restricted access and the approaching colder season.
It also outlines UNICEF’s emergency response through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). Between 26 and 29 October, UNICEF partners distributed 889 kits (hygiene, baby, dignity and recreational kits) reaching 249 households (1,403 people, including 716 children), while additional supplies have been mobilized to support further response and preparedness efforts.
The update includes UNICEF’s advocacy messages on the protection of children, humanitarian access, education, shelter, water and sanitation services, and the rights of children deprived of liberty.
It further presents the funding requirements of UNICEF’s 2026 HAC appeal, which remains 54 per cent unfunded, with a funding gap of USD 400.9 million.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-11-02) ---
Key Highlights

Families along the “Yellow Line” in Gaza are facing reported arson attacks and recurrent shootings as Israeli forces place additional yellow cement blocks nearby; despite shortages in humanitarian supplies, those displaced as a result of such pressures are receiving rapid support through available stocks.
Acute respiratory infections represent 60 per cent of all medical consultations reported by the Health Cluster between 18 and 25 October, followed by skin diseases (21 per cent) and acute watery diarrhea (18 per cent).
Amid multiple reports of strikes affecting humanitarian operations, UNOPS reported three security incidents over three consecutive days affecting its convoys and premises.
In the West Bank, humanitarian partners are supporting local authorities in responding to the needs of approximately 3,000 Palestinians reportedly displaced during an ongoing operation by Israeli forces in Hebron city, following a shooting attack by two Palestinians who killed two Israeli soldiers. Displacement figures remain under verification.
October has already recorded the highest monthly number of settler attacks resulting in casualties or property damage since documentation began in 2006, with a total of 397 incidents reported between 1 and 26 October.
Between 20 and 26 October, 62 settler attacks resulting in casualties or property damage were documented across 49 West Bank communities, including 38 olive-harvest-related attacks.
In Khirbet Ras al Ahmar, Tubas governorate, 24 Palestinian herding families comprising 94 people, including 40 children, were displaced amid increasing access restrictions and settler attacks.

Gaza Strip
The reporting period is 19-25 October 2026 unless otherwise specified.
Multiple Israeli airstrikes, shelling, shooting and naval fire were recorded, reportedly causing civilian casualties and further destruction. Israeli incursions and demolitions were also reported near the “Yellow Line.” Humanitarian partners continue to operate amid ongoing insecurity. Since 19 October, a UN site in Deir al Balah has come under fire. There have also been three separate shooting incidents near three UN schools. On 26 October UNOPS reported three incidents over three consecutive days affecting their convoys and premises. These were close proximity explosions; drivers detained at checkpoints, and multiple small arms rounds striking one of the UNOPS guesthouses.
Families in areas along the so-called “Yellow Line” continue to face reported arson attacks, recurrent shooting and the placement of new yellow cement blocks by Israeli forces, which signal that the area is unsafe. In recent days, new blocks have been observed in Ash Shuja’iyyeh and parts of Deir al Balah. On 25 October, tents sheltering displaced households were set on fire in the Ash Shuja’iyyeh area of Gaza city. The UN and partners visited the site on 26 October to assess needs, identifying 9 families affected by the incident, who received emergency assistance and were willing to relocate. This incident followed another fire reportedly instigated by armed Palestinians reportedly linked to Israeli forces a few days earlier, triggering the new displacement of 137 households. Humanitarian partners mobilized support for the affected families, including new tents, tarpaulins, blankets, and hygiene kits. Approximately 130 households remain at the site, living in difficult conditions.
According to the Ministry of Health in Gaza, which operates under the de-facto authorities, 33 Palestinians were reported killed and 188 injured between 20 and 26 October, bringing the total number of casualties since the ceasefire agreement to 1,559 fatalities and 5,608 injuries. Another 42 fatalities were retroactively added to the total number after their identification details were approved by MoH.
The Logistics Cluster reports that, between 19 and 25 October, out of 651 UN and partner trucks carrying humanitarian supplies manifested for entry into Gaza, 586 were successfully offloaded on the Israeli side of Kerem Shalom, of which 291 came from Ashdod, 187 from Egypt and 54 from Jordan. Compared with the previous week, the number of humanitarian trucks manifested for entry decreased by 16 per cent, but the percentage of offloaded trucks remained stable.
The private sector collected 343 commercial truckloads into the Gaza Strip between 19 and 25 October, based on data from the Gaza Chamber of Commerce shared with the Cash Working Group. This represents a 9 per cent increase compared with the previous week (12-18 October), during which a halt in commercial imports was reported in connection with a high fee said to be charged by the de facto authorities, with conflicting narratives over whether that fee was voluntary or imposed.
Of the total, the Chamber of Commerce indicates that 129 carried food supplies, 73 solid fuel, 68 shelter materials, 38 hygiene items, 25 cooking gas, 2 medicines and medical supplies, and one stationery. Seven trucks – or 2 per cent of the total – carried non-essential items, representing a significant drop compared with the 25-35 per cent recorded in the past week (12-18 October).
The reported halt in commercial entries seems to have had a direct impact on bakeries resuming daily production and prices, with the cost of flour and fresh products increasing by over 70 per cent compared to the week (12-18 October), based on information from the Gaza Market Monitoring Committee (GMMC). The Food Security Sector reports that some partners were forced to adjust the cooked meal menu, scale down or pause free bread and fresh produce distribution due to shortages of fruit, vegetables and wheat flour and high prices.
Flood risk mitigation efforts ahead of winter
As the winter approaches, conditions remain dire across the Strip. On 19 October, OCHA and humanitarian partners assessed six displacement sites in the An Nasser neighbourhood of the Khan Younis governorate, none of which has an active organization providing site management presence. Five sites that cumulatively host 750 households are situated in Al Joura, a low-lying area approximately 30 metres below the surrounding ground level. A sixth site, Al Ilm, hosts 250 households, with shelters located both along the cliff edge and in the low-lying area. The team examined flood and erosion risks, explored possible site-improvement measures, and documented priority humanitarian needs requiring partner follow-up. Families reported lacking enough shelter materials, sanitation, water access, food assistance, health care and cash-based support. The team has mobilized support accordingly.
The Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) Cluster conducted a rapid field assessment of the Abu Rashid stormwater basin and pumping station near the “Yellow Line” in Jabalya, North Gaza; the assessment revealed the basin is incapacitated due to lack of power and access being unsafe. Without an emergency rehabilitation and stabilization intervention, the stormwater lagoon is at risk of flooding during the winter, directly affecting nearby displacement sites at risk of flooding. A further detailed assessment is taking place to explore possible mitigation options.
In the Mawasi area of Khan Younis, the WASH Cluster also implemented mitigation works at a displacement site located within a stormwater basin to reduce the risk of flooding.
Meanwhile, the UN Development Programme (UNDP) reports that its teams are using recycled crushed concrete to raise and level shelter sites ahead of winter.
Communicable diseases
Communicable diseases accounted for 18 per cent of all medical consultations conducted by Health Cluster partners during the reporting period, with acute respiratory infections representing 60 per cent of reported diseases, followed by skin diseases (21 per cent) and acute watery diarrhea (18 per cent). An increase in probable viral and bacterial meningitis cases has also been observed since September, primarily in Gaza governorate. Case investigations, line-listing, cerebrospinal fluid follow-up, and laboratory confirmation activities are ongoing, while access to laboratory supplies remains critical for effective outbreak response.
Humanitarian response activities at a glance – Gaza Strip
19–26 October 2026 (unless otherwise specified)
Food Security: As of 26 October, 16 of 17 commercial bakeries supported through a “fuel-only” production model were operational, while 21 subsidized bakeries continued producing approximately 80,000 two-kilogram bread bundles daily. As of 22 October, partners prepared 586,000 meals daily through 90 kitchens and delivered them to 1,368 locations across the Gaza Strip. During the ongoing October distribution cycle, approximately 422,000 people (116,000 households) received food parcels and wheat flour through 28 distribution points, providing around 50 per cent of minimum caloric requirements. Partners also completed one round of fodder distributions to approximately 2,250 livestock holders and launched a second round targeting 2,250 herders and 730 working-animal owners.
Shelter and Site Management: Shelter Cluster partners reached 8,590 households with shelter and non-food item assistance, distributing 734 tents, 1,891 winter clothing kits, 44 bedding kits, 960 blankets, 1,858 toolkits, 2,190 sealing-off kits, 1,256 sheets of transparent plastic and 123 refugee housing units (RHUs).
Health: WHO started cascade training on polio, disease surveillance and adverse events following immunization across four governorates, targeting approximately 300 medical doctors from international and local NGOs. Partners equipped facilities with infection prevention and control supplies , including alcohol-based hand rub, chlorine, personal protective equipment and environmental cleaning materials. Between 21 and 27 October, 69 patients, including 12 children, were medically evacuated through the Rafah crossing alongside 93 caregivers. Since the announcement of a ceasefire agreement on 10 October 2025, WHO has supported the evacuation of 3,547 patients, along with 5,855 caregivers, through the Rafah and Kerem Shalom crossings, including 2,746 patients and 4,065 companions evacuated via Rafah since its reopening on 2 February 2026.
Water, Sanitation and Hygiene: Partners continued preparing for the cold and rainy season winterization preparedness activities in displacement sites, including through road improvement and flood-mitigation works. They distributed 260 water tanks, 6,500 jerry cans, 7,000 tarpaulins, 9,500 bars of soap and 5,000 kilograms of laundry soap.
Protection: Protection partners reached 13,454 people with direct protection services, including mental health and psychosocial support, psychological first aid, case management, legal assistance, explosive ordnance risk education, disability inclusion support and community accountability activities. An additional 1,803 people received protection-sensitive and intersectoral assistance, including food vouchers, hygiene items, menstrual hygiene management kits, sexual and reproductive health services and assistive devices. Protection monitoring activities included six focus group discussions and 73 key informant interviews, reaching 732 people across 13 neighbourhoods. Partners also supported 230 returnees with psychological first aid, emergency assistance and referral services. Child protection partners reached 4,122 children and 2,564 caregivers with mental health and psychosocial support services, including individual and group counselling, psychological first aid, trauma recovery interventions and parenting support. Partners provided case management services to 138 newly identified at-risk children, facilitated 86 referrals for specialized services, and continued support for 58 children in alternative care. Awareness-raising and community engagement activities reached 1,216 children and 1,064 caregivers. Emergency assistance included the distribution of 10,000 winter clothing kits and 600 stationery kits, while 147 vulnerable children received cash assistance.
Joint Distributions | Emergency Response: Joint Distribution Consortium processed appeals for help following 14 reported incidents affecting 525 households across Gaza. The appeals included seven in Gaza affecting 372 households, five in Khan Younis affecting 59 households, and two in Deir al Balah affecting 94 households. The reported incidents included nine airstrike incidents affecting 517 households, four accidental fire incidents affecting seven households, and one displacement incident affecting one household. Based on rapid assessments, 84 households were classified as high-need, 324 as medium-need, and 117 as low-need. Assistance was prioritized according to the type of incident, level of damage, and assessed household needs.
