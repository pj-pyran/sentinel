Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: FAO (2026-11-26) ---
• By mid-2026, conditional cash+ assistance from the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) had enabled 1 700 farming households in the Gaza Strip to produce enough fresh vegetables to meet the annual vegetable intake requirements of an estimated 121 000 people, based on the dietary recommendations of FAO and the World Health Organization.1 Restoring full access to agricultural inputs could increase this figure to an estimated 284 000 people.
• Conditional cash+ assistance provided by FAO to livestock-keeping households in the Gaza Strip contributed to increases in the sheep (33 percent) and goat (8 percent) populations between November 2025 and May 2026, the first recorded increase since October 2023.2 However, the sheep and goat populations remain at approximately one-quarter and one-third of pre‑conflict levels, respectively.
• With improved security and access following the October 2025 ceasefire, FAO has distributed approximately 430 tonnes of animal feed per month, about four times the monthly volume distributed before the ceasefire.
• An FAO analysis found that every USD 1 invested in livestock protection in the Gaza Strip during 2024–2026 generated approximately USD 2.30 in livestock asset value.

--- Report 2: DFS (2026-11-26) ---
Executive Summary
The Occupied Palestinian Territories face a catastrophic humanitarian crisis marked by severe food insecurity, mass displacement, and the near-collapse of essential services. In Gaza, the majority of the population relies entirely on humanitarian assistance, with widespread destruction of housing, health facilities, water infrastructure, and educational institutions compounding the challenges faced by displaced families. Harsh winter conditions, repeated evacuation orders, and constrained humanitarian access continue to threaten the safety and well-being of civilians, with children, pregnant women, and other vulnerable groups bearing a disproportionate burden.
In the West Bank, escalating military operations, settler violence, and movement restrictions have driven significant displacement and disrupted access to livelihoods, healthcare, and education. Economic contraction and rising unemployment have eroded household resilience, while demolitions and settlement expansion threaten further territorial fragmentation. Across both territories, humanitarian organizations are working to scale up food assistance, shelter support, health services, and protection programming, yet persistent access barriers, funding shortfalls, and ongoing hostilities continue to outpace the response, leaving Palestinians in urgent need of sustained international support and durable solutions.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-11-26) ---
Key Highlights

At least 600 aid workers have been killed in Gaza since October 2023, including 13 since the ceasefire was announced, following the killing of an UNRWA staff member in an Israeli strike on 23 November.
The World Health Organization reports 692 suspected meningitis cases between 1 October and 23 November, following a sharp increase in cases in mid- and late October, with infants under one year of age accounting for most cases.
Essential winter supplies in Gaza cost over eight times as much when procured locally as when procured internationally, while only 35 per cent of minimum winter shelter response requirements are funded, according to the Norwegian Refugee Council.
Child protection partners in Gaza report increasing child labour and hazardous responsibilities, exposing children to exploitation, explosive remnants of war and further disruptions to education.
According to the Logistics Cluster, 405 trucks carrying humanitarian supplies were offloaded on the Israeli side of Kerem Shalom Crossing between 16 and 23 November, a six per cent decrease compared with the preceding week.
Intensified movement restrictions, settler violence and demolitions in Masafer Yatta, in southern Hebron governorate, are severely disrupting access to essential services and livelihoods, heightening the risk of further displacement.
Israeli authorities demolish 79 Palestinian-owned structures across the West Bank in one week, including all remaining structures in Khirbet Samra in the northern Jordan Valley.
The Humanitarian Country Team calls for urgent protection of Palestinian olive harvesters amid unprecedented levels of harvest-related settler violence.
A US$40 million allocation from the occupied Palestinian territory Humanitarian Fund was launched. The 2026 Flash Appeal remains only 35.5 per cent funded.

West Bank
The reporting period is 17 to 23 November 2026 unless otherwise specified.
Israeli forces' operations, settler violence, demolitions and movement restrictions continued to drive displacement and undermine Palestinian livelihoods and access to essential services across the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. In herding and farming communities, particularly in Masafer Yatta and the Jordan Valley, the combined impact of violence, access restrictions and the destruction of essential infrastructure is increasingly undermining communities' ability to remain on their land.
During the reporting period, an Israeli settlement guard shot and killed a Palestinian man near Dura al Qar' (Ramallah). According to the Israeli military, a settlement security coordinator opened fire when two Palestinian men approached Beit El settlement, killing one, while the other was arrested. The Israeli military stated that the incident was under investigation.
Overall, 47 Palestinians, including six children, were injured during the reporting period, of whom eight were injured by Israeli settlers and 39 by Israeli forces. Twelve Palestinians (26 per cent) were injured in the context of settler attacks, including three in incidents related to the olive harvest. Operations by Israeli forces displaced 11 Palestinians, while demolitions displaced another 67.
The cumulative impact of violence, demolitions, displacement and movement restrictions is also taking a toll on Palestinians' mental health and psychosocial well-being. According to a report published by Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) on 19 November, exposure to violence accounted for approximately 70 per cent of recorded reasons for seeking mental health care from MSF teams in the West Bank between October 2022 and April 2026. The report also found that anxiety and depression-related symptoms accounted for about two thirds of reported mental health conditions. These needs are compounded by restrictions on access to health services and humanitarian assistance.

Since the beginning of October, intensified movement restrictions imposed by the Israeli authorities, alongside recurrent settler attacks, have increasingly undermined access to essential services, livelihoods and humanitarian assistance in Masafer Yatta, in southern Hebron governorate. Restrictions on vehicle access to the area designated by the Israeli authorities as “Firing Zone 918” have severely disrupted deliveries of food, water and animal fodder, as well as access to health and education services. These arrangements are insufficient to accommodate daily schooling, emergency medical needs and regular deliveries of essential supplies, including water.
According to the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) Cluster, water trucking operations serving more than 1,500 Palestinians across 10 of the 12 remaining communities within the Israeli-designated firing zone in Masafer Yatta remain severely constrained. The programme was initiated in late April following settler attacks in January and February 2026 that severed the communities’ water supply network, which partners remain unable to restore. The programme was recently expanded to cover three additional communities outside the firing zone. Following limited deliveries in November, Israeli settlers reportedly seized a water truck and physically assaulted its driver on 14 November near Isfai al Fouqa. Although residents and the village council subsequently retrieved the truck, deliveries were further disrupted. So far in November, limited quantities of water have reached only three communities inside the firing zone and two outside it.
Between 1 October and 23 November, Israeli forces confiscated at least 18 Palestinian-owned vehicles, including agricultural tractors, private vehicles and a vehicle belonging to a local organization. According to the village council, Israeli authorities had verbally communicated through the Palestinian District Coordination and Liaison Office that vehicles operating within the designated area, except those belonging to the Palestinian Ministry of Education, would be subject to confiscation. The closure of a road gate near Carmel settlement on 23 October has further restricted movement along key routes serving at least 5,800 Palestinians south of Yatta. The restrictions also coincide with the lambing season, a critical period for herding communities, with disruptions to fodder supplies threatening livestock health and productivity and the sustainability of household livelihoods.
The area was designated by the Israeli authorities as a firing zone in the 1980s. On 17 June 2025, the Israeli military issued a notice that the area would be used for military training. In conjunction with this announcement, the Israeli Civil Administration issued a planning directive allowing the retroactive dismissal of Palestinian planning applications, further heightening the risk of mass demolitions and displacement. Since 2009, Israeli authorities have demolished 422 Palestinian-owned structures in the firing zone area of Masafer Yatta, including 240 (57 per cent) that had been provided as humanitarian assistance. Nearly a quarter of these structures (97) were demolished in 2026 alone, displacing 118 Palestinians, including 58 children, and otherwise affecting 136 people. Agricultural, livelihood and water and sanitation structures accounted for approximately 60 per cent of all structures demolished this year. Schools, health clinics and other essential civilian infrastructure remain at risk of demolition.
Since January 2023, more than 160 settler attacks resulting in casualties or property damage have been documented in communities in Masafer Yatta located within the Israeli-designated firing zone, including 41 so far in 2026. Attacks have repeatedly targeted homes, water and electricity infrastructure, livestock, agricultural assets and vehicles used to transport essential supplies and students. In some cases, settlers have subsequently attacked homes belonging to families already displaced, further undermining prospects for return. Since the beginning of 2026, 50 Palestinians, including 29 children, have been displaced due to settler violence and related access restrictions. The combined impact of recurrent demolitions, sustained settler violence, intensified movement restrictions and the erosion of livelihoods is heightening the risk of further displacement.
Protection partners continue to provide legal assistance, including follow-up on vehicle confiscations and access restrictions, alongside psychosocial support, specialized child protection services, gender-based violence prevention and mitigation interventions, cash for protection and protective presence where access allows. Given restrictions on vehicle access, partners are also supporting community-based protection initiatives to identify urgent needs and facilitate referrals, documenting incidents and their humanitarian impact, and raising urgent protection concerns through existing coordination mechanisms.

--- Report 4: UNRWA (2026-11-25) ---
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

As reported by OCHA, recent rains affected more than 220,000 people and damaged over 40,000 shelters, 960 latrines, nine health facilities, and a number of temporary learning spaces across the Gaza Strip. While some UNRWA emergency shelters were temporarily used by some newly displaced families, safe alternative shelter remains extremely limited across Gaza, leaving many affected families with nowhere suitable to relocate. Inter-agency estimates assessed that over 5,000 people were reportedly sleeping in the open after the first rainfall.
The scale of needs continues to exceed the response capacity. Humanitarian partners, including UNRWA, are undertaking drainage, debris clearance and identifying alternative shelter for households in flood-prone sites and unsafe buildings. The response remains constrained by severe shortages of heavy machinery, engine oil and spare parts, pumps, and other essential supplies.
In partnership with other humanitarian agencies, UNRWA’s teams on the ground are distributing 40,000 winter clothing kits across Gaza, while preparing the distribution of approximately 10,000 tarpaulins and 150 tents to families with the most urgent shelter needs.
Israeli forces have continued to reinforce positions along the so-called Yellow Line of Israeli control in the Gaza Strip, with increased tank movements and shooting reported across multiple areas. The resulting insecurity has led to temporary suspensions of UNRWA humanitarian and programme activities, particularly in Khan Younis, Bureij, and Maghazi.
Stray-bullet fire remains a growing protection concern, affecting displaced persons, humanitarian facilities, and operations well beyond areas adjacent to the Yellow Line. During October alone, UNRWA recorded one person killed and 14 injured inside its emergency shelters. The pattern continued in November, with a five-year-old child injured at an UNRWA shelter in Khan Younis during the reporting period.
On 7 November, a new health centre was established within the Gaza Training Centre to enhance the delivery of essential healthcare services to the population of Gaza City, estimated at more than 670,000 people. Over the reporting period, UNRWA also opened a new temporary learning space at the Rawafid displacement site in northern Rafah, with more than 1,000 children attending in-person education during its first week.

Key Points
The Gaza Strip
Fatalities and injuries

Between 7 October 2023 and 15 November 2026, according to the Ministry of Health (MoH), as reported by the Health Cluster, 74,702 Palestinians were killed in the Gaza Strip and another 176,105 were injured. This includes 1,650 fatalities and 6,144 injuries between the announcement of a ceasefire agreement on 10 October 2025 and 15 November 2026.
UNRWA has recorded 395 colleagues killed in the Gaza Strip since the start of the war (311 UNRWA personnel and 84 persons who were supporting UNRWA[2]), as of 11 November.

Health crisis

Health facilities across Gaza reported a sharp rise in probable meningitis cases since August, with suspected viral cases 2.6 times and bacterial cases 4.1 times higher than the same period in 2025. In response, UNRWA’s health teams are strengthening surveillance and case detection, verifying vaccination status, reinforcing referral arrangements and infection prevention measures, and coordinating closely with the Ministry of Health and health partners. No outbreak has been declared, also pending further analysis.

WASH needs

According to the WASH Cluster’s Light Touch Monitoring Survey, completed in October, 85 per cent of the population of the Gaza Strip are experiencing frequent, unpredictable disruptions in access to safe, clean, and reliable water for daily drinking, cooking, and hygiene. Water insecurity thus remains a significant concern.
Access to basic hygiene supplies also remained severely constrained, with 76 per cent of households reporting insufficient access to essential hygiene items.

Displacement, site management

UNRWA continues to monitor the movement of displaced persons and displacement sites. A total of 113 UNRWA collective emergency shelters host approximately 60,000 internally displaced persons.

Shelter needs

According to the Shelter Cluster’s latest Fact Sheet of October 2026, an estimated 1.98 million people, representing 94 per cent of the population of the Gaza Strip, require shelter and non-food item assistance.
