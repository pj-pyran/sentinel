Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: UNRWA (2026-08-05) ---
All information updated for 15 - 28 July 2026 [1]
Days 1011 - 1024 since the start of the war in the Gaza Strip
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

UNRWA summer learning activities started on 18 July, with 39,509 children (21,666 girls, 17,843 boys, including 875 children with disabilities) participating between 20 and 26 July. Summer activities also started at the UNRWA Rehabilitation Centre for the Visually Impaired (RCVI).
On 21 July, UNRWA resumed operations at Beach Camp Health Centre in western Gaza City, which had been closed since sustaining damage during the September-October 2025 military escalation. On 29 July, UNRWA also opened the Bahrain Medical Point, in southern Gaza City. Together, the two facilities are expected to serve a large catchment area (almost 100,000 people), expanding access to essential primary healthcare services across densely populated areas of Gaza City.
The operating environment in Gaza continues to deteriorate, with a significant escalation in hostilities observed in the humanitarian area – also known as the “green zone” – west of the so-called “yellow line” of control designated by Israeli forces. There have also been additional advances of the yellow line across multiple areas, including in eastern Middle Area, Gaza City and Khan Younis. Multiple evacuation orders and shelter-in place orders were issued over the past week with several humanitarian infrastructure affected (including more than ten UNRWA premises).
Restrictions on the entry of lubricant oil and spare parts continue to pose a critical challenge for humanitarian operation, More than half of UNRWA’s fleet is no longer operational due to the shortage of engine oil and spare parts. In addition, 62 water wells have been affected by fuel and lubricant shortages, leading to a reduction of 42 percent in UNRWA’s production and distribution of clean water.

The occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem

On 27 July, the UNRWA Kalandia Training Centre in East Jerusalem was subject to an unauthorised entry by Israeli forces and Jerusalem Municipality officials, during which they inspected the premises while educational activities were ongoing. As a United Nations property, the Kalandia Training Centre is protected under international law.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-08-03) ---
Highlights

In Gaza, food insecurity has eased, but needs remain high, and declining aid coverage since February 2026 risks reversing gains, according to the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification analysis.
Gender-based violence is on the rise, with a 27 per cent increase in reported cases between January-March to April-June 2026, according to UNFPA.
Shortages of insulin, cardiac catheterization materials, hemodialysis supplies and critical diagnostic and monitoring equipment hinder the treatment of non-communicable diseases in Gaza.
In the West Bank, eight Palestinians, including one child, and two members of Israeli forces were killed in Nablus, Jenin and East Jerusalem; July was jointly with March 2026 the deadliest month for Palestinians in incidents involving settlers since October 2023.
In just one week, Israeli settler attacks displaced at least 20 Palestinian families and caused damage to homes, vehicles and livelihood assets; over 40 settler attacks resulted in casualties or property damage.
Israeli forces and officials entered the United Nations Kalandia Training Centre in East Jerusalem for the second time in less than a month, disrupting educational activities and breaching the inviolability of UN premises.

Overview
Heightened violence and Israeli military operations continue to drive civilian casualties, destruction of property, and displacement across the Occupied Palestinian Territory (OPT). Coupled with severe and tightening access restrictions, these practices are further hindering people’s ability to safely reach workplaces and essential services, while humanitarian needs remain acute. In the Gaza Strip, airstrikes, shelling and other attacks over the past week have reportedly resulted in civilian casualties, including young children. n the West Bank, a series of deadly incidents and an intensifying pattern of settler violence brought July to match March 2026 as the deadliest month for Palestinians in settler attacks since October 2023. Demolitions, settler violence and related access restrictions have displaced an average of 17 people, including eight children, per day in 2026 – double the daily average recorded over the preceding three years.
Gaza Strip
This section covers 20 to 26 July unless otherwise specified.
The humanitarian situation in Gaza remains dire. Ongoing military operations near the so-called “Yellow Line,” coupled with strikes reported in other locations of the Strip, are increasingly disrupting civilian life, damaging shelters, driving renewed displacement and restricting access to life-saving services. At the same time, 67 per cent of the population continues to face high levels of acute food insecurity, and the fragile gains made since the October 2025 ceasefire announcement risk being rapidly reversed as aid coverage declines amid growing funding constraints.
Shortages of essential medical supplies continue to hamper non-communicable disease services, while infectious diseases remain widespread due to overcrowding in displacement sites and poor water, hygiene and sanitation conditions. Against this backdrop, protection risks are on the rise, with women, children and other vulnerable groups disproportionately affected; a marked increase has been noticed in reported gender-based violence. All the while, shortages of basic supplies such as cooking gas and solar lanterns further undermine people’s ability to meet even their most basic needs.
According to the Ministry of Health (MoH) in Gaza, which operates under the de facto authorities, 30 Palestinians were reported killed, including those who died of wounds and had their bodies retrieved from under the rubble, and 134 people were injured between 22 and 29 July. This brings the overall casualty toll reported by MoH since the announcement of a ceasefire agreement on 10 October 2025 to 1,209 fatalities and 3,943 injuries.
Civilians continue to face significant security risks across Gaza, with incidents during the reporting period affecting both communities and service delivery points. In areas of North Gaza governorate, including Beit Lahia and Jabalya, ongoing Israeli military operations continued to severely disrupt people’s lives. Military advances and withdrawals occurred on multiple occasions throughout the week, while nearby strikes and shelling damaged shelters and forced affected households to relocate temporarily within and around displacement sites.
On 25 July, 13 families were temporarily displaced from Al Atatra site in Beit Lahia while in Jabalya, 25 families at Misr 16 site relocated internally following damage to their shelters. At Rahmaa Baynahum site in central Jabalya Camp, approximately 137 families temporarily sought refuge with relatives and friends or in open areas. Additional incidents at Zaad Al Khair site and Sumud site in Gaza Port resulted in the temporary displacement of nine families within the sites. Across all locations, affected households generally returned to their displacement sites within two to three days after humanitarian partners provided them with replacement tents and shelter materials.
Throughout the Gaza Strip, children remain exposed to severe and compounding protection risks, including hostilities, stray gunfire, explosive remnants of war, unsafe shelter, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions, disease outbreaks, family separation, school dropout, child labour, early marriage, neglect, violence and heightened risks of gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse for adolescent girls. Unsafe site conditions – including damaged shelters, poor lighting, open pits, sewage leakage, overcrowding and water shortages – continue to restrict safe access to services and increase protection risks.
Recent discussions that Site Management partners led with children in 24 displacement sites hosting more than 11,300 families (over 54,600 people) highlighted the impact of poor lighting on children. Participants said it undermines their safety when accessing sanitation facilities at night, limits their ability to study, and contributes to broader protection concerns within crowded displacement environments.
Between 19 and 25 July, according to the Health Cluster, acute respiratory infections remained the leading reported condition, amounting to 43.2 per cent of all communicable diseases, followed by acute watery diarrhea (22 per cent), underscoring persistent risks associated with overcrowding and inadequate WASH conditions. Skin diseases continued to represent a significant burden, with ectoparasitic infestations accounting for 18.9 per cent of cases, impetigo showing an upward trend (10.8 per cent), and chickenpox continuing to decline (4.9 per cent).
Meanwhile the humanitarian community’s ability to respond remains constrained by Israeli access restrictions into and within Gaza as well as funding shortfalls.
Attempts by the de facto authorities to inspect humanitarian warehouses for alleged smuggled items continue but at a slower pace compared with previous periods. Between 6 and 26 July, the UN recorded eight cases where individuals identifying themselves as agents of the de facto authorities searched humanitarian warehouses, causing disruptions to humanitarian operations and breaching the inviolability of United Nations premises. No alleged contraband was found.
Fragile Food Security Gains
On 23 July, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) released the findings of its latest Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis for the Gaza Strip. The analysis indicates that the scale-up of humanitarian assistance, enabled by improved access and reduced conflict intensity following the October 2025 ceasefire announcement, has driven improvements in food security and nutrition conditions across Gaza. However, needs remain high and recent gains are highly fragile.
Between mid-April and 30 June 2026, over 1.2 million people (59 per cent of the population) continued to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse levels of food insecurity, including 212,000 people in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and none in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5).
Looking ahead, the IPC projects a renewed deterioration in food security conditions between July and December 2026, with the number of people facing Crisis or worse expected to rise to 1.4 million (67 per cent of the population). The analysis noted that aid coverage has already declined since February 2026, and further reductions are expected due to funding constraints, and uncertainty regarding the renewal of operational authorizations for 37 international non-governmental organizations. According to IPC, coupled with continuing population displacement in an increasingly confined area of Gaza as militarized zones expand, dire health, water, hygiene and sanitation conditions, and volatile access for both humanitarian and commercial supplies, all these concurrent factors could rapidly trigger a sharp deterioration in food security and nutrition conditions, as livelihoods have collapsed and people have exhausted their coping mechanisms.
On the nutrition front, while acute malnutrition – particularly among children – has declined significantly, driven by a marked scale-up of nutrition programmes, the IPC expects an estimated 74,200 children aged 6-59 months and 24,600 pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW) to continue requiring treatment through April 2027.
According to the latest World Food Programme Palestine Market Monitor, food prices in Gaza continued to decrease throughout June and the first half of July, particularly for fresh fruits and vegetables, while wheat flour prices remained stable. Food intake continues to be significantly below the levels recorded before October 2023, especially for protein-rich foods as meat and eggs (consumed the equivalent of only once per week, on average), fruits (consumed the equivalent of half a day per week), and vegetables (consumed the equivalent of twice per week), WFP found.
WFP notes that, although the entry of commercial and humanitarian supplies continued to increase in June and July, compared with the low levels observed in March and April, during the regional escalation, market access slightly worsened in July, with 77 per cent of surveyed households reporting difficulties accessing markets mainly to due lack of cash and unaffordability of basic food items, compared to 67 per cent in May. Cooking gas supplies remain extremely limited, with approximately 73 per cent of households surveyed by WFP reporting that they relied on waste burning as an alternative to cooking gas in July.
Rising Gender-Based Violence
Women and girls in Gaza continue to face escalating and life-threatening risks of gender-based violence (GBV), with devastating consequences for their physical safety and mental wellbeing, including Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder, depression and suicide – according to the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) that leads the GBV response under the Protection Cluster. In April-June 2026, reported GBV incidents increased by 27 per cent compared with January-March 2026.
According to UNFPA, this reflects both worsening protection risks and expanded reporting, attributed to awareness raising sessions provided by protection partners (see below, in the section on response to GBV). The denial of resources, opportunities and services remains the most prevalent form of GBV, while psychological violence, sexual violence and forced marriage are increasing, particularly among adolescent girls, UNFPA says.
Male survivors of conflict-related sexual violence continue to face significant barriers to accessing confidential, survivor-centred services due both to stigma and to the inadequate resources available to GBV partners, which prevent them from recruiting more frontline service providers able to provide specialized support, UNFPA reports.
At the same time, caseworkers specializing in addressing GBV are managing unsustainable caseloads while many face GBV risks themselves within their homes and communities, further straining an already overstretched response. Despite an increase in GBV service delivery points from 74 to 84 in recent weeks, access to life saving services remains critically inadequate, with 17 Women and Girls’ Safe Spaces (WGSSs) still closed due to lack of funding and two others forced to close following movements of the “Yellow Line”, leaving many survivors without safe, confidential support.
Incoming Supplies
According to data collected by the Logistics Cluster, Ashdod remained the primary route for humanitarian cargo entering Gaza during the reporting period, with 308 UN and humanitarian partner trucks offloaded. Through Egypt, the offloading rate stood at 69 per cent, while the remaining 31 per cent of manifested trucks were either rejected and returned by the Israeli authorities or cancelled due to operational issues. In most return and rejection cases, no reason was provided; where specified, reasons included pallet conditions, cargo type and inconsistencies in information submitted. Via Jordan, aid truck volumes returned to normal levels following the resolution of the scanner malfunction at the Sheikh Hussein Bridge-Jordan River. Presently, the King Hussein Bridge-Allenby Crossing operates twice weekly (Mondays and Wednesdays) with a maximum of 60 trucks per convoy, while the Sheikh Hussein Bridge-Jordan River crossing operates Sunday to Thursday with a capacity of 15 trucks per day. More than two months following the closure of the Zikim Crossing in northern Gaza on 24 May, Kerem Shalom remains the only operational crossing point into Gaza.
Items that the Israeli authorities classify as having “dual use” are more difficult to secure approval for and bring in. Even when cleared, offloading items classified as such at Kerem Shalom remains limited to one day per week, creating significant bottlenecks that delay humanitarian assistance, disrupt supply pipelines, and constrain preparedness for the upcoming winter season. (For examples of such items, see the Shelter section below.)
The UN is only able to confirm the entry of supplies tracked by UN 2720. For breakdowns of those, see the online UN 2720 Mechanism Dashboard.

West Bank
This section covers 21 to 27 July unless otherwise specified.
The reporting period was marked by a series of deadly incidents that resulted in the killing of eight Palestinians and two members of Israeli forces, including a settlement security guard. These developments occurred amid an intensifying pattern of settler violence, with July matching March 2026 as the deadliest month for Palestinians in settler attacks since October 2023. Across the West Bank, lack-of-permit demolitions, settler violence and related access restrictions have displaced an average of 17 people, including eight children, per day in 2026 – double the daily average recorded over the preceding three years.
Concerns over access to education were heightened after Israeli forces and officials forcibly entered the United Nations Kalandia Training Centre (KTC) in East Jerusalem for the second time in less than a month, disrupting educational activities. At the time of the unauthorized entry, more than 100 students and staff were attending classes and sheltered in place while the operation was underway. In a statement issued on 28 July, the Humanitarian Country Team stated that the raid &quot;breached the inviolability of United Nations premises&quot; and &quot;impeded joint efforts by UN agencies and NGOs to provide humanitarian assistance and critical services.&quot;
Despite funding and operational constraints, as well as movement restrictions, Education Cluster partners continued to deliver summer learning programmes providing catch-up and remedial classes, recreational activities and psychosocial support to help children prepare for the new school year. Child protection partners also continued to provide critical protection services, reaching 2,320 children with disabilities since the beginning of 2026, as part of a Disability-Inclusion Action Plan focused on advocacy, capacity building, strengthening disability-disaggregated data collection and analysis, and improving the accessibility and inclusiveness of child protection services.
During the fourth week of July, Shelter Cluster partners reached 528 people (119 households) through 23 interventions, primarily minor-to-moderate shelter repairs (73 per cent), alongside the provision of essential non-food items and cash assistance. Assistance focused on communities affected by displacement, demolitions and settler violence in Nablus, Ramallah and Hebron governorates, with these three governorates accounting for about 62 per cent of all people reached. Meanwhile, more than 33,000 Palestine refugees displaced from Jenin, Tulkarm and Nur Shams refugee camps since early 2025 continue to face significant shelter needs.
Casualties and Settler Violence
During the reporting period, four deadly incidents resulted in the killing of eight Palestinians and two members of Israeli forces, including a settlement security guard, in Nablus, Jenin and East Jerusalem. On 23 July, one Israeli settler was stabbed and injured by a Palestinian and two Palestinians were shot and killed by an Israeli settler near Beit Furik following a fire that reportedly triggered confrontations between Palestinians, Israeli settlers and Israeli forces. On 24 July, four Palestinians from the same family and two members of Israeli forces, including a settlement security guard, were killed in Tell village. Palestinian sources reported that Israeli settlers from Havat Gilad settlement entered and attacked the village and Israeli media reported that the group had been hiking in the area and entered Areas A and B without coordination with the Israeli military. During the incident, a Palestinian man seized a rifle from an Israeli settler and reportedly opened fire.
Separately, on 23 July, Israeli forces shot and killed a Palestinian man at Shu'fat Camp checkpoint in East Jerusalem. The Israeli police stated that officers had ordered the man, whom they described as “behaving suspiciously,” to stop, but he did not comply; no Israeli personnel were injured. His body was returned to the family on 25 July. Also on 23 July, Israeli forces shot and killed a Palestinian man after he stabbed and injured an Israeli soldier at a checkpoint east of Jenin city.
On 24 July, a Palestinian man was reportedly attacked, stabbed and injured by Israeli settlers while his vehicle was stranded on Road 449 between Jericho and Ramallah after his vehicle ran out of fuel. According to the affected man, the settlers also seized his vehicle and personal belongings. Due to movement restrictions imposed following the deadly incidents in Nablus governorate, he was initially unable to reach hospital and received first aid in Al 'Auja town before being transferred to Jericho hospital after road access partially reopened.
Since the beginning of 2026, 76 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli forces or settlers or died of wounds sustained earlier, and three Israelis, including the two members of Israeli forces mentioned above, have been killed by Palestinians in the West Bank. More than 1,600 Palestinians have also been injured, including 46 per cent by Israeli settlers and the remainder by Israeli forces, while 24 Israelis, including 20 settlers and two soldiers, have been injured.
These incidents occurred amid an intensifying pattern of settler-related violence affecting Palestinian communities across the West Bank. In Tell village, they followed repeated settler attacks, including an arson attack on 18 July that displaced a Palestinian family.
Following the deadly incident in Tell village, Israeli settlers carried out multiple attacks across Nablus, Qalqiliya, Jenin, Ramallah, Jerusalem, Jericho and Bethlehem governorates. During the reporting period, at least 40 incidents involving Israeli settlers that resulted in Palestinian casualties, property damage or both were documented, about half of which occurred in Ramallah and Nablus governorates. At least 16 Palestinians were injured, including in physical assaults, stabbings, stone-throwing incidents and attacks involving live ammunition. The attacks also resulted in extensive damage to Palestinian property and livelihoods, including the burning or damage of at least 10 homes, more than 20 vehicles, two livelihood caravans, a stone crusher facility, a quarry and associated equipment, guard rooms, offices, agricultural structures and infrastructure. Hundreds of olive trees were bulldozed or otherwise damaged, while Palestinian vehicles travelling between communities were repeatedly attacked, further undermining access to livelihoods and essential services in areas already affected by movement restrictions imposed by Israeli forces.
Since the beginning of 2026, more than 1,380 settler-related incidents have been documented, affecting over 250 Palestinian communities and averaging about 6.6 incidents per day. During the same period, about 900 Palestinians were injured in the context of settler attacks, accounting for more than half of all Palestinian injuries documented across the West Bank, while more than 2,300 Palestinians were displaced due to settler attacks and related access restrictions.
Following the deadly incident in Tell village on 24 July, Israeli forces intensified movement restrictions across Nablus governorate, deploying additional forces, conducting search-and-arrest operations, and carrying out raids in several communities, including Tell village and Nablus city. The main checkpoints surrounding Nablus were closed, multiple road gates were blocked, and additional roadblocks and flying checkpoints were established, severely disrupting access to workplaces, health care, education and other essential services until some restrictions were eased on 26 July.
Attacks on Health Care
Attacks on health care and impediments to medical access continued across the West Bank during the reporting period. While fewer incidents were documented in 2026 than during the same period in 2025, this largely reflects the large-scale operations by Israeli forces launched across the northern West Bank in early 2025, which resulted in a sharp increase in attacks affecting health care. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), 53 attacks on health care were documented between January and June 2026, resulting in one fatality and 23 injuries, and affecting four health facilities and 44 ambulances. Under WHO's Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), these incidents include obstruction of or interference with the delivery of health services. Most incidents involved obstruction of access (50), the use of force (39), detentions (17) and militarized searches (10). Nearly half of all documented incidents occurred in Nablus governorate (26), followed by Ramallah (9), Hebron (5) and Salfit (4), reflecting the concentration of operations by Israeli forces in the northern West Bank over the first half of 2026.
Developments during the reporting period reflected many of these recurring patterns, particularly in Nablus governorate following the deadly incident in Tell village on 24 July. Following the incident, Israeli forces imposed extensive movement restrictions across Nablus governorate, closing checkpoints, road gates and other access points, while deploying additional forces and conducting search-and-arrest operations. The restrictions significantly impeded access to health care and hampered humanitarian operations. Medical teams reported multiple incidents in which ambulances transporting injured patients were delayed while attempting to reach hospitals. According to the Palestinian Ministry of Health, these restrictions also impeded access to emergency obstetric care. Israeli forces raided Nablus Specialized Hospital, where Palestinians injured during the Tell incident were reportedly arrested, disrupting access to care.
Recent developments in Sinjil village further illustrate the humanitarian impact of prolonged movement restrictions on access to emergency health care. Following a two-day search-and-arrest operation by Israeli forces on 15 and 16 July, the village's main northern entrance was closed and the road connecting Sinjil with nearby Jiljilya was blocked with an earth mound. Although residents later partially reopened the latter road, the main entrance remained closed as of the end of the reporting period, forcing residents to rely on lengthy detours or back-to-back ambulance transfers during medical emergencies. On 17 July, a Palestinian woman requiring urgent medical evacuation was delayed at the closed gate despite coordination efforts through the Palestinian District Coordination Office. According to medical sources, she died shortly after arriving at hospital. As of the end of the reporting period, the village's main entrance remained closed, constraining access to emergency health care and other essential services.
Movement restrictions also continued to affect access to specialized health care requiring permits to enter East Jerusalem or Israel. According to WHO, during the first half of 2026, 78 per cent of the 51,444 patient permit applications submitted to Israeli authorities were approved, while more than one fifth were either denied or delayed beyond the scheduled medical appointment. The 2026 approval rate is the highest rate since 2023, when 74 per cent of applications were approved. Adults aged 18 to 60 accounted for 77 per cent of applicants, and Al Makassed Hospital in East Jerusalem received 42 per cent of approved referrals.
Displacement
During the reporting period, Israeli authorities demolished 44 Palestinian-owned structures for lacking Israeli-issued building permits, which are nearly impossible for Palestinians to obtain. The demolished structures included 14 homes, two uninhabited residential structures, 22 WASH and agricultural structures, and six livelihood-related structures. As a result, 16 households comprising 86 people were displaced, including 76 people in Area C and 10 in East Jerusalem.
More than half of those displaced (47 people, including 20 children) occurred on 22 July in Nahhalin (Bethlehem governorate) and on 27 July in the North Beit Hanina Bedouin community (Jerusalem governorate), where the Israeli Civil Administration, accompanied by Israeli forces, demolished seven residential structures and associated livelihood assets. In both incidents, families were unable to remove many of their personal belongings before the demolitions.
Displacement also continued as a result of recurrent settler attacks, intimidation and the expansion of settlement outposts. In Jalud village, south of Nablus governorate, two Palestinian households comprising seven people, including three girls, were displaced after months of escalating settler intimidation, threats and isolation. The displacement followed the establishment of an Israeli settler outpost near their homes in April and the takeover of a nearby Palestinian house by Israeli settlers on 29 June. Since then, the families had faced repeated intimidation, damage to water and electricity infrastructure, and restricted access to the village centre and essential services. On the day of the displacement, settlers reportedly blocked humanitarian attempts to deliver food and medicine to the families, who left behind their homes and most of their household belongings.
Ten Palestinian Bedouin households were displaced on 23 July from Area B of Al Mazra'a ash Sharqiya (Ramallah governorate) for a second time. The families had previously been displaced from the fully depopulated Bedouin communities of Al Mu'arrajat Centre and Ras at Tin due to Israeli settler attacks and related access restrictions. Their latest displacement followed the establishment of a new settler outpost on 22 July, after months of recurrent settler intimidation and threats, including night-time incursions, prompting the families to relocate to other areas in Ramallah and Hebron governorates.
Between 23 and 24 July, at least 10 Palestinian families comprising 49 people were displaced in three settler-related incidents across Nablus governorate. Four families were displaced in Sarra after Israeli settlers set fire to at least six homes, while five additional families in Urif were temporarily displaced after four homes sustained fire damage. A Palestinian family of four was also displaced from the Al Mas'udiya area following years of settler harassment, intimidation, and restrictions on grazing and access to water. Following the violence in Tell village on 24 July, Israeli settlers reportedly established several caravans and began land-levelling works, raising concerns about the establishment of a new settlement outpost and increased pressure on nearby Palestinian communities.
These incidents reflect a broader pattern of recurrent displacement driven by demolitions, settler violence and the expansion of settlement outposts. Since January 2023, more than 6,200 Palestinians, including over 3,000 children, have been displaced, including more than 2,300 people since the beginning of 2026.

--- Report 3: ETC (2026-08-03) ---
Operation overview
Armed conflict between Palestinian militant groups and Israeli military forces began on 07 October 2023. The conflict has resulted in a severe humanitarian crisis and significant disruption to communications networks, impeding humanitarian operations. The WFP-led Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC Telecoms) was activated on 31 October 2023 to meet the urgent need to support access to reliable shared communications services for humanitarian organizations operating in Gaza.

--- Report 4: ETC (2026-08-03) ---
The WFP-led Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) was activated as the Emergency Telecommunications Cluster (ETC) on 31 October 2023 in response to the crisis in Gaza. This Situation Report provides a monthly update on LTC telecoms activities. All references to the LTC in this report relate specifically to the telecommunications area of the cluster1.
Summary Points
• LTC Telecoms continued coordinating with humanitarian, UN, and private-sector partners to address telecommunications equipment access constraints and support critical communications services for humanitarian operations in Gaza.
• The inter-agency Security Communications System (SCS) was further strengthened through progress on the Unified Radio Configuration Project, Very High Frequency (VHF) radio standardization, and security communications training.
• Telecommunications services remained severely constrained by infrastructure damage and restrictions on the entry of ICT equipment, spare parts, and consumables, limiting network expansion and affecting service continuity across Gaza.
