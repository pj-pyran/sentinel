Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: DFS (2026-11-19) ---
Executive Summary
The Occupied Palestinian Territories face a catastrophic humanitarian crisis marked by severe food insecurity, mass displacement, and the near-collapse of essential services. In Gaza, the majority of the population relies entirely on humanitarian assistance, with widespread destruction of housing, health facilities, water infrastructure, and educational institutions compounding the challenges faced by displaced families. Harsh winter conditions, repeated evacuation orders, and constrained humanitarian access continue to threaten the safety and well-being of civilians, with children, pregnant women, and other vulnerable groups bearing a disproportionate burden.
In the West Bank, escalating military operations, settler violence, and movement restrictions have driven significant displacement and disrupted access to livelihoods, healthcare, and education. Economic contraction and rising unemployment have eroded household resilience, while demolitions and settlement expansion threaten further territorial fragmentation. Across both territories, humanitarian organizations are working to scale up food assistance, shelter support, health services, and protection programming, yet persistent access barriers, funding shortfalls, and ongoing hostilities continue to outpace the response, leaving Palestinians in urgent need of sustained international support and durable solutions.

--- Report 5: Protection Cluster (2026-11-19) ---
INTRODUCTION
Findings from August–September 2026 highlight the combined impact of settler violence, movement restrictions, demolitions, and loss of land and property on the safety, livelihoods, and wellbeing of communities across the West Bank. The highest protection risk severity scores were recorded for attacks on civilians and civilian objects, including unlawful killings (58%); theft, extortion, forced eviction or destruction of personal property (58%); and restrictions on freedom of movement, siege and forced displacement (57%). These findings reflect continued threats to civilian safety, housing security, and access to essential services.
Qualitative findings from five Focus Group Discussions across Hebron, Ramallah, Jericho, and Jerusalem governorates highlight settler violence and lack of accountability as central concerns among participating communities. Some groups reported increasing attacks, while others described levels similar to the previous period. Communities reported physical harm, displacement, disrupted education, and persistent psychological distress, with children exposed to unsafe school journeys and young men, including community protection volunteers, facing heightened risks of assault and detention. Women and girls, older persons, and persons with disabilities face additional barriers to safe movement, access to support, and participation in community protection activities. Protection Committees and local networks continue to provide early warning, emergency support, documentation, and legal referrals, although limited resources and threats to responders constrain their efforts.
The Protection Monitoring System (PMS), a joint initiative led by the OPT Protection Cluster, systematically collects, analyzes, and disseminates information on protection risks through Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), ensuring that community perspectives remain central to the analysis. Building on monitoring undertaken since November 2025, this snapshot presents protection risk severity scores across monitored locations and highlights community-reported concerns, impacts, coping mechanisms, and capacities to inform coordinated protection response and advocacy.

--- Report 6: Insecurity Insight (2026-11-17) ---
Data collected by Insecurity Insight shows that over 3,500 attacks on health care took place across the State of Palestine between 07 October 2023 and 15 October 2026. 95% of all incidents were attributed to Israeli forces. In Gaza, where 2,413 attacks on health care were recorded, health facilities were damaged 459 times, 785 health workers were killed and 399 arrested. In the West Bank, where 1,046 attacks on health care were recorded, health facilities were damaged 33 times, 13 health workers were killed and 203 arrested while ambulances and health workers were prevented from providing vital medical care to people in need over over 630 times.
In Gaza, at least 37 health facilities were damaged multiple times in IDF aircraft and drone strikes and violent raids. Kamal Adwan Hospital was reportedly damaged on 46 occasions, including once since the October 2025 ceasefire. Three other hospitals that sustained repeated damage - Ahli Arab, Al-Aqsa Martyrs and Al-Awda hospitals - were also reportedly damaged since the ceasefire. These incidents highlight the repeated damage sustained by health facilities across Gaza, disrupting the provision of health care and putting patients' access to treatment at risk.
