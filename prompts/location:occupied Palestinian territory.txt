Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: UNRWA (2026-11-11) ---
Highlights
The Gaza Strip

Over the reporting period, UNRWA recorded several significant armed incidents affecting its personnel and displaced people inside UNRWA schools-turned-shelters. At least six displaced people, including an UNRWA student and an UNRWA staff member, were injured, and at least one displaced person was killed inside the yard of a school-turned-shelter. An UNRWA Job Creation Programme worker was also reportedly killed due to an airstrike by Israeli forces in an open area.
Security threats continued to affect UNRWA operations. Over the reporting period, Israeli forces issued shelter-in-place instructions affecting five UNRWA installations in Nuseirat, followed by a reported strike in their vicinity. Separately, militia activity in eastern Maghazi and southern Rafah led to the temporary suspension of activities at Maghazi Health Centre and UNRWA temporary learning spaces in the area.
UNRWA’s stock of lubricant oil has been completely depleted for several weeks, leading to progressive deterioration of equipment and prolonged suspension of operations. Generators at several UNRWA facilities have been out of service for more than two months, and staff transportation is also constrained by the lack of lubricant oil.
Despite such challenges, UNRWA continues efforts to restore and expand essential services. Additional classrooms at the Rehabilitation Centre for the Visually Impaired in Gaza City are being established to increase education capacity. Rehabilitation work is also ongoing to establish a health facility at the Gaza Training Centre to enhance and consolidate health service delivery in Northern Gaza.
UNRWA’s latest site sweep assessed winterisation needs across 113 displacement sites and identified 18 Designated Emergency Shelters as unsafe, including four at very high risk of collapse, six at high risk, and eight at medium risk. UNRWA joined inter-agency efforts to identify alternative shelter options and is working with humanitarian partners to secure tents for the voluntary relocation of affected families. Priority is being given to 270 households currently living in sections of four premises assessed as being at very high risk of collapse.
Since 25 October, a protest by former UNRWA staff at the Khan Younis Training Centre (KYTC), a key UNRWA logistics and operational hub, has disrupted UN humanitarian operations. Access to vehicles, warehouses, and other facilities has been restricted, affecting the movement and distribution of humanitarian supplies, staff transport, and other activities.

1. Key points
The Gaza Strip
Fatalities and Injuries

Between 7 October 2023 and 30 October 2026, according to the Ministry of Health, as reported by the Health Cluster, 74,594 Palestinians were killed in the Gaza Strip and another 175,834 were injured. This includes 1,580 fatalities and 5,873 injuries between the announcement of a ceasefire agreement on 10 October 2025 and 30 October 2026.
UNRWA has recorded 395 colleagues killed in the Gaza Strip since the start of the war (311 UNRWA personnel and 84 persons who were supporting UNRWA[2] ), as of 4 November. Health crisis • Acute respiratory infections increased ahead of the winter season, with a further increase expected in the coming weeks.

Wash, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs

According to the WASH Cluster’s Light Touch Monitoring Survey, completed in September, 84 per cent of the population of the Gaza Strip are experiencing frequent, unpredictable disruptions in access to safe, clean, and reliable water for daily drinking, cooking, and hygiene. Water insecurity thus remains a significant concern, despite signs of improvement - 91 per cent of the population reported water insecurity in the previous round.
Access to basic hygiene supplies also remained severely constrained, with 89 per cent of households reporting insufficient access to essential hygiene items.

Displacement, site management

UNRWA continues to monitor the movement of displaced persons and displacement sites. A total of 113 UNRWA collective emergency shelters host approximately 60,000 internally displaced persons.

Shelter needs

According to the Shelter Cluster’s classification, based on surveys conducted between 14 and 28 June, an estimated 1.98 million people, representing 94 per cent of the population of the Gaza Strip, require shelter and non-food item assistance (NFI). The assessment also suggests that 58 per cent of the population is facing critical or catastrophic shelter needs, meaning severe deficiencies in shelter conditions or lack of access to essential services and NFIs that expose them to serious health, safety, and dignity risks.

Operational implications and humanitarian response

Around 10,500 Palestinian UNRWA personnel continue to provide services and assistance to people in the Gaza Strip. In the occupied West Bank, UNRWA also plays a central role with around 4,500 of the Agency’s Palestinian personnel providing education, healthcare, and other services to Palestine Refugees.
All UNRWA international staff are prevented from entering the Occupied Palestinian Territory - the Gaza Strip and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. This follows the implementation of laws passed by the Israeli parliament on 28 October 2024[3] that purport to prohibit UNRWA’s operations in areas that Israel considers its sovereign territory, including occupied East Jerusalem, and seek to bar any contact by Israeli officials with UNRWA. The Israeli authorities have not granted the Agency’s international staff visas or permits to enter the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including Gaza, since the end of January 2025.
Since March 2025, the Israeli authorities have been blocking UNRWA from directly bringing humanitarian personnel and aid into the Gaza Strip. Despite the huge needs on the ground, UNRWA was therefore unable to distribute the aid it had pre-positioned outside Gaza, which included enough food parcels, flour, and shelter supplies for hundreds of thousands of people.

The occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem

According to OCHA, between 7 October 2023 and 23 October 2026, 1,145 Palestinians – at least 250 of them children – were killed in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Ninetyeight of them have been killed since the beginning of 2026.
Following a fatal shooting attack on Israeli soldiers on 23 October, Israeli forces imposed severe security conditions on the city of Hebron in the southern West Bank. More than 2,000 Palestinians were reportedly forcibly displaced from their homes as part of the large-scale operation, which also included extensive demolitions of houses and private property. The Director of UNRWA Affairs in the West Bank released a statement expressing grave concern at the situation. On the evening of 1 November, Israeli forces concluded the operation.
The annual olive harvest in the occupied West Bank continued despite persistent settler violence and harassment targeting participating Palestinians, as well as movement obstacles imposed by Israeli forces preventing Palestinians from accessing their agricultural land. October marked the worst month to date in 2026 for settler violence and harassment, in a year which has already surpassed preceding years for Palestinian casualties associated with settler violence.
Heightened security restrictions were in place in the occupied West Bank in the lead-up to the Israeli parliamentary elections on 27 October, with checkpoint closures and movement restrictions tightened.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-11-09) ---
Key Highlights

Heavy rains hit the Gaza Strip from the evening of 6 November 2026 through the weekend, causing widespread flooding, the collapse of damaged buildings, damage to shelters, humanitarian facilities and learning spaces, and disruptions to essential services. An infant reportedly drowned and several people were reportedly killed when a sand dune collapsed on their tents.
Humanitarian partners mobilized immediately to assess people’s needs, distribute emergency supplies and otherwise support those affected. Nevertheless, humanitarian stocks of tents, tarpaulins, blankets and clothes inside Gaza are virtually exhausted.
By 8 November, humanitarian partners received appeals from over 26,000 households whose shelters or other belongings were damaged, with the overall impact believed to be greater. Partners have assessed the needs of 1,877 households, and provided support to 422 households, including with non-food items, at 29 displacement sites.
Over 150,000 tents purchased by humanitarian partners and Member States are outside Gaza ready to be dispatched. This includes about 117,000 tents from the Egyptian Relief Committee alone in Al-Arish.
Humanitarian funding and Israeli approvals are urgently needed so that enough supplies and machinery can be brought into Gaza, including vacuum trucks, mobile dewatering pumps, engine oil, hoses and heavy machinery.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-11-09) ---
Key Highlights

As hundreds of Palestinians begin returning to their homes in two neighbourhoods of Hebron city following a 10-day operation by Israeli forces, humanitarian organizations are supporting local authorities in assessing damage and addressing outstanding needs.
Settler violence and access restrictions continue to disrupt the olive harvest, with 377 olive-harvest-related settler attacks recorded since the start of the season in September, already exceeding the total recorded during any previous harvest season; farmers continue to face physical assaults and theft of harvested olives and equipment.
Heavy rainfall and flash floods damaged shelters, belongings and livestock fodder of 37 Palestinian households across seven Bedouin communities in the northern Jordan Valley.
In the Gaza Strip, strikes continue to cause casualties in populated areas and affect humanitarian operations; attacks have caused reported civilian casualties and hit the vicinities of humanitarian convoys and facilities.
The Food Security Sector warns that the daily production and free distribution of meals could reduce by approximately 45 per cent if the current Israeli suspension of the entry of humanitarian and commercial supplies from Egypt continues.
As winter approaches, Israeli restrictions on the entry of engine oil and spare parts threaten essential water, sanitation and health services. Shelter conditions remain abysmal, with families’ needs far exceeding the limited resources available to the Shelter Cluster.
Maternal and newborn health services are overstretched, with neonatal unit occupancy reaching 151 per cent and special-care baby unit occupancy 199 per cent in September.

--- Report 4: CCCM Cluster (2026-11-09) ---
Overnight rainfall between 06 and 07 November triggered widespread flooding and rain-related incidents across displacement sites in Gaza, affecting thousands of displaced families living in makeshift shelters. As of 09:00 on 08 November, partners had submitted 268 site alerts, of which 264 relate to heavy rain and flooding incidents. Reports were received from 261 unique displacement sites, including 255 sites with Site Management Agency (SMA) support and 6 sites without SMA presence.
Site Management Cluster partners reported incidents and urgent needs through the Zite Manager platform, provided by the International Organization for Migration (IOM) and only captures alerts submitted by participating partners. Reported figures reflect only incidents recorded by partners operating displacement sites and do not capture all rain and flood related impacts across Gaza. The overall scale of damage and humanitarian needs is therefore likely significantly higher than reflected in this update. Based on a joint IOM and Cluster flood-risk assessment and analysis, an estimated 761,000 people (138,000 families) in Gaza displacement sites are likely to be impacted by flooding this winter.
Only 40 per cent of the nearly 1,700 active sites have an SMA presence through the Cluster. Site Management Cluster partners are mobilizing to support site improvement works and community-led flood mitigation activities, such as drainage improvements and slope stabilization, to enable safer, more dignified living conditions. Site Management partners continue to monitor conditions and update reported needs as they prepare a rapid response
