Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: Insecurity Insight (2026-11-17) ---
Data collected by Insecurity Insight shows that over 3,500 attacks on health care took place across the State of Palestine between 07 October 2023 and 15 October 2026. 95% of all incidents were attributed to Israeli forces. In Gaza, where 2,413 attacks on health care were recorded, health facilities were damaged 459 times, 785 health workers were killed and 399 arrested. In the West Bank, where 1,046 attacks on health care were recorded, health facilities were damaged 33 times, 13 health workers were killed and 203 arrested while ambulances and health workers were prevented from providing vital medical care to people in need over over 630 times.
In Gaza, at least 37 health facilities were damaged multiple times in IDF aircraft and drone strikes and violent raids. Kamal Adwan Hospital was reportedly damaged on 46 occasions, including once since the October 2025 ceasefire. Three other hospitals that sustained repeated damage - Ahli Arab, Al-Aqsa Martyrs and Al-Awda hospitals - were also reportedly damaged since the ceasefire. These incidents highlight the repeated damage sustained by health facilities across Gaza, disrupting the provision of health care and putting patients' access to treatment at risk.

--- Report 2: Protection Cluster (2026-11-16) ---
INTRODUCTION
Findings from the current reporting period highlight the cumulative impact of insecurity, deteriorating shelter conditions, and barriers to essential services on displaced populations across Gaza. The highest protection risk severity scores were recorded for attacks on civilians and civilian objects, including unlawful killings (82%), psychological and emotional abuse or inflicted distress (75%), and restrictions on freedom of movement, siege and forced displacement (74%). As winter approaches, communities reported growing fears of flooding and exposure to cold and rain, with damaged tents, overcrowding, and inadequate drainage undermining safety, privacy, and dignity. Damaged buildings, rubble, and suspected explosive remnants further threaten civilian safety, particularly for children moving or playing in hazardous surroundings.
Economic hardship and insufficient, irregular assistance are limiting households’ ability to afford food, medicine, transportation, and shelter materials. Older persons and persons with disabilities face additional barriers due to inaccessible facilities, unsuitable pathways, and shortages of assistive devices, increasing their dependence on caregivers. Families reported borrowing money and selling belongings or assistance items to meet immediate needs, while adults and, in some cases, children assume additional responsibilities to secure water and food. Community committees, volunteers, and informal support networks continue to facilitate assistance and resolve disputes, but their limited resources cannot meet the scale of needs.
The Protection Monitoring System (PMS) provides timely and systematic evidence through Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), combining quantitative severity scores with community perspectives. This snapshot presents findings for October 2026, examines protection risk trends since monitoring began in August 2024, and highlights community-reported concerns, impacts, coping mechanisms, and capacities to inform protection analysis and response planning.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-11-16) ---
Key Highlights

Flooding in Gaza has compounded an already dire situation, with the Site Management Cluster reporting about 46,000 households affected and over 40,000 family shelters damaged. Response is held back by Israeli restrictions alongside funding shortfalls.
Sewage-contaminated stormwater poses public health risks, compounded by flooded health facilities, suspended services, medicine shortages and a rise in probable meningitis cases and other diseases.
The latest rainfalls left thousands of young students in over 100 learning spaces without access to education, damaged 15 child and psychosocial spaces, and caused the collapse of a safe space serving 200 women and girls.
In the West Bank, demolitions by Israeli forces displaced 150 Palestinians in one week, including in areas affected by the E1 settlement expansion plan and the Jordan Valley, as well as in East Jerusalem.
Education-related incidents nearly doubled in the third quarter of 2026 compared with 2025, affecting 149 schools and nearly 28,000 students and education personnel across the West Bank.
Settler violence and access restrictions continued to displace families and disrupt the olive harvest, with 410 harvest-related settler attacks recorded since 1 September 2026.
