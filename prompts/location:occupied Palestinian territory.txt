Crisis / situation: occupied Palestinian territory

--- Report 1: Insecurity Insight (2026-09-22) ---
Data collected by Insecurity Insight shows that between 07 October 2023 and 31 August 2026, at least 3,464 incidents of violence against or obstruction of access to health care took place across the occupied Palestinian territory. 95% of all incidents were attributed to Israeli forces.

Gaza: 2,375 attacks on health care were recorded during which health facilities were damaged 455 times, 777 health workers were killed and 399 arrested. Patient access to medicine was obstructed on 303 occasions.
West Bank: 1,020 attacks on health care were recorded during which health facilities were damaged 32 times, 13 health workers were killed and 198 arrested. Patient access to medicine was obstructed on 626 occasions.

Other resources
Using data collected by Insecurity Insight, the Safeguarding Health in Conflict Coalition (SHCC) identified 432 incidents of violence against or obstruction of health care across Gaza in 2025. At least 190 incidents were recorded in the West Bank and East Jerusalem. Health workers were arrested, threatened, or injured; health facilities were damaged, violently raided, or surrounded; and ambulances and emergency medical teams were repeatedly delayed, blocked, or searched amid Israeli military incursions and increasingly restrictive movement and access constraints imposed across the West Bank. Most incidents were attributed to IDF troops armed with guns. Attacks on health care linked to Israeli settlers, typically armed with guns or rocks, more than doubled in 2025, compared to 2024, rising from around 2% to 9% of total reported attacks.
Despite the two ceasefires, incidents continued in Gaza throughout 2025, often increasing in conjunction with IDF military movements and ground operations. On average, 2.8 attacks were reported each week during the January 2025 ceasefire, and 1.9 attacks per week from the start of the October ceasefire through December 31, 2025. During both ceasefires, four health workers were killed and two others injured, and the IDF shelled a clinic in Jabalia IDP camp, injuring multiple people, including a pregnant woman. The IDF repeatedly fired at and bombed ambulances responding to emergencies.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-09-21) ---
Highlights

In Gaza city, 21 people, including 12 children, were reportedly killed, and dozens more injured, when a war-damaged residential building collapsed on 16 September; many remain in unsafe buildings for lack of better housing arrangements.
Search and rescue operations as well as the ability to repair damaged buildings and help those choosing to relocate from flood-prone areas ahead of the rains are limited by shortages of machinery, fuel, engine oil, spare parts and other supplies.
Cases of impetigo, a highly contagious bacterial skin infection primarily affecting young children, continued to increase, with a total of 45,190 reported between 2 August and 12 September. One new case of acute flaccid paralysis was reported, bringing the cumulative total to 21 in 2026.
Two temporary dumping sites have exceeded capacity and closed, raising concerns that Israeli restrictions on access to Gaza’s landfills near the Green Line result in increased public health risks.
While a US$2 million Humanitarian Fund allocation and a 1.5 million-litre fuel grant will help sustain health services and other critical services amid Gaza’s prolonged power crisis, more humanitarian funding is urgently needed alongside approvals for sustainable solutions.
In the West Bank, more than 1,100 structures have been demolished for lacking building permits in 2026, displacing over 1,500 people – over a third of the more than 4,300 Palestinians displaced so far this year.
Settler attacks are affecting an expanding geographic area: 275 Palestinian communities have been affected so far in 2026, nearly as many as the 279 affected during all of 2025, the highest annual figure recorded since 2006.
In Qusra, three Palestinian families have remained largely isolated for more than five weeks following the establishment of a nearby settlement outpost, with restrictions limiting their movement and access to education and humanitarian assistance.
In Umm Safa, settlement outpost expansion and a newly installed settler road gate have further isolated a Palestinian family from the rest of the village, amid recurrent settler attacks and intimidation.

--- Report 3: IOM (2026-09-21) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
Humanitarian needs across the Occupied Palestinian Territory remained severe throughout August 2026, with military activitiy, displacement and damage to civilian infrastructure continuing to deepen needs in Gaza, where most of the population remained internally displaced in overcrowded conditions and exposed to ongoing Israeli strikes.
A comprehensive shelter assessment in August found shelter needs to be near-universal across accessible areas of Gaza. An estimated 1.98 million people — 94 per cent of the assessed population — were experiencing Shelter Severity Phases 3 (Crisis), 4 (Critical) or 5 (Catastrophic) shelter needs, and all four assessed governorates were classied in Phase 4. Around two-thirds of households were living in tents or makeshift shelters, more than half of which were in poor or very poor condition. Environmental hazards were widespread, with 89 per cent of households reporting rodents inside their shelter and 71 per cent being aected by ooding since the previous winter. aected by ooding since the previous winter.
Brieng the Security Council on 26 August, the UN Acting Special Coordinator for the Middle East Peace Process, Ramiz Alakbarov, warned that around two-thirds of Gaza remained inaccessible or subject to severe access restrictions, while humanitarian actors — especially international NGOs — continued to be constrained by limited access, supplies and funding. With the 2026 Flash Appeal only 40 per cent funded, he called for winter support to be urgently scaled up, highlighting depleted shelter and NFI stocks and continued restrictions on the entry of critical humanitarian items.
Without sustained access to critical materials and additional funding, humanitarian actors will remain unable to meet shelter and other essential needs at the scale required.
