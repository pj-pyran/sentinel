Crisis / situation: Syrian Arab Republic

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-11-26) ---
Syria continues to witness large-scale returns of refugees and internally displaced persons (IDPs), alongside persistent humanitarian vulnerabilities and reintegration needs. Since December 2024, more than 1.8 million refugees and 2 million IDPs have returned. Meanwhile, 15.6 million people continue to require humanitarian assistance.
As returns continue, access to documentation, adequate housing, essential services, livelihoods and community support remains critical to ensuring sustainable reintegration and supporting Syria’s recovery.
In line with Syria’s National Recovery Priorities and the Government’s “No Tents, No Camps” vision, UNHCR supports return and reintegration efforts while continuing to provide protection and humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations.

KEY HIGHLIGHTS

Syria has made notable gains in security, reconstruction, and economic activity, with signs of a re-emerging banking sector and improving stability; however, economic recovery remains slow, fragile, and uneven, and the broader transition will take time.
Humanitarian needs remain significant despite progress, with 7.2 million people facing acute food insecurity and many returnees confronting damaged homes, limited livelihood opportunities, and overstretched basic services.
A successful transition and recovery require coordinated international support, including investment in national systems, government capacity, reconstruction, and essential assistance for vulnerable households.
