KEY HIGHLIGHTS

Syria remains at a critical juncture, with 7.1 million people facing acute food insecurity, including 700,000 experiencing severe food insecurity.
Rising return movements are placing additional strain on basic services, livelihoods, and social protection systems, highlighting the urgent need for sustained investment in nationally led systems to preserve social cohesion, and facilitate a stable transition.
Funding shortfalls have already forced a reduction in food assistance from 1.3 million to 650,000 people, limiting assistance to those with the greatest needs.
WFP urgently requires USD 201.7 million over the next six months to restore vital food assistance to 1.3 million vulnerable people affected by food insecurity, displacement, and economic hardship. These funds would also be critical to sustain nutrition programmes, protect livelihoods and continue supporting national social protection interventions.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-09-29) ---
August 2026
Syria has entered a phase marked by large-scale refugee and IDP returns alongside persistent humanitarian vulnerability and reintegration needs. While around 1.8 million refugees and over 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) have returned since December 2024, 15.6 million people still require humanitarian assistance. As return movements continue, ensuring sustainable reintegration through access to documentation, housing, essential services, livelihoods and community support is critical to helping returnees rebuild their lives and is central to Syria’s recovery. In line with Syria’s National Recovery Priorities and the Government’s “No Tents, No Camps” vision, UNHCR supports this transition while maintaining protection and humanitarian assistance for vulnerable populations.
