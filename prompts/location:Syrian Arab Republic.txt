Crisis / situation: Syrian Arab Republic

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-06-11) ---
CONTEXT OVERVIEW
Insecurity in southern Syria continues to affect areas across Quneitra Governorate and adjacent areas of Dar’a and Rural Damascus Governorates, covering 11 sub-districts in Dar’a (Ash-Shajara, Jasim, Nawa, Tassil), Quneitra (Al-Khashniyyeh, Fiq, Khan Arnaba, Quneitra), and Rural Damascus (Bait Jan, Qatana, Sa’sa’). These areas face a fragile and volatile security environment, compounded by climate-related pressures, years of underdevelopment and neglect and deteriorating socio-economic conditions. Despite broader political developments since late 2024, communities remain exposed to increased Israeli military activity along the ceasefire line, and localized tensions, which continue to disrupt civilian movement, limit access to agricultural land, and undermine livelihoods.
On 2 June 2026, OCHA facilitated an inter-agency mission to Quneitra Governorate, bringing together 50 participants from UN agencies, international and national NGOs. The mission covered seven locations across the governorate and assessed humanitarian conditions, access constraints and priority needs through consultations with local authorities, communities and service providers. Key observations from this mission are reflected throughout this snapshot.
SNAPSHOT RECOMMENDATIONS
Short-term support:
• Prioritize programmes that support access to income-generating and livelihood opportunities, with a focus on empowering women and girls, integrating persons with disabilities, and ensuring the protection and dignity of elderly people, returnees, and IDP families. Particularly in Quneitra Governorate, Ash-Shajarah sub-district in Dar’a,
• Expand Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), particularly as needs continue to evolve and the loss of income increases the burden on communities - especially vulnerable groups - limiting their ability to cover the cost of basic services and essential needs. Villages along the disengagement line, and the southern region of Quneitra Governorate, Ash-Shajara sub-district in Dar’a.
• Support Agriculture Recovery: Prioritize restoration of safe access to agricultural and grazing land. Expand support for agricultural recovery, increase investment in the rehabilitation of irrigation systems and infrastructure, and strengthen veterinary services. Villages along the disengagement line, and the southern region of Quneitra Governorate, Ash-Shajara sub-district in Dar’a, Qatana and Beit Jin sub-districts in Rural Damascus.
• Strengthen Health Services: Strengthen existing service delivery systems and institutional capacity, and reinforce referral pathways to ensure adequate coverage. Restore and expand mobile medical services, outreach activities, and referral mechanisms to improve access for vulnerable and underserved communities. Particularly in Qatana and Beit Jin sub-districts in Rural Damascus, all sub-districts in Quneitra and As-Shajara sub-district in Dar’a.
• Address Explosive Ordnance Contamination affecting livelihoods: Prioritize awareness-raising programmes and clearance activities, particularly in areas linked to agriculture and livestock, open spaces used by children, and locations near essential service infrastructure. Particularly in Quneitra and Dar’a.
Medium-term support:
• Support the rehabilitation of water and sanitation infrastructure, alongside strengthening operational capacity and maintenance resources. Particularly Ash-Saharaah, Nawa, Quneitra, Tassil
• Establish and rehabilitate wastewater treatment plants to reduce environmental risks affecting both populations and the agricultural sector. Particularly in Ash-Shajara, Nawa, Quneitra.
• Expand support to education services, including rehabilitation of educational facilities and strengthening learning processes. Increase investment in vocational and agricultural education to build local capacity and enhance community resilience.
• Strengthen protection services and related infrastructure, including legal services, street lighting systems, and civil registry centres.
• Support rehabilitation of houses in alignment with the no camps policy announced by the Government. Particularly in Quneitra and Dar’a.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-06-11) ---
Key Highlights

The cost of living in April 2026, as measured by the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB), remained stable at Old SYP 2.66 million compared to the previous month. Despite being 22 percent higher than its value in April 2025.
The Syrian pound depreciated in the parallel market, with the exchange rate weakening from Old SYP 11,972 per USD in March 2026 to Old SYP 12,906 per USD in April 2026. In contrast, the official exchange rate remained stable at Old SYP 11,055 per USD.
Syria advanced economic recovery efforts through closer cooperation with the World Bank and IMF, securing more than $425 million in financing for water, health, and railway projects, while recording its first state budget surplus since 1990 and launching a new foreign exchange and gold market in Damascus.
Trade and food supply chain showed notable improvement, driven by expanding regional trade with Jordan, the resumption of oil shipments from Iraq, strengthened logistics and transport links, higher domestic wheat production, and investments in food infrastructure, including new bakeries and measures to protect local agricultural production.
The WFP-rehabilitated bakery in Talkalkh, rural Homs, resumed operations with an upgraded 10-ton daily flour capacity, securing the primary bread supply for 20 to 30 surrounding villages.

--- Report 3: UNICEF (2026-06-10) ---
Key figures As of 7 June 2026
17,000 people directly affected by the floods.
700 families displaced, representing around 2,000 children.
Thousands of children exposed to heightened risks.
49 water treatment plants out of service in Deir-ez-Zor and Raqqa Governorates.
Situation Update
While the water level of the Euphrates River has receded significantly across Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor, and Raqqa governorates, and the flood wave is gradually stabilizing, the river flow remains at twice the seasonal average, putting children and their families at continuous risk and disrupting access to essential services and humanitarian assistance.
The flooding has already caused humanitarian impacts across communities located along the Euphrates. Approximately 3,400 households were affected, with over 700 families displaced. Reports also indicate the death of nine people by drowning, including children, due to the sudden rise in water, and the flooding of multiple informal settlements. Around 20,000 dunums of agricultural land have been flooded, and poultry farms and fishponds have been damaged.
Critical infrastructure has sustained extensive damage. Several bridges and river crossings have collapsed or are inaccessible, severely disrupting humanitarian and commercial access between the two banks of the river. Water infrastructure has also been severely damaged, with multiple water stations flooded, but the repair efforts have been tremendous. As for Raqqa Governorate, out of 86 affected water stations, only 3 remain out of service, but 46 water treatment plants remain out of service in Deir-ez-Zor Governorate. The Water Establishment and UNICEF’s support enabled the continuation of operations at the Euphrates Water Treatment Plant, which supplies water to approximately 400,000 people, despite the collapse of protective embankments and flooding on the plant premises.
Children continue to face heightened vulnerabilities as families recover from displacement, damaged shelters, and disruptions to basic services. Protection risks remain a key concern, including the risk of drowning in residual floodwaters and increased exposure to Unexploded Ordnance (UXO), as floodwaters may have shifted explosive remnants into residential and agricultural areas. Additional protection concerns include increased risks of violence against children, psychosocial distress, and challenges faced by families who have lost civil documentation required to access essential services and assistance. Damage to infrastructure and public facilities may also continue to affect children's access to education.
The flooding has occurred against a backdrop of ongoing public health challenges, including continued measles transmission in several areas. While floodwaters are receding, deteriorated WASH conditions continue to increase the risk of waterborne disease outbreaks and may further exacerbate disease transmission among already vulnerable populations. Disease surveillance in affected locations has reported cases of acute watery diarrhea and confirmed measles cases in the last few months, underscoring the need for sustained public health interventions.
Despite the gradual decline in water levels, continued humanitarian support remains essential to address immediate needs and prevent secondary impacts on affected populations. Timely assistance – including access to safe water, health and immunization services, child protection support, mine and child protection risk awareness, and cash assistance – will be critical to helping families recover and reducing the risks faced by children and caregivers. Without sustained support, affected communities may face prolonged disruptions to essential services and increased vulnerability after years of protracted crisis.
UNICEF and partners continue to coordinate closely with local authorities and humanitarian actors to monitor the evolving situation, assess remaining needs, and support response and early recovery efforts across affected governorates.

--- Report 4: IFRC (2026-06-10) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
25-05-2026
What happened, where and when?
Since mid-May 2026, Syria has experienced severe flooding and rapidly rising water levels along the Euphrates River, primarily affecting Deir ez-Zor and Ar-Raqqa governorates in addition to Jarabulus from Aleppo governorate, following a sharp increase in water inflow from upstream areas in Türkiye. The increased inflow, reportedly reaching approximately 2,200 m³ per second, is mainly attributed to seasonal snowmelt and the opening of spillways at upstream dams.
As reservoir levels exceeded 97 per cent of storage capacity, authorities initiated emergency water management measures, including the opening of three spillway gates at the Euphrates Dam for the first time in more than three decades. Water discharge levels have significantly increased and may continue rising in the coming days if inflow persists.
The flooding has resulted in the displacement of at least 1,626 families in Deir ez-Zor Governorate, partial flooding of displacement sites in Ar-Raqqa, around 30 families, damage to agricultural land and disruption to critical infrastructure, including water stations and local access routes. Approximately 50 rural water stations in Deir ez-Zor and several additional stations in Ar-Raqqa are currently out of service or at risk due to inundation and restricted access.
Precautionary evacuations are ongoing in several high-risk communities located along the riverbanks, while local authorities and emergency actors continue to monitor water levels and implement protective measures to safeguard critical infrastructure and vulnerable populations.
The situation remains highly volatile, with authorities warning that additional spillway openings may become necessary should water inflow continue to increase, potentially leading to further flooding, displacement, service disruption, and humanitarian needs across affected areas along the Euphrates basin.
