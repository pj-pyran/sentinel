Crisis / situation: Syrian Arab Republic

--- Report 1: Govt. Syria (2026-11-24) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
In October 2026, after receiving initial reports of children with acute flaccid paralysis (AFP) in Deir ez-Zor Governorate, Syria, the Ministry of Health (MoH) mounted a swift response, in coordination with partners.
Conflict, displacement, interrupted immunization and population movement have created immunity gaps. An unrelated variant VDPV1 detection was reported in wastewater in the area in February 2026.
HIGHLIGHTS
The MoH, supported by WHO,UNICEF and partners, is leading the outbreak response, including intensified surveillance and vaccination.
Emergency vaccination activities reached:

34 720 children in October 2026
54 382 in November 2026 (111% of the target of 49 000)

The high coverage can be attributed to population movement. Additional efforts are needed to determine target population in future rounds.
The next campaign is planned for 30 November 2026 in six districts of Deir ez-Zor Governorate, integrating child nutrition assessments, drinking-water chlorine level checks and chlorine distribution.
An expanded scope will follow in December 2026.
The risk of further spread within Syria and to neighbouring countries, including Iraq, remains a concern due to uneven vaccination coverage and immunity gaps, particularly among displaced, mobile and underserved populations.
COORDINATION OF THE RESPONSE
The MoH is coordinating national and subnational actors. WHO and UNICEF are mobilizing regional and global technical expertise, while the Global Polio Eradication Initiative (GPEI) provides technical and financial support. World Vision and the International Rescue Committee funded the first two vaccination rounds in October and November 2026.
Two coordination meetings involving national, regional and global response teams have reviewed the situation and agreed on priorities. The MoH Incident Management Team meets weekly through its Emergency Operations Centre. The remaining 13 governorates in Syria have been advised to intensify surveillance and preparedness. Cross-border coordination is particularly important for Iraq, given population movements and the history of wild poliovirus transmission across this border in 2013.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-11-23) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

Israeli military activity intensified across southern Syria, with around 280 armed incursions recorded in September alone, increasing protection concerns and affecting civilian movement, agricultural livelihoods and access to services in border communities.
Agricultural recovery remains at risk despite improved 2026 harvest conditions,with underfunding contributing to emergency agriculture assistance reaching only 12 per cent of its annual target by end-September, even as the 2026–2027 planting season was already underway.
Food affordability remains a major concern across Syria, with monitored food prices in September 25 per cent higher than a year earlier, while gaps in regular food assistance persisted across some of the country’s highest-severity areas.
Humanitarian partners reached an average of 2.48 million people per month with humanitarian assistance between January and September 2026, with 325 organizations operating across 5,097 communities.
The Syria Humanitarian Fund allocated US$5 million to address growing needs in south-west Syria, targeting more than 100,000 people across prioritized areas of Quneitra, Dar’a and Rural Damascus through integrated humanitarian assistance.
