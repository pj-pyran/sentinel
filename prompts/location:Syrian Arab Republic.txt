Summary
This Governorate Profile provides an overview of the humanitarian situation in Aleppo Governorate as of June 2026, including key humanitarian trends, priority needs, and operational considerations. Aleppo remains one of Syria’s most significant governorates from a humanitarian, economic, and demographic perspective, having experienced extensive conflict-related destruction, large-scale displacement, and recurring insecurity.
The profile outlines:

Background and context, including geographical location, administrative divisions, conflict impact, and recent developments affecting humanitarian conditions.
Humanitarian Overview, highlighting priority needs related to access to basic services, displacement, shelter, livelihoods, and early recovery.
Drivers of Humanitarian Needs, including infrastructure destruction, displacement and return dynamics, insecurity, economic decline, environmental pressures, and protection concerns.
Humanitarian Operating Environment, examining access constraints, security dynamics, administrative challenges, explosive hazards, and operational considerations affecting humanitarian response.
Key humanitarian indicators, including population figures, displacement statistics, return movements, people in need (PiN), response reach, sectoral priorities, and humanitarian presence.

Aleppo has experienced some of the largest return movements recorded nationwide since December 2024, while continuing to host one of Syria’s largest displaced populations. Despite improvements in access in some areas, humanitarian needs remain severe due to widespread infrastructure damage, service gaps, economic hardship, and protection risks. The profile highlights the importance of sustaining humanitarian assistance while scaling up recovery-oriented interventions to support durable solutions and resilience.
The profile is intended to support humanitarian planning, operational decision-making, advocacy, and situational awareness among humanitarian partners and stakeholders.

--- Report 15: OCHA (2026-07-06) ---
BACKGROUND
Humanitarian operations in Syria are taking place in an increasingly constrained financial environment, shaped by recent regulatory changes, limited banking sector capacity, and ongoing exchange rate volatility. The issue started with the change of the former government and continued during 2025 where many organizations bank accounts’ were blocked for a period and started to resume gradually. Some organizations have not unblocked their assets yet while majority did. in August 2025, an exempted use of foreign currency for humanitarian assistance was introduced by the central bank of Syria (CBS) for next six month ending on 25 of February 2026. This has facilitated the cash flow for many organizations operating in Syria, throw transfers from HQ and neighboring country to Syria by FSPs. When the exemption ended, another one was introduced by MoFA that extended for another six months starting from 19 of April 2026. Few weeks after that, CBS issued a new circular setting all transactions post 15 of May in SYP and leaving a space for external contracts to be paid in foreign currency which caused disturbance to some humanitarian activities due to exchange rate volatility. Post this circular, CWG made a survey to measure the impact on humanitarian operations in the country which is further detailed below.
KEY MESSAGES
• Liquidity shortages and exchange rate gaps are significantly reducing the value of humanitarian assistance, with losses estimated at up to 25 per cent by the time assistance reaches beneficiaries.
• Operational constraints are delaying assistance delivery, with partners reporting delays of several weeks due to withdrawal limits, restricted transfers, and limited banking liquidity.
• Liquidity constraints are reducing programme scale, with an estimated 3–5 per cent loss in programming, translating into reduced coverage or lower assistance levels.
• Fragmented exchange rate practices are driving inconsistencies in assistance, resulting in uneven transfer values across partners and locations and increasing operational complexity.
• Partners anticipate increasing reliance on cash assistance, with a large majority expecting cash needs to grow in the coming months, placing additional pressure on an already constrained system.
