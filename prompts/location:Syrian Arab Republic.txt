Crisis / situation: Syrian Arab Republic

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-06-08) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• Syria faces a severe food crisis, with only 18 percent of the population food secure. Severe funding shortfalls have forced WFP to halve emergency food assistance, increasing the risk of hunger, debt, and instability.
• Humanitarian aid and recovery must go hand in hand, as unmet basic needs risk undermining recovery. WFP support remains critical in stabilizing communities and markets.
• Urgent funding is needed to safeguard fragile gains and prevent further humanitarian, economic, and social deterioration. Sustained investment in livelihoods, agriculture, markets, and social protection is critical to rebuilding dignity, reducing dependency, and supporting long-term stability.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Food security conditions are gradually improving however, over 80 percent of Syrian families still cannot consistently meet their diverse, sufficient and nutritious food needs.
• Rising living costs are driving the Minimum Expenditure Basket up to approximately SYP 24,000. Even with a planned 50 percent wage increase, incomes cover only about one-third of basic household needs. Between January and March 2026, high inflation and currency depreciation have further reduced purchasing power. As a result, households are facing growing difficulty in affording essential goods and necessities.
• According to UNHCR, as of 19 May, over 448,582 people had crossed into Syria from Lebanon since the escalation in March, including over 371,548 Syrians (over 83,374 intending permanent return) and 82,189 Lebanese.
• Since 25 May, severe flooding along the Euphrates in Deir ez‑Zor and Raqqa has caused displacement, infrastructure damage, and livelihood losses during the peak harvest season in some of the most food‑insecure areas, further straining local food systems. This follows earlier localized flooding that also affected communities in Hasakeh, Deir ez‑Zor, and Aleppo. On 30 May, a WFP delegation, including the Country Director, visited Deir ez‑Zor to meet local counterparts and affected households, visit collective shelters, monitor distributions, and assess flood‑affected areas to guide response and planning.
• In April, WFP launched the development of its new Country Strategic Plan (CSP), which will define WFP’s priorities and operational direction in alignment with the new UNSDCF, with submission to the Executive Board scheduled for February 2027.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
Rising water levels in the Euphrates River have triggered flooding and flood risks in parts of Deir ez-Zor, with localized impacts also reported in Ar-Raqqa and Aleppo. The impact remains localized but significant. Reported impacts include disruption of movement between riverbanks, suspension of some crossings and ferry operations, damage to agricultural land, the collapse or suspension of some river crossings and bridges, and disruption to water stations and other essential services. Several water stations have been impacted or taken offline as a precaution. Efforts are ongoing to protect critical infrastructure, including water stations and pumping systems, and to maintain water supply through contingency measures such as water trucking. The main public health concerns are linked to safe water, sanitation, continuity of essential health services, access to emergency care and referral routes, and the increased risk of waterborne diseases in affected communities.
The escalation of the situation in south-west Syria has prompted preparations of sector level update of inter-sector gaps, needs and response in Quneitra and parts of Dar’a and Rural Damascus. Military operations and related movement constraints have further stressed an already fragile southern health system. Main effects include higher emergency/trauma pressure, disrupted access to primary health care points, weaker referral movement, and increased risk of interruption to maternal, child, NCD/dialysis, MHPSS and vaccination services. The risk is most acute in Quneitra, where 86,762 people are in need out of a population of 164,496. Quneitra is smaller governorate but highly fragile because it depends on one public hospital and limited outreach/referral depth. Rural Damascus is the main high-capacity but high-pressure service hub. Dar'a is a high-burden, high-activity governorate with mostly partial hospital/PHC functionality.
Key figures about the returns: Total Syrians Refugee Returnees: 1,667,078. Total IDPs: 5,542,227 (as of August 2025). 4,186,695 (76%) reside outside IDP sites. 1,355,532 (24%) are inside 1,531 IDP sites. As of April 2026, the CCCM records show that 870,436 individuals (169,722 households) are hosted in 1,275 IDP sites in Syria.
