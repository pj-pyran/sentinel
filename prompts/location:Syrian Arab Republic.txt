Crisis / situation: Syrian Arab Republic

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-11-24) ---
Executive Summary
Syria continues to experience significant population returns, with more than 1.8 million refugees and 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) having returned since December 2024. However, the pace of refugee returns has moderated throughout 2026, declining from over 268,000 in Q1 to approximately 131,000 in Q2 and 117,000 in Q3. Findings from the third quarter highlight persistent challenges affecting recovery and sustainable reintegration, particularly economic hardship, inadequate housing, gaps in civil documentation, limited access to essential services and localized protection risks.
This report draws on 9,842 household interviews conducted across all 14 governorates and 187 subdistricts, complemented by community consultations. Respondents comprised refugee returnees (45%), IDP returnees (29%) and host community members (26%). Building on three rounds of monitoring in 2026, the report identifies both improvements in selected indicators and persistent vulnerabilities, with variations across population groups and geographical areas.
Most surveyed returnees intend to remain in their current locations, although conditions for sustainable reintegration remain fragile. Among surveyed refugee and IDP returnees, 79% and 75%, respectively, reported intending to remain over the following six months. Improved security, family reunification and access to housing, land and property (HLP) were among the main reported factors influencing return decisions. However, damaged housing, limited infrastructure and services, livelihood constraints and security concerns remain important barriers to return. Phased returns, particularly among refugees returning from neighbouring countries, may also be linked to housing and livelihood constraints, although the monitoring does not establish the reasons for these arrangements.
Socioeconomic vulnerability remains a central concern, with important implications for protection and reintegration. Unemployment affected 31% of working-age respondents, rising to 36% among refugee returnees, with pronounced gender disparities. Food and nutrition remained the most frequently reported unmet need across population groups (65–73%), followed by economic opportunities. Financial pressures were also reflected in continued reliance on negative coping mechanisms, including depletion of savings (42%), deprioritization of essential needs (38%) and borrowing (26%). These findings point to persistent household vulnerability and associated protection risks.
Housing insecurity and unresolved HLP issues continue to affect households' living conditions and reintegration prospects. Refugee returnees reported higher unmet shelter needs (49%) than IDP returnees (35%) and host communities (19%). While most respondents expected to remain in their current accommodation, rental insecurity and affordability remained important concerns.
Gaps in civil documentation continue to restrict access to rights and essential services. Although modest improvements were recorded among returnees, 18% of refugee returnee households and 10% of IDP returnee households reported at least one member lacking essential documentation, compared with 6% among host communities. These findings reinforce the importance of accessible legal assistance and strengthened national civil registration systems.
Perceptions of safety and psychosocial well-being show some improvements, although localized protection concerns persist. In Q3, 87% of refugee returnees and 68% of host community respondents reported feeling safe, compared with 83% and 61% in Q2, respectively, while perceptions of safety among IDP returnees declined. Localized insecurity, movement restrictions and explosive ordnance contamination continued to pose protection risks. Reported stress-related symptoms also declined from 47% in Q1 to 38% in Q3, although economic hardship, insecurity and barriers to accessing mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services remained important concerns.
Women and children continue to face distinct protection risks. Community consultations identified concerns related to gender-based violence, harassment, restrictions on women's and girls' freedom of movement and unequal access to resources. Child protection concerns included child labour, child marriage, violence, exploitation and exposure to explosive ordnance. Although reported school non-attendance declined among refugee returnee and host community households, economic pressures, child labour and documentation gaps continued to present barriers to education.
Social cohesion challenges remain localized but require continued attention, particularly in communities experiencing substantial returns and economic pressures. Community consultations identified tensions related to competition over resources, identity-based narratives and perceptions of unequal access to opportunities. Findings also point to the continued use of community-based dispute-resolution mechanisms, highlighting opportunities to strengthen inclusive dialogue and locally appropriate approaches to addressing disputes.
Access to humanitarian assistance has declined despite persistent needs. Only 29% of surveyed households reported receiving assistance in Q3, compared with 33% in Q2 and 45% in Q1. Among households reporting difficulties accessing assistance, service unavailability (64%), limited information (24%) and the absence of service providers (24%) were the most frequently cited barriers. These findings highlight the importance of maintaining assistance responsive to assessed vulnerabilities, particularly in underserved and high-return areas.
Overall, three rounds of protection monitoring in 2026 suggest that improvements in some safety, psychosocial, and education indicators have not yet translated into a corresponding reduction in the structural barriers affecting sustainable reintegration. Economic insecurity, housing and documentation challenges, uneven service availability and localized protection risks remain interconnected, with different implications for refugee returnees, IDP returnees and host communities.
Advancing durable solutions, including sustainable reintegration for those who have voluntarily returned, requires integrated, area-based and protection-sensitive approaches that connect humanitarian assistance with recovery and longer-term development efforts. Priorities include expanding access to livelihoods and essential services, supporting adequate housing and HLP rights, strengthening civil documentation and access to legal assistance, sustaining community-based psychosocial support, mitigating gender-based violence and child protection risks, and reinforcing explosive ordnance risk education and mine action.
Continued support to national and local institutions and coordinated service delivery are essential, with UNHCR contributing through protection monitoring, legal assistance, technical engagement, community consultations and coordination with government counterparts and relevant recovery and development actors. These efforts will be critical to creating the conditions necessary for safe, dignified and sustainable reintegration.

--- Report 2: Govt. Syria (2026-11-24) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
In October 2026, after receiving initial reports of children with acute flaccid paralysis (AFP) in Deir ez-Zor Governorate, Syria, the Ministry of Health (MoH) mounted a swift response, in coordination with partners.
Conflict, displacement, interrupted immunization and population movement have created immunity gaps. An unrelated variant VDPV1 detection was reported in wastewater in the area in February 2026.
HIGHLIGHTS
The MoH, supported by WHO,UNICEF and partners, is leading the outbreak response, including intensified surveillance and vaccination.
Emergency vaccination activities reached:

34 720 children in October 2026
54 382 in November 2026 (111% of the target of 49 000)

The high coverage can be attributed to population movement. Additional efforts are needed to determine target population in future rounds.
The next campaign is planned for 30 November 2026 in six districts of Deir ez-Zor Governorate, integrating child nutrition assessments, drinking-water chlorine level checks and chlorine distribution.
An expanded scope will follow in December 2026.
The risk of further spread within Syria and to neighbouring countries, including Iraq, remains a concern due to uneven vaccination coverage and immunity gaps, particularly among displaced, mobile and underserved populations.
COORDINATION OF THE RESPONSE
The MoH is coordinating national and subnational actors. WHO and UNICEF are mobilizing regional and global technical expertise, while the Global Polio Eradication Initiative (GPEI) provides technical and financial support. World Vision and the International Rescue Committee funded the first two vaccination rounds in October and November 2026.
Two coordination meetings involving national, regional and global response teams have reviewed the situation and agreed on priorities. The MoH Incident Management Team meets weekly through its Emergency Operations Centre. The remaining 13 governorates in Syria have been advised to intensify surveillance and preparedness. Cross-border coordination is particularly important for Iraq, given population movements and the history of wild poliovirus transmission across this border in 2013.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-11-23) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

Israeli military activity intensified across southern Syria, with around 280 armed incursions recorded in September alone, increasing protection concerns and affecting civilian movement, agricultural livelihoods and access to services in border communities.
Agricultural recovery remains at risk despite improved 2026 harvest conditions,with underfunding contributing to emergency agriculture assistance reaching only 12 per cent of its annual target by end-September, even as the 2026–2027 planting season was already underway.
Food affordability remains a major concern across Syria, with monitored food prices in September 25 per cent higher than a year earlier, while gaps in regular food assistance persisted across some of the country’s highest-severity areas.
Humanitarian partners reached an average of 2.48 million people per month with humanitarian assistance between January and September 2026, with 325 organizations operating across 5,097 communities.
The Syria Humanitarian Fund allocated US$5 million to address growing needs in south-west Syria, targeting more than 100,000 people across prioritized areas of Quneitra, Dar’a and Rural Damascus through integrated humanitarian assistance.
