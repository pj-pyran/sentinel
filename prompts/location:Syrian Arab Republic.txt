Crisis / situation: Syrian Arab Republic

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-10-21) ---
Date of event
13-07-2025
What happened, where and when?
Beginning on 13 July 2025, As-Suwayda Governorate experienced a significant deterioration in the security situation following an escalation of violence that resulted in widespread humanitarian consequences. The hostilities led to civilian deaths and injuries and triggered large-scale displacement both within As-Suwayda and towards neighbouring governorates, particularly Daraa. The affected areas were already experiencing considerable socioeconomic pressures due to the prolonged economic crisis and the cumulative impact of years of conflict and recurrent emergencies across Syria.
The escalation further disrupted essential public services and significantly affected critical infrastructure. Widespread power outages were reported, while several health facilities either ceased operations or became overwhelmed by the influx of casualties, substantially reducing their capacity to provide essential services. The deterioration of basic services compounded existing vulnerabilities and increased humanitarian needs among both displaced populations and host communities.
Throughout the operational period, continued insecurity and repeated breaches of ceasefire arrangements hindered recovery efforts and prevented many displaced families from returning to their places of origin. Access to health services, WASH facilities and essential relief items remained limited, while the prolonged displacement of affected populations placed additional pressure on already overstretched services and resources within host communities. As a result, a sustained humanitarian response remained necessary to address urgent needs while ensuring the safety and access of humanitarian personnel.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-10-20) ---
Syria is experiencing large-scale refugee and IDP returns alongside persistent humanitarian vulnerability and reintegration needs.
While over 1.8 million refugees and 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) have returned since December 2024, 15.6 million people still require humanitarian assistance.
As return movements continue, access to documentation, housing, essential services, livelihoods and community support is critical to sustainable reintegration and Syria’s recovery.
In line with Syria’s National Recovery Priorities and the Government’s “No Tents, No Camps” vision, UNHCR supports return and reintegration while maintaining protection and humanitarian assistance for vulnerable populations.
