Crisis / situation: Syrian Arab Republic

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-10-20) ---
Syria is experiencing large-scale refugee and IDP returns alongside persistent humanitarian vulnerability and reintegration needs.
While over 1.8 million refugees and 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) have returned since December 2024, 15.6 million people still require humanitarian assistance.
As return movements continue, access to documentation, housing, essential services, livelihoods and community support is critical to sustainable reintegration and Syria’s recovery.
In line with Syria’s National Recovery Priorities and the Government’s “No Tents, No Camps” vision, UNHCR supports return and reintegration while maintaining protection and humanitarian assistance for vulnerable populations.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-10-19) ---
Syria is currently experiencing one of the largest return movements globally, with more than 1.8 million refugees and over 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) having returned, while millions more indicate they would consider returning if conditions improve.
Returnees in Syria face major barriers to livelihoods due to limited jobs, weak markets, and socio-economic challenges. As a result, many struggle to meet basic needs, making livelihoods support essential for sustainable reintegration.
UNHCR livelihoods and economic inclusion support is designed to contribute directly to UNHCR Syria’s overall strategy and overarching vision of enabling the safe, dignified, and voluntary return and reintegration of forcibly displaced persons by 2029.
