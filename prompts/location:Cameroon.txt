Crisis / situation: Cameroon

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-10-15) ---
Overview, current situation and key developments
The cholera outbreak in Minawao Refugee Camp remains active, although the number of new cases has
declined from the peak recorded at the end of August. As of 11 October, 459 cumulative cases had been
reported, of which 454 patients had recovered and one patient remained under observation, and four deaths
had been recorded.
• The response is showing encouraging signs of containment, with very few new cases reported. One
new case was recorded on 5 October. The decline follows intensified measures, including the camp-
wide oral cholera vaccination campaign, closure of contaminated open wells, disinfection of households
and public facilities, contact tracing and community sensitisation

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-10-15) ---
Cameroon’s Eastern Façade, covering the East,
Adamawa and North regions, hosts most of the country’s Central African Republic refugees, many displaced for more than a decade.
More than 80% live in host communities. Funding cuts and pressure on services are deepening vulnerabilities. At the same time, 87.3% of refugees say they wish to remain in Cameroon, making inclusion, access to national services and self-reliance urgent priorities.
UNHCR is shifting from emergency assistance to durable solutions, prioritising refugee inclusion in national systems, local development and self-reliance.
Peaceful coexistence largely prevails despite mounting pressure on basic services and a decline in humanitarian funding

--- Report 3: UNHCR (2026-10-15) ---
Cameroon has strong policies for refugee inclusion in education, but funding gaps are keeping children out of class. Less than one in four refugee children are enrolled; more than 152,000 remain out of school; and secondary education is almost out of reach in key refugee-hosting areas.
Predictable donor financing is urgently needed to turn policy commitments into safe classrooms, trained teachers and real learning opportunities — especially for girls, children with disabilities and adolescents at risk of being left behind

--- Report 4: UNHCR (2026-10-15) ---
UNHCR Cameroon is supporting a gradual shift from direct health service delivery towards sustainable refugee inclusion in national health systems. This approach strengthens Government ownership, expands refugees’ access to public services and aligns with the Global Compact on Refugees, Cameroon’s National Health Sector Strategy 2016–2027, and the UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework.
As national systems are increasingly taking the lead, targeted support remains critical to keep essential services for refugees with chronic and infectious diseases, pregnant women, malnourished children, referral patients and communities affected by public health emergencies.
