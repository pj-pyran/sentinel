Crisis / situation: Cameroon

--- Report 1: UNFPA (2026-07-28) ---
Cameroon continued to face a highly volatile and complex humanitarian environment in June 2026, driven by persistent conflict and compounding crises across the Far North, North-West, and South-West regions. In the North-West and South-West, the security situation remained unstable, characterized by ongoing localized insecurity, a fire incident, and the strict enforcement of &quot;ghost towns&quot;, characterized by night-time curfews and enforced socio-economic lockdowns, as well as general movement restrictions; these issues heightened protection risks for women and girls. Concurrently, the Far North region remained heavily impacted by a cholera outbreak alongside ongoing attacks by non-state armed groups, particularly within hard-to-reach areas already experiencing significant humanitarian pressure. Beyond these immediate security and health threats, humanitarian needs and vulnerabilities remained high across the Far North region, with almost 1,000 displaced individuals across divisions facing severe protection risks and limited access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services.
In June, UNFPA provided extensive support across 22 health facilities, deploying 33 midwives to assist 1,330 deliveries — including 157 emergency Caesarean sections — and conduct thousands of SRH consultations, such as treatments for sexually transmitted infections and family planning services. Simultaneously, the organization reached 4,649 individuals through gender-based violence prevention and mitigation efforts, which provided psychosocial support, case management, and the distribution of 225 dignity kits to strengthen resilience in displacement settings.
For 2026, UNFPA requires US $4,013,060 to implement critical reproductive health and protection services in Cameroon. As of June, UNFPA has secured US $2.4 million thanks to the generous support of the European Union, German Agency for International Cooperation, and the UNFPA Emergency Fund.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-07-27) ---
BACKGROUND
Classification of MEB/ SMEB change: In November 2024, WFP Cameroon, in collaboration with the national Cash Working Group (CWG), initiated the revision of the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB), the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB), and Cash Transfer Values (TV) for the three major crisis areas in Cameroon: the Lake Chad Basin (LCB) crisis in the Far North region; the Central African Republic (CAR) crisis affecting the North, Adamaoua, and East regions; and the North-West and SouthWest crisis.
A hybrid approach was used to estimate the MEB, combining household expenditure data (ENSAN 2024) with a rights-based approach to identify existing gaps. This revision builds on the previous update conducted in December 2022.
The process updated the TV for each crisis to cover an average of 49.3% of households' unmet basic needs, compared to 45.6% for the survival basket. The national CWG and the Inter-Sector endorsed these findings in February 2026.
