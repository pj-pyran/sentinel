Crisis / situation: Central Africa

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-10-21) ---
Irregular migration is a complex, dynamic, and fragmented phenomenon that is difficult to fully understand. Understanding migration routes, trends, risks, and migrant profiles is critical to protecting migrants and supporting evidence-based policies. With this purpose, the International Organization for Migration's Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) (1) and Missing Migrants Project (MMP) collect data on the departures, arrivals, deaths, and disappearances of migrants along migration routes(2).
This document presents the panorama of irregular migration from the coasts of West and Northern Africa to the Canary Islands, Spain. This route, which used to be active until 2006, has seen an increasing number of movements since 2020. Intensified border monitoring on this migration route has shifted departures southwards on the West African coast, making journeys longer and more dangerous.
During the first semester of 2026, 3,708 migrants reached the Canary Islands irregularly after crossing by boat from the coasts of West and Northern Africa, a decrease of 67 per cent compared to the same period in 2025 (11,321 migrants)(3).
Migrants face many dangers along the West African Atlantic Route, which puts their well-being at risk and too often results in their deaths and disappearances. During the first semester of 2026, 59 boats failed to reach the Canary Islands, resulting in the death or disappearance of 168 migrants. A disappearance in this context refers to a migrant who presumably died but whose remains were never recovered from the ocean. These figures reflect only the reported fatalities and are minimum estimates of the real magnitude of the crisis. Each number represents a person, as well as the family and community that they leave behind.
Data on the West Africa Atlantic Route remain scarce and incomplete. There is currently no harmonized approach to data collection except on arrival in the Canary Islands. Additional data is collected by IOM on migrant profiles of those who arrive and on intercepted vessels. There is very little data on the number of departures and attempts from West African coasts. Official data on migrant deaths and disappearances on this route is largely lacking.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-10-19) ---
This document presents the situation of irregular arrivals to Europe, from January 2021 to June 2026, and fatalities on migration routes to Europe, focusing on movements departing from West and Central Africa (WCA). Data on registered arrivals to Europe by sea and by land are based on quarterly data from national authorities and compiled by IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). Data on migrant fatalities is collected daily by IOM’s Missing Migrants Project from the media, eyewitnesses, NGOs, UN agencies, and governments. As documenting migrant deaths and disappearances is challenging, these figures should be considered as minimum estimates.
Between 2021 and June 2026, more than 980,000 migrants arrived irregularly in Italy, Spain, Greece, Malta, and Cyprus by sea and land, of which 251,721 were nationals from West and Central Africa. During the first semester of 2026, the number of arrivals in Europe decreased by 15 per cent, compared to the same period in 2021 when 51,531 migrants arrived irregularly. Between January and June 2026, 6,629 nationals of West and Central Africa arrived in Europe, representing 15 per cent of all arrivals.
