Crisis / situation: Bangladesh: Flash Floods - Jul 2026

--- Report 1: GBV AoR (2026-07-28) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
Due to the well-marked low persisting over Madhya Pradesh &amp; adjoining Uttar Pradesh in India, and the strong monsoon active over the North Bay, rainfall is forecasted across the Sylhet, Mymensingh, and Chattogram divisions of Bangladesh from 5–12 July 2026. The situation may be further aggravated by heavy rainfall in the adjoining upstream areas of Meghalaya, Assam, the Barak Basin, and Tripura in India. The above-normal rainfall may trigger rapid inundation and flooding in different places of the Sylhet and Chattogram Divisions The Needs Assessment Working Group (NAWG) issued the Anticipatory Impact Analysis on 08th July, followed by the first flash update#1 on 9th July 2026 and Flash Update#2. The latest update shows the rainfall has caused rising water levels and flooding in several locations across the Chattogram Hill Tracts. Landslides have been reported in Bandarban and Cox’s Bazar districts. The severely affected areas are Chattogram, CoxS Bazar, Sunamganj, Khagrachhari, Moulvibazar, Sylhet, Feni, Rangamati, Bandarban, and Cumilla.
The latest update is that 1.07 million people are affected in 10 districts. 36,500 people are displaced and have taken shelter in 1,7000 shelters. There are 217,259 girls, 317,817 women, and 12,266 persons with disabilities reported to have been impacted by the flash flood and landslides.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-07-27) ---
Bangladesh is experiencing a severe monsoon-induced ood and landslide emergency following a period of exceptionally heavy rainfall associated with an active monsoon system and a low-pressure area over the Bay of Bengal. The emergency began in early July 2026 and intensied between 9 and 18 July, triggering widespread ash oods, riverine ooding, waterlogging, and landslides across several parts of the country. Rainfall was particularly intense in the south-eastern and north-eastern regions, with Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar,
Bandarban, Khagrachhari, Rangamati, Sylhet and Sunamganj experiencing prolonged heavy precipitation. Rising water levels in major rivers, including the Sangu, Kushiyara, Monu and Khowai, resulted in widespread inundation of low-lying areas, landslides in hilly terrain, destruction of critical infrastructure, and disruption of essential services.
The emergency has aected multiple districts across Bangladesh, with the most signicant impacts recorded in Cox’s Bazar, Chattogram,  Bandarban, Rangamati, Khagrachhari, Sylhet, Sunamganj, Moulvibazar, Habiganj and adjacent ood-prone areas. The impacts have been particularly severe in the Chattogram Hill Tracts and Cox’s Bazar, where steep slopes, saturated soils and prolonged rainfall have increased the occurrence of landslides and infrastructure damage. In Cox’s Bazar, the eects have extended across all 33 Rohingya refugee camps and surrounding host communities, causing damage to shelters, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities, roads, retaining walls and community infrastructure, while disrupting access to basic services and livelihoods.
Although rainfall has gradually decreased and water levels in many rivers have started to recede, the emergency remains ongoing.
Localized ooding, waterlogging, contaminated water sources, damaged infrastructure and landslide risks continue to aect vulnerable communities. According to tInter-Cluster Coordination Group sitrep dated 13 July 2026, more than 1.1 million people have been aected across 10 districts. As of 18 July 2026, 374 unions and four municipalities across 56 upazilas had been aected, with approximately 38,500 people temporarily displaced and more than 267,000 households stranded or isolated by oodwaters. The oods and landslides have also resulted in reported fatalities and substantial damage to housing, livelihoods, agriculture, sheries, livestock and public infrastructure, generating signicant humanitarian needs that require sustained response and recovery support.
