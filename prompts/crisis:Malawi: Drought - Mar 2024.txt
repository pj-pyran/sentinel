Crisis / situation: Malawi: Drought - Mar 2024

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-11-25) ---
Description of the Event
Date when the trigger was met
21-10-2026
What happened, where and when?
Based on information available as of 21 October 2026, multiple forecasting and food security analyses indicate an elevated risk of severe drought impacts across southern Malawi and parts of the central region during the 2026/27 agricultural season. The latest FEWS NET Food Security Outlook projects worsening food security outcomes and estimates that between 1.5 and 1.99 million people may require humanitarian assistance should forecast conditions materialize, with needs expected to peak between January and March 2027.
These projections are supported by the 2026/27 Seasonal Climate Outlook issued by the Department of Climate Change and Meteorological Services (DCCMS), which forecasts delayed rainfall onset, prolonged dry spells, below-normal rainfall, and heightened drought risk across much of southern Malawi. Earlier regional analyses, including the ROSEA El Niño Preparedness Alert, also identified Malawi among countries facing increased risk of drought-related impacts associated with a strong El Niño event.
Taken together, these forecasts present a strong evidence base for anticipatory action before humanitarian impacts escalate. The anticipated consequences include reduced agricultural production, declining household income, reduced access to water, increased food insecurity, deterioration of nutrition outcomes, and heightened vulnerability among households with limited coping capacity.
In response to these forecast triggers, Malawi Red Cross Society (MRCS) seeks support through the DREF to implement anticipatory actions in five high-risk districts: Chikwawa, Balaka, Neno, Blantyre, and Mulanje. The operation will focus on protecting livelihoods, strengthening preparedness, improving household water security, promoting early action behaviour, and reducing the anticipated humanitarian impacts of drought before conditions worsen. Monitoring and rapid assessments will be used to track evolving risks and support operational decision-making throughout implementation.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-11-25) ---
WFP is supporting the Government-led 2026/27 Lean Season Response in Chikwawa, Nsanje and Phalombe, where beneficiary targeting and registration are underway ahead of planned food distributions.
From November, funding shortfalls are forcing WFP to reduce assistance in Dzaleka Refugee Camp from more than 65,000 refugees and asylum seekers to 35,000 of the most vulnerable. Additional funding is critical to extend assistance to more families in need.
WFP is supporting Government-led anticipatory action to protect food production, livelihoods and food security for 9,052 at-risk farming households in Nsanje, Mangochi and Zomba from forecast drought. Support includes agricultural extension services ahead of the planting season and cash assistance during the peak lean season.
