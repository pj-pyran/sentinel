Crisis / situation: Somalia: Dengue Outbreak - Feb 2026

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-09-02) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

In 2026, Somalia faces a deteriorating humanitarian situation driven by multi-season drought, escalating displacement, rising malnutrition and food insecurity, weakened basic services, and reduced operational capacity following funding declines since 2025. These factors have deepened vulnerabilities, particularly among children and displaced populations, with increasing risks of malnutrition, disease outbreaks, and protection concerns.
In the first quarter of 2026, UNICEF and partners sustained delivery of life-saving services. Over 89,000 children were treated for severe acute malnutrition, over 32,000 vaccinated against measles, more than 119,000 people received critical WASH supplies, and 57,000 learners were supported with access to education. In addition, nearly 6,000 children and caregivers accessed community-based mental health and psychosocial support services.
The UNICEF 2026 Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC) appeal remains critically underfunded at 18 per cent, significantly limiting the scale and continuity of response at a time when needs are rapidly increasing due to the ongoing drought and other shocks.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-09-02) ---
Highlights

Somalia’s humanitarian situation deteriorated sharply during the first half of 2026. The humanitarian caseload expanded from the 4.8 million people initially identified in the HNRP to approximately 6 million by June, driven by drought and other climate shocks, disease outbreaks, conflict, insecurity, displacement and contracting resources. An estimated 1.9 million children required acute malnutrition treatment, including 493,000 with severe acute malnutrition.
Despite limited funding, UNICEF maintained lifesaving assistance, treating approximately 200,000 children with severe acute malnutrition; providing 235,000 people with critical WASH supplies; enabling 309,000 people to access primary health care; and supporting 62,000 children with emergency education.
UNICEF’s Humanitarian Action for Children appeal was only 24 per cent funded at mid-year. Education and Child Protection are unfunded at over 90 percent. Severe underfunding constrained service coverage, supplies and partner capacity as risks for children intensified, leaving critical gaps among most vulnerable and hard-to-reach communities in Somalia.
