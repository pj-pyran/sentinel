Crisis / situation: Mauritania

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-09-21) ---
Mauritania is one of the principal refugee-hosting countries in West Africa. As of July 2026, the country hosts an estimated 305,000 refugees and asylum-seekers, the overwhelming majority of whom have fled insecurity and conflict in neighbouring Mali. While most refugees are concentrated in the region of Hodh Chargui in the southeast, a number of refugees and asylum-seekers also reside in urban areas, particularly Nouakchott and Nouadhibou. Hodh Chargui hosts approximately 290,000 refugees and asylum-seekers, representing over a third of the region's population. Established in 2012 and located in Hodh Chargui, Mbera Camp currently hosts around 120,000 refugees, while more than 170,000 live among host communities across the region. The volatile security situation in Mali continues to drive new arrivals. In this context, UNHCR and partners remain prepared to respond to further influxes while supporting the Mauritanian Government in strengthening inclusive systems and equitable access to protection and essential services At the same time, urban areas play an increasingly important role in Mauritania's forced displacement context. Currently, close to 14,000 refugees and asylum-seekers are living in Nouakchott and Nouadhibou, including refugees from Mali and 47 other nationalities. These cities are also key transit points for mixed movements towards North Africa and Europe. In line with its country strategy, UNHCR supports the Government in refugee registration and refugee status determination (RSD), while providing protection and promoting solutions for refugees and asylum-seekers.

KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• The Islamic Republic of Mauritania faces significant challenges related to land degradation and desertification, with vast portions of its territory being arid and only 0.4 percent of land classified as arable. Desertification drastically reduces soil fertility and productive capacity, negatively impacting both agricultural and pastoral livelihoods.
• According to the November 2025 Cadre Harmonisé analysis’ projections, 612,395 people people (12 percent of the population) could face crisis-or higher- level of food insecurity during the ongoing lean season (June–September), with Hodh Chargui (HEC) being the regions with the highest rates. In HEC alone, 46,268 in-camp refugees and 66,038 out-of-camp refugees are currently projected in phase 3+ for the 2026 lean season.
