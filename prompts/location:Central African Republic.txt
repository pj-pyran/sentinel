Crisis / situation: Central African Republic

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-11-11) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

In the south-east of the country, humanitarian partners are strengthening Ebola virus disease prevention efforts.
Mbomou: 357 households affected by heavy rainfall received emergency shelter kits and non-food items.
In Vakaga, insecurity continues to restrict humanitarian movements and access to affected populations.

GENERAL CONTEXT
Vakaga Prefecture – North-East

Armed clashes reported since 30 October in the areas surrounding Birao, Boromata, Tiringoulou and Am-Dafock continue to affect the operational environment. Ongoing insecurity has resulted in severe restrictions on humanitarian movements, particularly in the Boromata area, limiting humanitarian actors’ access to affected populations. The security situation has also forced communities to reduce their movements and has disrupted agricultural activities. In addition, transhumance-related concerns remain significant amid the onset of cross-border livestock movements, which could further exacerbate local tensions.

HUMANITARIAN NEEDS AND RESPONSE
Multisectoral
Nana-Gribizi Prefecture – Centre

On 4 November, a joint mission conducted by CIAUD, EDA, UNICEF, URU and OCHA to the Lazaret IDP site in Kaga-Bandoro highlighted a deterioration in living conditions for the 4,443 internally displaced persons (1,085 households) residing on the site since 2017 and wishing to integrate into the local community. Priority needs include protection, particularly freedom of movement and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response, as well as access to safe drinking water, shelter, education, healthcare and food assistance. Only two of the site's 13 water points are currently functional, while many shelters are in an advanced state of deterioration. Access to healthcare has also declined following the withdrawal of humanitarian organizations, with financial constraints limiting the use of available services. Despite some livelihood support initiatives, food insecurity remains a major concern. For the displaced population, the main priority remains the implementation of a durable local integration solution to end their displacement. Without such a solution, humanitarian needs are likely to persist, requiring continued assistance to affected populations.

Health
Mbomou Prefecture – South-East

No confirmed cases of Ebola virus disease (EVD) have been recorded in the Central African Republic (CAR). However, the ongoing outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) continues to pose a risk of importation, particularly in border areas of the south-east of the country. On 6 November, a joint mission was deployed to Béma, a locality bordering Yakoma in the DRC, where confirmed cases have been reported. The mission aimed to support preparedness and prevention efforts against a potential introduction of the disease into CAR. An assessment conducted by the World Health Organization (WHO), the World Food Programme (WFP) and OCHA found that community awareness of EVD was generally good, owing to the activities of the local communication committee. In addition, Red Cross volunteers trained in infection prevention and control are available in the area. Several priority actions remain urgent, including strengthening surveillance at all points of entry along the Mbomou River, deploying screening personnel at informal crossing points, completing the two isolation centres under construction in Béma, and operationalizing the rapid response team. WHO has provided the Ouango-Gambo Health District with preparedness and response equipment, including beds, tents, sprayers, stretchers, personal protective equipment (PPE) and chlorine solutions. WFP organized a two-day training session for community health workers on the nutritional management of patients in isolation.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-11-11) ---
In the second quarter of 2026, the humanitarian response involving cash transfers (cash and vouchers) in the Central African Republic mobilized 16 actors in 27 subprefectures, totaling 4.5 million USD. These interventions provided assistance to 128,400 people, representing nearly 10% of the 1.3 million people
targeted by the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan (HNRP). Against a backdrop of limited resources and persistent humanitarian needs, the actors focused their efforts on the most vulnerable populations and the highest-priority areas.
Compared to Q2 2025, geographic coverage decreased from 30 to 27 subprefectures (-10%), while the number of beneficiaries fell from 160,800 to 128,400 people (-20%). The amounts transferred also decreased from 5.4 to 4.5 million USD (-16.7%), reducing the actors’ ability to maintain the level of coverage achieved the previous year.
Food security remains the primary sector for the use of cash transfers, with 93,500 beneficiaries in 2026 compared to 76,600 in 2025. This increase reflects the persistence of food insecurity and the priority given to meeting basic needs. Cash transfers account for more than 97% of beneficiaries, largely due to the limited availability of digital financial services in several intervention areas. The fact that the number of sectors using cash transfers has remained stable further confirms the established role of cash as a multisectoral approach to humanitarian response.
Two major developments marked Q2 2026. First, funding for Multi-Purpose Cash Transfers (MPCT) increased from $17,400 to $96,000 (+452%), raising the coverage of their financial needs from 1.8% to 7.9%. Second, the response shifted its focus more toward emergency assistance: 89,900 people (70%) received emergency assistance, compared to 38,500 people (30%) who benefited from resilience-building activities. This trend illustrates the prioritization decisions made in a context of limited resources, with an emphasis on basic needs, while highlighting the importance of maintaining resilience interventions to provide sustainable support to vulnerable populations.
