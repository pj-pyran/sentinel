Crisis / situation: United Republic of Tanzania

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-05-19) ---
As of 31 March 2026, Tanzania hosts 164,901 refugees and asylum seekers, mainly from Burundi 77,032 and the DRC 87,163 and 706 from other countries who live in two camps, while some refugees live in villages and three old settlements in Kigoma, Katavi, and Tabora regions.
In collaboration with the Government, UNHCR, and partners, have been receiving asylum seekers fleeing violent clashes in the DRC. In March 2026, a total of 45 new arrivals were received in Tanzania, bringing the total number of arrivals to 4,243 since renewed violence in eastern DRC in January 2025.
UNHCR continues to provide protection and assistance to persons of concern. Some services, including primary health care and education, are also extended to the host community. In addition, UNHCR seeks durable solutions for refugees.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-05-18) ---
The Nduta Refugee Camp, which hosted more than 60,000 Burundian refugees since 1 October 2015 was officially closed on 30 April 2026 following the end of repatriation. As a result of the camp’s closure, WFP operations in Nduta have ceased, and will now focus on Nyarugusu Camp that hosts 107,968 refugees including 20,779 Burundians.
The CO conducted its first strategic quarterly donor briefing of 2026 on 16 April, bringing together 15 donors to share programme updates across refugee response, resilience, nutrition and school feeding.
As the result of the middle east war and global fuel prices rise, Tanzania cap fuel prices surged to record highs in April 2026 (33 percent) compared to March 2026, and further by 8 percent for petrol and 12 percent for diesel in May 2026, compared to April 2026. The CO is monitoring how this will affect operations and food security.
