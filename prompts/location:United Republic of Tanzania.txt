Crisis / situation: United Republic of Tanzania

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-08-18) ---
WFP continued life-saving assistance to 87,917 refugees in Nyarugusu Camp through General Food Distribution (GFD), at the 50 percent ration while maintaining supplementary feeding at 100 percent.
WFP supported Tanzania’s first operational test of the National Humanitarian Logistics SOPs through environmental sustainability training and an emergency simulation, strengthening national logistics preparedness, inter-agency coordination, information management and nationally led emergency response planning.
In Nyarugusu, WFP strengthened accountability by expanding mobile feedback helpdesks, improving refugees’ access to complaints and feedback mechanism and enabling faster identification of assistance concerns.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-08-17) ---
As of 31 July 2026, Tanzania hosts 130,924 refugees and asylum seekers, mainly from the DRC and Burundi. Of these, 89,574 live in Nyarugusu Camp, while 41,350 (mostly Burundian) refugees live in urban areas, villages, and old settlements in Dar es Salaam, Kigoma, Katavi, and Tabora regions.
In collaboration with the Government, UNHCR, and partners, have been receiving asylum seekers fleeing violent clashes in the DRC. In July 2026, a total of 175 new arrivals were received in Tanzania, bringing the total number of arrivals to 4,566 since renewed violence in eastern DRC in January 2025.
UNHCR continues to provide protection and assistance to the people we serve. Some services, including primary health care and education, are also extended to the host community. In addition, UNHCR seeks durable solutions for refugees.
