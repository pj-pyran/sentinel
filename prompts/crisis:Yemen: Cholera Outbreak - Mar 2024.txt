Crisis / situation: Yemen: Cholera Outbreak - Mar 2024

--- Report 1: IMC (2026-11-05) ---
FAST FACTS

319,356 people from 53,226 families have been displaced across Yemen—an increase of approximately 27% from our last situation report.
Eighteen of 19 International Medical Corps-supported health facilities remain operational across the west coast, despite continuing access constraints.

OUR FOOTPRINT

Across Yemen, International Medical Corps’ programming serves approximately 3.3 million people across north and south Yemen.
Our lifesaving multi-sectoral assistance prioritizes active conflict zones and high-risk environments, strengthening community resilience, self-reliance and capacity to withstand recurrent shocks and vulnerabilities in a protracted crisis.

OUR RESPONSE

Our mobile medical response has expanded to Abyan and Lahj, where two teams have provided 1,219 medical consultations, screened 444 children and 118 pregnant and lactating women for acute malnutrition, delivered 258 immunization services and conducted 67 antenatal care consultations.
Our supported health facilities have provided 23,347 medical consultations to people on the west coast.
We have provided 56 metric tons of essential medicines and medical supplies across Aden, Al Dhale’e, Lahj and Taiz and facilitated the transfer of 120 cartons of ready-to-use therapeutic food to Al Mudarabah and Ras Al Arah districts to address critical nutrition needs among displaced children.

--- Report 2: Health Cluster (2026-11-05) ---
Highlights
• Casualties rose again, led by Lahj. The HEOC register reached 9,730 (7,959 injured, 1,771 deaths) by 30 October, +658 in five days. The period average of ~132 per day is up from ~93 in Sitrep #9. Lahj accounted for 309 casualties (47%).
• Marib recorded the most deaths. Marib reported 36 deaths among 172 casualties (21%). On 30 October alone it reported 117 casualties, including 20 deaths, from Al-Jubah, Harib and the Al-Jawf front.
• West Coast services keep shrinking. In Al-Waziyah, all three partner-supported facilities are non-functional, and October service data from Dhubab could not be collected because of access constraints.
• Displacement has passed 300,000 people (IOM, 1 November), almost all in the past two months. More than 43,000 people have fled Al-Madaribah wa Al-Arah (Lahj) since early October, Aden now hosts more than 56,000, and prices of essential items in Taiz city have risen three- to fivefold.
• New IDP sites are opening without site management. The CCCM October master list records 28 new or reopened sites hosting 21,273 people, none yet covered by a CCCM partner, and 105 sites (74,456 people) in western Taiz districts where the controlling authority changed in October.
• Services reach a fraction of the displaced. WHO funds nine mobile circuits against a plan range of 30–40, and four fixed EMT Type 1 sites against 25–35. Interagency coordination with the Executive Unit for IDPs is needed to establish appropriate gathering points around which services can be planned.
• Cholera continues to decline from its W40 peak (639 in W40, 443 in W43) in the five most-affected governorates; W44 is about 23% incomplete. WHO has prioritised 23 districts (2.25 million people) for an ICG request for single-dose oral cholera vaccine.
• Supplies are still not moving freely. Sana'a airbridge flights on 29 October and 3 November were cancelled, and WHO's second consignment to Taiz city (a 10 MT test shipment with WFP) is awaiting a movement date.
• Only 12% of the plan is funded and the support is short in duration. WHO received US$3.7 million against the US$31 million 90-day plan, leaving US$27.3 million outstanding. A further US$7.3 million is under discussion with ECHO and CERF UF; if all arrive, 65% remains unfunded. This is critical to ensure continuity of the response beyond December 2026.
• Four links in the trauma chain remain unfunded: referral dispatch, a Tier 2 surgical site, ICU surge with fuel for 77 facilities, and rehabilitation after discharge. Without rehabilitation, patients who cannot be safely discharged occupy surgical beds, reducing capacity to admit the next arrivals.
