Crisis / situation: Yemen: Cholera Outbreak - Mar 2024

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-10-23) ---
Rising Displacement, Cholera and Acute Malnutrition Amid Taiz Escalation
Highlights

Escalating hostilities around Taiz and disruption of the main southern supply route are restricting civilian movement and deliveries of essential supplies.
IOM recorded over 200K people displaced (estimated 110,000 children) from 3 September to 10 October. New displacement rose by 26 per cent in the week ending 6 October.
During the period from 4 to 10 October 2026, the largest concentration of displaced people was reported in Taiz Governorate, hosting 19,950 people (70 per cent)
In Taiz, 98 health facilities are partially functional or non-functional. In Lahj, 85 schools have closed because of fighting.
UNICEF requires US$13.7 million to expand and sustain the response

SITUATION OVERVIEW
Hostilities around Taiz have intensified, disrupting the Taiz–Aden Road. Infrastructure damage and contested access have effectively cut the city’s main southern supply route, restricting humanitarian and commercial deliveries. Fighting continues around Taiz and along the corridors, increasing risks for families seeking safety and disrupting access to essential services.
In Aden, drone activity and anti-aircraft fire raise concerns for civilian safety and humanitarian operations. On 7 October, the Ministry of Transport reported missile and drone attacks at Aden International Airport, prompting the diversion of an incoming civilian flight and evacuation of passengers and staff. Further disruption could affect civilian travel and humanitarian access.

--- Report 2: Health Cluster (2026-10-23) ---
Highlights

Casualties continue to rise on four fronts and a new one has opened in Shabwah. The HEOC register reached 8,513 (6,939 injured, 1,574 deaths) by 19 October, +1,340 in eight days. Daily totals eased to 102–142 between 15 and 18 October, then jumped to 428 on 19 October, the highest single-day report this month, driven by Shabwah (Jardan, 205 casualties, 47 deaths) and Lahj (140).
Lethality is up. Deaths made up 21.8% of casualties this period (cumulative 18.5%). Marib accounted for 77 deaths in five days and Shabwah for 51 in two.
Taiz city remains the most critical gap. The main Haygat Al-Abd road is cut; 42 facilities in the city report severe shortages of medicines, fuel, oxygen, blood and reagents. The Joint Medical Emergency Committee estimates US$624,900 to keep five public hospitals running for three months (fuel and staff incentives).
Displacement keeps growing: IOM DTM counts 256,591 people (42,764 HH) as of 19 October, up from 201,217 on 7 October. RRM has reached 24,164 HH; in Taiz 629 HH were on alert on 19–20 October and none could be assisted.
Cholera is trending down but not over. Suspected cases in the five most-affected governorates fell from 639 (W40) to 562 (W41); W42 is incomplete. Part of the drop may reflect reduced care-seeking in conflict areas. MoPHP has asked WHO to support 10 DTCs for three months; supplies reached 8 priority DTCs on 15–18 October.
WHO delivered 168 trauma and emergency kits (US$121,198) to five hospitals in Abyan, Aden, Lahj and Marib on 13 October, completed cholera supply deliveries to 8 priority DTCs in Aden, Abyan and Al-Dhale'e on 15–18 October, and began emergency dispatches of TESK, IEHK and medicines to Ataq on 20 October in response to the Shabwah escalation.
