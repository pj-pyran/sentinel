Crisis / situation: Bangladesh: Measles Outbreak - Mar 2026

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-06-29) ---
KEY UPDATES

2026 JOINT RESPONSE PLAN (JRP): UNHCR, in close coordination with UN partners and the Government of Bangladesh, presented the 2026 update of the Joint Response Plan on 20 May, appealing for USD 710.5 million to reach up to 1.56 million people, including refugees and Bangladeshi host communities. The hyper-prioritized appeal — a 26% reduction from the 2025 JRP — covers only the minimum required to sustain lifesaving assistance. Reduced funding affects services across all sectors, including resilience and preparedness activities. As of 30 April, the appeal was 63% funded according to an internal analysis by the Rohingya Coordination Team. Read the updated Joint Response Plan. Read the press release.
TARGETING &amp; PRIORITIZATION EXERCISE (TPE): WFP introduced targeted and prioritized food assistance in the Cox’s Bazar camps and on Bhasan Char, with households categorized into three tiers by food insecurity level based on income and vulnerability data. From 1 April, food insecure households will receive USD 7 (USD 8 on Bhasan Char, reflecting higher market costs), highly food insecure households will receive USD 10 per person per month (USD 11 on Bhasan Char); and extremely food insecure households will continue to receive the full ration of USD 12 (USD 13 on Bhasan Char). UNHCR worked closely with WFP on the TPE rollout and to engage with community questions and concerns, including a messaging campaign and strengthening complaint and feedback mechanisms through joint WFP-UNHCR help desks at distribution points.
IMPACT OF GULF CRISIS: The global Gulf crisis drove up fuel and energy costs. In April and May, Bangladesh saw retail prices rise nearly 30% for liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and 12- 16% for octane, diesel and petrol. Impacts include increased transportation and generator costs, as well as higher financial requirements for LPG in 2026 than initially planned. Sustained LPG funding is critical: as a cleaner alternative to firewood, it keeps families from collecting and burning wood from surrounding forests — which raises health and protection risks, including tensions with locals. The supplier met all LPG distribution requirements for April and May 2026, and UNHCR continues to closely monitor the supply chain and delivery capacity. At the current supplier rate, LPG funding is expected to cover needs through end of September. Together, UNHCR and IOM provide LPG to some 230,000 Rohingya refugee households, including new arrival families, while Islamic Relief-Bangladesh provides to 7,500 households on Bhasan Char.
IDENTIFYING &amp; ASSISTING NEW ARRIVALS: Rohingya and other residents fleeing conflict in Myanmar’s Rakhine State continued to seek safety in Bangladesh, despite a closed-border policy that has limited safe access to asylum. Between 1 January and 31 May 2026, 1,893 individuals were reportedly denied access to Bangladesh, bringing the total to 14,380 since 2024, though many incidents continue to go unreported. Over the same period, 2,260 newly arrived individuals were biometrically identified through a joint exercise with the Government of Bangladesh, taking the total to 152,029 individuals (39,881 families) identified and issued family cards since the exercise launched in 2025 — some 12% of the current population. Unlike regular refugee registration, which has been suspended since May 2022, individual refugee identity documents are not issued through this process. UNHCR continues to advocate with government counterparts for full registration.

--- Report 2: UN RC Bangladesh (2026-06-28) ---
Bangladesh has reported more than 95,760 suspected and 11,390 confirmed measles cases and 596 suspected and 93 confirmed deaths across all 64 districts as of 25 June, with children under five accounting for 81 per cent of reported cases. Recent reporting suggests suspected cases are gradually declining, with the latest 7-day average at around 980 cases per day. However, transmission remains significant, with 996 suspected cases reported in the last 24 hours. More than 18.4 million children have been vaccinated through the nationwide measles rubella (MR) campaign, surpassing the initial target of 18 million children and reaching 102 per cent coverage. In the Rohingya refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar, the MR vaccination and Vitamin A supplementation campaign reached 94 per cent of the 178,024 targeted children. In Bhasan Char, the campaign achieved 99 per cent coverage.
