Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: UN Women (2026-09-07) ---
The Alert identifies significant protection and inclusion concerns in holding centres and temporary shelters, including overcrowding, lack of privacy and safe spaces, GBV and exploitation risks, inadequate WASH facilities, caste-based discrimination and barriers for persons with disabilities. It also highlights gaps in maternal and reproductive health services, food and nutrition, livelihoods and the growing burden of unpaid care work on women.
A central message is the critical role of women’s rights organizations (WROs) as frontline humanitarian actors. The Alert calls for WROs and organizations representing marginalized groups to be meaningfully involved in assessments, decision-making, targeting, monitoring and recovery, with direct and flexible funding to sustain their response capacity.
Key recommendations include ensuring safe and accessible shelters and WASH facilities, strengthening GBV and MHPSS services and referral pathways, maintaining maternal and newborn care, ensuring women’s equitable access to food, cash and livelihoods, systematically collecting sex-, age- and disability-disaggregated data, and establishing accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms.
UN Women is supporting the Government-led response through humanitarian coordination, advocacy and partnerships with WROs, organizations of persons with disabilities, youth-led and other community organizations. It is also mobilizing resources for its women-led Community Kitchen initiative, which provides emergency food assistance while supporting women’s leadership, livelihoods and longer-term recovery.

--- Report 2: Tzu Chi Foundation (2026-09-07) ---
SITUATION UPDATE
Heavy monsoon flooding across Dhading District, Bagmati Province, has severely damaged critical public health, educational, and transportation infrastructure in Galchhi Rural Municipality.
Thick silt and mud deposits, averaging up to 60cm deep, blocked primary access roads and disabled essential utility systems, disrupting healthcare delivery and isolating local student populations.
Buddhist Tzu Chi Foundation is coordinating with local administrators, hospital directors, military and police personnel, and community volunteer networks to mobilize emergency cleanup operations, restore public utilities, and prepare necessary relief distributions.
OPERATIONAL RESPONSE
Bhagawati Secondary School Emergency Cleanup
Bhagawati Secondary School in Dhading District was heavily impacted by mud and water deposits, rendering the school campus unsafe for classes. A massive emergency sanitation operation mobilised over 200 personnel, including parents, teachers, students, local villagers, and military personnel, to clear mud and sanitise the premises.
To restore water supply, the cleanup team inspected the school's 168-meter-deep well and installed large-capacity water-storage tanks to secure sanitary water for campus cleaning and long-term student needs.
A critical bottleneck remains: the collapse of a key connecting bridge has severely disrupted student commutes. Students who previously took a short 20-minute walk must now take a hazardous 1-hour detour. Tzu Chi and local authorities are currently evaluating safe alternative pedestrian routes

--- Report 3: ETC (2026-09-07) ---
This Situation Report provides an update on the humanitarian situation following the flash floods that struck Nepal on 26 August 2026. Since the activation of the Logistics &amp; Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) 1 on 02 September, the LTC Telecoms Area has supported humanitarian partners through telecommunications coordination, assessments, connectivity services and information management. The report highlights the latest situation, telecommunications impacts and LTC Telecoms activities.
Summary Points
• The 26 August flash floods have caused widespread humanitarian impacts across northern Nepal, with more than 1,250 fatalities, thousands of people still missing and over 84,000 people requiring humanitarian assistance as response efforts transition from search and rescue to sustained humanitarian support.
• Telecommunications services have been restored on an emergency basis across the affected areas, with priority mobile network sites returned to service through temporary power solutions. Some locations remain dependent on generators until electricity and fibre connectivity are fully restored.
• Following the activation of the LTC on 02 September, the LTC Telecoms Area is supporting the response through telecommunications coordination, assessments, connectivity planning, information management and the deployment of an LTC Telecoms Coordinator to Nepal.

--- Report 4: UNICEF (2026-09-07) ---
Highlights
• Nepal flash flood has affected an estimated 84,270 people, including 32,021 children and 1,854 persons with disabilities across 17 municipalities in six districts.
• Essential services for children and families are severely disrupted. Fifty water supply systems and 20 schools have been damaged, while four health facilities have been destroyed and two partially damaged.
• UNICEF is delivering lifesaving support across WASH, child protection, health, nutrition, education and risk communication, working closely with the Government of Nepal and humanitarian partners to reach the most affected communities.
• Around 48,000 people have been reached with essential WASH supplies, while damaged water supply systems were restored in five priority districts.
• 2,700 cartons of ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) have been provided for the treatment of children with severe acute malnutrition.
• Psychological first aid (PFA) and community-based psychosocial support has been provided to 1,029 flood-affected people, including 462 children, while specialized mental health and psychosocial support has reached 1,714 people, including 574 children and adolescents.
• Learning materials have been dispatched to support continuity of education, including 2,694 student kits, 148 teacher kits and 43 early childhood development kits.
• US$17.2 million is required to meet the immediate needs of children and families affected by the flash floods, with additional resources needed to support recovery and rehabilitation efforts.

--- Report 5: CORUS (2026-09-07) ---
Key figures

● 5,053 people missing
● 1,259 deaths recorded
● 12,038 people rescued
Highlights
● Corus teams are on the ground in Rasuwa and Nuwakot, assessing needs and coordinating with local authorities.
● Distribution of 500 hygiene kits and 1,500 sets of clothing is now underway for people affected by the flooding.
● Corus is procuring two 5,000-liter water tanks to help address urgent water storage and distribution needs identified through the WASH cluster in Nuwakot.
Situation Overview
On August 26, 2026, an ice-and-rock avalanche in the upper Bhote Koshi watershed near the ChinaNepal border triggered a massive debris flow and flash flood that swept down the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli river system. The disaster caused extensive loss of life and widespread damage to homes, roads, bridges, hydropower facilities and other critical infrastructure. Rasuwa and Nuwakot are the two most severely affected districts. Local education authorities estimate that approximately 600 students remain unaccounted for across the two districts, although verification is ongoing.
Search, rescue and relief operations continue. Some communities remain isolated because roads and bridges have been damaged or washed away. An alternate road route to Rasuwa has opened, but conditions remain rough and slippery, particularly with continued rainfall. Air transport remains important for reaching communities that cannot be accessed by road and delivering relief supplies.
Authorities continue to monitor upstream water accumulations and debris blockages. Further flooding, landslides and debris movement remain possible.

--- Report 6: Plan International (2026-09-07) ---
A major flash flood entered Nepal through the Bhote Koshi River at approximately 9:00 AM on 26 August 2026, causing significant loss and damage across Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts. Timure, Rasuwagadhi and Syabrubesi are among the worst affected areas, with impacts reported on communities, markets, roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure.
The cause of the flood remains under investigation, with authorities assessing the possibility of a glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF) or another upstream hydrological event. The Government of Nepal has mobilised security forces for search, rescue and relief operations and issued alerts for communities downstream along the Trishuli River corridor.
Our humanitarian response
Plan International Nepal is responding to the flash floods and landslides in the most affected areas, prioritising children and adolescents, girls, young women, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities and other marginalised groups.

--- Report 7: IFRC (2026-09-07) ---
TIMELINE
26 August 2026: A massive surge of water enters the Bhote Koshi River bordering China. Floodwaters rapidly impact Timure, Rasuwaghadi, and Syafrubeshi towns in Rasuwa District and other towns further downstream in Nuwakot and Dhading Districts. The Government of Nepal issues evacuation orders and mobilises security forces. The Prime Minister’s Office convenes a high-level emergency meeting to assess the evolving situation and coordinate response measures. Additionally, the government holds a second emergency meeting and declares three districts and 15 municipalities as crisis-affected areas for three months while expediting relief and rescue operations.
26 August 2026: The NRCS activates its EOC on 26 August 2026. The NRCS is supporting the response through the mobilisation of staff and volunteers, search and rescue support, first aid and health assistance, emergency relief distributions, shelter and household items, WASH support, and dead body management, including the recovery, safe handling, identification, and dignified management of deceased persons. Other humanitarian partners are supporting complementary food, shelter, health, WASH, and protection activities, while international assistance and funding continue to be mobilised.
26 August 2026: The IFRC allocates CHF 999,934 from its Disaster Response Emergency Fund (DREF) to support the immediate response, targeting 20,000 of the most affected people in Rasuwa, Nuwakot, and Dhading. A CHF 100,000 Emergency Advance Payment request is signed enabling immediate access to funding.
28 August 2026: The IFRC launches a Federation-wide Emergency Appeal of CHF 25 million (CHF 18 million secretariat) to provide early recovery and recovery support to approximately 28,000 people in three districts.
28 August 2026: Multiple IFRC surge role alerts are issued, and the Regional Operational Coordinator, Communications Coordinator, and WASH Coordinator arrive in Nepal to provide technical support.
DESCRIPTION OF THE EVENT
On 26 August 2026, a major ice–rock avalanche occurred in the upper Bhote Koshi watershed near the China–Nepal border. An estimated 1km2 of ice and rock detached from a slope, picking up loose proglacial sediments downstream and transforming into a highly mobile sediment- and water-laden debris flow. Initial assessments suggest that the resulting outburst flood in the upper watershed was an order of magnitude or more greater than normal seasonal flooding.
A powerful debris flow of sediment moved rapidly downstream, causing widespread destruction across Rasuwa, Nuwakot, and Dhading districts. Reports indicate that water levels in the Trishuli River rose up to nine metres above normal levels, inundating settlements and damaging critical infrastructure, including roads, bridges, schools, police stations, markets, health facilities, telecommunication towers, electricity poles, financial institutions, vehicles, hydrometeorological stations, customs infrastructure, and multiple hydropower projects. Timure border market was among the hardest-hit areas. The disaster has disrupted transportation along several key highways and roads, while authorities have restricted accessto river corridors due to ongoing risks. The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) issued a red alert for downstream districts, while the Government of Nepal convened an Emergency Disaster Council meeting chaired by the Prime Minister to coordinate search and rescue operations, damage assessments, and humanitarian response. Relief supplies have been mobilised from the Kathmandu Humanitarian Staging Area, with health response coordination led through national emergency mechanisms and partners. The scale of the physical damage, service disruption, and impacts on communities along the river corridor has created significant humanitarian needs requiring immediate relief and early recovery support.
