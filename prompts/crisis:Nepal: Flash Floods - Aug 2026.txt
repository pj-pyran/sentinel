Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: Govt. Nepal (2026-10-12) ---
Brief Summary
A low-pressure system originating over the Bay of Bengal resulted in heavy to very heavy rainfall across various parts of Nepal from 25 to 28 September 2026. The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) and the Center for Hydrology and Water Resources Research (CHWRR) initiated continuous monitoring and analysis of the development, movement, and potential impacts of the weather system from 23 September 2026 . Through the Flood Forecast Bulletin issued on 24 September 2026, advance information was disseminated regarding the potential risks of heavy rainfall, flooding, and landslides.
On the same day, the Hon. Minister for Energy, Water Resources and Irrigation and the Hon. Minister for Home Affairs visited the Flood Forecasting Information Center and were briefed on the prevailing risk situation. To further strengthen preparedness and early warning measures, from 25 September 2026, the flood forecasting lead time for major rivers was extended from 3 days to 5 days, while the forecasting lead time for flash floods in smaller rivers and streams was increased from 24 hours to 48 hours.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-10-12) ---
As of 2 October 2026, CHF 13.04 million in confirmed multilateral hard pledges recorded against the CHF 18 million IFRC Secretariat Emergency Appeal (approximately 72% per cent). Continued funding is required to sustain the response and scale up early recovery interventions.
On 26 August 2026, an ice-and-rock avalanche occurred in the upper Lhende Khola watershed, around 20 km upstream of the Rasuwagadhi border point. The avalanche triggered a debris flow and severe flooding downstream along the Bhotekoshi–Trishuli–Narayani river system. The flooding caused widespread erosion and sediment deposits and damaged settlements, agricultural land, productive assets and critical infrastructure. The most serious humanitarian impacts happened in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading, although impacts were also reported further downstream along the river corridor.
The extent of infrastructure damage continues to affect access and recovery efforts. According to the Government-led Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment (RDNA)3, around 69 km of road was damaged, including 55 km classified as completely damaged. The assessment also identified damage to 37 motorable bridges and 68 trail-bridge spans. Of the motorable bridges assessed, 33 were reported washed away. Damage to roads and bridges, as well as electricity, water-supply and communication networks, has disrupted essential services, economic activities and access to affected communities. Access remains difficult in some locations, particularly in Rasuwa, where relief supplies continue to be airlifted to communities that cannot be reached by road because of damaged roads and bridges.
According to the latest Government statistics4, dated 1 October 2026, 1,453 people had been reported dead, 5,179 remained reported missing, and 13,795 had been rescued. The number of people reported missing still requires further verification and reconciliation. In the context of the access barriers and the ongoing assessments, the Government RDNA also cautions that figures for deaths, missing people and rescues should not necessarily be treated as separate or mutually exclusive counts.
According to the Nepal Red Cross Society (NRCS) Situation Report5, displacement has decreased as families continue to leave collective accommodation, but considerable temporary shelter and recovery needs remain. NRCS reported that 1,107 people were staying in 21 holding centres across Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading as of its latest update. In the very beginning, there were around 33 holding centers, which are gradually decreasing, depicting movement of people to alternate places. Thus, the number of people staying in these centres does not represent the full displaced population. Some affected families are staying with relatives or host families, while others have moved to different forms of temporary accommodation. The Government has also announced monthly cash support for displaced families choosing to live outside holding centres, providing NPR 15,000 (CHF 82) per month for households of up to four members and an additional NPR 2,000 (CHF 10.75) for each additional household member, for up to six months or until alternative accommodation is arranged. As displacement becomes more dispersed, continued household-level verification remains important to understand which families can safely return home and which will require temporary accommodation, reconstruction support or possible relocation. IFRC-supported ongoing emergency response efforts have distributed shelter items, including NFI sets, mattresses, blankets, etc., to approximately 9,905 people and the operation plans to prioritize emergencies and transition shelter going forward. The IFRC shelter coordination surge supports coordination among humanitarian partners in affected areas. Collectively, 51 actors have provided shelter and essential household items to around 23,000 people (5,900 households), against 25,709 people initially targeted in holding centres. Most households have since moved to temporary sites. By 2 October, basic services and access had largely been restored, except in some hard-to-reach areas of Rasuwa District.
More than a month after the disaster, the response is increasingly moving from immediate lifesaving assistance towards restoring essential services, temporary and transitional shelter and early recovery. This transition is already visible through household assessments, temporary site development and work to rehabilitate damaged water-supply systems, while emergency assistance continues in locations where access remains constrained. Humanitarian needs nevertheless remain, particularly among displaced families and communities that are difficult to reach. Unstable slopes, accumulated sediment, obstructed channels, continuing rainfall and the possibility of further flooding exacerbate the challenges for the affected people. Continuing rainfall is also affecting temporary shelter conditions and has already interrupted some shelter works, increasing the importance of adequate weather protection, sanitation, protection services and winterization as seasonal conditions change.

--- Report 3: Govt. Nepal (2026-10-12) ---
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
On 26 August 2026 (10 Bhadra 2083), a severe flash flood travelled through the Lhende Khola–Bhotekoshi–Trishuli–Narayani river system. It was caused by an ice and rock avalanche, rather than monsoon rainfall. A hanging glacier and rocky mass covering approximately 2.0 km2 broke away at an altitude of about 5,200 metres on the northern side of Langtang Lirung in Rasuwa district and struck the Lhende Glacier at 3,800 metres. The resulting avalanche and subsequent flow down the Lhende Khola generated the flood. The impact produced a strong seismic signal of magnitude 5.2, recorded at 08:37.
Travelling at approximately 188 km/h from its source, the flood covered 20 km within seven to eight minutes and reached the Nepal–China border at Rasuwagadhi at 08:45. It destroyed the hydrological stations at Syafrubesi at 08:50 and Betrawati at 09:20.

--- Report 4: Govt. Nepal (2026-10-12) ---
Highlights
• The Government of Nepal has decided to provide immediate cash relief for temporary accommodation and livelihoods to families with completely destroyed and uninhabitable homes across 15 local levels in Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading,
Tanahun, and Gorkha, with NPR 15,000 per month for families of up to four members and an additional NPR 2,000 per additional member, for up to six months, exclusively for those opting for self-arranged temporary accommodation instead of holding centers.
• Similarly, if any family opts not to avail of the aforementioned cash relief facility, an alternative option has been provided wherein the government itself will arrange temporary housing until permanent housing is constructed; to collect the necessary details regarding this, forms have already been dispatched to all concerned local levels.
• The government has completed the preliminary damage and loss assessment, while the Post- Disaster Needs Assessment (PDNA) process is currently underway, based on which necessary resources will be secured to accelerate reconstruction and rehabilitation efforts.
• A total of 339 patients received treatment across 19 health facilities, of whom 271 have been discharged, 32 remain under treatment, 32 were referred to other hospitals, and 4 individuals passed away during treatment.
• According to respective districts information, 825 affected persons are currently taking shelter in 9 holding centers in Rasuwa District, 1,613 in 22 holding centers in Nuwakot District, and 94 in 2 holding centers in Dhading District. In total, 2,532 affected persons are currently taking shelter across 33 holding centers.
• Three Shelter Cluster partner meetings have been successfully conducted, finalizing both the ‘Rehabilitation and Shelter Management Action Plan 2083’ and the Detailed Damage Assessment (DDA) Action Plan.
• In collaboration with the Department of Urban Development and Building Construction (DUDBC) and partner organizations, various prototype options for 1-room, 2-room, and 3/4-room emergency transitional shelters have been prepared and discussed.
• For the first time in Nepal following the Bhote Koshi floods, live monitoring of river conditions has been initiated under the leadership of the Center for Hydrology and Water Resources Research (CHWRR) by installing a CCTV camera in Timure, approximately 70 meters above the river level. Its live feed is connected to CHWRR’s 24/7 operated flood forecasting service.
