Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-09-04) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
The 26 August flash floods in central Nepal have caused significant loss of life, displacement and damage to homes, roads, bridges and essential services. Government-led response operations are ongoing, while access constraints and incomplete verification of displacement and housing impacts remain key challenges.
As of 3 September 2026, authorities reported 1,259 bodies recovered and 5,053 people missing. A total of 12,038 people have been rescued, including 583 foreign nationals, with more than 21,402 security personnel mobilized.
Preliminary humanitarian analysis indicates that approximately 84,270 people have been affected across 17 municipalities in six districts. In Nuwakot, initial reports indicate that 1,897 households have been displaced, 1,267 houses completely damaged and 1,253 houses partially damaged.
Health partners continue to assess needs across flood-affected areas and facilities hosting an estimated 5,384 displaced people, while working to maintain essential services, supplies, staffing and referral pathways.
Priority needs include temporary shelter and essential household items, safe water and sanitation, food assistance, health services and referrals, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), protection support and access to affected communities.
Damaged roads and bridges, unstable riverbanks and disrupted telecommunications and electricity continue to constrain assessments, logistics and delivery of assistance to hard-to-reach communities.
IOM is supporting the Government-led response through surge support, site assessments, coordination, health, protection, MHPSS, non-food items (NFI), early recovery and Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) activities to help inform targeted assistance to affected and displaced communities.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-09-04) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• WFP has dispatched 85.8mt of food rations sufficient for 15 days to Dhading, Nuwakot and Rasuwa, along with 2.8 mt of BP-5 and 40 boxes (0.45 mt) of fortified biscuits, reaching 8,000 people.
• WFP has facilitated 101 transport movements to date, including 57 services supporting Government-led relief operations and 44 WFP dispatches delivering assistance to flood-affected communities.
• The Logistics Cluster has been activated and is consolidating partner requirements to determine common logistics services.
Situation Overview
• Relief efforts continue across flood-affected districts following the 26 August floods, as search efforts, access constraints and major needs persist. Reported fatalities have risen to 1,259, with 5,083 people still missing.
• Floods severely damaged settlements, transport infrastructure and key economic assets along the Bhotekoshi–Trishuli corridor, with major disruptions continuing on the Rasuwagadhi and Betrawati–Rasuwagadhi routes and at several critical road and bridge crossings.
• Access to affected communities remains a major operational challenge, particularly in Rasuwa, where three local governments across the Trishuli River are still cut off by road.
• Damaged transport links, unstable terrain, compound by monsoon rains and ongoing flood risks continue to constrain relief operations, and restoration of key infrastructure may take several months.

--- Report 3: HOPE (2026-09-04) ---
Project HOPE has established three mobile medical units in Nepal, in coordination with Volunteer Corps Nepal. Located in hard-to-reach communities along the Trishuli River, health providers with VCN began providing care this week, improving access to essential, lifesaving care for flood survivors and those impacted by last week’s massive glacier avalanche, flash floods, and landslides.
Situational Context
The scale of devastation in Nepal and the enormity of the future recovery process is beginning to set in for affected communities in Nepal. Humanitarian responders and community members are navigating villages where entire stories of buildings are buried under mud. Infrastructure has been deeply impacted, health facilities have been wiped out, and access to essential services is limited. Health threats loom large and the mental health impacts of living through the disaster are becoming more and more apparent as time goes on.
Current reporting varies between local sources and connectivity remains strained, limiting the flow of information. At the time of this report, estimates show at least 1,250 dead and 1,800 missing in Nepal. Chinese authorities have also reported 21 fatalities and 541 people missing in Tibet. These numbers include tourists from dozens of countries and local community members. The affected area is a popular tourist area for hiking, trekking, and religious pilgrimage as Tibet’s Mount Kailash is a sacred site for Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, and other religious groups.
Nepali authorities continue to proceed with mass burials as the number of deceased washing ashore is exceeding morgue capacity in some locations. This disaster left a roughly 40-mile path of destruction, but the flow of debris traveled as far as 62 miles and there are reports of bodies washing ashore up to 250 miles downstream in Nepal and 100 miles downstream in neighboring India.
An estimated 3,458 displaced people are living across 27 displacement sites in Nuwakot and Rasuwa, primarily in schools, according to OCHA reporting**.** Significant access gaps remain in hard-to-reach locations. Parts of Tarkeshwar report receiving no assistance, all wards in Aamachhodingmo remain inaccessible by road, and four rural municipalities in Dhading lack safe drinking water. UNICEF estimates that 22,000 children are without access to safe water.
Damaged infrastructure, including 41 destroyed bridges and blocked highways, is limiting the delivery of available relief supplies to remote communities. The scale of reconstruction needs is also significant as approximately 20,000 homes will need to be rebuilt, including 7,500 that were destroyed, according to Nepal’s National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority.
Access varies across the affected communities, as continued rainfall is exacerbating muddy roads and other disrupted infrastructure. In the coming months, affected communities will need support to meet nearly every basic need across the health; water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH); mental health and psychosocial (MHPSS); food; essential non-food (NFI) item; and other humanitarian sectors.

--- Report 4: OCHA (2026-09-02) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

The Rasuwa floods have claimed 1,003 lives, with 3,916 people still out of contact and 11,814 rescued (NDRRMA, 1 September, 13:00). Six days on, urgent needs include dignified management of the dead, family tracing, and psychosocial support.
3,458 displaced people are living in 27 displacement sites in Nuwakot and Rasuwa, mostly in schools. Key gaps remain in who is displaced, where they are, and their needs, making displacement tracking and multi-sector assessments urgent.
Relief is not reaching all affected areas. Parts of Tarkeshwar (Nuwakot) have received no assistance, all wards of Aamachhodingmo (Rasuwa) remain inaccessible by road, and four rural municipalities in Dhading lack safe drinking water.
Access remains the main constraint. While relief supplies are available, damaged infrastructure, including 41 destroyed bridges and blocked highways, is limiting delivery to remote communities.
Overcrowded camps, unsafe water, damaged health facilities, and disrupted referral pathways are increasing public health and protection risks, especially for women and children.
The Government is leading the response through centralized relief operations and is prioritizing cash assistance, employment support, and nationally led recovery efforts.
Following a joint Resident Coordination Office, OCHA, and WFP mission to Nuwakot on 31 August, partners are establishing a coordination hub in Bidur to strengthen operational coordination and address response gaps.

--- Report 5: IFRC (2026-09-02) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event 26-08-2026
What happened, where and when?
A major flash flood struck Nepal's Rasuwa district on 26 August 2026, sending a powerful surge of water, sediment and boulders through the Bhote Koshi and Trishuli river systems. The flood breached riverbanks and travelled downstream through the Bhote Koshi into the Trishuli River, with extensive impacts into Nuwakot and Dhading Districts. (ICIMOD, 26 August 2026). The flooding was so massive that there are reports that the water level at the Trishuli River rose to 9 meters above the normal level, engulfing towns, bridges, schools, hydropower, etc. on the way downstream. (ICIMOD, 26 August 2026)
The disaster caused severe damage to settlements, markets, police stations, roads, and hydropower facilities, with Timure border market heavily impacted and villages along riverbanks in Rasuwa, Nuwakot, and Dhading reporting destruction. Authorities have reported severe damage in Timure market, customs office, multiple hydropower projects, etc. The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) has issued a red alert for downstream districts including Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, and Chitwan, while the Government convenes an Emergency Disaster Council meeting chaired by the Prime Minister to coordinate search and rescue, preliminary damage assessment, and humanitarian standby operations, with relief dispatches mobilised from the Kathmandu Humanitarian Staging Area and health logistics coordinated through Health Emergency Operation Center, Ministry of Health and Food Safety, and WHO.
Travel has been discouraged on Prithvi Highway, the Galchi-Nuwakot-Rasuwa-Syabrubesi road, Muglin-Narayangadh road. Kathmandu Valley Traffic Police has reportedly halted vehicles heading toward Rasuwa and other affected districts. People working, fishing, or conducting activities near the Trisuli River have been restricted from going into the river corridor.
A DREF of approximately CHF 1 million was launched on 26 August to kickstart the response and CHF 100,000 Emergency Advance Payment was provided as immediate support to the Nepal Red Cross Society (NRCS) to start lifesaving activities. On 27 August, the IFRC launched an Emergency Appeal with a Federation-wide funding requirement of CHF 25 million (18 million IFRC Secretariat) as part of the support to NRCS for conducting response and recovery activities in the affected districts.
