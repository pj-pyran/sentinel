Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: Plan International (2026-10-15) ---
A major flash flood entered Nepal through the Bhote Koshi River at approximately 9:00 AM on 26 August 2026, causing significant loss and damage across Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts. Timure, Rasuwagadhi and Syabrubesi are among the worst affected areas, with impacts reported on communities, markets, roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure.
The cause of the flood remains under investigation, with authorities assessing the possibility of a glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF) or another upstream hydrological event. The Government of Nepal has mobilised security forces for search, rescue and relief operations and issued alerts for communities downstream along the Trishuli River corridor.

--- Report 2: Govt. Nepal (2026-10-15) ---
NDRRMA prepared this report using information received from the District Administration Office and the District and National Emergency Operation Centers. It summarizes search, rescue and relief operations following the Budhigandaki flood in Gorkha and Dhading districts.
SITUATION OVERVIEW (BASED ON TECHNICAL REPORT)
At approximately 04:20 on 11 October 2026 (25 Asoj 2083), a snow avalanche at about 6,000 m elevation and a major landslide involving glacier ice and debris at approximately 5,500 m occurred in the upper reaches of Ungdilung Khola, Chumnubri Rural Municipality–6, between Chamar Himal and Syerang Himal. The area contains relatively weak rocks of the Tibetan Tethys Himalaya.
Smaller landslides were observed both before and after the principal failure. The mixed flow of snow, water and debris traveled south down the steep Ungdilung valley, rising as much as 300 m along the valley sides. At about 2,000 m elevation, large quantities of debris accumulated at the narrow confluence of Ungdilung and Syar Khola, repeatedly blocking and releasing the water flow. Similar temporary blockage may also have occurred at the Syar Khola–Budhigandaki confluence. These processes produced multiple flood surges downstream.
The unusually large volume of debris from Ungdilung Khola appears to have contributed more to flood height than the water impounded in Syar Khola. Mud-laden flooding first reached Salleri at approximately 04:30. The largest surge, with an estimated height of 20–25 m, swept away the hydropower camp at Salleri at approximately 06:30. It damaged buildings, especially at Jagat, and infrastructure including permanent bridges at Soti Khola and across the Budhigandaki.
About one hour before the highest surge, the flow in Syar Khola had stopped. Field observations indicate that water had accumulated for approximately 1.5 km upstream. Smaller surges followed the main surge.
Compared with typical floods, the flow carried an unusually high proportion of fine soil relative to sand and rocks, giving it the characteristics of a viscous mudflow. This is consistent with the soil-rich layered rocks and debris at the source area. A preliminary estimate by the Department of Mines and Geology indicated that the landslide released energy equivalent to that of a magnitude 3.5 earthquake.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-10-14) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

Recovery planning is accelerating as humanitarian partners and local authorities transition from emergency response towards early recovery.
Road access to Rasuwa has significantly improved following the opening of the Acrow Bridge at Betrawati, restoring a key humanitarian and logistics corridor.
A new humanitarian hub in Dhunche will strengthen coordination and support sustained operations in remote and hard-to-reach communities across Rasuwa.

--- Report 4: Govt. Nepal (2026-10-14) ---
NDRRMA prepared this report using information received from the District Administration Office and the District and National Emergency Operation Centres. It summarises search, rescue and relief operations following the Budhigandaki flood in Gorkha.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
At around 5:30 am on 11 October 2026 (25 Asoj 2083), a large landslide occurred between Lokpa and Chumling in Chumnubri Rural Municipality-6, Gorkha district, blocking the Budhigandaki River flowing through the same location. The resulting debris- aden flood caused physical damage. So far, 5 people (2 women and 3 men) from Ungilung, Chumchet, Chumnubri Rural Municipality-6 remain out of contact. To date, a total of 302 people have been rescued (289 by helicopter and 13 by ground), including 19 foreign nationals. Nepal Army helicopters have flown 156 sorties for search, rescue and relief management, with four Nepal Army helicopters in continuous use.

--- Report 5: Govt. Nepal (2026-10-13) ---
This report has been prepared by the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) on the basis of information received from the District Administration Office and the District and National Emergency Operation Centres. It covers the response actions taken in the course of search, rescue and relief management of the situation created by the Budhigandaki flood in Gorkha.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
At around 5:30 am on 11 October 2026 (25 Asoj, 2083), a major landslide occurred between Lokpa and Chumling in Chum Nubri Rural Municipality-6, Gorkha district. The landslide blocked the Budhigandaki River, triggering a large flash flash flood carrying debris and causing physical damage. So far, 5 people remain out of contact, comprising 2 women and 3 men from Ungilung, Chumchet, Chum Nubri Rural Municipality-6. The Nepal Army has rescued 95 people, including 9 foreign nationals, from various affected areas using 4 helicopters.

--- Report 6: WFP (2026-10-13) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• Following the 11 October floods, WFP dispatched 100 kg fortified biscuits and government relief items to Gorkha district.
• During the second round of food assistance, WFP distributed 34.2 mt of food to 955 households (4,107 people), provided 0.52 mt of specialized nutritious foods to 634 students and reached 4,108 households so far with multi-purpose cash transfers in Dhading, Nuwakot and Rasuwa.
• The Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster received and stored 1,747.3 m³ of humanitarian cargo and dispatched 393.3 mt of partner relief items through logistics hubs in Kathmandu and Nuwakot.
Situation Overview
• Only six weeks after the devastating floods in Rasuwa, flash floods triggered by a landslide dam outburst on the Budhi Gandaki River on 11 October caused significant damage across parts of Gorkha and Dhading districts. According to the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA), five people are missing and 302 people have been rescued.
• NDRRMA reports that the floods swept away two motorable bridges and two suspension bridges, creating significant access challenges, damaged three hydropower projects and affected more than 550 households. Schools, homes and other critical infrastructure also sustained damage across the affected areas.
• WFP will participate in a joint UN multi-sector needs assessment to inform the humanitarian response and identify priority needs following the Budhi Gandaki floods.
