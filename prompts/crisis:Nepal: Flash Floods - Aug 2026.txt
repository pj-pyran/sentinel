Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: ETC (2026-09-11) ---
This Situation Report provides an update on the humanitarian situation following the flash floods that struck Nepal on 26 August 2026. Since the activation of the Logistics &amp; Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) 1 on 02 September, the LTC Telecoms Area has supported humanitarian partners through telecommunications coordination, assessments, connectivity services and information management. The report highlights the latest situation, telecommunications impacts and LTC Telecoms activities.
Summary Points
• Following the activation of the Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) on 02 September, a Telecoms Coordinator arrived in Kathmandu on 05 September for an initial two-week mission. The deployment is supporting the identification and prioritization of telecommunications requirements at common humanitarian operational locations, strengthening coordination with government and response partners.
• The Government has requested support to deploy sustainable backup-power solutions for telecommunications towers, prioritizing inaccessible sites where disrupted fuel supply routes had rendered generator-dependent operations unsustainable and placed continuity of critical connectivity at risk.
• The LTC Telecoms Coordinator is developing a twomonth response plan outlining priority telecommunications requirements, coordination actions and potential support activities.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-09-11) ---
Highlights

An estimated 84,270 people, including 32,021 children and 1,854 persons with disabilities, are affected across 17 municipalities in six districts. As of 7 September, 3,630 displaced people are staying in 37 holding centres across three districts.
Essential services remain severely disrupted, with 46 water supply systems, 23 schools and seven health facilities damaged.
Critical WASH supplies have been provided for 48,600 people, and water supply systems serving 17,500 people have been restored.
Mental health and psychosocial support have reached 1,952 people, including 782 children, through community interventions, and 2,893 people, including 1,382 children and adolescents, through specialist services.
541 unaccompanied and separated children have been reported to date, with 18 reunited with their families and follow-up support provided for all identified children.
112 children under-five have been screened for malnutrition, and 57 pregnant and breastfeeding women received infant and young child feeding counselling.
Education supplies including student, school and early childhood development kits have been dispatched to support more than 9,500 children.
Lifesaving information reached an estimated 1.67 million people through seven radio stations, alongside digital emergency information.
US$17.2 million is required to meet the immediate needs of children and families affected by the flash floods.

--- Report 3: WFP (2026-09-10) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• WFP has dispatched 114.8 mt of food rations, sufficient for 15 days, to Dhading, Nuwakot and Rasuwa, including 13.3 mt via heli operations.
• Innovative delivery modalities, including humanitarian cargo drones, are being used to reach affected populations.
• The Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster has activated free common services to humanitarian partners.
• The UN system-wide Nepal Rasuwa Flood Flash Appeal seeks USD 49.6 million to assist 84,000 people across six districts for four months.
Situation Overview
• Relief efforts continue across flood-affected districts following the 26 August floods, as search efforts, access constraints and major needs persist. Reported fatalities have risen to 1,369, with 5,132 people still missing.
• Some 3,500 displaced people are sheltering in Government displacement centres across Nuwakot, Rasuwa, Dhading and Kathmandu, many in school buildings.
• In Gosaikunda and Amachhodingmo, in upper Rasuwa, nearly 14,500 people remain disconnected without road access, electricity or phone signal since the flood.
• Access to affected communities remains a major operational challenge while transport links, unstable terrain, compound by monsoon rains and ongoing flood risks continue to constrain relief operations.
