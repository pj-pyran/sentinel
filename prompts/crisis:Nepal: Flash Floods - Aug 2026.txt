Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-09-16) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
The 26 August flash floods in central Nepal have caused significant loss of life, displacement and widespread damage to homes, infrastructure and essential services. Government-led search and rescue operations are on their twelfth day, while access constraints and ongoing verification of displacement and housing impacts remain key challenges.
As of 14 September, the Government of Nepal reports 1,395 bodies recovered, 5,130 people missing and 13,737 people rescued following the 26 August floods, with figures expected to change as access improves. Preliminary humanitarian analysis estimates 84,270 people affected across 17 municipalities in six districts.
The Government-led Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment (RDNA) indicates approximately 7,570 houses affected, impacting around 32,963 people, alongside damage to roads, bridges, agricultural land and critical infrastructure. Priority needs include shelter and essential household items, WASH, food, health and MHPSS services, protection and access to affected communities. Longer-term support requirements include private housing and settlement, economic activities and livelihood rebuilding, repair and replacement of damaged household energy systems, telecommunications, debris and sediment management, and disaster and climate risk management as the damage to infrastructure continues to constrain humanitarian access and delivery. A Rapid Damage Needs Assessment (RDNA) has also been published by the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA), which further elaborates on the post-disaster needs.  73% of sites reported inadequate sanitation facilities 46% of sites have inadequate water-storage capacity 51% reported no child protection services or referral pathways 54% reported no safe and private spaces for women and girls
IOM is supporting the Government-led response through site assessments, coordination, health, protection, non-food items (NFI), recovery and Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM).

--- Report 2: Plan International (2026-09-14) ---
A major flash flood entered Nepal through the Bhote Koshi River at approximately 9:00 AM on 26 August 2026, causing significant loss and damage across Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts. Timure, Rasuwagadhi and Syabrubesi are among the worst affected areas, with impacts reported on communities, markets, roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure.
The cause of the flood remains under investigation, with authorities assessing the possibility of a glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF) or another upstream hydrological event. The Government of Nepal has mobilised security forces for search, rescue and relief operations and issued alerts for communities downstream along the Trishuli River corridor.
