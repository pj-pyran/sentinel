Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: Nepal Red Cross (2026-11-09) ---
One month after the devastating flash flood, thousands of people remain displaced, and uncertainty remains about the future. The flood response has entered a longer-term relief and early recovery phase, while search and rescue efforts continue in parallel with initiatives to restore access, basic services, and support for displaced and affected families. According to the latest update from the NDRRMA, 1,455 people have lost their lives, 5,285 remain missing, 13,795 have been rescued. To date, authorities have identified and handed over 112 bodies to their families. Currently, 1,085 people are taking shelter in 17 holding centers across Rasuwa, Nuwakot, and Dhading districts, including 843 people in 9 centers in Rasuwa, 121 people in 4 centers in Nuwakot, and 121 people in 4 centers in Dhading. Recognizing the need for greater flexibility and dignity among displaced families, the Government of Nepal will provide monthly cash assistance to those who choose not to stay in holding centers. The support will help cover accommodation and livelihood expenses for up to six months, or until alternative housing is arranged. Each family who do not wish to stay in holding centers and until alternative housing arrangement will receive NPR 15,000 per month for families up to four members, with an additional NPR 2,000 per month for every family member beyond four, enabling families to make decisions that best suit their circumstances during recovery.

On 11 October 2026, a flash flood in the Budhi Gandaki River, triggered by the breach of a river blockage near Chumling, caused widespread damage in Gorkha and Dhading districts. The flood destroyed bridges, roads, hydropower facilities, and homes, displacing families, while NRCS mobilized staff and volunteers for early warning and response activities.

--- Report 5: UNICEF (2026-11-09) ---
Highlights

Children are bearing the brunt of the disaster. More than 32,000 children were affected by the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi flash floods on 26 August 2026, with many losing loved ones, homes and belongings, and facing heightened risks to their health, education, protection and well-being. Further flash flooding along the Budhi Gandaki River on 11 October affected communities, causing additional damage to infrastructure and access routes.
Since the onset of the crisis, UNICEF has worked alongside the Government of Nepal and partners to deliver lifesaving support, reaching more than 50,000 people with safe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, over 18,000 people with mental health and psychosocial support and 66,000 people with oral cholera vaccination, while providing almost 1,800 children with continued access to learning and reaching over 1,500 children through child-friendly spaces.
Winter poses an urgent new threat to affected children and families. With homes, clothing, and essential household items lost, thousands face freezing temperatures. UNICEF has procured 8, 514 sets of warm clothes for children, out of which distribution has been undertaken for 1,234 children. In addition, UNICEF has distributed 1,162 thermal blankets, alongside cash assistance to more than 1,000 households, heated learning spaces, and support for newborns and pregnant women.
Recovery must strengthen resilience to future disasters. UNICEF’s response, in line with Government plans, has entered a recovery-focused phase. Rebuilding schools, health facilities, and water systems provides an opportunity to establish safer, more inclusive and climate-resilient services, while investments in preparedness and early warning systems can better protect children before crises strike.
