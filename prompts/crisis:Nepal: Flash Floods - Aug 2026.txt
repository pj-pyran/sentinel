Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-11-26) ---
Highlights

Almost three months after the August floods, the response has transitioned to recovery, while continued humanitarian support remains necessary, particularly for winterization, protection, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), health, nutrition, education and cash assistance.
UNICEF has continued to strengthen early detection of disease threats, with no outbreaks reported since the 26 August floods. Nearly 1,500 flood-affected children have received winter clothes and thermal blankets. Mental health and psychosocial support reached 19,500 children and caregivers; 35 child-friendly spaces provide affected children with a safe place to learn, play and heal. In addition, UNICEF-established temporary learning centres (TLCs) have provided almost 1,800 children with continued access to education.
Nutrition services reached more than 13,000 children and 6,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women. Humanitarian cash assistance through government systems has reached 2,021 affected households in seven municipalities.
Recovery and reconstruction needs are substantial. The Government-led Post-Disaster Needs Assessment (PDNA) estimates total recovery and reconstruction needs at US$5.05 billion, including US$2.73 billion for infrastructure and US$413 million for social sectors.
UNICEF continues to restore essential services and rehabilitate damaged health, WASH and education systems. Rehabilitation of around 36 km of pipelines maintained or restored water access for 42,143 people. Support to construct semi-permanent TLCs continues.
UNICEF has received US$14.7 million of the US$17.2 million required for the humanitarian response (85 per cent funded), while early recovery and rehabilitation remain largely unfunded (31 per cent funded).

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-11-24) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

On 6 November, the National Planning Commission published the preliminary findings of the Post-Disaster Needs Assessment (PDNA), estimating total damage and losses from the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi floods at NPR 472.66 billion (USD 3.18 billion) and recovery and reconstruction needs at NPR 706.65 billion (USD 4.76 billion).
According to the preliminary PDNA, the disaster resulted in 1,464 deaths, 5,285 people missing, and 13,795 people rescued, underscoring the scale of its human impact across affected districts.
CARE Nepal, Plan International, and UN Women released a joint Rapid Gender Analysis (RGA) of the flood response. The assessment found that women, children, persons with disabilities, and other marginalized groups face disproportionate recovery challenges, including livelihood loss, increased care burdens, psychosocial distress, and barriers to accessing services and decision-making, underscoring the need for gender-responsive and inclusive recovery support.
