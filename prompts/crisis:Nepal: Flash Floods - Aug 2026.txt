Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-11-24) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

On 6 November, the National Planning Commission published the preliminary findings of the Post-Disaster Needs Assessment (PDNA), estimating total damage and losses from the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi floods at NPR 472.66 billion (USD 3.18 billion) and recovery and reconstruction needs at NPR 706.65 billion (USD 4.76 billion).
According to the preliminary PDNA, the disaster resulted in 1,464 deaths, 5,285 people missing, and 13,795 people rescued, underscoring the scale of its human impact across affected districts.
CARE Nepal, Plan International, and UN Women released a joint Rapid Gender Analysis (RGA) of the flood response. The assessment found that women, children, persons with disabilities, and other marginalized groups face disproportionate recovery challenges, including livelihood loss, increased care burdens, psychosocial distress, and barriers to accessing services and decision-making, underscoring the need for gender-responsive and inclusive recovery support.

--- Report 2: Nepal Red Cross (2026-11-23) ---
Situation Update
Following the devastating flash flood, thousands of people remain displaced, and uncertainty remains about the future. The flood response has entered a longer-term relief and early recovery phase, while search and rescue efforts continue in parallel with initiatives to restore access, basic services, and support for displaced and affected families. According to the latest update from the NDRRMA, 1,457 people have lost their lives, 5,285 remain missing, 13,795 have been rescued. To date, authorities have identified and handed over 112 bodies to their families. Currently, 417 people are taking shelter in 11 holding centers across Rasuwa, Nuwakot, and Dhading districts, including 229 people in 6 centers in Rasuwa, 67 people in 1 center in Nuwakot, and 121 people in 4 centers in Dhading. Recognizing the need for greater flexibility and dignity among displaced families, the Government of Nepal will provide monthly cash assistance to those who choose not to stay in holding centers. The support will help cover accommodation and livelihood expenses for up to six months, or until alternative housing is arranged. Each family who do not wish to stay in holding centers and until alternative housing arrangement willreceive NPR 15,000 per month for families up to four members, with an additional NPR 2,000 per month for every family member beyond four, enabling families to make decisions that best suit their circumstances during recovery.
