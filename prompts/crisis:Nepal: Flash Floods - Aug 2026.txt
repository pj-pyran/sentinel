--- Report 2: UNFPA (2026-08-31) ---
On 26 August 2026, severe flash floods affected the Bhote Koshi and Trishuli river corridors, initially impacting Rasuwa and subsequently downstream areas of Nuwakot, Dhading and towards Chitwan. The floods have caused significant loss of life and extensive damage to critical infrastructure. Approximately 10,000 households have been affected, 626 people are currently reported to have died and more than 2,400 people are missing.
Based on available population data, 310,076 people live in the affected areas, including approximately 86,820 women of reproductive age and 4,430 pregnant women. Damage to health facilities, roads, bridges and electricity infrastructure is disrupting access to life-saving sexual and reproductive health services, including emergency obstetric referrals, as well as complicating the delivery of essential humanitarian assistance.
UNFPA has mobilized 2,000 dignity kits for affected women and girls, with delivery to affected communities facilitated through the Government of Nepal and the Nepal Red Cross Society. The response also includes the deployment of two mobile health units, alongside reproductive health supplies, to help maintain life-saving reproductive and maternal health services in affected areas. Remote psychosocial support services have also been activated to provide immediate remote assistance to affected populations, with a standby roster of trained personnel available for rapid deployment as psychosocial needs emerge.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-08-31) ---
This report is produced by the Office of the United Nations Resident Coordinator in Nepal, in collaboration with humanitarian partners and the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA). It covers the period from 27 to 31 August 2026.
HIGHLIGHTS

According to NDRRMA’s latest bulletin (31 August 2026, 09:00), the death toll has reached 903, with 267 people injured (156 discharged) and 4,247 individuals still reported missing.
A total of 10,451 people have been rescued to date, including 252 foreign nationals and 3,344 Nepalis rescued by helicopter.
Government-led search and rescue efforts have mobilised 20,819 personnel across affected areas.
The Government has allocated emergency financial assistance of NPR 10 million (~65,000 USD) each to Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts, and NPR 0.5 million to Dhading.
Government relief operations have deployed 34 trucks, 35 jeeps, and 8 helicopters, alongside 5 helicopter deliveries of medical supplies to support affected communities.
Joint rescue operations are ongoing at hydropower projects including Trishuli 3A, Haku Besi, Trishuli 3B, and Chilime, involving Nepali, Indian, and Chinese rescue teams.
A command post in the Government of Nepal disaster management authority has been activated, and 15 emergency sectoral coordination teams have been deployed to support health, communications, roads, and electricity restoration.
Transportation on the Mugling-Malekhu section of the Prithvi Highway remains completely blocked due to toe-cutting at Krishna Bhir and Parewa Bhir.
Power supply has been restored in Dhading and Nuwakot; restoration work continues in Rasuwa.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
Five days after the flash flood of the Trishuli River swept through Raswuwa, Nuwakot, and Dhading, the Government of Nepal’s response efforts continue at scale, with more than 10,000 people rescued and over 20,000 personnel mobilised for search and rescue operations, while authorities report 903 fatalities and 4,247 people still missing.
The Government has officially declared a flood emergency in 15 municipalities and designated two additional municipalities as impacted, underscoring the scale of humanitarian needs across the affected areas.
The Government created 15 Task Teams to streamline response actions: Emergency Communication Restoration Team, Electricity Restoration and Backup Power Team, Search, Rescue and Evacuation Team, Emergency Health and Medical Response Team, Dead Body Management Team, International Assistance and Donor Coordination Team, Transport and Road Connectivity Team, Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment Team, Missing Persons and Family Assistance Team, Relief (Food, Water and Essential Supplies), Airport Coordination Team, a team to make public the names of the rescued, disappeared and the dead, Camp Management Team, and Social Media Regulation/Management.
International assistance has been robust and continues to grow, reflecting strong global solidarity and support for the response effort.

--- Report 4: UNICEF (2026-08-31) ---
Highlights

Nepal flash floods have affected an estimated 65,000 people, including 22,100 children, across Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha and Tanahun districts. An estimated 903 people have been reported dead, while approximately 4,247 remain missing. The death toll continues to rise.
Many families have been displaced, with most staying with host families, while others are sheltering in Government-run holding centers.
Critical infrastructure has been badly affected, including six health facilities and at least 19 schools destroyed or damaged, disrupting children’s access to healthcare, safe water, education and protection services.
UNICEF delivers life-saving support across water sanitation and hygiene (WASH), health, nutrition, child protection, social protection, education and risk communication, while strengthening local and national systems and capacities to sustain service delivery, support recovery and build resilience to future emergencies.
Critical UNICEF supplies for about 19,000 people have been provided to Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts. These include hygiene kits, buckets, soap, disinfectant, collapsible jerrycans, water purification products and risk communication materials.
UNICEF urgently requires US$17.2 million to support immediate humanitarian response for affected children and families. Internal resources have been mobilized to kick-start the response, but additional funding is urgently needed.

--- Report 5: HAI (2026-08-31) ---
CURRENT SITUATION

The flash flood and debris flow that struck Nepal’s Bhote Koshi–Trishuli river system on 26 August has evolved into a large-scale humanitarian emergency, with rapidly rising casualties, hundreds of people still unaccounted for, widespread infrastructure damage and a continuing risk of secondary flooding along downstream river corridors.
The latest reports indicate a rise in death toll to 553, with 977 still missing. More than 3,700 people have been rescued. The figures remain provisional and continue to change as search, rescue and body recovery operations progress.
At least 93,000 people are estimated to have been affected and require urgent humanitarian assistance, according to the IFRC.
35 motorable bridges, 45 suspension bridges and approximately 40 km of roads have been damaged, severely affecting connectivity and access to affected communities.
Electricity, telecommunications, drinking-water and hydropower infrastructure remain disrupted or damaged, while the destruction of roads and bridges is continuing to constrain humanitarian access.
Secondary flood risk remains high following the reported breach of a debris-created barrier lake near the Chhochen Khola–Purepu Tsangpo confluence, upstream of the Bhotekoshi. Nepalese authorities have been conducting aerial reconnaissance and monitoring water levels as communities downstream remain at risk of another surge.

--- Report 6: Tzu Chi Foundation (2026-08-31) ---
Locations: Galchi Rural Municipality, Dhading District; Chitwan District; Kathmandu, Nepal
Situation Overview
Flash floods have affected Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts in northern and central Nepal. Damaged infrastructure and restricted access continue to hamper relief efforts. In Galchi Rural Municipality, Dhading District, flooding destroyed a suspension bridge and damaged local infrastructure.
Operational Response
Medical Outreach and Dignified Management of the Deceased
On 28 August, a team from Tzu Chi Malaysia did an assessment visit to a hospital in Chitwan District that is receiving and managing bodies recovered from upstream flood-affected areas. The hospital reported acute shortages of mortuary capacity, body bags and Personal Protective Equipment (clothing). There was also a lack of protective supplies for security personnel, health workers and other responders handling bodies. Tzu Chi immediately procured 120 sets of Personal Protective Equipment (clothing) together with masks and gloves in Kathmandu. The supplies are being prepared for air transport to the hospital in Chitwan District.
On 29 August, Tzu Chi Singapore prepared 300 body bags for delivery to Singapore Changi Airport. The items are planned for air shipment to Kathmandu to support a dignified management of the deceased, subject to transport arrangements and coordination.

--- Report 7: UN Women (2026-08-30) ---
On 26 August 2026, severe flooding along the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli rivers affected communities across Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha and Chitwan districts, causing loss of life, displacement and damage to homes, infrastructure, agricultural land and livelihoods. An estimated 157,050 women and girls living in Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts have been impacted directly and indirectly.
The differentiated impacts on women and girls are not yet fully known. Initial information points to heightened risks associated with displacement and protection, access to safe WASH and essential health services, food and nutrition insecurity, loss of livelihoods and increased unpaid care responsibilities, and barriers to accessing humanitarian assistance and participating in recovery.
This Gender Alert draws on available emergency information, initial reporting from women’s rights organizations, pre-crisis gender data and evidence from previous disasters in Nepal to identify emerging gender risks and priority actions for the humanitarian response.

--- Report 8: WFP (2026-08-30) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• WFP has dispatched 31.8 mt of food assistance to date and previously delivered 2.8 mt of BP-5 for immediate relief. This includes 27 mt dispatched to Dhading for approximately 750 households (3,225 people) and 6 mt to Rasuwa for approximately 170 households (730 people) for 15 days.
• WFP continues to support the Government-led response by transporting relief supplies and operating the Kathmandu Humanitarian Staging Area, which remains open 24/7 to enable storage, coordination and onward dispatch of emergency assistance.
• WFP has deployed field teams to monitor food distributions and gather operational information, while preparations are underway to strengthen post-distribution monitoring through beneficiary mapping, enumerator training and surge monitoring capacity.
Situation Overview
• Northern Nepal continues to face the severe impacts of the 26 August flash flood event affecting Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts. The disaster has caused extensive loss of life and widespread destruction of transport, energy and communications infrastructure.
• The death toll has risen to approximately 734 people, with around 242 injured and 2,448 people still reported missing in Nepal as search and recovery operations continue. More than 8,198 people have been rescued to date.
• Extensive damage to infrastructure continues to hamper access, including approximately 40 km of roads, more than 80 bridges, and at least 14 hydropower and energy facilities along the Bhote Koshi-Trishuli corridor
