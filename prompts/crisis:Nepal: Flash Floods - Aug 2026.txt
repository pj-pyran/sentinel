Crisis / situation: Nepal: Flash Floods - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: DFS (2026-08-27) ---
A series of flash floods struck the Bhote Koshi River in Rasuwa district, northern Nepal, on the morning of 26 August 2026, entering from the Tibet Autonomous Region of China at around 09:00 Nepal Standard Time (NPT) and surging downstream through the Trishuli River system into Nuwakot, Dhading and beyond. The border settlements of Timure and Syabrubesi, the Rasuwagadhi customs and border crossing, and roughly a dozen riverside settlements in northern Rasuwa took the earliest and heaviest impact, before the surge moved through Betrawati and Trishuli Bazar in Nuwakot, Galchhi and Dhunge Bazaar in Dhading, and onward past Muglin into the Narayani river system, where deaths were subsequently recorded as far away as Chitwan, Gorkha, Tanahu and Nawalparasi East.
Casualty figures rose sharply and repeatedly through the day as search teams reached previously cut-off areas, and this report treats every figure below as provisional. By the morning of Thursday 27 August, the NDRRMA and Nepal Police put the confirmed death toll at 165, with 73 people injured, up from the roughly 160 (157 bodies recovered) reported late on Wednesday, and superseding Wednesday-evening figures of 95 from the Prime Minister’s Office (PMO) and 72 from the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA). Hundreds of people remain unaccounted for. As of a Nepal Police bulletin issued around 08:15 on Thursday 27 August, 826 people were listed as missing in Nepal alone: 579 travellers (466 foreign nationals and 113 Nepalis), 85 security personnel (44 Nepali Army, 28 Nepal Police and 13 Armed Police Force), 161 residents of Rasuwa and one of Nuwakot. Most of the missing foreign travellers were moving toward the Kailash Mansarovar pilgrimage route through Tibet, and included at least 133 Indian pilgrims alongside citizens of Malaysia (55), the United States (47), Australia (34), the United Kingdom (33), Canada (24) and other countries. A further 85 security personnel (Nepali Army, Nepal Police and Armed Police Force), 60 hydropower-project workers, 26 bank employees and 15 customs officers were separately reported missing or out of contact A further 60 hydropower-project workers, 26 bank employees and 15 customs officers were separately reported out of contact. On the Chinese side of the border, state media (CCTV) reported 3 confirmed deaths and 558 people missing, 260 of them foreign nationals, around the Gyirong Port trade hub in Xizang (Tibet), bringing the combined Nepal-and-Tibet missing total to more than 1,300, though the two countries’ counts of foreign nationals may overlap.
The flood caused severe damage to infrastructure across a roughly 60-kilometer valley corridor. The Department of Roads confirmed that the entire 42-kilometer road linking Betrawati (Nuwakot) to the Rasuwagadhi border crossing had been destroyed at multiple points, including several concrete bridges, cutting Nepal’s principal overland trade and pilgrimage route to Tibet; a further 16-kilometer stretch toward the border, recently upgraded with Chinese government financing, was also swept away. The Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) reported damage to 14 hydropower projects with a combined capacity of approximately 748 MW (9 operational plants (354 MW) and 5 under construction (394 MW)) including the Rasuwagadhi, Chilime, Sanjen Khola, Upper Trishuli-1, Trishuli and Devighat plants and a 220 kV substation; the Ministry of Energy’s earlier same-day estimate had put the loss at 431 MW, illustrating how fast the damage assessment was still moving. Nine commercial bank branches were swept away, with 26 staff unaccounted for, and the Rasuwa customs office in Timure lost contact with 15 employees.
The trigger has not been officially confirmed. Nepal’s Department of Hydrology and Meteorology and independent glaciologists advanced a preliminary hypothesis, based on satellite imagery shared by Chinese counterparts through the International Center for Integrated Mountain Development (ICIMOD), that an ice-and-rock avalanche blocked the Lhende Khola, a Bhote Koshi tributary on the Nepal-China border, roughly 20 km upstream of the Miteri Bridge, forming a temporary debris-dammed lake that subsequently burst The US Geological Survey initially logged a magnitude-4.4 earthquake at 08:37 local time shortly before the flood, but has since revised its analysis, concluding that the seismic signal was in fact a magnitude-5.2 glacial collapse, not a tectonic earthquake, based on nearby seismic stations, long-period seismic waves and satellite imagery. Meteorologists ruled out heavy local rainfall as the cause in Rasuwa itself, while noting that satellite data showed rainfall on the Tibetan side of the border. A broadly similar flood on the same river in July 2025 was ultimately attributed to the drainage of a supraglacial lake; ICIMOD scientists say such cascading glacial hazards are increasing at an “unprecedented” pace across the Hindu Kush Himalaya region.
The Government of Nepal has mobilized the Nepali Army, Armed Police Force and Nepal Police, supported by military and private helicopters, for search, rescue and relief operations, and the Cabinet has ordered free medical treatment for the injured, financial assistance to bereaved families, and the reopening of roads, restoration of power and telecommunications, and provision of food and shelter for those displaced. Nepal has asked China and India for assistance; China has pledged relief funding, and India is sending humanitarian supplies, while Indian authorities in neighboring Bihar state have opened the Valmikinagar Barrage on the Gandak River as a precaution and prepared to evacuate up to 10,000–12,000 people downstream. The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC) has released emergency funding to the Nepal Red Cross Society, which has begun distributing water, tarpaulins, blankets and first-aid supplies, though many of the worst-affected communities in Rasuwa remain difficult to reach because roads and bridges are destroyed. As of the data cut-off, no government or humanitarian body had published a consolidated total for people displaced, sheltering or in need, and this report treats that figure as a priority information gap for the days ahead.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-08-27) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Northern Nepal was struck by a suspected Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF) on the morning of 26 August after a sudden surge of water entered the Bhotekoshi River from the Tibet Autonomous Region of China, causing extensive damage across Rasuwa District and downstream areas of Nuwakot District. Preliminary assessments suggest a possible ice avalanche and damming of the Lhende River upstream.
• According to the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA), 10,000 households require immediate relief. At least 157 deaths have been confirmed and nearly 245 people remain missing, including security personnel, customs officials, foreign tourists and residents, while search and rescue operations continue (RisingNepal).
• A significant number of tourists and trekkers are also unaccounted for in the affected area, both foreign nationals and Nepali citizens, many of whom may be in locations without communications as phone towers are impacted.

--- Report 3: Qatar Charity (2026-08-27) ---
August–November 2026 | Total Planned Response: USD 1,069,000
1. Executive Summary
On 26 August 2026, an ice-and-rock landslide in the upper transboundary watershed near the Nepal–China border triggered a major debris-laden flash flood through the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli–Narayani river system. As of 27 August, at 09:00 Nepal time, the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA), provisional figures report 10,000 households require immediate relief, 165 bodies recovered, 247 people out of contact, 73 injured, and extensive infrastructure damage, including 35 motorable bridges and 45 suspension bridges swept away and approximately 40 km of paved road damaged. Evacuation orders and downstream alerts remain active because the upstream blockage lake may not have fully drained. QC has approved a USD 69,000 quick life-saving intervention for immediate food and emergency shelter assistance. An additional USD 1,000,000 is urgently needed to expand the relief response and support priority early recovery interventions, bringing the total planned intervention to USD 1,069,000.

--- Report 4: IFRC (2026-08-26) ---
Description
A major flash flood struck Rasuwa district after a sudden and intense surge of water entered the Bhote Koshi River from the Tibet (China) side at approximately 9:15 AM. The flood rapidly impacted the Timure, Rasuwagadhi, and Syabrubesi areas, causing significant damage to settlements, markets, police infrastructure, roads, and hydropower facilities. Authorities have warned that rising water levels could affect downstream communities along the Trishuli River corridor.
Current Situation

The flood is suspected to have originated from a high-altitude outburst upstream in Tibet, though the exact cause remains under investigation.
Water levels reportedly rose dramatically, with flood waves estimated at 8-10 meters in some locations.
Authorities have reported severe initial damage in:

Timure Market
Timure Customs Office
Rasuwagadhi area
Syabrubesi settlement
Multiple hydropower projects, including damage to the Chilime Hydropower Project
Three Nepal Police facilities: Timure Area Police Office, Rasuwagadhi Police Post, and Syabrubesi Police Post


Initial reports indicate that 30-40 drivers may be missing, although verification is ongoing. Extent of causaulities and missing persons remain unclear.

Areas at Risk
High-alert warnings have been issued for communities located along the Bhote Koshi and Trishuli rivers, particularly in: Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, Chitwan.
Residents living along riverbanks have been advised to move to safer locations and remain vigilant until further notice.
Transport and Access

Travel has been discouraged on: Prithvi Highway, Galchhi-Nuwakot-Rasuwa-Syabrubesi Road, Muglin-Narayangadh Road, Kathmandu Valley Traffic Police have reportedly halted vehicles heading toward Rasuwa and other affected districts.
People working, fishing, or conducting activities near the Trishuli River have been ordered to leave the river corridor immediately. Initial data are not available due to communication cut off in affected areas, will be updated in next field report.
