Crisis / situation: Haiti

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-11-04) ---
HIGHLIGHTS
• 1.49 million people displaced in August, up from 1.47 million in April, as the crisis spreads to rural areas and provincial towns.
• 335,000 people lack adequate access to health care in Artibonite, where insecurity has forced the closure of 21 health facilities.
• 253,725 children aged 6 to 59 months are at risk of acute malnutrition by May 2027. The national prevalence of global acute malnutrition rose from 5.1 per cent in 2023 to 7.4 per cent in 2026.
• Health partners conducted 74,614 medical consultations through 120 mobile clinic deployments.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-11-04) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Haiti’s humanitarian crisis continued to deepen in September amid persistent insecurity, large-scale displacement, economic deterioration and climaterelated shocks. An estimated 5.8 million people faced acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above), including 1.8 million in IPC Phase 4. The number of internally displaced persons exceeded 1.49 million, equivalent to approximately 12 percent of the population – while over 200,000 forced repatriations have been recorded in 2026.
• Insecurity continued to expand beyond Port-auPrince, with intensified clashes between the GSF and armed groups. Attacks, abductions and civilian casualties reported across Artibonite, Centre and other departments disrupted livelihoods, markets and access to basic services. The Central Bank of Haiti projected an eighth consecutive year of economic contraction, with consumer prices rising 17% year-onyear (food contributed 10.6 of the 17 points). Rising prices, limited livelihood opportunities and reduced purchasing power further constrained households’ ability to meet basic needs, placing additional pressure on overwhelmed social services.
• By the end of September only 10% of the adult population (or roughly 717,000 people) had registered to vote (well below the minimum target). The Provisional Electoral Council appointed a new President of the Council, suggesting further uncertainty over long delayed elections set for December 2026, as insecurity, access constraints and logistical challenges continue to affect the electoral process.
