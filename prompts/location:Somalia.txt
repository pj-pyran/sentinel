Crisis / situation: Somalia

--- Report 1: FAO (2026-05-12) ---
Current Situation
The Shabelle River is now at a moderate flood-risk condition, following a steady rise in river levels over the past week. The most critical situation is at Belet Weyne, where the river level has reached 6.75 m, exceeding the moderate flood-risk threshold of 6.50 m by 0.25 m. This represents a weekly increase of 0.55 m, and the level is now 1.01 m above the same period in 2025 and 1.14 m above the long-term mean. Downstream, the river is also rising at Bulo Burte, where the current level is 5.74 m, which is still 0.76 m below the moderate floodrisk threshold of 6.50 m. However, the station has increased by 0.32 m over the past week, and is now 0.74 m above the 2025 level and 1.05 m above the long-term mean, indicating active downstream propagation of flood waters. At Jowhar, the river stands at 4.88 m, only 0.12 m below the moderate flood-risk threshold of 5.00 m. Although the level has slightly decreased by 0.02 m compared to last week, it remains flood-sensitive and vulnerable to additional upstream inflows, weak riverbanks, canal diversions, and local rainfall.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-05-11) ---
Between 6 and 7 May, inter-clan conflict broke out in Laashimo valley, between Damalaxagare and Shimbiraale, near Carmala in Sanaag Region, displacing 1,500 households (approximately 9,000 individuals), of whom 85% are children and women. The violence began over access to a water point and grazing land. Both communities are pastoralists, and ongoing drought has intensified competition over scarce resources, increasing tensions and conflict among nomadic communities. The latest clashes were triggered by long-standing unresolved tensions over revenge. Laashimo Valley remains difficult to reach due to years of unresolved hostilities and limited humanitarian presence.
