Crisis / situation: Libya

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-06-03) ---
Key Highlights:

National prices increase at a slower pace as regional trends diverge: The national Full Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) increased by +0.5 percent in March 2026 to LYD 1,134.00, marking a slowdown compared to February (+5.0 percent). The moderate rise was driven by a sharp increase in the East (+8.2 percent), while the West (−0.4 percent) and South (−5.1 percent) recorded declines. This reflects continued exchangerate pass-through, partly offset by easing in western and southern markets.
Western region records easing but remains elevated: The West recorded a slight decrease of −0.4 percent to LYD 1,125.14, with declines observed in key markets including Tripoli (−4.4 percent to LYD 1,064.44) and Zwara (−5.6 percent to LYD 1,060.34). Despite this easing, prices remain elevated, reflecting strong urban demand
and sensitivity to import costs.
Eastern region drives national increase amid continued price pressures: The East recorded a sharp increase of +8.2 percent to LYD 1,225.04, remaining the highest-cost region. Significant increases were observed in Ejdabia (+16.0 percent to LYD 1,198.56) and Tobruk (+12.2 percent to LYD 1,071.61), reflecting continued exchange-rate passthrough and supply constraints.
Southern region records notable decline following earlier increases: The South decreased by −5.1 percent to LYD 1,066.10, with declines across markets including Sebha (−7.4 percent to LYD 875.43) and Ghat (−8.7 percent to LYD 954.00). The decline suggests easing supply pressures following the Ramadan peak, though structural
constraints persist including long supply chains and high transport costs.
Macroeconomic pressures continue to shape market dynamics: Exchange-rate pressures and import costs remain the primary drivers of prices, while recent plans by the Central Bank of Libya to inject US dollar liquidity have yet to ease retail prices in the short term. Additional pressures, including fuel shortages, logistics constraints, and localized disruptions, continue to contribute to market volatility.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-06-03) ---
Key Highlights:


National MEB increases sharply following March stabilization: The national Full Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) increased by +10.3 percent in April 2026 to LYD 1,250.90, following relative stabilization in March (+0.5 percent). The increase was driven primarily by a sharp rise in the West (+19.9 percent), while the South also recorded a substantial increase (+10.0 percent). In contrast, the East stabilized, decreasing slightly by −1.1 percent.


Western region drives the national increase: The West recorded the largest regional increase, rising by +19.9 percent to LYD 1,349.35, becoming the highest-cost region in April. Significant increases were observed in Tripoli (+35.9 percent to LYD 1,446.29), Zwara (+22.1 percent to LYD 1,369.42), Azzawya (+21.1 percent to LYD 1,357.56), and Alkhums (+18.2 percent to LYD 1,360.88).


Eastern region stabilizes following earlier increases: The East decreased slightly by −1.1 percent to LYD 1,211.41, following sharp increases in February and March. Declines were recorded in Ejdabia (−13.5 percent), Benghazi (−3.5 percent), and Al Bayda (−0.8 percent), partially offset by increases in Tobruk (+5.5 percent), Al Kufra (+5.1 percent), and Derna (+2.7 percent).


Southern region records renewed increases after March decline: The South increased by +10.0 percent to LYD 1,172.24, reversing the decline recorded in March. Increases were widespread, including Wadi Alshati (+16.3 percent), Sebha (+15.2percent), Ghat (+12.5 percent), Al Jufra (+9.5 percent), and Ubari (+9.2 percent).


Macroeconomic pressures continue to shape market dynamics: Exchange-rate pressures and import costs remain the primary drivers of prices, while recent plans by the Central Bank of Libya to inject US dollar liquidity have yet to ease retail prices in the short term. Additional pressures, including fuel shortages, logistics constraints, and  localized market disruptions, particularly in the south, continue to contribute to market volatility.
