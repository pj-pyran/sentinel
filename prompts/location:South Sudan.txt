Crisis / situation: South Sudan

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-07-27) ---
KEY MESSAGES
• Food basket costs remained virtually unchanged week-on-week, at SSP 119,538 per person per month on average. The reported price stability in Unity and Lakes states is due to limited data availability.
• Average fuel prices decreased by 3 to 4 percent in formal and informal markets, respectively, driven by lower prices in Abyei, Malakal, Makpandu, Torit and Wanjok. Fuel prices remained highly volatile in Abyei.
• The South Sudanese Pound remained stable on average, trading between SSP 6,000 and SSP6,670 per USD on parallel markets. However, the currency depreciated by 3 percent in Abyei. The gap between the average parallel exchange rate and the official rate stood at 37 percent.

--- Report 2: Concern (2026-07-27) ---
The Concern Worldwide (CWW) Accountability to Affected Populations project, supported by WFP, aims to create a bottom-up feedback and communication mechanism between responders and people we serve, that is community-based, easily accessible, and built on mutual understanding and genuine information-sharing.
Through local responders and community correspondents, Concern, Community Engagement Network (CEN), and the Radio Community (TRC) work together to deliver a complete community feedback mechanism that is accessible to all participants regardless of their literacy levels and access to assets and internet. Using TRC's and CEN’s longstanding experience in radio broadcasting, the project ensures a wide outreach beyond people with access to written updates.
Together, and alongside WFP, the project partners respond to information and feedback gaps. By consolidating and sharing the feedback received, the project increases accountability to affected populations in the targeted areas.

--- Report 3: WFP (2026-07-27) ---
KEY MESSAGES
• Food basket costs averaged 1percent lower than the previous week. Eastern Equatoria and Upper Nile recorded declines of over 10percent, driven by price decreases in Kapoeta South, Malakal, Renk and Torit.
• Fuel prices rose by over 15percent week-on-week in Mabior, Abyei, and Pibor, while falling by 15percent in Rumbek. The national average remained stable. Fuel prices were highest in Pibor, returning to SSP 50,000 per liter.
• The South Sudanese Pound traded between SSP6,000 and SSP6,600 per USD on parallel markets, with no change in the national average. In Renk, the parallel exchange rate fell from SSP 6,600 to SSP 6,300, reflecting a 5percent appreciation.

--- Report 4: WFP (2026-07-27) ---
KEY MESSAGES
• National food basket costs remained stable weekon-week, averaging SSP 123,909. Central Equatoria continues to be the most expensive state, while higher-than-averagefood costs persisted in Unity over the last month (+11 percent MoM). Sharp commodity price movements in markets such as Aweil Town, Bunj, Makpandu, and Torit indicate continued localized market volatility.
• Nationally, fuel prices stood at over 60 percent above pre-Middle Eastern crisis levels. Fuel prices rose marginally on average between weeks, but notably increased in several locations across Northern Bahr el Ghazal, including Aweil Town, Wanjok, and Wedwil.
• The SSP depreciated slightly week-on-week, while the spread between formal and parallel exchange rates continued to be high at 39 percent.

--- Report 5: WFP (2026-07-27) ---
KEY MESSAGES
• Food basket costs increased by 3 percent over the past week, reaching an average of SSP 122,604 per person per month. The increase was driven primarily by rising prices in Eastern Equatoria and Unity states, where traders reported supply chain disruptions and additional transport-related tariffs.
• Fuel prices remained broadly stable across markets, except in Bentiu where it increased by 20 percent and Bunj where it decreased by 14 percent.
• The South Sudanese Pound depreciated slightly week-on-week, trading between SSP6,200 and SSP6,900 per USD on parallel markets. The spread between parallel and official exchange rates remained elevated at 39 percent.
