Crisis / situation: South Sudan

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-10-06) ---
WFP assisted 1.88 million people in August, including 1.7 million crisis-affected people reached through the August distribution cycle—77 percent of the 2.2 million people targeted.
SMART surveys show a worsening nutrition crisis in South Sudan, with global acute malnutrition rates of 28.8 percent in Akoka County and 36.2 percent in Guit County both far above emergency thresholds. Food insecurity, disease, displacement and flooding are driving the crisis, while concerns are also rising in Lafon County.

--- Report 2: WHO (2026-10-06) ---
Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with medical complications is a major cause of morbidity and mortality among children in South Sudan, requiring timely identification, referral and access to quality inpatient care. Stabilization centers provide a critical component of the national nutrition response, managing children with severe acute malnutrition who require specialized clinical treatment and monitoring. Understanding how these services perform is essential for assessing access to care, quality of care, service demand, and the strength of the systems that support nutrition programming.
This factsheet examines the performance of inpatient nutrition services in South Sudan during January to June 2026 using data from the Early Warning, Alert and Response System (EWARS), the Nutrition Information System (NIS), and partner medical-supplies consumption reports. The analysis combines program monitoring data with patient-level information to assess screening activity, admissions, treatment outcomes, average weight gain (AWG), length of stay (LOS), geographic variation.
