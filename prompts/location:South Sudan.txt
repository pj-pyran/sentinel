--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-09-02) ---
Contextual Background
Majoknythiou Reception Centre, located in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, is one of the principal entry points for South Sudanese returnees and refugees fleeing the ongoing conflict in Sudan. Since 1 June 2025, the reception centre has received approximately 6,727 households (20,344 individuals), resulting in sustained humanitarian needs and placing considerable pressure on reception facilities, essential services, and the resilience of surrounding host communities. In response, ACTED implemented Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) targeting vulnerable returnee and refugee households residing at Majoknythiou Reception Centre and the surrounding communities of Rum-Mawel, Dhony-Dhony, Machar Akoon, Warayen, Mabokdakloc, Yiththiou, Majokyith, and Tomkieu.
These communities continue to experience increased demand for basic services and livelihood opportunities due to the continuous influx and settlement of displaced populations, while existing infrastructure and service delivery remain limited. Through its sustained operational presence, ACTED has supported beneficiary reception and registration, vulnerability assessments, community engagement, and the delivery of MPCA. To date, the programme has assisted 1,040 vulnerable returnee and refugee households (approximately 6,240 individuals), enabling families to address their most urgent needs with dignity, flexibility, and autonomy in prioritizing household expenditures. The intervention has been implemented in close coordination with humanitarian partners and local authorities, supported by robust beneficiary targeting and verification processes, accessible Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM), and comprehensive PostDistribution Monitoring (PDM), thereby strengthening programme quality, accountability to affected populations, and evidence-based decision-making.
This report presents findings from a Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) survey conducted among 362 randomly selected beneficiary households to assess the effectiveness, relevance, and accountability of the MPCA intervention. The assessment evaluates how beneficiaries utilized the cash assistance, the extent to which priority needs were met, beneficiary satisfaction with the assistance received, access to complaints and feedback mechanisms, and the overall effectiveness of cash assistance in improving household wellbeing. The findings demonstrate that MPCA enabled households to meet critical immediate needs, particularly food, shelter, healthcare, transportation, debt repayment, and other essential household expenditures. Beneficiaries generally reported high levels of satisfaction with the assistance and valued the flexibility provided through unconditional cash transfers.
However, the assessment also highlights that cash assistance alone cannot fully address the longer-term challenges affecting displaced households. Continued population movements, limited livelihood opportunities, rising market prices, and increasing pressure on host community resources continue to constrain recovery and resilience. The findings reinforce the importance of predictable, timely, and accountable cash assistance delivered alongside complementary investments in livelihoods, protection, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), health, and shelter to support sustainable recovery and strengthen household self-reliance. Building on its established field presence, experienced programme and MEAL teams, and strong community engagement and accountability systems, ACTED remains well positioned to support the priorities of the Cash Working Group by delivering high-quality, evidence-based, and accountable cash programming while contributing to coordinated humanitarian responses across Majoknythiou Reception Centre and the surrounding host communities.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-09-02) ---
South Sudan Humanitarian Update (as of 31 August 2026)
HIGHLIGHTS

Acute nutrition needs increase across South Sudan.
Critical life-saving nutrition supplies reportedly exhausted in Upper Nile State.
Safe water access remains a critical challenge amid insecurity, drought and funding gaps.
Below-average rainfall, prolonged dry spells and above normal temperatures reported across the country.
More than 296,000 children reached with education, but major gaps remain.
The Humanitarian Coordinator visits Central Equatoria State to assess Ebola preparedness measures.
