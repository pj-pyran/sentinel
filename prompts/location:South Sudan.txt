Crisis / situation: South Sudan

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-09-16) ---
ANALYSIS &amp; TRENDS
Conflicts across Jonglei and Upper Nile have internally displaced more than 323,000 people since 2025. The latest escalation in Akobo has led to large-scale population movements, with more than 100,000 people fleeing from Akobo and surrounding areas into Ethiopia.
As security conditions have improved in some areas, returns have gradually increased. By 1 July, IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) had recorded more than 61,000 returnees in Akobo County. However, ongoing insecurity, seasonal flooding, and the limited availability of basic services mean that many families are returning to highly precarious conditions. Akobo remains a critical humanitarian hub and a key cross-border corridor between South Sudan and Ethiopia, making developments in the area highly consequential for both internal displacement and regional population movements.
Separately, in July UNHCR and its partners observed, 8,292 individuals arriving in South Sudan through the Akobo Boat Dock and Dibole entry points from the Tiekidi, Kule, Nguenyiel and Jewi refugee camps in Ethiopia. This represents a 10 per cent decrease from the 9,250 arrivals recorded in June. Children accounted for 60 per cent of arrivals, while 70 per cent of returning households were female-headed and 5 per cent of returnees were aged 60 or above. Many families are returning with few belongings and are rebuilding their lives in areas where services, shelter and livelihood opportunities remain extremely limited.
Improved security and family reunification are encouraging returns. However, returnees also cited reduced humanitarian assistance and limited access to food, healthcare, education, water and sanitation in Ethiopia as factors motivating their return.
The journey remains difficult. With heavy rains rendering many roads impassable, the Matar–Akobo boat route is often the only viable option, while the high cost and limited availability of commercial boats have slowed arrivals. Families report journeys of up to seven days from Gambella, sometimes waiting for days for transport without adequate food, shelter or basic services.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-09-15) ---
KEY MESSAGES
• At SSP 133,524 per person per month, average food basket costs were marginally lower than the previous week, while remaining 3 percent above the level recorded four weeks earlier.
• Livestock price dynamics, such as the 25 percent drop in the price for a goat in Aweil, reflected the current severe water deficit, which drives increased livestock sales to cope with high cereal prices. This compromises households’ terms of trade.
• The informal exchange rate held steady at SSP 7,312 per USD on average, while the official rate was SSP 5,627 per USD. This maintained a persistent 30 percent premium to the official exchange rate.
• Fuel prices remained elevated and virtually unchanged from the previous week on both formal and informal markets.
