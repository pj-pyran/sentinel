Crisis / situation: South Sudan

--- Report 1: FAO (2026-09-08) ---
Support to the vulnerable livelihoods of agropastoralists in South Sudan
Objective
To safeguard the livestock assets of agropastoral and pastoral households affected by floods.
Activities implemented
• Trained 62 community animal health workers (CAHWs) in disease surveillance and equipped them with animal health kits comprised of essential supplies (antibiotics, vaccines and dewormers).
• Built one multipurpose solar-powered borehole in Tonj South.
• Distributed vegetable seeds and tools to 25 people (women and youth) and provided training on good vegetable production practices.
• Constructed a milk bar in Juba County and trained 30 people, including 28 women, in milk production, processing and marketing and financial management.
Results
• Enhanced CAHWs’ capacities to actively carry out livestock interventions, accurately recognize and identify diseases, report animal health issues and administer vaccines effectively, leading to a reduction in disease incidents and livestock mortality.
• Protected livestock assets of 17 661 households through the treatment of over 605 000 animals by trained CAHWs.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-09-07) ---
Overview:
UNICEF and partners completed Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) missions to Wiechjol in Akobo County and Waat in Nyirol County, Jonglei State, restoring immediate access to life-saving health, nutrition and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services for conflict-affected, displaced and returnee communities. After months of disrupted humanitarian access, the missions delivered approximately 8.3 metric tons of critical supplies and provided nutrition screening, primary healthcare, household water-treatment support, hygiene promotion and referrals for children and pregnant and lactating women requiring further care. The intervention provided a critical humanitarian lifeline for communities that had been largely cut off from assistance following months of conflict, population displacement, and disrupted service delivery. Through an integrated package of health, nutrition and WASH interventions, UNICEF and partners delivered immediate life-saving support to conflict-affected, displaced and returnee populations. The missions screened 1,666 children for acute malnutrition, provided 1,241 primary health care consultations, reached 2,075 people with hygiene-promotion messages, and distributed essential hygiene supplies, including soap to 3,250 households and buckets to 948 households. Teams also identified and referred vulnerable children and women requiring additional care, including 146 children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 58 pregnant and lactating women at nutritional risk. As the first humanitarian assistance delivered in several months to these locations, the missions also generated critical information on humanitarian needs and service gaps, helping inform follow-up actions and the gradual restoration of essential services in hard-to-reach areas of northern Jonglei.
