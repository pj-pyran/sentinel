Crisis / situation: South Sudan

--- Report 1: Health Cluster (2026-06-02) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS – APRIL 2026
Throughout April 2026, Health Cluster partners continued delivering integrated emergency health services across conflict-affected, flood-prone and outbreak-affected areas of South Sudan despite severe operational constraints, insecurity and funding shortages. Humanitarian needs remained critically high, particularly in Upper Nile, Jonglei, Unity and Western Equatoria states, where conflict, displacement, disease outbreaks and deteriorating access conditions continued to place extreme pressure on already fragile health systems. In Upper Nile State, continued refugee and returnee arrivals from Sudan significantly overstretched health services and humanitarian response capacities, particularly in Renk and surrounding transit areas. World Vision and partners maintained integrated emergency primary healthcare services at Transit Center 2 and surrounding communities, conducting 3,906 outpatient consultations, supporting routine immunization services for 641 children under five years of age and providing antenatal care services for 323 pregnant women. A total of 25 skilled deliveries were conducted during the reporting period, while 2,890 individuals were reached through community-based health and nutrition awareness activities focused on disease prevention, hygiene promotion and healthy practices.
In Jonglei State, humanitarian operations remained heavily constrained by insecurity, flooding and repeated attacks on health infrastructure. Akobo County continued experiencing severe service disruptions following looting and vandalism of Akobo Hospital and surrounding health facilities. WHO, UNICEF, SCI, CARE and other partners conducted operational coordination, assessments and response planning missions to support restoration of essential health services and continuity of outbreak response activities.
In Fangak County, HFO and partners continued integrated outpatient, nutrition, maternal health and immunization services across multiple facilities supported under SSHF, ECHO and Global Affairs Canada projects. Under SSHF-supported interventions, HFO reported 5,114 outpatient consultations, 211 antenatal care consultations, 15 skilled deliveries, screening of 762 children and 336 pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition and routine immunization activities.
In Canal/Pigi County, humanitarian operations remained constrained by flooding, access restrictions and insecurity despite relative improvement in security conditions. COSEDA reported that severe flooding displaced nearly 40,000 people and submerged multiple schools and health facilities.
In Ayod County, WART continued supporting 13 health facilities under HSTP programming while also implementing emergency cash assistance activities in partnership with CRS and IOM. In Western Equatoria State, Tambura, Ezo and Yambio counties remained significantly affected by insecurity, Mpox transmission, displacement and operational disruptions.

--- Report 2: Protection Cluster (2026-06-02) ---
South Sudan faces multiple challenges contributing to this crisis, including cross-border influx, state armed and inter-communal conflicts, as well as adverse effects from climate phenomena like floods and droughts, all exacerbated by severe economic turmoil and governance issues. To address the needs of the IDP population, it is essential to differentiate between groups. ne significant category comprises IDPs who have been residing in settlements for extended periods without experiencing or marginally affected by recent conflict-related shocks, totaling close to a million individuals. These individuals require targeted integration assistance and durable solutions.The other category comprises recent returnees (in IDP-like situations), people temporarily displaced by natural hazards and inter-communal conflict and those fleeing en-masse from the ongoing conflict between state armed actors.
