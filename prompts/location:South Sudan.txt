Crisis / situation: South Sudan

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-11-12) ---
South Sudan is currently facing a severe humanitarian crisis characterized by extensive internal displacement. The underlying causes of these displacement dynamics are varied and include communal clashes, land disputes, insecurity, violence, and disasters. The situation is further complicated by the ongoing conflict in neighbouring Sudan, which has led to increased cross-border movements, and an influx of refugees and returnees, straining already limited resources.
Key Findings:

19,376 IDPs (3,591 households) were recorded across six counties (Leer, Ibba, Mundri West, Rumbek Centre, Abyei AA, Malakal)
The top three priority needs of IDPs were water (29%), food (26%), and sanitation (17%)
1,319 returnees (273 households) were recorded across four counties (Akobo, Nyirol, Maiwut, Uror)
The top three priority needs of returnees were health (30%), food (27%), and water (24%)

--- Report 2: Nutrition Cluster (2026-11-11) ---
The third quarter of 2026 saw a marked increase in the volume of nutrition services delivered by Nutrition Cluster partners, even as pressure on the treatment supply pipeline re-emerged towards the end of the period. By the end of September 2026, a total of 1,314,866 beneficiaries had been admitted into Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) programmes during the reporting period. These admissions comprised 299,408 children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) enrolled in Outpatient Therapeutic Programmes (OTP), 592,103 children with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) treated through Supplementary Feeding Programmes (SFP), 11,426 admissions to Stabilisation Centres (SCs), and 411,929 Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women (PBW) receiving nutritional support.
Despite persistent operational constraints, the response has continued to make steady progress toward the 2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP) targets. By the end of September 2026, partners had reached 57% of the annual SAM target, 89% of the MAM target, and 76% of the target for PBW with acute malnutrition (compared with 36% for SAM, 51% for MAM and 41% for PBW at the end of June). Admissions during the third quarter (July–September) totaled 552,847, 39% more than in the second quarter (398,073). The increase was driven by the TSFP child and PBW programmes, where admissions rose by 47% and 73% respectively. In contrast, SAM admissions to OTP decreased by 5% (from 113,534 to 108,212) and SC admissions by 7%: monthly OTP admissions fell from a high of 40,714 in June to 34,022 in September, despite higher screening volumes over the same period. This trend warrants close attention, as the seasonal burden of acute malnutrition is expected to remain high through the final quarter of the year, and correlates with increased stock outs of supplies in some locations. Sustained, predictable funding and an uninterrupted supply of therapeutic and supplementary foods will be essential to prevent programme coverage from declining in the fourth quarter.
