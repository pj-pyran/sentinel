Crisis / situation: South Sudan

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-11-20) ---
Highlights
▪ South Sudan: Conflict across Jonglei and Upper Nile States has displaced more than 323,000 people since January 2026, with Northern Jonglei at the epicenter of the current crisis. At the same time, UNHCR is preparing for climate shoks due to El Nino, including localized flooding, worsening food insecurity and disruptions of livelihood and humanitarian actions.
▪ Ethiopia: The environment remained volatile in the Gambella region, throughout August, with localized security incidents affecting security and humanitarian access. On 23 August, an armed attack on a public bus along the Itang corridor killed one person and injured 15 others, resulting in the cancellation of a humanitarian convoy. Meanwhile, expanding military operations in parts of Jonglei and Upper Nile continued to drive displacement into Ethiopia.
▪ Uganda: On 19 August, the Office of the Prime Minister (OPM) reinstated prima facie refugee status for asylum-seekers from the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), South Sudan and Sudan, following a review of conditions in the three countries. The directive took immediate effect and also granted refugee status to individuals from these countries whose claims were undergoing individual Refugee Status Determination.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-11-20) ---
Highlights
▪ South Sudan: Conflict across Jonglei and Upper Nile States continued to drive displacement, with more than 440,000 people displaced since January 2026 and Northern Jonglei at the centre of the crisis. Meanwhile, spontaneous returns from Ethiopia increased amid localized security improvements. In Eastern Equatoria, drought resulted in crop failures, water shortages, livestock losses and severe malnutrition.
▪ Ethiopia: In Gambella, insecurity intensified in September, resulting in deaths, injuries and localized displacement, including attacks on a public convoy and clashes near the UNHCR compound. Movement restrictions and the suspension of convoys along the Itang corridor from 28 September disrupted food distributions, water supplies and medical referrals.
