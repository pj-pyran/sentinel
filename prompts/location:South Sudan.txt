Crisis / situation: South Sudan

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-10-20) ---
Operational Context
Between January and June 2026, Renk County remained a major entry point for people fleeing the conflict in Sudan (, with continued arrivals driven by insecurity, forced returns, deteriorating socioeconomic conditions, limited access to basic services, and declining humanitarian assistance.
Between January and June 2026, border monitoring conducted by UNHCR, IOM, and ACTED recorded 59,615 new arrivals comprising 19,237 households, of whom 60% were South Sudanese and 40% were Sudanese nationals. Women and children represented the majority of arrivals (80%), including 42% adult women, 28% children aged 0-11 years, and 10% adolescents aged 12-17 years.
To facilitate safe and organized movement from border entry points, 26,806 individuals (11,010 households) received Onward Transportation Assistance (OTA) from Joda to the Renk Transit Centre. Among them, 18,974 individuals (7,072 households) identified as Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs) were prioritized for transportation due to heightened vulnerabilities, while 7,832 individuals (3,938 households) received support as non-PSNs. In addition, 4,828 refugees and returnees were assisted with onward transportation from Renk through Malakal to their final destinations.
The OTA assistance played a critical role in decongesting border reception center and the Renk Transit Centre, while ensuring timely access to registration, protection, health, nutrition, shelter, and other essential humanitarian services. The intervention contributed significantly to safeguarding the dignity, safety, and wellbeing of newly arrived refugees and returnees, while strengthening the overall humanitarian response and population management efforts in Renk County.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-10-20) ---
Overview
The first half of 2026 marked an important transition period in Renk County, with humanitarian actors gradually complementing life-saving interventions with activities aimed at advancing durable solutions for refugees, returnees, and host communities. While the county continued to receive significant numbers of Sudanese refugees and South Sudanese returnees, resulting in sustained humanitarian needs, partners increasingly invested in initiatives that foster self-reliance, local integration, social cohesion, and equitable access to essential services.
Between January and June 2026, humanitarian partners, working closely with the Commission for Refugee Affairs (CRA), the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), and relevant county-level ministries and departments including Education, Agriculture, Fisheries, Lands, Gender, Child and Social Welfare, Infrastructure, and Rural and Urban Water departments, strengthened efforts to support durable solutions. Key areas of focus included;
✓ Ensuring access to legal identity and civil documentation
✓ Promoting self-reliance through livelihoods, agriculture, and skills development;
✓ Strengthening community-based protection mechanisms
✓ Fostering social cohesion and peaceful coexistence among refugees, returnees, and host communities ✓ Expanding access to quality education and;
✓ Improving infrastructure and basic service delivery in the areas of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), health, and education.
These strategic investments were designed not only to address immediate humanitarian needs but also to support long-term recovery, resilience, and sustainable development. These interventions contributed to creating an enabling environment for refugees, returnees, and host communities to rebuild their lives and strengthen their socio-economic well-being across Renk County.
