Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-09-25) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
According to the latestinformationfrom the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management(UNGRD), as of September 16, 335 people have been reported dead, 105 remain missing,364 have been rescued, and 4,516 injured,while 486,917 people have been affected by the August 10, 2026 earthquake. In addition, 206,931 homes have been damaged and 36,478 destroyed, while 402 health facilities,4,431 educationalinstitutions,and 6,094 communitycentershavebeen affectedacross 16 departmentsand 500 municipalities. During the National Unified Command Post (PMU) on September 19, UNGRD consolidatedterritorial informationprovided by different sectors following the earthquake. According to the Unified Registry of Disaster-Affected Population (RUD), 364,670 familiesand 778,279 people had been registeredas of September 19. The registry also reports 84,855 habitablehomes, 104,037 uninhabitablehomes, 19,143 destroyedhomes,and 119,752 damagedhomes. Earthquake-related needs continue across severalsectors, includingshelter,health,water,sanitationand hygiene(WASH), and educationin emergencies. These needs are compounded by natural hazardsthat are further affectingvulnerablehouseholds. In severalmunicipalitiesin Chocó, IDEAM issuedhigh-level landslidehazard alertsassociatedwith intenseand prolonged rainfall,while forest firesremain activein three municipalitiesin Valledel Cauca. More than one month after the earthquake,and followingthe completion of search and rescue operations,governmentefforts are shifting towards comprehensive recovery, with a focus on updating information on affected populations, housing and livelihoods recovery, infrastructurerehabilitation,and the protectionof culturalheritage. In this context,the Ministryof Environmentactivateda support mechanismfor the recovery of Green Businesses(Negocios Verdes) affected by the earthquake,particularlyin areassuch as Chocó and Valle del Cauca. Meanwhile,the Ministryof Culture conductedfieldvisitsin Caldas, Risaralda,Quindío, and Valle del Cauca to assess damage and advance a reconstructionplan. The initialassessmentidentified40 nationally designatedculturalheritageassetsaffected.

--- Report 2: EHP Colombia (2026-09-24) ---
This Flash Update is produced by OCHA Colombia with inputs from humanitarian partners and official reports. Information is current as of 23 September at 6:30 p.m. This document will continue to be issued on a weekly basis through the end of September.

More than one month after the magnitude 7.4 earthquake, approximately 487,000 people have been affected across 500 municipalities in 16 departments, with the most severe impacts reported in Valle del Cauca, Risaralda, Chocó, Quindío and Caldas. The potential onset of a severe El Niño event could further exacerbate humanitarian and recovery needs.
Communities are facing multiple, simultaneous crises. In addition to the impact of the earthquake, people continue to experience pre-existing effects of the armed conflict, including displacement, confinement and restricted access to essential services. These factors increase population vulnerability and place additional strain on institutional response capacities.
The Government and international partners continue to deliver a coordinated response. Institutions within the National Disaster Risk Management System (SNGRD) are providing sectoral assistance in coordination with territorial authorities. United Nations agencies and humanitarian partners continue to work together to deliver health care, water, sanitation and hygiene services, food security support, shelter, protection and early recovery assistance. To date, 31 organizations have supported more than 137,000 people in 53 prioritized municipalities.
Significant needs and funding gaps persist. While recovery efforts are advancing through the development of the Specific Action Plan for Recovery (PAE), the humanitarian response still faces a 52 per cent funding gap against the US$619 million required to address the multisectoral needs of affected populations through the end of 2026.
