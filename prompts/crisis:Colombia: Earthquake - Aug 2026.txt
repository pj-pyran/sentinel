Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: EHP Colombia (2026-10-01) ---
This Flash Update is produced by OCHA Colombia based on inputs from humanitarian partners and official reports (as of 30 September, 18:00 local time). This document concludes the weekly update, with resources available for reference regarding the situation and response.

Nearly two months after the earthquake, the Government is moving into the recovery phase through the Specific Action Plan for Recovery (PAE in spanish). However, it remains important to address the impacts in remote rural areas facing multiple challenges, where the effects of the earthquake, armed conflict, and other emergencies converge.
Gaps persist in the identification of affected populations, damage assessments, and access to essential services. Rural, ethnic, refugee, and migrant communities face greater risks of exclusion, insufficient protection, and limited recovery opportunities.
Complementing the government response, at least 35 humanitarian organizations have assisted 239,000 people across 90 municipalities through interventions in health, food assistance, shelter, WASH, protection, education, and cash transfers, as well as technical and legal assistance.
Recovery efforts face challenges in ensuring safe returns from temporary shelters, strengthening local capacities, and guaranteeing equitable access to institutional assistance, particularly in areas with limited state presence and multiple impacts.

With this document, the weekly publication comes to an end. Resources for consultation and reporting on the situation and response will remain available. These products were made possible through continued partner reporting, which should be maintained, and the support of 3iS in developing visual resources.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-10-01) ---
Situation Overview
According to the latest information from the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD), as of September 28, 335 people have been reported dead, 105 missing, 364 rescued, 4,516 injured, and 486,917 affected following the earthquake of August 10, 2026. In addition, 206,931 homes were damaged, 36,478 homes destroyed, 402 health facilities affected, 4,431 educational institutions affected, and 6,094 community centers affected, across 16 departments and 500 municipalities. According to information from the Single Registry of Affected Persons (RUD), as of September 26, 364,668 families and 778,274 people have been registered, including 84,855 habitable homes, 104,038 uninhabitable homes, 19,142 destroyed homes, and 119,752 damaged homes. More than six weeks after the earthquake, the needs of the affected population have evolved from immediate assistance toward requirements related to durable housing solutions, livelihood recovery, infrastructure rehabilitation, and sustained access to essential services. In Cali, Valle del Cauca, approximately 20,000 families are still reported to be displaced from their homes, while structural assessments and demolitions continue. The institutional and international cooperation response continues to include humanitarian assistance and financial support, while increasing attention is being placed on evacuated families, housing assessments, subsidies, educational infrastructure, and continuity of services. Over the past week, the World Bank approved an additional US$200 million in financing to support the response and recovery following the earthquake, including reconstruction needs and the strengthening of disaster risk management and financing mechanisms. This new financing adds to the US$200 million previously disbursed, bringing the total resources mobilized by the institution for the emergency to US$400 million.
