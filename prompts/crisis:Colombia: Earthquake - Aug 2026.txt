Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-09-08) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• WFP is transitioning from immediate lifesaving earthquake assistance to a targeted response based on government registries, food security assessments and market analysis. Of the approximately 103,210 people identified as requiring assistance across priority departments, available resources currently allow WFP to assist 61,100 people.
• Wildfire continues to increase humanitarian needs and operational complexity, particularly in Tolima, where more than 50,000 hectares have been affected and authorities reported significant impacts on agricultural production, livestock and rural livelihoods. Additional resource mobilization efforts continue to address remaining funding gaps, while USD 5.5 million is still required to support 30,000 wildfire-affected people through cash-based transfers.
• WFP received a USD 4.6 million Internal Resource Allocation (IRA), enabling the immediate scale-up of earthquake response activities.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-09-08) ---
On 10 August 2026, Colombia was struck by a magnitude 7.4 earthquake, with its epicenter in San José del Palmar, Chocó. Given the scale of the emergency, the National Government declared a national disaster situation and an Economic, Social and Ecological Emergency. Since then, Chocó, Valle del Cauca, Risaralda, and Caldas have remained priority departments due to the extent of the impact.
In Colombia, the emergency response is led by the National Government, within the framework of the National Disaster Risk Management System (SNGRD) and under the coordination of the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD). At the territorial level, departmental and municipal authorities, and local UNGRD personnel guide the identification of needs and response priorities.
The international humanitarian community complements the National Government- led response by aligning its capacities with the priorities identified by the authorities. At the national level, this coordination takes place through the Humanitarian Country Team (EHP) and, at the departmental level, through Local Coordination Teams (ELCs), in coordination with territorial authorities.
