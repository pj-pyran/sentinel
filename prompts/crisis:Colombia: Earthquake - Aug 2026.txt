Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-10-29) ---
A. SITUATION ANALYSIS
Description of the crisis
On 10 August 2026, at 07:34 local time, a 7.4 Mw earthquake struck western Colombia. The epicentre was located in the municipality of San José del Palmar, Chocó Department, at a depth of 103 km. The earthquake was felt across much of Colombia, as well as in Ecuador, Panama and Venezuela. According to the Colombian Geological Survey (SGC), it was the strongest earthquake recorded in Colombia in the 21st century.
Subsequent seismic activity continues to be monitored by the SGC, which has identified two distinct patterns in Chocó Department. As of 29 September, 333 aftershocks associated with the main earthquake had been recorded, mainly concentrated in San José del Palmar, El Litoral del San Juan and Sipí, at depths ranging from 70 to 105 km. According to the SGC, this activity has progressively decreased in frequency and magnitude. In parallel, a separate seismic swarm remains active in the vicinity of Istmina, Medio San Juan, Sipí and El Litoral del San Juan, at depths ranging from 30 to 70 km.
According to Situation Report No. 84 issued by the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD) on 21 September 2026, the earthquake has affected 500 municipalities across 16 departments. Valle del Cauca, Risaralda, Chocó, Quindío and Caldas account for 94 per-cent of all affected families nationwide. The report indicates that 778,281 people (326,648 families) have been affected, with 335 deaths, 4,516 people injured and 105 people reported missing. The UNGRD also reports 36,478 houses destroyed and 206,931 damaged, as well as 758 buildings collapsed and 6,047 damaged. Damage has also been reported to 4,431 educational facilities, 402 health facilities and 6,094 community facilities, as well as 182 water supply systems, 531 roads, 133 bridges (119 vehicular and 14 pedestrian) and five airports. Direct physical damage to infrastructure is estimated at COP 42.1 trillion (approximately USD 13.2 billion), equivalent to around 2.3 per cent of Colombia's Gross Domestic Product (GDP), with damage concentrated mainly in Risaralda, Valle del Cauca, Chocó, Caldas and Quindío.
The Single Registry of Disaster-Affected People (Registro Único de Damnificados – RUD) provides complementary figures, recording 778,279 people (364,670 families), as well as 104,037 uninhabitable houses, of which 19,143 are reported as destroyed, and a further 119,752 damaged houses. Differences between the two sources reflect their respective data collection and consolidation methodologies. While UNGRD figures are compiled from reports submitted by territorial authorities, the RUD records households formally registered as disaster-affected and serves as the basis for accessing national government assistance. RUD figures remain subject to eligibility verification, data cross-checking and data cleaning processes. They may therefore include duplicate records and are subject to revision in subsequent updates.
Search and rescue operations have concluded. A total of 1,849 rescuers were deployed, including 16 accredited Colombian Urban Search and Rescue (USAR) teams comprising 779 rescuers; four international teams from the United States, Ecuador and Israel, comprising 138 personnel; and 34 additional support teams comprising 932 personnel. These operations resulted in the rescue of 356 people and the recovery of 259 bodies. International teams completed their missions and returned to their respective countries between 19 and 23 August.
The emergency is unfolding in a context of concurrent seismic, hydrometeorological and climate-related hazards. This evolving situation requires the continued provision of assistance to people with immediate humanitarian needs, while progressively integrating disaster risk reduction and resilience considerations into early recovery interventions.
The El Niño phenomenon represents an additional factor influencing the evolution of the emergency. According to an official communication issued by the Institute of Hydrology, Meteorology and Environmental Studies (IDEAM) on 8 October 2026, the conditions required to officially declare an El Niño event in Colombia had been met for five consecutive overlapping three-month periods. IDEAM also indicated a probability exceeding 80 per cent that the event would reach strong to very strong intensity during the first quarter of 2027, and a 75 per cent probability that it would rank among the strongest events observed since 1950. IDEAM further confirmed that Colombia's second rainy season would continue during October and November 2026, particularly affecting the Andean, Pacific and Caribbean regions, although rainfall levels were expected to remain below normal due to the influence of El Niño. Wildfires have already been reported in areas affected by the earthquake and prioritized for the response.
This situation may have differentiated impacts across earthquake-affected areas. Reduced rainfall and higher temperatures could increase the risk of water shortages and wildfires in some locations, while parts of the Pacific region may remain exposed to episodes of heavy rainfall and landslides. In this context, information published in September 2026 identified Valle del Cauca, one of the departments prioritized by the operation, among the areas under a red alert for wildfires issued by IDEAM. This highlights the coexistence of hazards that could further exacerbate the vulnerabilities of affected communities. Consequently, recovery and rehabilitation activities will need to consider multiple risk scenarios and the potential additional impacts on water systems, livelihoods, shelter, infrastructure and essential services. This requires a multi-hazard approach, with operational planning and implementation adapted to evolving conditions and risks at the local level.
The institutional context of the response has also evolved since the onset of the emergency. Through Decree 1261 of 2026, the National Government declared a State of Economic, Social and Ecological Emergency in response to the severe public calamity caused by the earthquake, with the aim of facilitating the adoption of extraordinary measures to address its impacts.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-10-27) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• More than 778,000 people remain affected by the earthquake across 16 departments. As of 18 October, WFP had reached 47,078 people. Available resources will allow to extend assistance to 109,178 people based on government registries, food security evidence and market analysis.
• The earthquake response is unfolding amid overlapping humanitarian pressures, including deteriorating security conditions, elevated wildfire risk, El Niño and other weather-related emergencies. Access restrictions, displacement and confinement are compounding needs in conflict-affected areas, while dry conditions and belowaverage rainfall are placing additional pressure on rural livelihoods, water availability and food security.
• WFP is mobilizing complementary resources across programmes to preserve response continuity and expand coverage.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• More than 50 days after the 10 August earthquake, 778,282 people remained affected across 16 departments. The earthquake has added to Colombia’s already complex humanitarian context, where conflict, displacement, access restrictions and climate-related shocks continue to affect vulnerable populations. With 104,048 homes uninhabitable and 6,903 people living in temporary settlements, critical gaps persist in food security, shelter, WASH and protection, particularly in remote and conflict-affected areas.
• The earthquake has further intensified Colombia’s pre-existing humanitarian challenges, compounding needs and constraining relief operations in priority areas where communities face overlapping impacts from conflict, displacement and disaster-related losses. Between 1 January and 6 October, around 2,000 violent events affected 974,245 people nationwide. Humanitarian access restrictions affected 678,620 people, while attacks against civilians, displacement, armed actions and explosive devices affected more than 92,000 people.
• Weather-related risks remained elevated amid persistent dry conditions and below-average rainfall, adding pressure to communities and response capacities already affected by the earthquake. Wildfire risk was particularly high: as of 6 October, the National Hydrology and Meteorology Institute (IDEAM) reported alerts in 32 municipalities, including red alerts in Tolima, Cundinamarca and Huila, while UNGRD recorded 416 wildfire events across 18 departments during September, affecting more than 26,500 hectares. These conditions also increase the risk of water shortages, livelihood losses and other climate-related impacts.
