Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-08-19) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck western Colombia on 10 August, prompting the Government to declare a National Disaster Situation and activate a national response through the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD) and the Unified Command Post (PMU).
• As of 17 August, authorities reported 292,043 people affected, 304 fatalities, 4,548 injured people and 426 missing people across 472 municipalities in 15 departments, with the most severe impacts in Valle del Cauca, Risaralda, Chocó and Caldas.
• Damage assessments continue to increase, with 134,342 homes damaged and 29,554 homes destroyed, alongside significant impacts on critical infrastructure and at least 413 roads affected.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-08-18) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
On 10 August 2026, at 7:34 a.m., a magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck with its epicenter in San José del Palmar, Chocó Department. In response, the National Government declared a national disaster situation through Presidential Decree No. 1171 of 2026. In the days following the event, response operations have concentrated on the departments most severely affected: Valle del Cauca, Chocó, Risaralda, Caldas, and Quindío.

SITUATION UPDATE
• The Colombian Geological Service has reported a total of 183 aftershocks, including 15 events above magnitude 3 and two above magnitude 4.
• Information regarding damages and affected populations continues to be consolidated, incorporating new territories and updating initial estimates as Damage and Needs Assessments (EDAN) and territorial reports progress.
• Since the event, damage and impact assessments have been carried out in affected areas, identifying differentiated impacts across municipalities and departments.
• Increased rainfall in several departments, particularly Chocó, has worsened the situation of affected populations.

--- Report 3: WFP (2026-08-18) ---
BOGOTA, Colombia – The United Nations World Food Programme (WFP) is scaling up emergency operations in Colombia, reaching survivors in some of the country’s most affected and vulnerable regions with critical food assistance following the 7.4-magnitude earthquake that struck on 10 August.
WFP teams have been supporting government-led relief and recovery efforts with more than 2,000 people receiving food assistance in Pereira and Quibdó on Sunday alone. Operations will expand throughout the week with WFP deploying food stocks and leveraging its network of field offices and logistics hubs in Cali, Quibdó and Medellín to reach affected communities.
&quot;The solidarity shown in the immediate aftermath of the earthquake has been extraordinary,” said Nils Grede, WFP Country Director in Colombia. “Communities, businesses and individuals have stepped forward to help those affected, but we know from experience that recovery does not end when the first wave of support subsides. Sustained humanitarian assistance will be essential in the weeks and months to come, and WFP is committed to standing alongside affected communities for as long as they need support.”
As the scale of humanitarian needs becomes clearer, WFP is appealing for an initial USD 23 million to deliver emergency food assistance to 100,000 people over three months and continues to adapt its response to conditions on the ground. In remote communities where markets are not functioning and access remains difficult, WFP is prioritizing in-kind food assistance. In urban centres such as Valle del Cauca and Risaralda, where markets and basic services are beginning to recover, cash-based assistance can be rapidly scaled up, allowing families to buy the food they need while supporting local businesses and economic recovery.
In close coordination with government authorities and humanitarian partners, WFP is also supporting the broader response through logistics expertise and common services. Four mobile storage units are on standby and additional relief items are available for deployment from the WFP-managed Humanitarian Response Depot in Panama.
Even before the earthquake, close to seven million people in Colombia faced moderate or severe food insecurity, with the worst-hit regions also impacted by conflict and displacement. In Chocó and Valle del Cauca, close to one third of the population is food insecure. These vulnerabilities are now deepening as families grapple with the loss of their homes, livelihoods, and access to essential services.

--- Report 4: EHP Colombia (2026-08-18) ---
This Flash Update is produced by OCHA Colombia in collaboration with humanitarian partners, based on official reports (as of 17 August, 20:00 local time)
More than 186,000 people have been affected across 15 departments and 472 municipalities nationwide. The emergency continues to evolve and figures may increase as assessments progress in the most affected areas.
The Government continues to implement a comprehensive and sustained response across affected areas through the activation of the National Disaster Risk Management System, humanitarian assistance, restoration of essential services and support measures for affected families.
The response is further strengthened by complementary support from the international community, the United Nations, humanitarian organizations and the private sector, with a focus on saving lives, addressing urgent needs and supporting the recovery of affected communities.
