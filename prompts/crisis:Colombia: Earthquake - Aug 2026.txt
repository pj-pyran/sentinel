Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-09-03) ---
On 10 August 2026, Colombia was struck by a magnitude 7.4 earthquake, with its epicenter in San José del Palmar, Chocó. Given the scale of the emergency, the National Government declared a national disaster situation and an Economic, Social and Ecological Emergency. Since then, Chocó, Valle del Cauca, Risaralda, and Caldas have remained priority departments due to the extent of the impact. In Colombia, the emergency response is led by the National Government, within the framework of the National Disaster Risk Management System (SNGRD) and under the coordination of the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD). At the territorial level, departmental and municipal authorities, and local UNGRD personnel guide the identification of needs and response priorities. The international humanitarian community complements the National Government- led response by aligning its capacities with the priorities identified by the authorities. At the national level, this coordination takes place through the Humanitarian Country Team (EHP) and, at the departmental level, through Local Coordination Teams (ELCs), in coordination with territorial authorities.

--- Report 2: EHP Colombia (2026-09-02) ---
The earthquake has triggered a large-scale humanitarian emergency. More than 466,600 people have been affected, including 331 fatalities, thousands of people injured or missing, and more than 36,000 homes destroyed. The emergency continues to affect entire communities, with severe damage to basic infrastructure, education facilities and access to essential services.
Urgent needs persist, alongside risks of compounded impacts. In addition to the damage caused by the earthquake, many communities are facing compounded impacts from other emergencies and risks associated with armed conflict. The Government response is prioritizing health, water, temporary shelter and humanitarian assistance; however, humanitarian needs remain significant.
Humanitarian action and international support remain essential. At least 29 humanitarian organizations have provided assistance to nearly 123,000 people across 49 municipalities, complementing the response led by national and local authorities. The international community has mobilized more than US$40 million, while the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan Addendum continues to seek funding to address the needs of affected populations through the end of the year.
Coordination among authorities, humanitarian organizations, the private sector and international partners remains critical to saving lives, restoring essential services and supporting the recovery of affected communities.
