Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-10-23) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• From January to July 2026, WFP assisted approximately 423,427 people across Colombia, providing life-saving food assistance and cashbased transfers to populations affected by conflict, climate shocks and mixed migration.
• On 10 August, a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck central-western Colombia, affecting an estimated 466,606 people across 15 departments. Preliminary assessments indicate disruptions to livelihoods, local markets and essential services, increasing food security risks among vulnerable households and generating additional humanitarian needs related to shelter, early recovery and food assistance.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Despite being an upper-middle-income country, Colombia continues to face a complex humanitarian and food security crisis. WFP's joint food security assessment in the 15 most vulnerable departments found that 37 percent of households, equivalent to approximately 7 million people, experience moderate or severe food insecurity. Nationally, food insecurity remains at around 25 percent, with Atlántico, Sucre, and La Guajira reporting the highest prevalence of severe food insecurity.
• The humanitarian situation is further compounded by persistent violence, forced displacement, the presence of non-state armed groups, mixed migration flows, natural hazards, and economic shocks. From January to July 2026, an estimated 814,000 people were affected by violence, while more than 990,000 people were impacted by weather-related events across the country. According to the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), 6.9 million people require humanitarian assistance in Colombia, including 6.6 million in need of food security and nutrition support. Colombia's decades-long armed conflict has resulted in 10.3 million registered victims, approximately 90 percent of whom are internally displaced persons. In 2025 alone, violence affected more than 1.6 million people and added approximately 150,000 new victims to the official registry.
• This already challenging context was further aggravated by the 7.4-magnitude earthquake that struck Colombia on 10 August 2026, affecting an estimated 466,606 people across 15 departments and approximately 145 municipalities. Preliminary assessments indicate humanitarian needs related to shelter, food security, livelihoods and early recovery, while disruptions to local economies, markets and informal livelihoods have reduced household incomes and increased vulnerability among affected populations. WFP's rapid food security impact analysis identified Chocó, Valle del Cauca and Risaralda as the departments with the highest concentrations of highly vulnerable earthquake-affected populations, followed by Quindío and Caldas, highlighting the potential for increased food insecurity among households with limited capacity to withstand additional shocks.
• Colombia also hosts one of the largest migrant populations in Latin America, with approximately 2.8 million migrants residing in the country. By the end of June 2026, 51,938 migrants had entered and transited Colombia irregularly, representing a 38 percent decrease compared to the same period in 2025.
• WFP has operated in Colombia since 1969. Through its Country Strategic Plan, WFP supports national priorities on food security, humanitarian response, early recovery, and peacebuilding, contributing to SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) and SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals).

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-10-23) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• From January to August 2026, WFP assisted approximately 523,709 people across Colombia, providing life-saving food assistance to populations affected by conflict, natural shocks and mixed migration. Armed conflict remains a key driver of humanitarian needs, with displacement, confinement and access constraints continuing to affect vulnerable communities.
• On 10 August, a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck central-western Colombia, affecting an estimated 778,274 people across 15 departments. The earthquake caused significant damage to infrastructure and housing, with more than 242,000 homes affected and 521 roads damaged or destroyed, limiting access to affected communities and the delivery of humanitarian assistance.
• El Niño conditions continued to intensify during August and are expected to persist into early 2027. By the end of August, 306 municipalities remained under fire-risk alerts, while high temperatures and reduced rainfall continued to pressure water resources, agriculture, and energy generation. Up to 2.9 million people could be affected, exacerbating food insecurity and humanitarian needs.

• Despite being an upper-middle-income country, Colombia continues to face a complex humanitarian and food security crisis. WFP's joint food security assessment in the 15 most vulnerable departments found that 37 percent of households, equivalent to approximately 7 million people, experience moderate or severe food insecurity. From January to August 2026, an estimated 856,000 people were affected by violence, while more than 1.5 million people were impacted by weatherrelated events across the country.

• Prior to the August earthquake, the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) had identified 6.9 million people with humanitarian needs in Colombia, driven by armed conflict, displacement, food insecurity, climate-related shocks and mixed migration flows. After the earthquake, the Humanitarian Country Team issued an Addendum to the HRP, estimating that an additional 1.2 million people had humanitarian needs as a result of the emergency, increasing the total number of people in need to 8.1 million. Looking ahead, El Niño is expected to further compound humanitarian needs. OCHA estimates that between 1.7 and 3 million additional people could fall into food insecurity.

By the end of August 2026, 69,072 migrants had entered and transited Colombia irregularly, representing a 39 percent decrease compared to the same period in 2025.
