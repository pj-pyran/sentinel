Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: UNFPA (2026-08-13) ---
On 10 August, Colombia was struck by a 7.4-magnitude earthquake, affecting 380 municipalities, with the most significant impacts reported in Chocó, the Eje Cafetero and Valle del Cauca. Preliminary reports indicate that 181 people have died and 2,754 have been injured, while search and rescue operations remain ongoing.
The earthquake’s epicentre was located in San José del Palmar, in Chocó Department. Characterized by remote and rural areas, Chocó relies heavily on river and air transport as the primary means of access, creating significant challenges for the delivery of essential services and supplies. Existing structural vulnerabilities in the department further exacerbate the impact of the disaster and the difficulties faced by affected communities.
The healthcare network has been significantly affected, with 1,172 health facilities reportedly impacted. At the same time, the surge in demand for medical care resulting from the large number of people injured has placed considerable pressure on already strained health services. Disruptions to essential healthcare services pose heightened risks to pregnant women and newborns.
Preliminary assessments indicate that more than 19,000 homes have been damaged and 3,971 destroyed, displacing thousands of people. Displacement, overcrowding in temporary shelters, disruption of community support systems and economic hardship are increasing protection risks, including gender-based violence, exploitation and abuse, particularly for women and girls.
UNFPA is on the ground, working with partners to mobilize resources and rapidly assess and respond to the most urgent sexual and reproductive health and protection needs of women, girls, adolescents and other people in vulnerable situations. Priority areas include maternal healthcare, emergency obstetric care, family planning, prevention of and response to gender-based violence, and the clinical management of rape.

--- Report 2: HOPE (2026-08-13) ---
Project HOPE's Emergency Response Team is on the ground in the Valle del Cauca and Risaralda departments of Colombia, rapidly assessing needs after a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck 150 miles west of Bogotá and caused widespread damage, landslides, and impacted critical infrastructure across a large geographic area.
Situational Context
There have been over 29 aftershocks since a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck near San Jose del Palmar, Colombia, and caused extensive damage across a wide geographic area that includes rural, urban, hard-to-reach, and low-income communities hundreds of miles from the epicenter. More than 357 municipalities across western Colombia have been impacted, with the full extent of the damage unknown due to internet connectivity issues.
The earthquake has damaged or destroyed more than 9,400 homes, killed at least 254 people, and injured at least 2,595 more — thousands of people are likely missing, and these numbers are expected to increase in the coming days and weeks.
Across the impacted region, 84 health facilities sustained damage and many have been forced to reduce or suspend services. In El Águila, San Rafael Hospital suffered near-complete destruction and, in Cali, Hospital Universitario del Valle Evaristo García suffered damage that forced it to move patients outside and reduce services, despite being one of the department's primary tertiary hospitals. Public officials have declared a “red alert” for hospitals in Cali as facilities are fully at capacity due to both the damage caused by the disaster and the subsequent surge of patient needs. Departments have established shelters across the region as impacted individuals attempt to avoid residing in damaged buildings. Shelters have noted the need for increased support for hygiene supplies and larger provisions of potable water. Damage to municipal water systems has left some communities without running water until assessments and repairs can be completed. Information remains limited on health, water, and medical needs in more rural areas — especially in Chocó Department — due to limited road access and connectivity issues.

--- Report 3: Fundación Abba Colombia (2026-08-13) ---
Executive Summary
On August 10, 2026, Colombia was affected by a major earthquake with a magnitude of 7.4, occurring at approximately 7:34 a.m., with its epicenter in the department of Chocó, near San José del Palmar. The earthquake was widely felt across the country and caused significant damage, particularly in Chocó and several areas of the Coffee Region.
The department of Chocó is facing a particularly complex situation due to the combination of earthquake-related damage, existing infrastructure limitations, historical access challenges, and limited local capacity to respond to an emergency of this magnitude.
According to reports available as of August 11, approximately 500 homes had been affected and around 20 structures had collapsed in San José del Palmar. In Quibdó, at least 40 homes were reported destroyed, with 13 people killed and 131 injured. Authorities and emergency response organizations continue to conduct search and rescue operations, damage assessments, and assistance to affected populations.
The situation remains dynamic, and figures will need to be updated as access is gained to rural and hard-toreach communities.
In response, Fundación ABBACOL is coordinating a humanitarian response with the support of local communities and stakeholders, initially prioritizing psychosocial support and needs assessments while seeking national and international cooperation to expand the response and mobilize humanitarian assistance to affected communities.

--- Report 4: EHP Colombia (2026-08-13) ---
This Flash Update is produced by OCHA Colombia in collaboration with humanitarian partners, based on official reports (as of 12 August, 18:30 local time)
The emergency continues to escalate and has exceeded the local response capacity, with at least 49,214 people affected, 30,324 families impacted, 3,771 injured people, and 241 fatalities across 13 departments of the country. The damage to housing, social infrastructure, and essential services highlights a large-scale humanitarian crisis that requires a sustained and coordinated response.
Humanitarian needs are multisectoral, and significant response gaps remain, particularly in Risaralda, Valle del Cauca, Chocó, and Caldas. Damage to more than 54,900 homes (destroyed and damaged), educational institutions, health facilities, water supply systems, and roads is limiting access for thousands of people to basic services, protection, healthcare, safe water, and livelihoods.
The national and international response is underway and continues to expand to save lives and complement institutional capacity. While search and rescue operations, humanitarian assistance, and rapid needs assessments continue, the USD 5 million CERF allocation represents a critical boost to scale up the emergency response and support the populations most affected by the earthquake in Colombia.

--- Report 5: WFP (2026-08-12) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• A 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck western Colombia on 10 August, prompting the Government to declare a National Disaster Situation and activate a national response through the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD) and the Unified Command Post (PMU).
• OCHA reports impacts across 16 departments and 357 municipalities, including areas already affected by conflict, displacement and recurrent climaterelated shocks, raising concerns over increased humanitarian needs and access constraints.
• The USGS estimates that approximately 2 million people experienced strong shaking and indicates a one-in-three probability that fatalities could exceed 1,000 people, highlighting the potential severity of the event.
• WFP currently operates emergency response activities in Chocó, Valle del Cauca and Risaralda, reaching approximately 100,000 people, and maintains field offices in Quibdó and Cali, providing an operational platform for assessments and response planning.
• All WFP personnel have been accounted for. WFP offices in Quibdó and Cali reported infrastructure damage and business continuity measures have been activated.
Situation Update
• At 07:34 local time on 10 August 2026, a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck western Colombia, with its epicenter near San José del Palmar, Chocó Department, at a depth of approximately 96 km. The earthquake was felt across the country, triggering evacuations and emergency response activations in multiple departments and major urban centers. Several aftershocks have been reported following the main event.
• The departments of Chocó, Valle del Cauca, Risaralda, Caldas and Quindío are among the most affected. Following an emergency meeting convened at UNGRD, the President declared a National Disaster Situation and established a centralized PMU to coordinate response efforts and consolidate official information.
• According to the latest official government balance, as of 7:00am COT - 11 August 2026, the earthquake has resulted in 176 confirmed fatalities and 2,503 injured people. Authorities also reported 7,436 damaged houses, 1,136 destroyed houses, and 48 collapsed buildings. Damage to essential infrastructure includes 377 health facilities, 791 educational facilities and 377 community centers, while 69 roads have been affected.
• According to Colombia´s Civil Aviation Authority six airports remain affected and suspended for commercial operations, pending safety and infrastructure assessments, and one additional airport has reported damage to its runway.
• Search and rescue operations remain ongoing in the most severely affected areas and casualty figures are expected to evolve as assessments continue.
• According to the USGS modelling, approximately 2 million people experienced strong shaking across western and central Colombia. USGS impact modelling also estimates a 33 percent probability that fatalities could eventually exceed 1,000 people, reflecting the severity of ground shaking and the concentration of exposed populations in affected urban areas. These figures are predictive estimates and remain subject to confirmation through ongoing government-led assessments.
• National and local authorities have mobilized Urban Search and Rescue (USAR) teams, emergency services, and technical assessment teams to support affected communities and restore critical services. The Government has indicated that updated information will be released regularly through the PMU as new reports are received from departmental and municipal authorities.
• The earthquake has affected several areas already facing humanitarian challenges linked to conflict, displacement, poverty and food insecurity. Damage to transport infrastructure, public services and local markets may further increase humanitarian needs and constrain access to food and basic services for vulnerable populations. WFP is monitoring the evolving situation and coordinating with government authorities, UN agencies and humanitarian partners to assess food security implications and potential response requirements.
• According to the National Route Institute (INVÍAS), the earthquake affected at least 13 locations across the national road network. The most significant access constraints include the total closure of the Cali-Loboguerrero corridor in Dagua (Valle del Cauca), the Cartago-Alcalá corridor, the Manizales-Fresno corridor in Caldas, and the Santa Cecilia-Asia corridor in Pueblo Rico (Risaralda). Multiple landslides have also affected strategic transport routes in Quindío, including the Calarcá-Cajamarca and Armenia-Montenegro-Alcalá corridors.
• Access assessments remain ongoing, particularly in earthquake-affected areas of Chocó, where access limitations continue to constrain damage assessments and the identification of humanitarian needs. Official estimates of the total number of affected people are not yet available.

--- Report 6: EHP Colombia (2026-08-12) ---
SP
Este Flash update es producido por OCHA Colombia en colaboración con los socios humanitarios y reportes oficiales. Corte a 11 agosto (17:30 horas)
La magnitud e impacto de la emergencia supera lo esperado. El terremoto de magnitud 7,4 registrado el 10 de agosto en San José del Palmar (Chocó), junto con 29 réplicas significativas, ha generado una emergencia humanitaria de gran escala en Colombia. Hasta el momento, 13 departamentos y 357 municipios reportan afectaciones, con un saldo preliminar de 224 personas fallecidas, 2.595 heridas y severos daños en viviendas e infraestructura esencial, siendo Risaralda, Valle del Cauca, Chocó y Caldas los departamentos más impactados.
La respuesta se implementa y coordinada a pesar de desafíos operacionales. El Gobierno Nacional, a través del Sistema Nacional de Gestión del Riesgo de Desastres (SNGRD), lidera las operaciones de respuesta, rescate y asistencia inicial, con el apoyo complementario de Naciones Unidas y socios humanitarios nacionales e internacionales. No obstante, la continuidad de las afectaciones a servicios esenciales, incluyendo electricidad, agua, salud y conectividad, así como las restricciones de acceso derivadas de daños en al menos 73 vías, representan desafíos críticos para la asistencia y recuperación temprana de las comunidades afectadas.
Persisten necesidades humanitarias y brechas de información. Las necesidades prioritarias identificadas incluyen búsqueda y rescate, alojamiento temporal, atención en salud, seguridad alimentaria, educación y protección. Sin embargo, persisten importantes vacíos de información sobre el número total de personas damnificadas, la ubicación y condiciones de los albergues, así como sobre las necesidades específicas a nivel municipal, limitando una respuesta plenamente focalizada y basada en evidencias.
ENG
This Flash Update is produced by OCHA Colombia in collaboration with humanitarian partners and based on official reports. Reporting cut-off: 11 August (5:30 p.m.).
The scale and impact of the emergency exceed initial expectations. The 7.4-magnitude earthquake recorded on 10 August in San José del Palmar, Chocó Department, together with 29 significant aftershocks, has triggered a large-scale humanitarian emergency in Colombia. To date, 13 departments and 357 municipalities have reported impacts, with a preliminary toll of 224 fatalities and 2,595 injuries, as well as extensive damage to housing and critical infrastructure. Risaralda, Valle del Cauca, Chocó and Caldas are the most affected departments.
Response efforts are being implemented and coordinated despite operational challenges. The National Government, through the National System for Disaster Risk Management (SNGRD), is leading response operations, including search and rescue efforts and the provision of initial assistance, with complementary support from the United Nations and national and international humanitarian partners. However, continued disruptions to essential services, including electricity, water, health care and telecommunications, together with access constraints resulting from damage to at least 73 roads, pose critical challenges to humanitarian assistance and early recovery efforts in affected communities.
Humanitarian needs and information gaps persist. Priority needs identified to date include search and rescue, temporary shelter, health care, food security, education and protection. However, significant information gaps remain regarding the total number of affected people, the location and conditions of shelters, and specific needs at the municipal level, limiting a fully targeted and evidence-based response.
