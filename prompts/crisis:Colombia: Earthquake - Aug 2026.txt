Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-08-18) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
On 10 August 2026, at 7:34 a.m., a magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck with its epicenter in San José del Palmar, Chocó Department. In response, the National Government declared a national disaster situation through Presidential Decree No. 1171 of 2026. In the days following the event, response operations have concentrated on the departments most severely affected: Valle del Cauca, Chocó, Risaralda, Caldas, and Quindío.

SITUATION UPDATE
• The Colombian Geological Service has reported a total of 183 aftershocks, including 15 events above magnitude 3 and two above magnitude 4.
• Information regarding damages and affected populations continues to be consolidated, incorporating new territories and updating initial estimates as Damage and Needs Assessments (EDAN) and territorial reports progress.
• Since the event, damage and impact assessments have been carried out in affected areas, identifying differentiated impacts across municipalities and departments.
• Increased rainfall in several departments, particularly Chocó, has worsened the situation of affected populations.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-08-18) ---
BOGOTA, Colombia – The United Nations World Food Programme (WFP) is scaling up emergency operations in Colombia, reaching survivors in some of the country’s most affected and vulnerable regions with critical food assistance following the 7.4-magnitude earthquake that struck on 10 August.
WFP teams have been supporting government-led relief and recovery efforts with more than 2,000 people receiving food assistance in Pereira and Quibdó on Sunday alone. Operations will expand throughout the week with WFP deploying food stocks and leveraging its network of field offices and logistics hubs in Cali, Quibdó and Medellín to reach affected communities.
&quot;The solidarity shown in the immediate aftermath of the earthquake has been extraordinary,” said Nils Grede, WFP Country Director in Colombia. “Communities, businesses and individuals have stepped forward to help those affected, but we know from experience that recovery does not end when the first wave of support subsides. Sustained humanitarian assistance will be essential in the weeks and months to come, and WFP is committed to standing alongside affected communities for as long as they need support.”
As the scale of humanitarian needs becomes clearer, WFP is appealing for an initial USD 23 million to deliver emergency food assistance to 100,000 people over three months and continues to adapt its response to conditions on the ground. In remote communities where markets are not functioning and access remains difficult, WFP is prioritizing in-kind food assistance. In urban centres such as Valle del Cauca and Risaralda, where markets and basic services are beginning to recover, cash-based assistance can be rapidly scaled up, allowing families to buy the food they need while supporting local businesses and economic recovery.
In close coordination with government authorities and humanitarian partners, WFP is also supporting the broader response through logistics expertise and common services. Four mobile storage units are on standby and additional relief items are available for deployment from the WFP-managed Humanitarian Response Depot in Panama.
Even before the earthquake, close to seven million people in Colombia faced moderate or severe food insecurity, with the worst-hit regions also impacted by conflict and displacement. In Chocó and Valle del Cauca, close to one third of the population is food insecure. These vulnerabilities are now deepening as families grapple with the loss of their homes, livelihoods, and access to essential services.

--- Report 3: EHP Colombia (2026-08-18) ---
This Flash Update is produced by OCHA Colombia in collaboration with humanitarian partners, based on official reports (as of 17 August, 20:00 local time)
More than 186,000 people have been affected across 15 departments and 472 municipalities nationwide. The emergency continues to evolve and figures may increase as assessments progress in the most affected areas.
The Government continues to implement a comprehensive and sustained response across affected areas through the activation of the National Disaster Risk Management System, humanitarian assistance, restoration of essential services and support measures for affected families.
The response is further strengthened by complementary support from the international community, the United Nations, humanitarian organizations and the private sector, with a focus on saving lives, addressing urgent needs and supporting the recovery of affected communities.

--- Report 4: HOPE (2026-08-17) ---
Project HOPE's Emergency Response Team is on the ground in the Valle del Cauca and Risaralda departments of Colombia, distributing hygiene supplies and rapidly assessing needs after a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck 150 miles west of Bogotá causing widespread damage, landslides, and impacted critical infrastructure across a large geographic area.
Situational Context
The full scale of the impact continues to be assessed since a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck near San Jose del Palmar, Colombia, on Monday August 10, continued to be assessed. Damage extends across a wide geographic area, including urban and rural areas across a range of socio-economic levels. More than 395 municipalities across western Colombia were affected and connectivity and access issues continue to limit a full assessment.
As of this morning, August 13, Colombia’s national disaster management agency (UNGRD) reports 273 dead, 3,824 injured, and 377 missing nationwide. Other sources state the number of missing at nearly 500. Approximately 75% of fatalities are concentrated in Quibdó, Pereira, and Valle del Cauca.
Over 12,500 homes have been destroyed and nearly 75,000 homes have been damaged, and 172 structures collapsed. Search and rescue operations, led by the National Risk and Disaster Management System, are transitioning from active rescue to recovery in the hardest hit areas. Chocó’s SAR phase was formally closed yesterday after a family of four was located alive. In Pereira and Cali, teams report shifting to recovery of bodies as the window for finding and rescuing survivors narrows, though efforts continue.
With 216 health centers sustaining damage, the need for hygiene, water, and medical support is significant. Access to more rural areas, especially Chocó, continues to be difficult. Chocó also sustained a 4.2 magnitude aftershock this morning, and officials stated the aftershock hindered utility restoration efforts.

--- Report 5: IFRC (2026-08-17) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
10-08-2026
What happened, where and when?
On 10 August 2026, at 07:34 a.m. local time, a magnitude 7.4 (Mw) earthquake struck western Colombia. According to the Colombian Geological Service (SGC), the epicentre was located in the municipality of San José del Palmar, department of Chocó, at a depth of 103 km.
The SGC identified it as the highest-magnitude earthquake recorded in Colombia during the twenty-first century. The main event was followed by at least 103 aftershocks, including a magnitude 4.8 event recorded near San José del Palmar 44 minutes later. Further aftershocks may continue to occur.
The earthquake was felt across much of Colombia and was also reported in parts of Panama and Venezuela. Initial reports indicated possible impacts in as many as 16 departments. As information was subsequently verified and consolidated, the latest available humanitarian update, issued on 12 August 2026, reported impacts across 14 departments and 395 municipalities. The most severe impacts are concentrated in Chocó and Valle del Cauca, in western Colombia, and in Risaralda, Caldas and Quindío, which comprise the Coffee Region (Eje Cafetero). Major affected urban areas include the departmental capitals of Quibdó, Cali, Pereira, Manizales and Armenia.
Preliminary information indicates significant loss of life and injuries, as well as damage to housing, public buildings and essential infrastructure. Access and communications constraints, particularly in remote and rural areas of Chocó, continue to complicate the consolidation of information and the identification of affected communities and priority humanitarian needs.
Following the earthquake, the coordination mechanisms of the National Disaster Risk Management System (SNGRD) were activated at national and territorial levels. The National Government convened the National Committee for Disaster Management and established a Unified Command Post at the headquarters of the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD) to coordinate search and rescue
operations, emergency assistance, initial damage and needs assessments, and the deployment of response capacities.
Given the magnitude of the earthquake, its geographical reach and the severity of the reported impacts, the National Government declared a national disaster situation on the same day. This declaration enables the administrative, budgetary and coordination mechanisms required to mobilise resources, support affected territorial authorities and facilitate the immediate response and subsequent recovery.
The situation remains fluid. Assessments by the Colombian Red Cross Society and the competent government authorities are ongoing, including in remote and hard-to-reach areas. Consequently, the situation overview, geographical scope, identified needs and reported figures are expected to evolve as further information is verified and consolidated.
