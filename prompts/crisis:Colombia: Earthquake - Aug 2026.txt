Crisis / situation: Colombia: Earthquake - Aug 2026

--- Report 1: EHP Colombia (2026-09-10) ---
One month after the earthquake, more than 466,000 people remain affected across 16 departments. While response and early recovery efforts continue, extensive damage to housing and critical infrastructure is sustaining acute humanitarian needs, particularly in Risaralda, Valle del Cauca, Chocó, Quindío and Caldas.
The Government, the National System for Disaster Risk Management (SNGRD) and international partners have assisted more than 135,000 people through multi-sectoral interventions in 53 priority municipalities. Close coordination among authorities, humanitarian actors and affected communities remains critical to address urgent needs and accelerate sustainable recovery solutions.
Recovery efforts face growing challenges amid concurrent crises, including armed conflict, wildfires, drought and flooding. Additional and timely funding is needed to address humanitarian gaps, scale up assistance and support the recovery of affected communities through the end of 2026.
The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), in partnership with the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD) and the National Government, is advancing post-earthquake early recovery efforts. Through thematic coordination platforms, UNDP is supporting resilient and sustainable reconstruction informed by the rapid assessment, which highlights the extent of damage to critical infrastructure.

--- Report 2: HOPE (2026-09-10) ---
Four weeks after a 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck near San José del Palmar in Chocó Department, communities in Colombia continue to require humanitarian support as families attempt to recover. Colombia’s National Unit for Disaster Risk Management’s official count has reached 311 people killed, 4,449 injured, and 240 missing. Communities in the affected area have experienced over 50 substantial aftershocks in the last month, threatening already fragile infrastructure. Across 448 municipalities in 15 departments, over 57,000 families were affected and more than 80,000 homes damaged. Over 12,000 homes were completely destroyed and some 260 health centers were left damaged. The chaos of the past month has disrupted access to healthcare and essential services like mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS).
