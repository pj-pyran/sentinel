Crisis / situation: Cambodia

--- Report 1: FAO (2026-07-15) ---
Flood Early Warning
With floods forecasted during the next 10 days, protect roots from standing water and secure the orchard. On low ground, raise soil mounds around young trees 30-50 cm (about knee height) but do not bury the graft union. Open diversion channels one hand wide and one palm deep to lead water to safe outlets; keep inlets free of trash. Move tools, fertilizers, and crates to higher shelves (about one palm above floor). Mulch 10-15 cm (about a palm to a palm-and-a- half) with dry rice straw or chopped prunings, keeping a hand-width gap at the trunk. Harvestable fruit in very low plots should be picked early and stored above floor level. After floods recede, remove (rogue) trees that do not recover and dispose of plant waste away from the orchard; wash tools with soapy water.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-07-13) ---
Key Messages

Drier- and warmer-than-normal conditions persisted across much of Cambodia in June 2026, contributing to reduced soil moisture and vegetation stress.
With an intensifying El Niño, more erratic weather patterns are expected during July–October 2026, bringing less rainfall to the northwestern, northern, and southern regions, heavier rainfall to western, coastal, and northeastern areas, and warmer-than-normal temperatures across the country.
These conditions could heighten the risk of alternating dry spells, localized flash flooding, and heat stress, with potential impacts on water resources, crop production, public health, energy demand, and livelihoods, underscoring the importance of monitoring MoWRAM weather updates and strengthening preparedness measures.
