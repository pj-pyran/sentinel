Crisis / situation: Cambodia

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-11-06) ---
Highlights

The World Meteorological Organization’s (WMO) September 2026 update indicated a near-100% likelihood that El Niño would persist through February 2027. Its October update highlighted that the event was intensifying and expected to peak around December. [1, 2]
Cambodia’s potential El Niño risk window is expected to come late 2026 to mid-2027 (FAO, WFP). The regional outlook indicates an increased likelihood of below-normal rainfall in parts of eastern Mainland Southeast Asia, and above-normal temperatures across much of Southeast Asia including Cambodia. Local impacts will vary. [4]
Cambodia’s Ministry of Water Resources and Meteorology (MOWRAM) reported that the country’s rainy season was expected to end around the third week of October 2026, with rainfall gradually decreasing as the country transitioned towards the dry season. The Ministry urged efficient water use and conservation. [3]
UNICEF Cambodia’s forecast for 16–29 October indicates below-normal rainfall—up to 20 mm below the 1996–2025 historical average—in parts of Kampong Cham, Kampong Chhnang, Kampong Thom, Kratie, Mondulkiri, and Ratanakiri provinces. Other areas are forecast to receive above-normal rainfall—at least 20 mm above the historical average. Above-normal maximum temperatures are not identified as a concern in this two-week forecast, and no meteorological drought is expected.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-11-04) ---
KEY MESSAGES
Fuel and fertilizer prices remained elevated during Q3 2026. Nationwide, diesel prices were up 49% year-on-year (YoY), while urea fertilizer prices in the Tonle Sap provinces increased by 19.4% YoY.
Trade with the Middle East remained volatile due to fluctuations in fuel and LNG imports, highlighting Cambodia's continued exposure to external energy shocks.
The food basket cost remained broadly stable during Q3 2026, although food costs remained higher than a year earlier, especially in rural areas.
Q3 2026 was characterized by easing inflation compared with Q2 levels. However, inflation in September remained 5.3% above 2025 levels, indicating persistent cost pressures on households.
Food purchasing power weakened in Q3 2026 as the wage-to-food ratio declined by 5.1% nationwide and 4.3% at the provincial level, driven by falling non-agricultural wages, while food purchasing power of agricultural labourers remained stable.
BACKGROUND
The ongoing conflict in Middle-East (ME), compounding existing global crises, has intensified regional and global economic uncertainty, with increasing spillover effects on international commodity markets. This has led to disruptions to key trade routes, heightened security risks in major maritime corridors, and increased volatility in global energy markets, driving up transportation and insurance costs. These pressures are being transmitted through global supply chains, affecting the prices and availability of fuel, fertilizer, and selected food commodities, particularly vegetable oil, in Cambodia, an import-dependent country.
To better understand and monitor the potential impacts of the ME crisis on domestic market dynamics, since June 2026, WFP Cambodia has expanded its remote market monitoring from 10 provinces around the Tonle Sap Lake (36 markets), to 24 provinces nationwide (63 markets). Combined with secondary analysis of available prices, income/expenditure, and trade, this approach tracks price fluctuation of food, energy, and fertilizer, with the objective of identifying emerging risks such as price surges, supply constraints, and trade disruptions that may undermine household purchasing power and food security, particularly among vulnerable populations.
