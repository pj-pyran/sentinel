Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-11-03) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

Protracted displacement and limited returns: The number of internally displaced people back in their areas of origin increased by 13 per cent since September, to 964,689, while around 304,500 people remain displaced outside their areas of origin.
Explosive ordnance contamination and access restrictions: Landmines, explosive remnants of war and continued military activities continue to threaten civilian safety and restrict access to agricultural lands, particularly during the olive harvest season, undermining livelihoods and recovery.
Education resumes amid damaged infrastructure and continued use of schools as collective shelters. The academic year commenced in mid-September although approximately 57 public educational facilities remain in use as collective shelters and despite extensive damage to schools across South and Nabatieh governorates.
Shelter consolidation underway. Humanitarian partners are prioritizing safe, voluntary and dignified relocation for IDPs still in shelters scheduled to close, with targeted assistance for vulnerable displaced households inside and outside shelters amid funding and access constraints.

KEY HIGHLIGHTS

WFP is preparing to launch a two-month mixed modality assistance pilot for recently displaced Syrian households in collective shelters in Jabal Mohsen in Tripoli.
The Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster facilitated seven convoys to hard-to-reach locations in South Lebanon and supported two regional Jordan-Lebanon convoys.
WFP and UNHCR Representatives undertook a high-level advocacy mission to Berlin, engaging Members of Parliament, BMZ, GFFO and key policy stakeholders. The mission reinforced the need for sustained international support to Lebanon, spanning humanitarian funding, social protection and capacity strengthening.

--- Report 3: UNHCR (2026-11-03) ---
The first edition of the Inter-Agency Response Update provides an overview of the evolving humanitarian situation, response efforts, and coordination priorities under the 2026 Lebanon Response Plan (LRP) Addendum. It highlights continued displacement, with over 304,500 people remaining outside their areas of origin, alongside persistent protection risks, damaged infrastructure and restricted humanitarian access, particularly in south Lebanon. The update also outlines ongoing efforts to support displaced households inside and outside collective shelters, facilitate safe and dignified returns, maintain essential services and strengthen winter preparedness. It further examines operational challenges and coordination efforts to address emerging needs amid funding and access constraints.
