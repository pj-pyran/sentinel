Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: Health Cluster (2026-07-14) ---
Situation Update
Despite the lower intensity of hostilities observed in Lebanon since mid-June, the health response continues to operate in a fragile and unpredictable environment. Insecurity, access constraints, return movements, damaged infrastructure, and service disruptions continue to affect operational planning and the continuity of essential health services, particularly in conflict-affected, border, and return areas.
Since 6 July, no new incidents have been reported through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA). Cumulative figures since the ceasefire announcement on 17 April remain unchanged, with 65 documented attacks on healthcare resulting in 39 deaths and 175 injuries. Overall, since 2 March, 211 incidents have been recorded, causing 135 deaths and 406 injuries. The continued pause in newly reported attacks on healthcare is an important positive indicator. However, it has not yet led to predictable access or a sustained improvement in service availability.
In parts of South and Nabatieh governorates, including border villages and return areas and some locations north of the Zahrani River, health service delivery remains constrained by movement restrictions, insecurity, transportation barriers, damaged infrastructure, variable facility functionality, and fragmented referral pathways. Constraints remain especially pronounced in parts of Nabatieh, where disrupted referral arrangements and uneven facility functionality continue to affect continuity of care for patients with chronic conditions, follow-up needs, and specialized care requirements. Prolonged insecurity and uncertainty also continue to contribute to increased MHPSS needs among affected communities, displaced and returning populations, and healthcare workers. According to the MoPH 28 PHCCs and 03 hospitals remained closed and recent partner updates indicate gradual resumption of services in some affected PHCCs following temporary closure, cleaning, or rehabilitation activities including the CMR service temporarily relocated to Saida will resume in the Tyre area starting 15 July. However, facility functionality remains uneven, with several PHCCs still partially damaged, under rehabilitation, or reliant on temporary arrangements, including PSU-based services and displaced health teams operating from alternative locations.
Health partners are continuing to deliver PHCC- and PSU-level services across multiple governorates. These include outreach, primary healthcare consultations, vaccination, chronic disease care, nutrition support, emergency referrals, and support to shelters and community gathering points. Partner reporting also indicates continued provision of acute and chronic medications, child vaccination, nutrition screening and counselling, IYCF support, nutritional supplies, assistive devices, and hygiene-related items. However, coverage remains uneven as damaged or variably functional facilities, access limitations, and rehabilitation or temporary service arrangements continue to affect availability of care in some locations. Hospitals in Saida and Beirut also continue to experience pressure linked to referrals from the South. Partners are working to expand emergency room and hospital support in selected facilities, including in Bekaa, the South, Beirut and Mount Lebanon, and Tripoli.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-07-13) ---
The Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) Q1 Dashboard presents a snapshot of the cluster’s activities and achievements. Between 2 and 16 March 2026, the team conducted extensive gaps and needs analysis and deep assessments to evaluate the impact of the escalation in Lebanon. The findings from these assessments informed operational planning and triggered the activation of the Cluster on the 16 March. The Cluster’s Logistics response focuses on coordination, information management, and facilitating access to common services, including storage and transport through convoys to hard-to-reach areas. The Telecommunications response focuses on coordination with government counterparts and humanitarian partners, as well as the deployment of emergency telecommunications equipment and services across the country where it is needed the most.
