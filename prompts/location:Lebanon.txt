--- Report 2: IOM (2026-08-27) ---
CONTEXT
The humanitarian situation in Lebanon continues to be shaped by ongoing displacement, population movements and persistent insecurity in conflict-affected areas. As of 26 August, IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) identified 334,353 internally displaced persons (IDPs), including 19,543 individuals residing in 196 collective sites. A further 823,337 individuals were reported as residing in their areas of origin or habitual residence after previous displacement. During the reporting period, renewed hostilities highlighted the fragility of the security environment despite the ceasefire announced on 19 June. On 15 August, intensified airstrikes in Nabatieh Governorate reportedly prompted significant temporary population movements, with available reporting indicating that many of those who moved returned within a short period. Further strikes were reported on 26 and 27 August, underscoring the continued volatility of the security environment, although their implications for population movements were not yet fully reflected in the latest available displacement figures. Attacks affecting healthcare personnel and civilian infrastructure were also reported, further disrupting access to essential services and increasing protection concerns in conflict-affected areas. While large-scale displacement has continued to decline, many conflict-affected households continue to face significant challenges in rebuilding their lives and accessing essential services. Extensive damage to housing, water systems, electricity networks, health facilities, schools and agricultural land continues to constrain recovery efforts, particularly in South Lebanon. Many families remain unable to return to their homes and continue to rely on temporary accommodation arrangements while awaiting rehabilitation works and the restoration of basic infrastructure and services. Against this backdrop, humanitarian and recovery needs remain closely intertwined. Continued humanitarian assistance, alongside investments in shelter rehabilitation, restoration of basic services, livelihoods recovery, protection, and mental health and psychosocial support, will be critical to support safe, voluntary and sustainable solutions for displacement-affected populations and help conflictaffected communities recover from the impacts of continued hostilities.

--- Report 3: OCHA (2026-08-27) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

World Humanitarian Day was marked by the Humanitarian Coordinator visiting Tebnine (18 August) and a Town Hall with humanitarian partners (19 August). Discussions highlighted the continued challenges facing frontline responders and essential service providers operating in conflict-affected areas.
Continued military activity across southern Lebanon is undermining civilian safety and return prospects. Airstrikes, artillery shelling, demolitions, and land-clearing operations continued across South and Nabatieh governorates. Military activity also damaged civilian infrastructure, including a public water reservoir in Mansouri (Tyre District), while a suspected explosive remnant of war in Mayfadoun (Nabatieh District) reportedly killed three people and injured another.
Humanitarian partners reported increasing access constraints affecting assessments, monitoring activities, service restoration efforts, and referral pathways in hard-to-reach areas, limiting the delivery of assistance and support to conflict-affected populations.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
The humanitarian situation in Lebanon remains volatile, with continued military activity, prolonged displacement, and challenging return conditions sustaining significant humanitarian and protection needs. Israeli military activity continued across South and Nabatieh governorates during the reporting period, including airstrikes, artillery shelling, demolitions, and land-clearing operations affecting communities in Nabatieh, Marjaayoun, Bint Jbeil, Jezzine and Tyre districts. The most intense incidents were reported around the Ali al-Taher heights (Nabatieh District, Nabatieh Governorate), where strikes and shelling persisted throughout the reporting period. Additional demolitions were reported in Aita al-Shaab (Bint Jbeil District), Houla and Khiam (Marjaayoun District), and Majdal Zoun (Tyre District), while artillery fire also affected the outskirts of Houla (Marjaayoun District) and Rashaya al-Fakhar (Hasbaya District). Continued insecurity, destruction of civilian infrastructure, and movement restrictions continue to expose civilians to protection risks, hinder humanitarian access, and constrain safe and sustainable returns.
Military activity continues to affect civilian infrastructure and access to essential services. In Mansouri (Tyre District, South Governorate), land-clearing operations and shelling reportedly damaged or destroyed a public water reservoir serving the community, raising concerns over access to water. In Ain Arab (Nabatieh Governorate), Israeli forces reportedly installed an iron gate restricting access to part of the village, further limiting access and return prospects. Extensive damage to housing, roads, water networks, electricity systems, and other public infrastructure continues to be reported across conflict-affected areas of southern Lebanon.
The security situation remains particularly volatile in Nabatieh Governorate despite continued return movements. The escalation around the Ali al-Taher heights that began in mid-August continued throughout the reporting period, with additional strikes reported around Nabatieh, Kfar Rumman and Nabatieh al-Fawqa (Nabatieh District), where houses were reportedly destroyed. While no new displacement orders were reported, ongoing insecurity continues to impede returns and contribute to concerns over renewed displacement among returning families.
Explosive ordnance contamination remains a significant protection concern. On 19 August, an explosion involving a suspected explosive remnant of war in Mayfadoun (Nabatieh District) reportedly killed three people and injured another. The incident underscores the continuing threat posed by unexploded ordnance (UXO) and explosive remnants of war (ERW) to civilians living in or returning to conflict-affected communities. Contamination continues to affect residential areas, agricultural land, access routes, and public infrastructure, restricting movement and delaying recovery efforts.
Population movements continue, though displacement and return trends are showing signs of stabilization. According to the latest Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), 348,898 people remained displaced across Lebanon as of 19 August, a decrease of less than one per cent compared with the previous reporting period. At the same time, 817,458 people had returned to their communities, although the pace of returns continues to slow. The majority of displaced people originate from conflict-affected districts in South and Nabatieh governorates, particularly Bent Jbeil, El Nabatieh, Sour, and Marjaayoun.
Collective shelter populations continue to decline. As of 24 August, 21,000 people remained accommodated in 209 collective shelters, a decrease of three per cent compared with the previous reporting period. However, most displaced people continue to reside outside collective shelters, with 72 per cent living in rented accommodation and 12 per cent in host community arrangements. Protracted displacement, rising living costs, and declining economic opportunities continue to place significant strain on affected households and host communities.
Return movements remain concentrated in southern Lebanon but continue to face significant challenges. Sour and El Nabatieh districts account for the largest numbers of returnees, while returns remain constrained by insecurity, damage to housing and infrastructure, limited access to essential services, and UXO contamination. Humanitarian actors continue to emphasize that returns should not be equated with a reduction in humanitarian needs.
Humanitarian and protection needs remain acute among displaced populations, returnees, and host communities. Key concerns include explosive ordnance contamination, barriers to accessing services, economic hardship, housing insecurity, documentation challenges, psychosocial distress, and heightened protection risks. Women, girls, children, older persons, and persons with disabilities remain particularly vulnerable. Declining household resources, rising living costs, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to erode coping capacities and deepen humanitarian needs.
Recent consultations with local authorities and communities in conflict-affected areas highlighted that restoring basic services remains a key prerequisite for sustainable return. Priority needs identified include reliable water and electricity supply, rehabilitation of schools ahead of the academic year, livelihood support, agricultural recovery assistance, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and strengthened municipal capacity to restore local services. Communities also emphasized the need for longer-term recovery investments, including support to small businesses, agriculture, solar energy systems, water infrastructure, and employment opportunities.
Humanitarian access remains complex and unpredictable, particularly in areas of South and Nabatieh governorates affected by ongoing military activity. Insecurity, access restrictions, damaged roads, telecommunications disruptions, UXO contamination, and evolving security conditions continue to affect humanitarian operations, monitoring activities, and service delivery.
Access to essential services remains under pressure. In addition to conflict-related damage, humanitarian partners have raised concerns regarding potential disruptions to water and wastewater services following electricity supply cuts by Électricité du Liban (EDL) affecting entities with unpaid bills. The South Lebanon Water Establishment remains particularly vulnerable due to reduced operational capacity and financial constraints. Any interruption in electricity supply could affect water pumping, treatment, and wastewater facilities in South and Nabatieh governorates, with implications for public health and continuity of water and sanitation services.
Funding constraints continue to hamper the response at a critical stage. According to Financial Tracking Service, as of 24 August, the 2026 Lebanon Flash Appeal requiring US$639.9 million have only received US$332.4 million (52 per cent) one week before the end of its six-month timeline. Humanitarian actors warn that declining resources may place essential activities, including food assistance, cash-for-rent support, protection services, operational coordination, and nutrition interventions, at risk in the coming months. Humanitarian partners continue to emphasize the importance of sustained de-escalation and cessation of hostilities, respect for international humanitarian law, protection of civilians and civilian infrastructure, safe and unhindered humanitarian access, support for safe, voluntary and dignified returns, UXO clearance, restoration of essential services, and predictable funding to sustain humanitarian and early recovery efforts.
