Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: UNFPA (2026-08-31) ---
Persistent humanitarian and protection challenges continue to affect communities across Lebanon. Displacement and returns remain volatile, particularly following the 5 August escalations and the subsequent evacuation warning issued for al-Mansouri.
As of 6 August 2026, a total of 25,503 people—53 per cent of them women and girls—remain displaced across 242 collective shelters. Displaced people report receiving limited information or inadequate notice about shelter closures and available alternative accommodation. This increases the risk of involuntary or secondary displacement, disrupts access to essential services, and contributes to further psychological distress.
Protection concerns remain acute among people experiencing prolonged displacement and those returning to conflict-affected areas. Women and girls, adolescents, older people and persons with disabilities face heightened risks associated with overcrowding, damaged housing, limited privacy, gender-based violence, financial hardship, and barriers to essential services.
UNFPA estimates that approximately 390,000 women of reproductive age are among the displaced and returnee population, including around 16,000 pregnant women, with an estimated 1,800 expected to give birth each month. Many pregnant women are returning to areas where health infrastructure has sustained significant damage. Three hospitals and 27 primary health centres remain closed, the continued need for life-saving sexual and reproductive health supplies and services, particularly maternal healthcare and emergency obstetric care.
Since the onset of the crisis in the beginning of March, UNFPA has reached 43,445 displaced people with sexual and reproductive health services and 47,126 people with gender-based violence prevention and response activities. This support has been delivered through 55 health facilities, nine mobile medical units, 24 mobile GBV teams and 19 women and girls’ safe spaces, serving people in collective shelters and host communities.
However, access constraints and funding shortfalls continue to limit UNFPA’s ability to reach people in need. UNFPA has been appealing for US$25 million to address the urgent needs of 450,000 people. To date, only 16 per cent of the required funding has been secured, leaving a significant gap in the resources needed to sustain critical humanitarian and protection services.

--- Report 2: Islamic Relief (2026-08-31) ---
Conflict and protection: Attacks have continued in Lebanon since the ceasefire in March 2026, causing 16,585 casualties, further displacement, delayed returns, and ongoing violations of international law (see page 2).
Humanitarian funding: Funding shortfalls continue to undermine humanitarian efforts, with only 52% of the 2026 UN Lebanon Flash Appeal funded.
Our response: Despite access constraints and funding cuts, Islamic Relief Lebanon has expanded its emergency response and adapted programmes to reach those most affected as displacement patterns evolved (see page 6).
Advocacy: Islamic Relief advocates for a permanent ceasefire, protection of civilians and civilian infrastructure, safe and dignified returns, accountability for violations, respect for international humanitarian law, and increased humanitarian assistance (see page 5).

--- Report 5: Anera (2026-08-31) ---
WAR HAS ACCELERTED DECLINING PROSPECTS
This report captures a critical inflection point for Palestinian refugees in Lebanon, where long-standing structural vulnerabilities have converged with the impact of the 2026 war. The conflict has not created these inequalities, but it has sharply exposed and intensified them, revealing how fragile existing systems had already become.
Even before the latest escalation, years of economic collapse and declining international funding had pushed many families into survival mode, as access to healthcare, education and livelihoods steadily eroded, while humanitarian institutions were forced to scale back services due to sustained funding shortfalls.
Anera conducted the research, data collection and roundtable consultations that inform this report just months before the escalation. While conditions have since deteriorated further, the findings remain highly relevant in documenting the shrinking support systems shaping how communities are experiencing the conflict’s impact. For Palestinian communities, largely excluded from national safety nets, there are no viable alternatives when aid contracts.
In December 2025, Anera brought together 15 community-based and international organizations to examine how funding cuts were reshaping daily life in Palestinian communities. Participants described a humanitarian landscape in which needs were rising as resources declined, with Palestinian refugees among the hardest hit. They warned that reduced support was accelerating deterioration across nearly every aspect of life faster than existing systems could respond. The subsequent escalation of war has only intensified these pressures, adding new emergency demands and underscoring how little margin for shock remains.
Addressing this crisis requires more than data. It requires close attention to frontline perspectives in order to understand both immediate needs and longer-term consequences. Drawing on organizational perspectives and direct testimony, this report shows that essential services have been reduced to a minimum, prevention and development programming has largely collapsed, and women and children are bearing a disproportionate share of the burden. Ultimately, what emerges is a reality that risks being overlooked: a long-standing lack of legal rights combined with the steady erosion of services and future prospects that the war has accelerated but did not begin. By bringing together the views and experiences of refugees and the organizations that serve them, this report offers a clearer understanding of the crisis and a call for renewed coordination and investment in Palestinians in Lebanon.
