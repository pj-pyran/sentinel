Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-07-06) ---
Highlights

Despite ceasefire announcements, airstrikes, displacement orders, and civilian casualties continued in May 2026, with more than 3,400 people killed and over 10,200 injured. Children accounted for 9 per cent of total casualties.
Emergency rapid needs assessments reveal that multilayered and cross-sectoral humanitarian needs are increasing, and severity deepening.
In May, UNICEF and partners continued to adapt programming to maintain critical services. UNICEF-supported water interventions reached over 167,000 people and wastewater interventions benefited 110,000 people; primary healthcare satellite units reached 19,391 women and children; the Call and Learn hotline provided academic support to 18,386 children; and community-based MHPSS sessions reached 6,931 children and caregivers.
UNICEF's 2026 Humanitarian Action for Children appeal requires US$388.5 million and is 49 per cent funded. Significant gaps persist in social protection, the Palestinian Programme in Lebanon, and child protection.

--- Report 2: WHO (2026-07-06) ---
Situation overview
Impact of ongoing airstrikes
• Since 18 June 2026, the security situation in Lebanon has remained fragile despite a decline in the overall intensity of hostilities following the latest ceasefire announcement.
• Daily airstrikes, shelling, demolitions, military drone activity, and other localized military operations have continued to be reported, particularly in southern Lebanon, posing risks to civilians, limiting safe returns, and highlighting the potential for renewed escalation despite ongoing diplomatic efforts.
• As of 1 July 2026, a total of 25,783 hostilities had been recorded since 2 March 2026, according to the DRM.
Casualties and injury overview
• MoPH reported a cumulative number of casualties since 2nd March has reached 16,494 including 4,298 deaths (including 253 children and 391 women) and 12,196 injuries as of 6:00pm on July 2, 2026., Fig.1 Causality Distribution by day since March 2, 2026.
• Out of the total injuries, 6,287 (51%) were treated at Emergency rooms as outpatient, 4,953 (42%) admitted to regular wards and 956 (7%) admitted to ICU.
• For total casualties since March 2,2026, the majority of fatalities and injuries occurred in Nabatiyeh (7,038 casualties), followed by the South (6,922casualties), and Mount Lebanon (1,202 casualties), Additional cases were reported across other governorates. Fig 2.
• Of the total casualties, 14,223 (86%) were male and 2,271 (14%) females.
• 8% of the total casualties are children, 253 children are dead, and 1,036 children are injured. Fig 3. Shows age distribution of casualties.
• Nationality data shows that 15,571 casualties (94%) were Lebanese, 533 casualties (3%) were Syrian, 233 casualties (2%) were Palestinian, and 157 casualties (1%) from other nationalities, Fig 4.
• Between 17 April and 2 July 2026 (ceasefire period), MoPH reported a cumulative number of casualties of 6,071, including 1,792deaths and 4,279 injured. Fig.5. Casualty Distribution by Day since April 17, 2026.
• Out of a total of 6,071 post-ceasefire casualties, 5,320 were male (88%) and 751 were female (12%), while children accounted for 6% of the total.
• The majority of cases were reported from South (3,036) and Nabatiyeh (2,758). The toll also included 1,792 deaths. Among the injured, 1,805 were admitted to regular wards, 2,074 were treated in emergency rooms, and 400 required admissions to intensive care units.
