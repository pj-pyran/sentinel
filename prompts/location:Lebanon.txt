Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-08-12) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

At least 4,335 deaths and 12,277 injuries recorded due to hostilities since 2 March, with 355 people killed and 276 injured since the 19 June ceasefire announcement, according to the Ministry of Public Health.
352,793 people remain displaced within Lebanon as of 6 August, while 834,651 people have reported returning to their areas of origin. As of 10 August, 22,800 Internally Displaced Persons (&gt;53% women and girls) remain in 232 collective shelters.
On 5 August, a new displacement order was issued for Mansouri in Tyre District (South Governorate), the first displacement order since 11 July. At least 130 families were newly displaced from Mansouri to Tyre following the displacement order.
5 August demolition of a well and 7 August fires at Chaqra and Wadi Saluki water stations in Nabatieh Governorate, underscoring the continued destruction of infrastructure that limits civilian access to basic services.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
The humanitarian situation in Lebanon remains critical, as ongoing military activities, unstable returns, and prolonged displacement continue to drive humanitarian needs and exacerbate protection concerns. Displacement remains significant across the country, with access to adequate shelter, essential services, and livelihood opportunities posing persistent challenges for displaced populations. Returnees, particularly in conflict-affected areas of South and Nabatieh governorates, continue to face insecurity, recurring military activity, and widespread destruction of infrastructure and basic services. These conditions are hindering safe returns while amplifying protection risks and humanitarian needs among affected communities.
An increase in military activities was reported during the period covered across South and Nabatieh Governorates, with the first new displacement order in nearly a month issued on 5 August, for Mansouri, a village in Tyre District (South Governorate). Prior to the displacement order, approximately 130 families were present in Mansouri – all families were displaced to Tyre after the order, with 50 families now hosted in existing collective shelters, and the remaining 80 hosted by family members in Tyre city. Humanitarian partners supported the Tyre Union of Municipalities in providing emergency assistance, including non-food item kits, to the newly displaced families. Both ahead of and following the displacement order, artillery shelling on Mansouri village occurred daily from 3 August through 10 August, with airstrikes occurring on 5-7 and 9-10 August.
A total of 133 conflict incidents involving shelling, airstrikes, detonations and demolitions were reported between 4 and 10 August, compared with 97 incidents reported during 28 July-3 August and 130 incidents during 21-27 July. This increase was driven primarily by a surge in reports of artillery shelling (some 83 incidents), of which half were reported as targeting Mansouri as well as Ali el Taher and Haddatha (both Nabatieh Governorate). Airstrikes, shelling, demolitions and other security incidents were also reported in locations across Bint Jbeil, Marjaayoun, Hasbaya and Nabatieh districts in Nabatieh Governorate, as well as Tyre district in South Governorate.
The 5 August displacement order for Mansouri was only the second such order issued since the latest ceasefire announcement on 19 June. The first, on 11 July, involved leaflets dropped over the same municipality declaring it a &quot;threatened area&quot; and urging civilians to evacuate. Continued insecurity, destruction of civilian infrastructure, and recurring displacement are further exacerbating humanitarian and protection concerns by prolonging and triggering displacement, hindering safe returns, and increasing risks to civilian safety.
Ongoing destruction of critical infrastructure, agricultural land, and residential buildings continues to reduce access to services and exacerbate protection concerns. New incidents affecting water infrastructure were reported, including a fire at the Chaqra water station in Bint Jbeil District on 7 August and demolition activities affecting a water well at Wadi Saluki in Marjaayoun District on 5 August, followed by fires affecting the Wadi Saluki water station on 7 August. The incidents affecting the two water facilities further impacted access to water for the villages of Bint Jbeil and Chaqra in Bint Jbeil district and Mais al-Jabal in Marjaayoun district (Nabatieh Governorate). With water access already severely constrained by widespread damage to water infrastructure, continued attacks on civilian assets are further disrupting access to safe water for affected communities. In addition, reported incendiary strikes caused extensive fires in forested and mountainous areas of western Bekaa Governorate, resulting in further damage to agricultural land and environmental resources.
Conflict-related damage continues to pose threats to civilians. On 5 August, a building previously damaged by an airstrike collapsed in Haret Hreik, Baabda district (Mount Lebanon). While no casualties were reported, the incident underscores the ongoing dangers associated with conflict-damaged structures and highlights the need for timely debris removal, structural assessments, and rehabilitation of damaged infrastructure to reduce risks to affected populations.
The Ministry of Public Health reported 4,335 people killed (including 392 women and 253 children) and 12,250 injured (1,452 women and 1,037 children) as of 10 August. Since the latest ceasefire announcement on 19 June, 355 people have been killed and 276 injured. Since 2 March, 135 health care professionals have been killed while on duty and 410 injured, according to the World Health Organization (WHO).
Displacement levels remain significant nationwide. As of 6 August, 352,793 people remain displaced across Lebanon, according to the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), representing a 2 per cent decrease compared with 30 July. 61 per cent of the displaced are from three districts in Nabatieh Governorate – Nabatieh, Bint Jbeil, and Marjaayoun, and 20 per cent are from Tyre, South Governorate. While overall displacement continued to decline across most districts, increases in the number of displaced people were recorded in several districts, including Tyre (increase of 1,551), Nabatieh (increase of 1,217) and Bint Jbeil (increase of 60). Beirut (Beirut Governorate), Saida (South), Chouf (Mount Lebanon) and Tyre (South) host the largest caseload of IDPs, with 106,800 in Beirut and 63,800 in Saida. For displaced households outside collective shelters, where there is continued strain on local households and municipal systems, access to health care and medication, rental support, restoration of services, access to protection services, and emergency livelihoods interventions remain critical to support households' basic needs and safety.
Collective shelters continue to host displaced people from communities facing barriers to return. 22,800 remain hosted across 232 collective shelters in Lebanon as of 10 August (DRM). Most remaining shelters are in Mount Lebanon Governorate (96) and Beirut Governorate (50), with 7,600 and 7,000 people respectively hosted in each. In South Governorate, 42 shelters remain, hosting 6,100 people. IDPs in collective shelters continue to face challenges in accessing basic services. Limited fuel availability is affecting the continuity of electricity and water services, heightening health, hygiene and safety risks. Displaced households continue to identify cash assistance as a critical need to help cover essential living costs.
Persistent barriers to return contribute to slower return rates and ongoing humanitarian and protection needs. 834,651 people had begun the return to their areas of origin as of 6 August, representing a 2 per cent increase from the previous week (30 July). Notably, an additional 12,240 people returned to Nabatieh district (Nabatieh Governorate), 1,210 to Tyre district (South) and 929 to Bint Jbeil (Nabatieh). Returnees continue to face significant humanitarian needs and protection concerns, particularly for those returning to areas of South and Nabatieh governorates where continued military activities continue to pose risks to civilians, trigger new displacement, and impact service access. Returnees have expressed key priorities for housing, restoration of basic services, cash assistance, and livelihoods support to enable safe and sustainable returns. Protection needs also remain high as access to healthcare, mental health and psychosocial support, GBV and child protection services, Housing Land and Property (HLP) assistance, and mine clearance remain critical gaps.
Funding shortfalls continue to pose barriers to sustained humanitarian assistance as the end of the six-month Flash Appeal approaches. The 2026 Lebanon Flash Appeal has received less than half of the $639.9 million required as of 3 August, or $307.6 million (48.1 per cent).
