--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-11-20) ---
Find the RAIS assistance summary for January – October 2026, broken down by month, covering all organisations that reported into the system during this period. This is shared so each organisation can check what has been recorded against its name — month by month — and flag any corrections.
Missed reporting for a previous month? You can still back-report it in RAIS. Back-reported records will be included, so the figures will be correct in the next RAIS Assistance Report dashboard.
How to read this summary: each monthly column shows two figures. For in-kind and service assistance (CRI items, shelter kits and repairs, home visits, counselling), the top number is quantity distributed and the smaller number below is cases (no value shown). For cash-based assistance (Emergency Financial Assistance, EPCA, Protection Cash Assistance, MCAP, PCAP, ATM-credited cash, cash for food/education/shelter, NDA), the top number is cases and the smaller number below is value disbursed (USD). A dash (–) means no assistance of that type was reported by that organisation in that month.
A note on what &quot;cases&quot; means: the case figures in this summary are not unique beneficiaries. A &quot;case&quot; is a household or family that received assistance during that specific month. The same household may be counted more than once within this period — for example, if it received the same type of assistance in multiple months, or received different types of assistance (cash and/or in-kind) from the same organisation or from different organisations, during the same month or across different months. As a result, summing cases across months or across assistance types will overstate the number of distinct households reached.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-11-20) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• As needs continue to outpace initial planning assumptions, WFP requires immediate, flexible, and front-loaded funding at scale to sustain and expand assistance in a highly fluid operational environment.
• Since the beginning of the escalation on 2 March, WFP has reached around 1.1 million unique conflict‑affected people with food and cash assistance in shelters and across communities.
• In October, the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MEHE), with WFP and UNICEF, launched Lebanon’s first National Nutrition and Food Environment Report, with findings establishing a national baseline and providing evidence to inform policies and programmes.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Lebanon's political and security environment remains fragile, amid unresolved conflict with Israel. Persistent Israeli military operations across southern Lebanon have caused civilian casualties, infrastructure damage, and disruption to livelihoods and essential services. Since the renewed escalation on 2 March 2026, Israeli military operations have resulted in 4,389 deaths and more than 12,424 injuries as of end-October 2026.
• Despite increasing returns, Lebanon’s humanitarian situation remains critical. By end of October, 965,000 people had returned to their area of origin of whom 27 percent are still locally displaced within their governorate. Meanwhile, over 304,000 remained displaced and 11,200 stayed in shelters.
• The September 2026 Integrated Food Security Phase classification (IPC) analysis shows that 1.05 million people (21 percent of the analysed population) are experiencing acute food insecurity, with conditions projected to deteriorate further in 2027. Lebanese residents account for the largest share of the affected population, representing 65 percent (686,000 people) of those acute food insecure. Conflict-affected areas, in southern Lebanon, continue to face significant food security pressures. The situation is primarily driven by persistent economic hardship, high living costs, limited employment, and localized insecurity.
• The conflict has severely disrupted education, with 86 teachers killed or injured and 436 education facilities affected as of end of October 2026. Additionally, 101 schools were damaged or destroyed, while 57 public schools remain occupied as collective shelters, limiting access to education.
