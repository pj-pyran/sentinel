--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-11-25) ---
Context
Lebanon continued to face significant humanitarian, recovery and security-related challenges amid a complex political and security environment. Discussions on post-United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) arrangements and the future security architecture in southern Lebanon, continued diplomatic engagement to support Lebanon's stability, and heightened regional tensions remained prominent throughout October. Efforts to advance recovery and reconstruction, which would facilitate dignified and sustainable IDP returns, continued alongside calls for sustained humanitarian funding as needs remain high among many of those still displaced or who have returned to conflict-affected districts. The Lebanon Response Plan 2026 Addendum provides a framework for coordinated support to address immediate humanitarian needs while strengthening resilience, restoring essential services and laying the foundations for longer-term recovery in vulnerable communities across the country.
Security conditions remained volatile, particularly in southern Lebanon. Continued airstrikes, shelling and demolition operations affected civilian infrastructure, agricultural land and access to services. Extensive damage to housing and public infrastructure, coupled with concerns over renewed escalation and displacement, continued to affect return prospects and recovery efforts in conflict-affected areas, while explosive remnants of war and movement restrictions remained key concerns.
Lebanon remains among the countries most affected by forced displacement globally, hosting one of the largest refugee populations per capita. As of end-October 2026, 851,171 refugees and asylum-seekers had active files with UNHCR, including 838,700 Syrians (809,409 long-staying Syrian refugees and 29,291 recorded post-December 2024 arrivals) and 12,471 refugees and asylum-seekers of other nationalities. The estimate of Syrian arrivals since December 2024 stood at 132,996. At the same time, 304,520 people remained internally displaced, while 964,689 had begun returning to their district of origin. October protection monitoring in southern Lebanon, Beirut and Mount Lebanon found that some families moved back and forth between their home communities and areas of displacement. Damaged homes, insecurity, unexploded ordnance and limited services continued to make it difficult for families to remain in their areas of origin. Across Lebanon, IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Mobility Snapshot of 29 October 2026 finds 80% of people who remained displaced renting accommodation, with high rents and eviction risks flagged as key concerns.
