Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-08-20) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

At least 4,346 deaths and 12,303 injuries recorded due to hostilities since 2 March, with 366 people killed and 302 injured since the 19 June ceasefire announcement, according to the Ministry of Public Health.
351,558 people remain displaced within Lebanon as of 13 August, while 863,172 people have reported returning to their areas of origin. As of 17 August, 22,400 Internally Displaced Persons (&gt;53% women and girls) remain in 221 collective shelters.
Attacks affecting healthcare personnel on 11 and 12 August, as well as destruction of civilian infrastructure, continued during the reporting period, undermining access to essential services and heightening protection risks for conflict-affected communities.
Some 111,000 people temporarily displaced following intensification of military activity in Deir al-Zahrani and Ansar (Nabatieh Governorate) on 15 August; the majority of whom returned within 48 hours.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
Humanitarian needs remain acute amid ongoing military activities, protracted displacement, and extensive destruction and basic service disruptions. While the overall number of displaced persons decreased slightly during the reporting period, significant barriers to return remain. Moreover, renewed displacement was reported from Deir al-Zahrani and Ansar (Nabatieh Governorate) following the intensification of military activities and strikes on the two localities on 15 August. Health and water services continued to face disruptions, including reports of attacks affecting medical teams in Nabatieh city and Zawtar El Charqiyeh (Nabatieh Governorate), and ongoing impacts on water infrastructure and service provision in conflict-affected areas. Populations that remain displaced, have returned, or are hosting displaced families continue to require sustained support to address protection risks, meet basic needs, and restore critical services damaged or disrupted by the conflict.
Intensified military activity was reported in parts of the South and Nabatieh governorates during the reporting period, further constraining return movements and exacerbating humanitarian concerns in affected areas. On 15 August, airstrikes in Deir al-Zahrani and Ansar (Nabatieh Governorate) resulted in 11 fatalities and 19 injuries, including four children and one woman killed and one child and 11 women injured, according to the Ministry of Public Health (MoPH). The strikes triggered temporary displacement from the affected areas, with local authorities reporting that approximately 102,000 people (70 per cent of families) in the Cheqif Union of Municipalities, where Ansar and Deir al-Zahrani are located, were displaced. Approximately 8,800 people (30 percent of families) were also displaced from localities within the Iqlim al-Tuffah Union of Municipalities (areas surrounding the targeted localities). Most families returned within 24 to 48 hours following the intensification of airstrikes, although approximately 85 people remain displaced in collective shelters in Houmin, Nabatieh district (Nabatieh Governorate), and Saida City (South Governorate). In Saida, the Disaster Risk Reduction provided mattresses and blankets to displaced families, from prepositioned DRR supplies available to respond to new displacements.
A total of 168 conflict incidents involving shelling, airstrikes, detonations and demolitions were reported between 11 and 17 August, indicating a continued escalation of military activity in South and Nabatieh governorates. This represents an increase from 133 incidents reported between 4 and 10 August and 97 incidents between 28 July and 3 August. While the frequency of artillery shelling remained broadly consistent with the previous reporting period, the number of airstrike incidents tripled, increasing from 12 to 36. The intensification of military activity continues to drive humanitarian needs and protection risks, contributing to further destruction of homes and civilian infrastructure, disruptions to essential services, barriers to return, heightened exposure to explosive ordnance, and prolonged displacement. Airstrikes, shelling, demolitions and other security incidents were reported across Bint Jbeil, Marjaayoun, Hasbaya and Nabatieh districts in Nabatieh Governorate, as well as in Tyre district in South Governorate.
The Ministry of Public Health reported 4,346 people killed (including 393 women and 257 children) and 12,303 injured (1,465 women and 1,039 children) as of 17 August. Since the latest ceasefire announcement on 19 June, 366 people have been killed and 302 injured.
Attacks affecting on-duty healthcare workers and education infrastructure continued during the reporting period, highlighting ongoing protection concerns and challenges to the delivery of essential services. On 11 August, an ambulance responding to casualties from a drone strike in Nabatieh city (Nabatieh Governorate) was targeted, damaging the vehicle and exposing medical teams to additional risk, according to WHO. On 12 August, local authorities reported the demolition of a health clinic in Zawtar El Charqiyeh (Nabatieh Governorate) as well as a public school, which had a pre-conflict enrolment of 130 children, and a children’s centre serving more than 260 children, according to UNICEF. Since 2 March, WHO has recorded 135 health-care workers killed while on duty and 410 injured in Lebanon.
Displacement levels remained high across Lebanon, with only marginal reductions reported during the reporting period. As of 13 August, 351,558 people remained displaced nationwide, according to DTM report, representing a decrease of just 0.35 per cent compared with the previous week (6 August). The largest reductions were recorded in districts hosting the highest numbers of internally displaced persons (IDPs), including Beirut district (Beirut Governorate), where the caseload declined by 3,718 people, Saida district (South Governorate), which recorded a decrease of 4,935 people, and Chouf district (Mount Lebanon Governorate), where the caseload fell by 3,035 people. At the same time, increases in displacement were reported in Tyre district (South) by 9,917 people and Nabatieh district (Nabatieh Governorate) by 4,143 people, reflecting continued population movements linked to insecurity and barriers to sustainable return. For displaced households living outside collective shelters, ongoing pressures on host communities and municipal systems continue to drive needs for health care, medication, rental assistance, protection services, restoration of essential services, and emergency livelihood support to meet essential needs.
Displaced families continue to reside in collective shelters as damaged housing, disruptions to essential services, access constraints, and diminished coping capacities continue to hinder returns. As of 17 August, 22,400 people were accommodated across 221 collective shelters nationwide, according to the Disaster Risk Management Unit. Shelter populations remained largely stable during the reporting period, with the total number of occupants decreasing by approximately 400 people (1.75 per cent) compared with the previous week. Ensuring access to basic services, including reliable water and electricity supply, remains a key challenge, while overcrowding and limited privacy continue to affect living conditions in some sites. These constraints underscore the continued need for sustained support to collective shelters and the populations who remain displaced within them.
Returns continue, though ongoing insecurity and destruction of homes, infrastructure and services remain barriers. 863,172 people have begun returning to their areas of origin as of 17 August, a 3 per cent increase from 6 August. The number of returned IDPs increased most significantly in Nabatieh district (Nabtieh Governorate), with an additional 24,484 returnees bringing the total to 252,430. This was followed by Marjaayoun district (Nabatieh), which recorded 1,800 additional returnees for a total of 11,718, and Bint Jbeil,district (Nabatieh) where 1,762 additional returnees brought the total to 39,384. Despite continued return movements, conditions in many conflict-affected areas remain challenging. Damage to housing, infrastructure and essential services continues to affect returnees and residents, while ongoing security incidents are contributing to protection concerns. Electricity and water networks remain disrupted in several locations, and health services continue to face significant operational constraints. Local authorities also report continued restrictions on access to agricultural land, limiting livelihood opportunities, while constraints on repair and rehabilitation activities are slowing the emergency restoration of essential services. Sustained support remains critical to address immediate needs and facilitate safe, dignified and sustainable returns.
As the end of the six-month Flash Appeal approaches, funding constraints continue to undermine humanitarian efforts. The 2026 Lebanon Flash Appeal has only received $316.8 million (49.5 per cent) of the 639.9 million required as of 17 August. These funding shortfalls are threatening service continuity, restoration of critical services, and provision of timely humanitarian assistance for conflict-affected communities.

--- Report 2: UNICEF (2026-08-19) ---
Highlights

Children and caregivers continue to bear the brunt of conflict and instability. Despite successive ceasefires, 4,278 people have been killed and 12,196 injured since 2 March, including 253 children killed and 1,036 injured.﻿
The June ceasefire contributed to reduced hostilities and accelerated returns, although airstrikes continued in southern Lebanon. Approximately 704,445 people remained displaced in June, with returns shaped by insecurity and damage to critical infrastructure. 340 schools have been damaged or destroyed since March, putting the September return to education of at least 100,000 children at risk.
By mid-year 2026, UNICEF supported 1.2 million children and women with access to primary healthcare, 1.75 million people with access to safe water, 288,090 people with rapid response supplies, and 97,163 children 6-59 months with screening for wasting.
UNICEF's 2026 HAC appeal requires US$ 388.5 million and is 53 per cent funded, leaving a gap of US$ 182.2 million.
