Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-09-09) ---
In 2025, UNHCR supported 20 partner-run Community Development Centers (CDCs) and 6 Government-run Social Development Centers (SDCs) across Lebanon. Through these 26 centers, women, men, boys, and girls of diverse ages and backgrounds accessed information, psychosocial, legal, learning, and other protection interventions, whether in person, online or through outreach. Over the past year, centers played a key role in empowering communities, building capacities, and supporting persons with specific needs. This report analyzes data and measures the impact of center activities from January to December 2025.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-09-08) ---
Since January 2025, 681,612 Syrians known to UNHCR have returned to Syria and consequently been inactivated from UNHCR’s records. This includes refugees confirmed to have returned¹ and those inactivated due to prolonged absence and loss of contact² . Among the 681,612 Syrian returnees, 87% (591,396) returned spontaneously to Syria; this includes 11% (65,500) who returned under duress in late 2024 and remained in Syria; 19% (112,372) identified by the Lebanese General Security Office (GSO) while crossing through an Official Crossing Point (OCP) without a Repatriation Form; 6% (35,903) identified as returning due to force majeure after 2 March 2026; and 64% (377,621) who were inactivated due to prolonged absence or loss of contact, or whose return was confirmed by UNHCR through interviews with family members. The spontaneous departure figures reflected in the chart indicate the date of inactivation from UNHCR’s database, rather than the date of departure, which explains the high number of inactiva-tions in December 2025 following the completion of the presence verification exercise.
