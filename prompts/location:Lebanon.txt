Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: Govt. Lebanon (2026-06-03) ---
The National Mental Health Programme (NMHP) at the Ministry of Public Health continues to lead and coordinate the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) Coordination Group, co-chaired by WHO and UNICEF, with 100+ partner actors integrating MHPSS across sectors in Lebanon's emergency response. This snapshot provides key updates on the national MHPSS response following the escalation of the war. It highlights selected indicators on service delivery and response efforts across multiple platforms.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-06-02) ---
CONTEXT
The operational environment in Lebanon remains highly volatile, with continued hostilities undermining already fragile ceasefire conditions. Despite the extension of the ceasefire until 1 July 2026, airstrikes and displacement orders persist, affecting at least 61 localities across southern Lebanon, particularly in Nabatieh and Tyre districts, as well as areas south of the Zahrani River.
Displacement dynamics remain fluid and unpredictable, characterized by recurrent and precautionary movements. Secondary displacement continues to be reported as families relocate in response to shifting security risks rather than pursuing sustainable return options. Recent displacement waves have also extended beyond southern Lebanon, including movements from Beirut’s southern suburbs following displacement orders. These trends are increasing pressure on host communities in Beirut, Mount Lebanon, Aley, Chouf, and Saida, while collective sites, hosting over 130,000 individuals, are facing severe overcrowding and reduced capacity to absorb additional influxes.
The humanitarian situation is further aggravated by continued attacks on civilian infrastructure. Health facilities and personnel remain directly affected, with incidents resulting in casualties among healthcare workers. Between 25 and 29 May alone, three such incidents were reported, resulting in one fatality and three injuries. Cumulatively, attacks on healthcare infrastructure continue to rise, while access to essential health services, particularly in areas south of the Litani River, remains critically constrained. Overall casualty figures are also increasing, with over 3,300 people killed and more than 10,000 injured since early March 2026, reflecting the sustained intensity of hostilities and their cumulative humanitarian impact.
Humanitarian operations face growing constraints due to insecurity, access limitations, and supply chain disruptions, particularly in the South and Nabatieh governorates. These challenges are affecting the continuity and scale of assistance delivery and increasing the risk of pipeline breaks across critical sectors, including health, shelter, and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH).
Protection risks are intensifying, particularly within overcrowded collective sites. Reports indicate heightened risks of genderbased violence (GBV), alongside broader vulnerabilities linked to prolonged displacement, deteriorating living conditions, and loss of livelihoods. Women and girls represent a significant proportion of the displaced population, heightening exposure to protection risks. Overall, coping capacities are being progressively eroded, driving increased reliance on humanitarian assistance. In this context, maintaining operational continuity, ensuring sustained humanitarian access, and supporting the delivery of critical services remain essential to prevent further deterioration of humanitarian conditions.

--- Report 3: DRC (2026-06-02) ---
Key Findings
Sharp increase in referral activity between March and April: Between 02 March and 30 April 2026, RIMS recorded a total of 1,255 referrals, with referrals increasing from 440 cases in March to 815 cases in April. April alone accounted for 65% of all recorded referrals, reflecting a substantial escalation in humanitarian needs alongside improved outreach, stronger coordination mechanisms, and increased utilization of referral pathways.
Humanitarian needs became increasingly concentrated in protection and survival-related sectors: While March referrals were relatively distributed across multiple sectors, April referrals became heavily concentrated in Nutrition (27%), Child Protection (19%), Protection (16%), GBV (15%), and Cash Assistance (15%). Together, these five sectors accounted for more than 90% of all referrals in April, indicating a shift toward increasingly interconnected survival, protection, and coping-related vulnerabilities.
Nutrition emerged as the dominant and fastest-growing sector: Nutrition referrals increased from 38 cases in March to 217 cases in April, representing a 471% increase and making Nutrition the largest referral sector in April. Findings indicate growing concerns related to maternal and infant nutrition, stress-related breastfeeding challenges, unsafe formula feeding practices, and deteriorating living conditions among displaced populations.
Protection risks intensified across multiple dimensions: Child Protection, General Protection, and GBV referrals all increased significantly between March and April. Partners reported rising child labour, early marriage, psychosocial distress, intimate partner violence, eviction threats, legal documentation challenges, and increased vulnerabilities among persons with disabilities and elderly individuals. These trends reflect the cumulative effects of displacement, financial strain, overcrowding, and prolonged instability.
Cash assistance needs increased sharply, reflecting worsening economic vulnerability: Cash Assistance referrals increased from 18 cases in March to 124 cases in April, representing a 589% increase. Households increasingly required financial support to cover rent, medications, transportation, diapers, and other essential daily expenses, reflecting growing economic pressure and reduced household coping capacity.
Health and education referrals declined proportionally despite continued underlying needs: Health referrals decreased from 20% of referrals in March to 4% in April, while Education referrals declined from 12% to 2%. This likely reflects a reprioritization toward immediate survival and protection needs, alongside possible barriers in access, outreach, and service availability, rather than a reduction in underlying sectoral needs.

--- Report 4: Health Cluster (2026-06-01) ---
Situation Update
Despite the adoption of the resolution on the protection of health care in Lebanon and the extension of the ceasefire, attacks on health care continue. Since 25 May, eleven incidents have been recorded, resulting in four deaths and twenty four injuries among health-care workers. Since the ceasefire announcement on 17 April, a total of 44 attacks have been documented, causing 30 deaths and 101 injuries. Since 2 March, 190 attacks on health care have been recorded through the Surveillance of Attacks on Healthcare (SSA) system, resulting in 128 deaths and 332 injuries.
These persistent attacks represent a serious failure to protect health care and underscore the urgent need to safeguard medical personnel, facilities, and uninterrupted access to life-saving services. Beyond immediate casualties and damage to infrastructure, such attacks severely under-mine health system functionality, restrict access to care, suppress service utilization, and contribute to deteriorating health outcomes, particularly among vulnerable populations.
Furthermore, these violations are driving widespread psychological distress and long-term mental health impacts among both affected communities and health-care workers, while simultaneously weakening the system’s capacity to respond to growing and increasingly complex health needs.
Findings from Health Sector coordination team field visits highlight compounding and interlinked vulnerabilities, including high health needs, rising household economic pressures, and inadequate food assistance, all of which are undermining health outcomes. Partners also reported growing resistance to generic medicines, further affecting treatment adherence and continuity of care.
These findings are consistent with both the Health Sector Partner Capacity Assessment (April) and the Rapid Health Assessment (May 2026), which identified gaps in essential medicine availability. These shortages are disrupting care, particularly for patients with chronic conditions and specialized needs. Collectively, these challenges underscore the urgent need to strengthen community engagement, improve risk communication, and implement a more integrated response addressing both health needs and underlying socio-economic drivers.
