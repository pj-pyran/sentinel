Crisis / situation: Lebanon

--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-07-23) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

At least 4,328 deaths and 12,230 injuries recorded due to hostilities since 2 March, with 348 fatalities and 226 injuries since the latest ceasefire announcement, according to the Ministry of Public Health.
741,111 people reported returning to their areas of origin, while 412,700 people remain displaced (as of 15 July), including 29,700 Internally Displaced Persons (over 53 per cent women and girls) in 278 collective shelters as of 20 July.
As some communities enter their fifth month of displacement, declining resources are placing critical interventions, including food assistance, cash-for-rent, protection services, and operational coordination, at risk of disruption from September 2026 without additional funding.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
Humanitarian and protection needs persist in Lebanon, as people experiencing prolonged displacement and those returning to conflict-affected areas continue to require urgent support. While the latest ceasefire announcement led to significant returns of over 700,000 people, ongoing security incidents in southern Lebanon continue to impede safe and sustainable returns, exacerbate protection risks, and disrupt service delivery and infrastructure rehabilitation. Reports of airstrikes, artillery shelling, demolitions and sonic grenades continued daily across South and Nabatieh governorates, including in Bint Jbeil, Marjayoun and Nabatieh districts in Nabatieh Governorate, and Tyre District in South Governorate.
As of 15 July, 412,700 people remain displaced across Lebanon, according to the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). Although this represents a decrease of approximately 550,000 people since the ceasefire announcement, return movements have slowed in recent weeks, indicating that displacement levels are stabilizing as obstacles to return persist. The trend is also reflected in collective shelter occupancy, with 29,700 internally displaced persons (IDPs) residing in 278 collective shelters nationwide. While shelter populations have continued to decline gradually since early July, needs remain acute. Communities in collective shelters report deteriorating conditions in some shelters, sustained pressure on services, increasing reliance on debt to meet basic needs, and limited access to livelihood opportunities.
Returns continue, though at a slower pace, as challenges to return persist. As of 15 July, 741,111 people have begun returning to their areas of origin (DTM), including approximately 136,000 people who have returned to Nabatieh Governorate and 104,000 to South Governorate. Since early July, the largest increases in returnee populations were recorded in Saida District, South Governorate (4,875), Tyre District, South Governorate (3,835), and Bint Jbeil District, Nabatieh Governorate (1,841). Humanitarian assistance and protection services remain critical in areas of return, where widespread damage to housing, roads, water and electricity networks, and agricultural land continues to drive needs. Given ongoing insecurity and uneven access to services, returns remain fluid and may be reversible.
Operational pressures are increasing as the response enters its fifth month following the escalation of hostilities on 2 March. Communities in collective shelters report a reduction in the range and frequency of assistance compared with the 2024 response, reflecting the challenges of sustaining support at scale amid protracted displacement and deepening humanitarian needs. Food Security, Nutrition, Shelter and Protection partners anticipate critical funding shortfalls in the next six weeks without additional resources. Anticipated gaps include daily meal provision, cash-for-rent assistance, protection services, nutrition partner capacity, national and regional convoy services.
Humanitarian and protection needs remain acute among displaced populations, returnees, and host communities. Key concerns include explosive ordnance contamination, housing insecurity, economic hardship, civil documentation challenges, psychosocial distress and heightened protection risks, particularly for women, girls, children, older persons, persons with disabilities, and households experiencing prolonged displacement. Declining coping capacities, rising living costs and limited livelihood opportunities continue to deepen vulnerabilities of conflict-affected people.
Access to essential services remains constrained across many conflict-affected areas. Damage to water, electricity, health and public infrastructure continues to disrupt service delivery and increase reliance on temporary solutions. Water and electricity remain among the most frequently reported service gaps in return areas. Several hospitals and primary healthcare centres, particularly in hard-to-reach areas of South and Nabatieh governorates, remain closed or only partially operational due to infrastructure damage, staffing shortages and access constraints.
The Ministry of Public Health reported 4,328 people killed (including 392 women and 253 children) and 12,230 injured (1,450 women and 1,036 children) as of 13 July. 135 health care professionals have been killed on duty and 406 reported as injured since 2 March. Since the 20 June ceasefire announcement, 348 people have been killed and 226 injured.
Funding constraints remain a major challenge to sustaining humanitarian assistance. As of 20 July, the 2026 Lebanon Flash Appeal has received $284.8 million, or 44.5 per cent of the $639.9 million requested.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-07-22) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS

In June 2026, WFP assisted 663,000 people across all activities, including 194,000 conflict-affected people reached through emergency food and cash assistance.
Through its SCOPE Card Pilot initiative, WFP reached 336 newly displaced Syrians outside formal shelters in Dhour El Hawa, using pre-issued SCOPE cards to streamline beneficiary verification and access to food assistance.
In close collaboration with the Ministry of Social Affairs, WFP continues to support the development of Lebanon’s Integrated Beneficiary Registry (IBR), advancing efforts toward a unified and more efficient national social protection system.

--- Report 3: UNRWA (2026-07-22) ---
All information is valid for 17 June - 14 July 2026
Key Points

Following US-facilitated negotiations in Washington, Lebanon and Israel moved towards a conditional ceasefire on 3 June, contributing to a de-escalation of the conflict.
Amid renewed escalation between the United States and Iran on 25 June and a further intensification of tensions on 13 July following incidents affecting commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, the situation continues to be closely monitored for potential spillover effects on the operating environment in Lebanon.
UNRWA launched its emergency response in Lebanon on 4 March 2026. The Agency has been operating two collective emergency shelters: Siblin Training Centre in Saida Area and Battir School in Nahr El Bared Camp in northern Lebanon.
By 14 July, all displaced persons accommodated in the two UNRWA emergency shelters had departed. Following this, UNRWA formally closed both facilities as emergency shelters. Battir School was closed on 11 July 2026, followed by Siblin Training Centre, which was closed on 13 July 2026.
Between 17 June and 14 July, no security incidents affecting on-duty UNRWA personnel or facilities were reported.

--- Report 4: SARI Global (2026-07-21) ---
Political Context: IDF Escalation Amid Withdrawal Pressure
The 14 to 20 July 2026 period recorded the highest IDF operational tempo of the post-MoU environment: 92 incidents, 57 percent of all activity, a sharp increase from the prior week’s 71. The escalation was concentrated in Nabatieh and included airstrikes, a guided missile strike that killed two on the Ali al-Taher heights on 16 July, and a systematic campaign of burning and demolishing residential properties across southern border communities.
This intensification comes precisely as US pressure on Israel to redeploy from both Lebanon and Syria has become public, with President Trump reported to have urged the Israeli prime minister to withdraw. Israel has still not redeployed from the two Lebanese pilot zones it committed to vacate under the framework agreement, and the US-brokered talks addressing that stalemate produced no announced movement. Against this backdrop, an operational surge and a housing-destruction campaign indicate that Israel is consolidating its southern Lebanon posture rather than preparing to reduce it.
