Crisis / situation: Venezuela: Earthquakes - Jun 2026

--- Report 1: UNFPA (2026-08-12) ---
Venezuela continues to face significant humanitarian needs following the earthquakes of 24 June 2026. The most urgent needs among affected populations include healthcare, including sexual and reproductive health (SRH); food and safe water; mental health and psychosocial support; shelter; sanitation and hygiene; and specialized protection services, particularly for gender-based violence (GBV).
The loss of livelihoods, disruptions to health and protection services, and precarious living conditions—particularly for people residing in temporary shelters—are increasing the risk of GBV and harmful coping strategies. Currently, 24,477 people are residing in 107 transitional shelters, where overcrowding, limited privacy in sleeping and bathroom facilities, and inadequate lighting are heightening protection risks, particularly for women, girls, and adolescents.
Numerous health facilities in affected areas have sustained structural damage, while others are facing shortages of medicines, equipment, and health personnel, placing additional strain on an already overstretched health system. UNFPA is supporting the continuity of life-saving maternal, newborn, and obstetric care through its support to maternity wards in 16 hospitals across La Guaira, Caracas, and Miranda. UNFPA is also providing on-the-job training for health personnel and delivering critical medical equipment and supplies, including 62 Inter-Agency Reproductive Health Kits, which will support the needs of approximately 2,250 people.
UNFPA and its implementing partners are also delivering SRH and GBV services across four transitional shelters. To date, 4,327 people have received prenatal care, family planning services, psychosocial support, GBV case management, and dignity kits. In addition, 4,186 people have been reached through SRH and GBV awareness-raising and information sessions.
UNFPA is appealing for US$10 million to support the emergency response to the earthquakes in Venezuela. To date, US$4 million has been mobilized, leaving a 60 per cent funding gap. Additional support is urgently needed to sustain the delivery of life-saving SRH and GBV services to affected populations.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-08-11) ---
Situation Report No. 21 provides an update on IOM's response to the 24 June earthquakes in the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, covering the period from 7 to 10 August 2026.
Highlights

On 10 August, IOM launched the distribution of non-food items in Mare II and Algarín following household registration and verification activities in Mare Abajo II and surrounding communities.
IOM and its partner Ven Da Tu Mano maintained mobile health and mental health services in temporary accommodation sites. Cumulative services reached 3,267 consultations at the José María Vargas Sports Complex and 2,864 consultations in César Nieves, while surveillance of diarrhoeal diseases was reinforced.
From 17 July to 7 August, NFI distributions supported by IOM and Fundación Vivienda Popular reached a cumulative total of 2,244 kits in La Guaira, Miranda, Capital District and Carabobo. The Playa Grande temporary accommodation site closed on 10 August.

IOM activities
IOM maintained the management of five temporary accommodation sites during the reporting period, including three sites co-managed with the Norwegian Refugee Council. Activities included service coordination, population registration, household verification and needs monitoring. At the José María Vargas Sports Complex, essential services were maintained for 751 people from 202 households, while population movements in Campo de Golf were monitored.
Household verification in Mare Abajo II supported the launch of NFI distributions in Mare II and Algarín on 10 August. IOM and Fundación Vivienda Popular also supported the provision of NFIs in sites and communities managed by the authorities. On 7 August, 70 kits were distributed at the Colegio Olivia Andrade temporary accommodation site in La Guaira.
IOM and Ven Da Tu Mano continued providing mobile health and mental health services, including care for chronic conditions and mental health needs associated with the emergency. In César Nieves, health teams responded to an incident affecting approximately 20 adults and 12 children, providing medical care, monitoring hydration status and strengthening surveillance of diarrhoeal diseases.
IOM expanded household verification in Mare Abajo II and surrounding communities and continued updating population records in César Nieves and José María Vargas. Needs assessments in community settlements and formal accommodation sites also continued in La Guaira, Miranda and Carabobo.
Protection activities included coordination of access to case management, health, legal assistance and specialized services. At José María Vargas, 40 women participated in information sessions on violence against women and girls and safeguarding. IOM also facilitated inter-agency coordination to update service coverage and schedules, strengthen referral mechanisms and monitor population movements.
IOM planned response
IOM will:

Complete NFI distributions in prioritized communities surrounding Mare Abajo II.
Address identified infrastructure gaps in Mare Abajo II, particularly drainage for newly installed showers and adjustments to tent anchoring systems.
Continue health surveillance and medical follow-up for diarrhoeal diseases and support continuity of treatment for hypertension, diabetes and other chronic conditions.
Strengthen referral pathways for mental health, legal assistance and specialized protection services.
Complete household verification in prioritized communities and expand needs assessments and population movement monitoring in Carabobo and other affected locations.
Maintain updated information on available services, operating schedules and referral mechanisms in temporary accommodation sites.
Strengthen accessible community information and feedback mechanisms.

--- Report 3: WFP (2026-08-11) ---
HIGHLIGHTS
One month after the earthquakes in Venezuela, access to food remains one of the top concerns for families across the affected areas.
WFP has reached more than 120,000 people with food assistance through four modalities, since no two families are living through this period the same way. Assistance include hot meals in shelters, ready-to-eat rations for those still on the move, food baskets for households that can still cook and in communities who lost their sources of income, and prepaid electronic vouchers in areas where markets are functioning, giving families the power to prioritize their own needs.
The agency is also serving as the logistics backbone of the humanitarian response, moving relief supplies into hard-to-reach communities. WFP's logistics services have supported NGOs and UN agencies with the transport and storage of supplies spanning shelter, health, protection, and WASH.
To date, WFP has secured one third of the USD 80 million required to sustain and scale the response. Efforts continue to close the gap and safeguard the support for 500,000 people in 3 months.
Situation Update

A month after two powerful earthquakes struck north-central Venezuela, the World Food Programme (WFP) continues to provide food assistance to communities impacted by the devastation. This unprecedented response, tailored to different needs in each affected area, has already reached more than 120,000 people and, with proper funding, hopes to reach a total of half a million over the next two months.
Following needs assessments, WFP has expanded the scope of its assistance from assistance in temporary shelters, community kitchens and communities in the most populated cities, to remote communities in La Guaira, where residents have retained cooking capacities, but whose livelihoods have been severely affected.
WFP's response extends well beyond La Guaira. The operation plan spans seven states, considering both families directly displaced or affected in their homes, and those whose livelihoods were disrupted by the earthquake — people whose sources of income collapsed overnight.
