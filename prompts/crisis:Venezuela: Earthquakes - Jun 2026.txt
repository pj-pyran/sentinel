Crisis / situation: Venezuela: Earthquakes - Jun 2026

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-08-05) ---
Highlights

UNICEF continued to scale up its humanitarian response across earthquake-affected areas, supporting the continuity of essential health, nutrition, WASH, child protection, and education services for children and families in temporary camps and affected communities.
To date, UNICEF has reached 25,483 people with primary health care, 22,424 children, adults, and pregnant women with routine and catch-up vaccinations, 9,412 people with critical WASH supplies, 5,804 children and caregivers with nutrition services, and 6,134 children, adolescents, and caregivers with child protection services, including MHPSS.
UNICEF is supporting the Post-Disaster Needs Assessment (PDNA), leading the WASH and education sectors. The PDNA will inform recovery priorities and help accelerate the restoration of essential services.
In addition to its US$137.6 million 2026 HAC appeal, UNICEF requires US$65.7 million to sustain critical interventions and support the implementation of the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan Addendum.

--- Report 2: ChildFund International (2026-08-05) ---
Context
Five weeks after the June 24 earthquakes in Venezuela, the death toll and affected population continue increasing. OCHA has formalized the national response into a six-month Humanitarian Response Plan Addendum (July–Dec. 2026).

Toll: 5,546 dead, 16,740 injured, 6,462 rescued alive. Rescuers describe no longer searching for survivors, and international search and rescue missions are scaling back. There are critical information gaps related to missing persons.
Children: CECODAP estimates that over 1,400 children and adolescents remain at significant risk of harm due to the earthquake.
Displacement: As of July 26, 107 transitional camps for affected populations are housing 24,477 people (87% capacity). Most displaced people remain concentrated in Caracas and La Guaira.
Education: According to civil society organizations, over 900 schools have been affected, including 18 school facilities that have totally collapsed and 40 that have severe structural damage. 120 teachers have been reported dead.
Economic effects and reconstruction costs: June inflation hit 13.8%, more than double May's rate. National reconstruction cost estimates now range from USD 12–15 billion. Government and private sector banking institutions are exploring credit programs for housing repair and replacement.
Humanitarian funding request: OCHA's Humanitarian Response Plan Addendum requests USD 298 million to reach 1.3 million people over six months (JulyDecember 2026). This is in addition to the USD 930 million humanitarian funding required for 6.2 million people in Venezuela prior to the earthquake.
Priority needs as per the OCHA Rapid Needs Assessment: health (53%), safe water (52%), food security (50%), psychosocial support (27%), sanitation/hygiene (22%), protection/safety/dignity (18%), shelter (17%).

--- Report 3: IOM (2026-08-04) ---
The nineteenth Situation Report provides an update on IOM’s response to the 24 June earthquakes in the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, covering the period from 31 July to 3 August 2026.
Highlights
Between 31 July and 3 August, IOM continued its multisectoral response in temporary accommodation sites and affected communities through interventions in shelter and camp coordination and management, health, protection, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), information management, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).
During the reporting period, IOM distributed 245 plastic tarpaulins, 234 solar lamps, 234 collapsible water containers, 235 kitchen sets, 227 hygiene kits and 74 mosquito nets to address priority shelter and household needs of earthquake-affected families.
To date, IOM-supported teams have delivered 7,615 medical consultations to 6,733 earthquake-affected people through health, mental health and psychological support services in temporary accommodation sites and affected communities.
IOM initiated the characterization of earthquake-affected community settlements, reaching 20 settlements in La Guaira, one in Miranda and one in Carabobo, expanding needs and displacement monitoring beyond temporary accommodation sites.
IOM Activities
IOM continued co-managing five temporary accommodation sites in La Guaira: Campo de Golf, Mare Abajo II, Jose Maria Vargas Sports Complex, Playa Grande and Cesar Nieves, supporting service delivery, population registration, community information services and operational coordination.
At Campo de Golf, the allocation of new family tents, expansion of solar lighting and improvement of community and service spaces continued. At Mare Abajo II, community outreach activities began to promote available services, identify needs and strengthen communication channels with site residents, while household registration and verification activities continued. At Jose Maria Vargas Sports Complex, essential services were maintained for 796 people from 221 households, including registration, community orientation, connectivity and restoring family links.
During the reporting period, IOM distributed 245 plastic tarpaulins, 234 solar lamps, 234 collapsible water containers, 235 kitchen sets, 227 hygiene kits and 74 mosquito nets to earthquake-affected families. To date, IOM has delivered 4,987 kitchen sets, 4,580 plastic tarpaulins, 3,441 solar lamps, 3,313 collapsible water containers, 2,527 hygiene kits, 2,384 buckets, 1,066 mosquito nets and 822 emergency shelter kits.
In WASH, IOM continued expanding access to safe water and sanitation services through the activation of a potable water system at Campo de Golf, water quality monitoring, chlorination activities, sanitation infrastructure improvements and waste management support. To date, IOM has provided 3,313 collapsible 10-liter water containers, 285 semi-collapsible 10-liter water containers and 2,527 hygiene kits.
IOM and its implementing partner Ven Da Tu Mano continued delivering health, mental health and psychological support services through mobile teams. During the reporting period, 90 consultations were conducted in Cesar Nieves, while mobile health services at Jose Maria Vargas Sports Complex reached a cumulative total of 2,509 consultations. To date, IOM-supported interventions have provided 7,615 medical consultations to 6,733 earthquake-affected people.
IOM continued registration and information management activities through the unified Information Management System (IMS) across the five temporary accommodation sites. During the reporting period, IOM initiated data collection in earthquake-affected community settlements, characterizing 20 settlements in La Guaira, one in Miranda and one in Carabobo, while Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) teams conducted additional assessments in Carabobo.
In protection, IOM continued providing legal assistance, case management, child protection activities, trafficking in persons prevention, violence against women and girls prevention and community information services. Community outreach activities were launched in Mare Abajo II, and IOM supported the coordination of a documentation campaign that enabled 182 people to access national identification services.
As co-lead of the Infrastructure and Temporary Accommodation Working Group together with UNHCR, IOM continued supporting operational coordination, service mapping, information management and joint planning across temporary accommodation sites.
In PSEA, IOM conducted refresher sessions with 24 humanitarian workers, distributed 1,000 informational leaflets and key messages to NFI teams, and carried out awareness activities with 15 community leaders and 17 humanitarian workers in Playa Grande.
IOM Planned Response
IOM will continue strengthening infrastructure, service delivery, WASH interventions, shelter support and Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) activities in temporary accommodation sites.
IOM will also continue delivering health, mental health, psychological support and protection services while strengthening referral pathways for specialized assistance.
In addition, IOM will maintain mobility monitoring, registration activities, information management, community settlement assessments and coordination with authorities and implementing organizations to support evidence-based response planning.

--- Report 4: ETC (2026-08-04) ---
This Situation Report provides an overview of the twin earthquakes that struck Venezuela on 24 June 2026 (magnitudes 7.2 and 7.5), and their impact on telecommunications. It outlines the activities of the WFP-led Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC)1 Telecoms Area in supporting emergency communications for the humanitarian response. The Cluster is currently not formally activated for this emergency.
Summary Points
• Since 05 July, an inter-agency LTC Telecoms Coordinator has been deployed in Caracas to strengthen in-country coordination and lead efforts to identify connectivity gaps and operational requirements for targeted humanitarian response support. A new inter-agency LTC Telecoms Coordinator will assume the role on 03 August and continue supporting the response.
• The inter-agency LTC Telecoms Coordinator is working with UN OCHA, Comisión Nacional de Telecomunicaciones (CONATEL), Compañía Anónima Nacional Teléfonos de Venezuela (CANTV), Movistar, Digitel, G-Network, emergency.lu, Grupo Telefónica, and other partners to deploy connectivity solutions in the most affected areas.
• Through the Internet for Communities solution, GNetwork’s fibre internet connection will remain free for all users at Polideportivo, César Nieves, and a third site until the end of the year or until the emergency ends. This helps address immediate concerns related to service contracts and recurring connectivity costs.
