Crisis / situation: Venezuela: Earthquakes - Jun 2026

--- Report 1: HOPE (2026-07-23) ---
Project HOPE continues to expand its humanitarian response across northern Venezuela. The team is significantly increasing access to healthcare for displaced and affected communities by operating eight mobile medical units (MMU), which are providing primary and maternal healthcare services in shelters and underserved communities. Project HOPE also continues to provide additional clinical support at health facilities in Distrito Capital and Miranda, with expansion underway to facilities in La Guaira. To reduce the risk of secondary public health emergencies, Project HOPE is also connecting families to mental health support, providing essential medicines, distributed additional hygiene kits through complementary funding, and recently launched a water trucking initiative.
Situation Overview
Humanitarian needs remain high as communities transition from the immediate aftermath of the earthquakes into a prolonged recovery phase. While debris removal and damage assessments continue, thousands of displaced people remain in temporary shelters with persistent needs for healthcare, safe water, sanitation, and protection services. Overcrowded living conditions, intermittent rainfall, and disruptions to essential services continue to increase the risk of infectious disease transmission. At the same time, health facilities face the pressure of a continued increase in demand for services, including for acute injuries and routine health needs that were interrupted by the disaster. As operations evolve, humanitarian partners are increasingly focused on restoring access to primary healthcare and strengthening local health services to support communities through recovery.

--- Report 2: ChildFund International (2026-07-23) ---
1. Context
Three weeks after the 24 June earthquakes, the death toll and affected population continue increasing. OCHA has formalized the national response into a six-month Humanitarian Response Plan Addendum (July–Dec. 2026).
Toll: 5,278 dead, 16,740 injured, 6,462 rescued alive. Of the confirmed dead, 7% remain unidentified, mainly due to missing fingerprint records and identity system gaps.
Displacement: As of July 21, 107 transitional camps for affected populations have a total capacity of 27,822 people and current occupancy of 23,587 people. As of July 20, 41 camps are in Caracas, 28 in La Guaira, 28 in Miranda, and 10 in Aragua. Of the total camp population, 12,474 people are in La Guaira, 8,554 in Caracas, 1,877 in Miranda, and 682 in Aragua. These numbers are a snapshot, as the population in shelters changes constantly.
Debris: An estimated 1 million tons of debris has resulted from the earthquake. Of this, only 6,700 tons has been cleared in La Guaira so far, representing just 1% of the total.
Humanitarian funding request: OCHA's Humanitarian Response Plan Addendum requests USD 298 million to reach 1.3 million people over six months (JulyDecember 2026). This is in addition to the USD 930 million humanitarian funding appeal required for 6.2 million people in Venezuela prior to the earthquake Limited humanitarian operational presence: OCHA's own access assessment finds that Aragua, Carabobo, and Yaracuy have a relatively limited humanitarian operational presence. These states are accessible; the gap is a lack of resources. Priority needs as per the OCHA Rapid Needs Assessment: health (53%), safe water (52%), food security (50%), psychosocial support (27%), sanitation/hygiene (22%), protection/safety/dignity (18%), shelter (17%).
Economic constraints: June inflation hit 13.8%, more than double May's rate. National reconstruction cost estimates now range from USD 12–15 billion (internal field sourcing).

--- Report 3: IFRC (2026-07-23) ---
A. SITUATION ANALYSIS
Description of the crisis
On 24 June 2026, two powerful earthquakes struck north-central Venezuela in rapid succession, with preliminary magnitudes of 7.2 and 7.5. Authorities have reported more than 1,000 aftershocks since the event, creating a high risk of further structural collapse. The epicentres were located near Morón and Yumare, in the states of Carabobo and Yaracuy, west of Caracas. The impact was severe and widespread, with tremors felt across much of the country, including the Capital District, La Guaira, Aragua, Carabobo, Miranda, Falcón, and neighbouring states.
The Government of Venezuela declared a National State of Emergency the same day and designated La Guaira, where the greatest concentration of damaged infrastructure and rescue operations was concentrated, as a disaster zone. According to the Global Disaster Awareness and Coordination System (GDACS), an estimated 9.73 million people have been affected, including approximately 530,000 exposed to extreme earthquake intensity.
The event occurred during the San Juan festivities, a national celebration that attracts large numbers of people to coastal areas; and as many workplaces were closed, this also led to families having many or all members present inside their home when the earthquakes happened. In La Guaira, a popular destination to spend this festivity, this resulted in higher population concentrations in public spaces and along the coastline at the time of the event, increasing the number of people potentially exposed to the impacts of the earthquakes.
As of 15 July, 4,829 people were reported dead, 16,740 injured and approximately 17,907 had lost their homes due to severe damage to housing1. It is expected for these figures to continue to rise in coming days as the Search and Rescue phase focused on extraction of survivors moves towards a focus on recovery, identification and return to family members of bodies retrieved from collapsed structures. Response teams from over 29 countries deployed to Venezuela, including the United Nations Disaster Assessment and Coordination (UNDAC) team and Urban Search and Rescue (USAR) teams operating under the coordination of the International Search and Rescue Advisory Group (INSARAG)2. International search and rescue deployments are progressively concluding, with several Movement teams having completed their missions. A response focused on search, recovery, debris-removal, shelter provision and efforts to bridge emergency relief to recovery and reconstruction efforts continues under national leadership, with a broad range of bilateral and multilateral support focused on humanitarian assistance, restoration of essential services and recovery.
According to OCHA, 2,501 infrastructure assets had sustained some degree of damage, with 856 affected buildings, including 190 that have collapsed.3 A comprehensive technical evaluation of structures is underway by respective authorities to identify homes that can be repaired and those that are too dangerous to return to. Evaluation of safe areas to build permanent housing for those affected is also taking place. Damage to residences, commercial buildings and critical infrastructure also disrupted the functioning of electricity, water-supply, telecommunications and transport systems, as well as public transportation and airport operations. Damaged roads and bridges have also restricted access and affected movement to and within some affected areas. The healthcare capacity has been particularly affected, with limited supply of water systems. More than 400 schools were also reported as affected, while some have been repurposed as temporary shelters until the academic cycle resumes after the summer. Damage to water systems has further affected the availability of basic services.
Moreover, it is important to note that the main affected area, La Guaira, fulfils multiple critical functions for the country. Its proximity to the Capital, Caracas, the presence of the country’s main international airport (Maiquetía) and one of its major ports, makes it a strategic transportation, logistics, trade, tourism, fishing and service hub. Consequently, the incident impacted not only local communities and visitors present, but also essential infrastructure and activities that facilitate connectivity and the supply of goods to the capital and other regions of the country. This increases the number of people and logistical capacity affected.
The physical impacts on affected people remain significant, with thousands of injured, many requiring continued medical care, rehabilitation or referral services, and with families displaced after the destruction or severe damage of their homes. Beyond these physical impacts, the earthquakes have also had significant effects on the mental health and psychosocial well-being of affected people. Many families have experienced loss and grief, injury, displacement, separation from relatives and loss of homes, livelihoods and community support networks. The magnitude of the tragedy on human terms, continued aftershocks, uncertainty regarding housing and recovery, and exposure to damaged environments and recovery operations has contributed to heightened levels of stress, anxiety and sadness among the affected populations.
Notably, La Guaira’s geographical location is prone to various types of emergencies. In December 1999, torrential rainfall triggered catastrophic landslides, flash floods and debris flows across the then State of Vargas—now La Guaira—causing widespread destruction and displacement. The exact death toll remains uncertain; however, the United States Geological Survey estimated that between 15,000 and 30,000 people were killed. The current earthquake has therefore affected communities with lived and intergenerational memories of one of Venezuela’s most severe disasters.

--- Report 4: OCHA (2026-07-23) ---
Situation Report No. 29
Earthquakes in Venezuela
22 July 2026
Time: 9:00 pm
Highlights

Authorities report 1,463 aftershocks since the 7.2 and 7.5 magnitude earthquakes that struck on 24 June.
Official toll: 5,398 people dead and 16,740 injured.
The United Nations system and its partner organizations continue scaling up the multisectoral response to the emergency. To address the needs of 1.3 million people affected by the earthquakes over the next six months, an additional appeal of US$299 million was launched

Situation overview
Venezuela is facing a national emergency following two consecutive high-magnitude earthquakes (7.2 and 7.5) that struck on 24 June (18:00 local time) in the north-central region, with the epicentre located along the San Felipe–Yumare–Montalbán axis (Yaracuy/Carabobo). Authorities report 1,463 aftershocks.
Twenty-four days since the earthquakes, authorities report a toll of 5,398 people dead and 16,740 injured since the onset of the emergency. Authorities also indicated that 17,907 people lost their homes.
The Government response remains focused on the management of transitional camps and assistance to affected populations. The United Nations and its partner organizations, in coordination with the authorities, continue implementing multisectoral response activities and scaling up assistance. On 10 July, United Nations Under-Secretary-General for Humanitarian Affairs and Emergency Relief Coordinator Tom Fletcher announced a US$299 million addendum to the Humanitarian Response Plan to address the multisectoral needs of 1.3 million people affected by the earthquakes over the next six months. The addendum complements the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan, increasing the target population from 5.5 million to 6.2 million people and raising total funding requirements from US$632 million to US$931 million.

--- Report 5: IOM (2026-07-22) ---
The fifteenth Situation Report provides an update on IOM’s response to the 24 June earthquakes in the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, covering the period from 20 to 21 July.
Highlights
Between 20 and 21 July, IOM continued its multisectoral response in transitory camps and affected communities through interventions in shelter and site coordination, health, protection, WASH and information management.
To date, IOM has reached 8,542 unique individuals through 15,460 instances of assistance and services, mainly in shelter and transitory camp coordination, protection, health and WASH.
IOM and its partner Fundación Vivienda Popular have distributed 3,055 kitchen sets, 2,590 tarpaulins, 2,039 buckets, 1,676 solar lamps and 819 shelter kits to affected households.
IOM activities
IOM continued managing and coordinating five transitory camps in La Guaira and advanced the operational transition of three of them to the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC).
At José María Vargas Sports Complex, IOM maintained essential services for 316 families and supported registration activities. Coordination and registration continued at César Nieves and Playa Grande, while preparations were finalized at Tanaguarena Golf Course for the installation of additional infrastructure.
In WASH, IOM continued expanding access to safe water storage and hygiene items. To date, 1,552 collapsible water containers, 1,009 hygiene kits and 92 semi-collapsible water containers have been distributed.
IOM and its partner Ven Da Tu Mano have provided 5,552 medical consultations, including general and specialized care, medication distribution and psychological support. Mobile health and MHPSS services remained operational at José María Vargas and César Nieves.
IOM continued household registration and population data updates across five camps and conducted a mobility intentions survey in José María Vargas, César Nieves and Playa Grande to identify needs, displacement patterns and intentions to return or relocate.
In protection, IOM continued providing guidance, legal assistance, documentation support and referrals to specialized services, with 130 people assisted and supported to date.
IOM planned response
IOM will continue the operational transition of three camps to NRC, assistance distributions and infrastructure improvements, while strengthening WASH, health, mental health and protection services.
It will also strengthen camp and mobility monitoring, multisectoral assessments inside and outside the sites, and coordination with authorities and partners.

--- Report 6: CESVI (2026-07-22) ---
OPERATIONAL CONTEXT
Nearly one month after the earthquakes, the humanitarian response in Venezuela is rapidly transitioning from the immediate emergency phase to a more structured, large-scale operation. Indeed, humanitarian needs remain immense: over 23,000 people live in transitional camps, thousands more remain displaced in host communities or informal settlements, and damaged housing and basic infrastructure continue to limit access to safe shelter, water, sanitation, healthcare and livelihoods.ii
As aftershocks continue – over 1,300 registered up until now – the humanitarian response continues to expand in scale and complexity. Humanitarian actors are now planning assistance for 1.3 million people affected by the earthquakes over the coming six months, yet significant humanitarian gaps remain, as many communities remain underserved. This requires humanitarian actors that can rapidly identify unmet needs, coordinate effectively with existing response structures and deliver assistance where it is needed most.
Against this backdrop, CESVI has paired rapid operational deployment with early engagement in humanitarian coordination mechanisms, ensuring that field evidence collected from the first days of the emergency directly informed operational planning, strengthened partnerships and supported the targeting of communities that remained outside the initial humanitarian response.

--- Report 8: UNICEF (2026-07-22) ---
Highlights

More than three weeks after the earthquakes of 24 June, official figures report 5,208 deaths, 16,740 people injured, and 17,907 people left without housing.1 Humanitarian needs remain significant, particularly among displaced families residing in transitional camps and communities with disrupted access to essential services.
UNICEF continues to scale up multi-sectoral assistance, focusing on health, nutrition, WASH and child protection services for children and families affected by the earthquakes, reaching over 7,519 people in the initial phase of the response. Health services through mobile health teams have reached around 2,108 people, while vaccination campaigns have reached 6,466 people. More than 2,452 children have been screened for acute malnutrition. WASH services are expanding at temporary shelters reaching 7,180 with critical hygiene supplies and 2,861 benefiting from access to safe drinking water. Psychosocial support and recreational activities have reached more than 2,726 children, adolescents and caregivers. Additionally, more than 4,400 people have been reached with hygiene promotion messages and critical information on available life-saving services.
UNICEF will reach 150,000 people with critical emergency supplies within the coming weeks, including 49,000 with health supplies, 72,000 with micronutrients, and 29,000 with WASH items. An additional 49 tons of emergency supplies arrived on 14 July to replenish in-country emergency stockpiles.
In addition to the US$137.6 million requested under the 2026 Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC) appeal and aligned with the addendum to the Humanitarian Response Plan 2026, UNICEF requires US$65.7 million to respond to the humanitarian impact of the earthquakes.
