--- Report 1: OCHA (2026-07-04) ---
Situation Report No. 10
Earthquakes in Venezuela
03 July 2026
Time: 7:00 pm
Highlights

Following the 24 June earthquakes of magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 (18:00 local time), authorities report more than 800 aftershocks.
At least seven (7) states have been impacted. La Guaira remains the most affected state.
Official toll: 2,645 people dead and 12,666 injured.
A total of 6,462 people have been rescued.
Urban Search and Rescue (USAR) teams remain deployed in the affected areas, in coordination with OCHA, more than one week after the earthquakes.
The United Nations system and its partner organizations, in coordination with the Government, are responding with multisectoral actions and will continue to scale up assistance to affected people.

Situation overview
Venezuela is facing a national emergency following two consecutive high-magnitude earthquakes (7.2 and 7.5) that struck on 24 June (18:00 local time) in the north-central region, with the epicentre located along the San Felipe–Yumare–Montalbán axis (Yaracuy/Carabobo). Authorities report 890 aftershocks.
More than a week after the earthquakes, authorities report a toll of 2,645 people dead, 12,666 injured, and 6,462 rescued since the onset of the emergency. Authorities also reported that approximately 15,050 people lost their homes.
The Government response remains focused on search and rescue operations, the preparation and expansion of transitional camps and and the assistance to affected populations. La Guaira remains the most affected state. The United Nations and its partner organizations, in coordination with the authorities, are implementing multisectoral response activities and will continue to scale up assistance to affected people.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-07-03) ---
Disaster Overview
An earthquake with a magnitude of 7.2 in Morón, Venezuela followed by a 7.5 at a depth of 10.0 km (6.21 miles) at 23 km SE of Yumare, Venezuela were reported June 24, at 22:05 UTC with a 50 km distance and 30 seconds time difference.
The Government of Venezuela has declared a State of Emergency with the initial estimate of 9,730,000 people potentially affected with 530,000 of them exposed to severe to extreme earthquake intensity according to the Global Disaster Awareness and Coordination System (GDACS) estimations. Additionally, as of July 1st , 2,295 deaths and approximately 11,267 injuries have been reported, along with damage to critical infrastructure, with a figure of 1,645 affected buildings including 38 hospitals.
Government's response with support of the Venezuela Red Cross and several national and international response teams is focused on search-and-rescue operations, emergency medical care, and damage assessment. The impact has been severe and widespread, affecting critical infrastructure, public services, and homes in several states, with the greatest concentration in the Capital District, La Guaira, Miranda, Carabobo, and Yaracuy.

--- Report 3: HOPE (2026-07-03) ---
Key Updates

Project HOPE continues to expand emergency response activities as communities grapple with the immense toll of this disaster.
Mobile medical unit services are operational in La Guaira, in conjunction with our long-time partner, SAMU.
Project HOPE continues to support 14 partner health centers and supply health workers with IPC and WASH materials.
The team is improving access to potable water and hygiene through water trucking, distribution of water purification tablets, and the delivery of 372 family hygiene kits.
Project HOPE is being responsive to community needs by conducting additional assessments. Current data shows critical needs for specialized medical services, mental health support, NFI kits, and more.
