Crisis / situation: Bangladesh

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-07-27) ---
Key Messages
Inflation: General inflation eased slightly in June reaching 9.16 percent, down from 9.42 percent in May 2026, driven by lower price pressure across both food and non-food sectors. Food inflation declined to 8.6 percent, down from 9.06 percent in the previous month attributed to improved seasonal supply. Non-food inflation also eased slightly hitting 9.61 percent in June 2026 from 9.71 percent in May.1 This marginal decline unlikely to have provided meaningful relief to households amid recent hikes in fuel oil, electricity, and transport tariffs. On a year-on-year basis, both food and non-food inflation increased by 1.21 and 0.24 percentage points respectively. The overall inflation has remained at a persistently high level that continues to outpace wage growth for 53rd consecutive months 2 , disproportionately affecting people in lower-income groups. Both rural and urban food inflation showed slight downward trends in the reporting month, hitting 8.52 and 8.76 percent, respectively.
Bangladesh Economy: The country's gross foreign exchange reserves revived sharply in June hitting USD 37.58 billion, about 9 percent higher as compared to May 2026. The reserves stood at USD 32.93 billion as per IMF (BPM6) calculations.3 Officials referred the reserves as satisfactory, having a year-on-year growth of about 26 percent, backed by higher remittance inflows, lower import-payment obligations and the Central Bank's dollar purchases from commercial banks in this period. This stable growth in reserves would cover around five to six months of import bills,4 which may help keep the country's foreign exchange market stable. The remittance inflow stood at USD 2.8 billion in June 2026, a decline by about 18 percent from the previous month, largely due to temporary post-Eid seasonal slowdown. --- Report 2: WFP (2026-07-27) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• WFP strengthened Bangladesh’s climate resilience and disaster preparedness ahead of the 2026 monsoon season by updating an anticipatory action database for over 500,000 households and allocating USD 12 million to enable rapid activation.
• With WFP’s support, Bangladesh launched its first sovereign flood insurance scheme, covering over 100,000 vulnerable households.
• WFP continued providing critical assistance to 1.1 million Rohingya refugees while supporting nutrition, education and resilience activities for refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar and Bhasan Char.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Bangladesh continues to face significant food security, nutrition and climate-related challenges despite notable socioeconomic progress. Economic pressures, elevated food prices and global commodity market volatility continue to affect vulnerable households, while climate-related shocks, including floods and cyclones, threaten livelihoods, agricultural production and food security.
• In 2025, an estimated 16 million people were projected to experience crisis-level or worse food insecurity, while the country’s first IPC Acute Malnutrition analysis estimated that 1.6 million children under five were acutely malnourished, including 144,000 children severely malnourished, alongside nearly 117,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW). A new PC is expected to be released in July 2026.
• Bangladesh remains climate-related hazards, with monsoon-related risks and the potential El Niño conditions in late 2026 posing additional risks to vulnerable communities. At the same time, the country hosts approximately 1.2 million Rohingya refugees, with limited prospects for large-scale returns.
