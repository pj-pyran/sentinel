Crisis / situation: Bangladesh

--- Report 1: HRCBM (2026-11-26) ---
Between 1 January and 31 October 2026, the Human Rights Congress for Bangladesh Minorities (HRCBM) documented 1,445 incidents affecting religious, ethnic, and Indigenous minority communities across all 64 districts and eight administrative divisions of Bangladesh. The Persecution Continues combines national and district-level statistics with representative cases to examine recurring violence, dispossession, and barriers to justice.
The documented incidents include 286 reported murders and unexplained deaths, 408 attacks on homes, land, and other property, 251 attacks on temples or other religious sites and incidents of religious vandalism, and 30 blasphemy-related incidents across 14 districts. The report also documents missing-person cases, abductions, physical assaults, and sexual violence. These figures are documented minimums. Fear of retaliation, stigma, and barriers to reporting and verification contribute to underreporting, particularly of sexual violence.
The report examines how disputed allegations of religious insult can lead to arrests and retaliation against families and communities, how land grabbing and intimidation can pressure families to leave their homes, and how delays in registering complaints, inadequate investigations, and threats against victims and witnesses can obstruct access to justice. HRCBM calls for equal protection, impartial investigations, effective remedies, and direct participation by affected communities in transitional justice. It identifies patterns that warrant independent examination as possible crimes against humanity.
For journalists, researchers, and human rights practitioners, the report provides monthly trends, district-level tables, case accounts, and an explanation of HRCBM’s verification methodology. It also records selected submissions to national and international institutions and the responses known at publication. Please cite the DOI for the version consulted. The Zenodo record provides access to later versions as corrections, additional corroboration, and case developments become available.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-11-25) ---
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
PROTECTION ENVIRONMENT SNAPSHOT
The protection environment across the 33 camps and on Bhasan Char further deteriorated in Q3, driven by worsening insecurity and economic hardship. The Protection Sector’s Q3 Joint Protection Monitoring Report, which combines monitoring inputs from seven partners including UNHCR, recorded a 39% increase in serious security incidents from Q2, led by abductions and kidnapping for ransom, alongside rising armed group tensions, recruitment activity and violent assaults. Tighter border controls raised the share of refoulement incidents from 14% in 2025 to 31% in 2026 and, for the first time, affected refugee card holders. Maritime departures more than doubled compared with 2025. Gender-based violence and child protection risks, including child marriage and sexual violence against children, also increased.
Cross-border movements declined during the Q3 rainy season. The number of people affected by refoulement incidents fell by 74% from Q2 before rising steadily in September as travel conditions improved, while community sources reported increased new arrivals due to deteriorating security in Myanmar. The 15 September suspension of new-arrival registration (the Government-approved biometric identification process for enrolling newly arrived Rohingya to access humanitarian assistance, except shelter allocations) heightened vulnerability for new arrival families, who now have no access to food or assistance. UNHCR is in discussions with authorities to address concerns about eligibility and recommend improvements to the joint Government of Bangladesh–UNHCR registration process with increased involvement of government personnel. No timeline has yet been confirmed to resume enrolment.
The Protection Sector avoided pipeline breaks in Q3, but reduced funding for the 2026 Joint Response Plan (JRP) forced service reductions. Data reported by the Sector showed a 30–40% reduction in community-based protection activities as of 30 September, affecting some 150,000–200,000 refugees. Overall, only 21% of people with specific protection needs (30,322) received targeted support. With chronic underfunding of prevention and risk mitigation activities, an estimated 150,000 children and adolescents remained at risk of abuse, exploitation, violence and neglect, and more than 500,000 women and girls at risk of gender-based violence.
Against this backdrop of reduced services, harmful coping mechanisms increased in the Cox's Bazar camps as economic hardship deepened. Three trends reflect the worsening environment: Domestic trafficking and exploitative labour grew, with economic hardship contributing to 75% of trafficking cases, up from 61% in Q2. Abductions and kidnappings rose from 78 incidents in Q2 to 84 in Q3 (according to UNHCR data). Intimate partner violence also remained highly prevalent—linked to unemployment, drug use, online gambling, polygamy, family conflict, harmful gender norms and dowry disputes.
Meanwhile, UNHCR led transformation efforts to save costs and improve efficiency—including shifting awarenessraising to digital and refugee-led approaches; replacing static information and feedback centres with mobile teams; joint planning and stronger referral pathways; and integration of protection programming and resources through 280 multipurpose centres.
