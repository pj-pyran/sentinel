--- Report 3: IFRC (2026-07-20) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
05-07-2026
What happened, where and when?
Since 5th July 2026, Cox’s Bazar District has experienced an intense heavy monsoon rainfall associated with a monsoon depression over the Bay of Bengal. Although the system later weakened into a well-marked low-pressure area inland, active monsoon conditions continued to bring widespread heavy rainfall, rough sea conditions, and localized severe weather impacts across the district. As of 12 July 1800 hours BST, Cox’s Bazar had experienced a cumulative rainfall of 810 mm since 5th July, recorded as the highest continuous rainfall volume for monsoon season in Cox's Bazar compared to the previous years.
The prolonged rainfall has caused significant humanitarian impacts across both the 33 Rohingya camps and host communities in Cox’s Bazar district. In the camps, heavy rainfall and saturated slopes triggered landslides, flooding, wind-related damage, and infrastructure disruptions, damaging shelters, WASH facilities, access routes, pathways, retaining walls, and essential service points.
The impact has also extended to adjacent host communities, where heavy rainfall worsened flooding to at least 49% of Cox's Bazar district's administrative jurisdiction particularly in Chakariya, Pekua, and Matamuhuri sub-districts, disrupting mobility, communication, livelihoods, and access to basic services along damaged shelters, critical infrastructures, and services.
In response to the escalating situation, Bangladesh Red Crescent Society (BDRCS) activated its Population Movement Operation (PMO) Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) and mobilized staff and volunteers to support early warning dissemination, emergency relocation, incident monitoring, damage assessment, search and rescue support, and immediate assistance to affected households using available contingency resources. BDRCS has also continued coordination with camp Disaster Management Committees (DMCs), local authorities, Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP), Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), the Rohingya Coordination Platform (RCP)-Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) Working Group, Site Management, and relevant sector actors. However, with continued rainfall forecast and ground conditions already saturated, the risk of further flooding, landslides, displacement, damage to shelters and services, and depletion of existing response capacity remains high.

--- Report 4: Bangladesh Red Crescent (2026-07-20) ---
Description
Bangladesh has experienced widespread flooding, flash floods, riverine inundation, and landslides across its south-eastern and north-eastern regions following several days of exceptionally heavy monsoon rainfall. The emergency was initially triggered by a low-pressure system that brought intense rainfall across large parts of the country, resulting in severe flooding, waterlogging, river overflows, and landslides in vulnerable areas. Rainfall totals were particularly high in Chattogram, Teknaf, Bandarban, and Sylhet districts. Chattogram recorded 298.8 mm of rainfall on 9 July and 203 mm on 10 July, while Teknaf received 245 mm on 10 July. On 11 July, the highest rainfall was recorded in Jaflong, Sylhet (156 mm), followed by Lama, Bandarban (155 mm), and Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar (139 mm).
Although weather conditions have gradually improved, the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD) forecasts that active monsoon conditions will continue through 20 July, bringing light to moderate rainfall accompanied by temporary gusty winds across the country. Maritime ports of Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Mongla, and Payra remain under Local Cautionary Signal No. 3. Hydrological conditions have improved significantly since the peak of the emergency. As of 16 July, only the Kushiyara River remained above the danger level at two monitoring stations, Markuli in Sunamganj and Fenchuganj in Sylhet. Water levels along the Surma River and other major rivers in the north-eastern region have continued to recede, and flood conditions in low-lying areas of Sylhet and Sunamganj are expected to improve further. Similarly, water levels in the Teesta, Dharla, and Dudhkumar rivers in Rangpur Division have fallen and are projected to continue decreasing. Nevertheless, localized flooding, waterlogging, and landslide risks persist in several areas where saturated soils, damaged infrastructure, and access constraints continue to affect communities. The Bangladesh Red Crescent Society (BDRCS) Emergency Operations Centre remains active and continues to monitor the evolving situation.
At the peak of the emergency, flood conditions deteriorated rapidly. Several rivers, including the Sangu, Kushiyara, Monu, and Khowai, exceeded danger levels, with the Sangu River in Bandarban rising more than two metres above its danger threshold. Rising river levels threatened low-lying communities in Chattogram, Feni, Khagrachhari, Sylhet, Sunamganj, Netrokona, Sherpur, Mymensingh, and the northern river basins, causing widespread disruption to livelihoods, infrastructure, transportation networks, and basic services.
The humanitarian impact remains significant. According to the Needs Assessment Working Group (NAWG), an estimated 1,116,174 people have been affected across 10 districts. The Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR) reports that approximately 38,500 people were temporarily displaced, while more than 1,000 evacuation shelters were opened to accommodate affected populations. Government reporting further indicates that 267,918 households were stranded or isolated by floodwaters across seven districts. Media reports citing MoDMR indicate that at least 59 people lost their lives nationwide as a result of floods and landslides.
Housing damage has been extensive across the affected areas. Preliminary government assessments indicate that 15,881 houses were damaged in Bandarban, 13,860 in Chattogram, 1,040 in Khagrachhari, 1,613 in Cox’s Bazar. However, the full extent of housing damage remains unclear as standing water, damaged infrastructure, and access constraints continue to hamper detailed assessments. According to the MoDMR situation update on 18 July 2026, a total of 374 unions and four municipalities across 56 upazilas have been affected by the ongoing floods.
Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) systems have been severely disrupted across 27 upazilas in 10 districts. In many areas, water supply infrastructure remains submerged, damaged, or contaminated by floodwaters carrying faecal matter, agricultural chemicals, industrial waste, and other pollutants. As of 12 July, the Department of Public Health Engineering (DPHE) reported that more than 12,360 latrines and 3,583 water points had been fully or partially damaged, increasing risks of waterborne disease outbreaks and reducing access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities.
The floods have also caused substantial damage to agriculture, livestock, and fisheries, threatening household incomes, food security, and rural livelihoods. According to the Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE), approximately 54,470 hectares of cropland and 324,358 farming households have been affected by heavy rainfall and associated hazards. Crop losses alone are estimated at more than 54,409 hectares of agricultural land. The fisheries sector has sustained particularly severe damage, with preliminary assessments from the Department of Fisheries (DoF) indicating losses of 7,613 metric tons of fish and prawns. Floodwaters washed away 21,764 fishponds and 784 commercial enclosures covering 8,966 hectares, resulting in estimated losses of approximately USD 18.3 million.
The livestock sector has also been heavily impacted. Approximately 266,038 cattle and buffalo, 173,592 goats and sheep, 2.85 million chickens, and 62,811 ducks have been affected. Reported animal deaths include 62 cattle, 139 goats, 59 sheep, 154,528 chickens, and 2,063 ducks. Total losses in the livestock sector are estimated at BDT 576.2 million (approximately USD 4.7 million). Combined losses across the fisheries and livestock sectors are estimated at approximately USD 23 million.
The most severely affected districts include Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Bandarban, Rangamati, Khagrachhari, Moulvibazar, and Habiganj, where communities continue to face challenges related to damaged housing, disrupted livelihoods, limited access to essential services, and deteriorated infrastructure.
In Cox’s Bazar, the situation remains particularly concerning due to the combined impact on Rohingya refugee camps and surrounding host communities. Across the 33 refugee camps, flooding and landslides affected more than 28,000 people, damaged over 3,100 shelters, and displaced more than 4,700 individuals. Multiple casualties and injuries were reported as a result of landslides and flood-related incidents. UNHCR has confirmed 15 deaths among Rohingya refugees, while thousands of people have been left homeless following landslides and shelter collapses, further increasing humanitarian needs among already vulnerable populations.
The Government of Bangladesh, led by the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR), has maintained a coordinated multi-agency response to support flood- and landslide-affected communities. Response measures have included the opening of more than 1,000 evacuation shelters, the distribution of cash assistance, rice, baby food, safe drinking water, and other relief items, as well as the deployment of the Bangladesh Army, Navy, Coast Guard, Fire Service and Civil Defence, and local authorities to support rescue, evacuation, and relief operations. The Government has also mobilised 516 medical teams across 50 affected upazilas to address urgent health needs and reduce public health risks. In addition, the Government has allocated 3,350 metric tons of rice, BDT 23.5 million in cash assistance, and 4,200 bundles of corrugated galvanized iron (CGI) sheets alongside BDT 12.6 million for shelter repair and reconstruction support for affected households across seven districts. National and district-level coordination mechanisms remain active to monitor the evolving situation, facilitate early warning dissemination, support affected communities, and prioritize assistance to the hardest-hit areas. Efforts are ongoing to restore essential services, support displaced populations, and address immediate humanitarian and early recovery needs as floodwaters continue to recede.

Summary
The Bangladesh Red Crescent Society (BDRCS) activated its Central Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) immediately following the onset of the flood emergency and continues to coordinate response efforts across flood- and landslide-affected districts in south-eastern and north-eastern Bangladesh. The EOC remains operational to facilitate real-time information management, situation monitoring, and coordination with district units, government authorities, the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC), the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), Participating National Societies (PNSs), and other humanitarian partners, ensuring a timely, coordinated, and effective response. To support immediate response operations, BDRCS has mobilized emergency resources through its contingency funds and partner contributions. A total of BDT 2.6 million has been released to district units in Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Bandarban, Rangamati, Khagrachhari, Moulvibazar, and Habiganj to facilitate rapid response activities and assist affected communities. To strengthen evacuation, rescue, and emergency response capacities, BDRCS deployed a wide range of rescue equipment and emergency supplies, including 12 rubber rescue boats, 110 life jackets, 135 rescue helmets, 135 rescue boots, 145 pairs of rescue gloves, five generators, and 5,500 jerrycans, together with rescue ropes, rope bags, float plates, and rescue panels. These assets have enhanced the operational effectiveness of district units and volunteers engaged in life-saving interventions in affected communities. BDRCS has provided emergency food assistance to 6,450 flood-affected households across Bandarban, Khagrachhari, Rangamati, Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Moulvibazar, and Habiganj. Distributed food packages included rice, puffed rice, lentils, edible oil, salt, sugar, biscuits, oral rehydration salts (ORS), soap, sanitary pads, safe drinking water, and other essential household items. These interventions have helped address immediate food security and basic needs among affected populations. To respond to critical health and WASH needs, BDRCS deployed four Mobile Water Treatment Units (MWTUs) and distributed more than 5,114 litres of safe drinking water to affected communities in Bandarban and Chattogram districts. In addition, two emergency mobile medical teams were deployed with essential medicines and relief supplies to provide primary healthcare services and support disease prevention efforts in flood-affected areas. In Cox’s Bazar, BDRCS continues to respond through its Population Movement Operation (PMO), supporting both Rohingya refugees and host communities affected by flooding and landslides. Response activities include early warning dissemination, emergency relocation support, search and rescue operations, and coordination through Camp Disaster Management Committees (DMCs) and humanitarian partners across all 33 refugee camps. PMO teams have provided emergency shelter assistance to 494 affected households in Camps 11 and 12, while WASH volunteers continue to assess damaged facilities and coordinate rehabilitation efforts with sector partners. Health teams remain engaged in community outreach, referrals, household visits, and the provision of essential health services, including outpatient and maternal healthcare. Through its extensive volunteer network and field teams, BDRCS continues to address the urgent needs of flood- and landslide-affected refugees and host communities. Recognizing the scale of the emergency, BDRCS formally requested IFRC support on 8 July 2026 to launch a Disaster Response Emergency Fund (DREF) operation for flood- and landslide-affected populations in the Rohingya camps and adjacent host communities in Cox’s Bazar. In response, IFRC approved and launched a CHF 475,000 DREF operation to support life-saving interventions, including early warning dissemination, evacuation support, search and rescue, emergency shelter assistance, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, food assistance, cash and voucher assistance (CVA), volunteer mobilization, and operational coordination. The DREF operation complements ongoing efforts by BDRCS, government authorities, and humanitarian partners while strengthening support to the most vulnerable communities. To strengthen Red Cross Red Crescent Movement coordination and review the evolving humanitarian situation, BDRCS convened an emergency coordination meeting on 16 July with IFRC, ICRC, and in-country Participating National Societies (PNSs). The meeting provided updates on the flood situation, damage assessments, response achievements, and operational challenges across affected districts. Given the continued deterioration in humanitarian conditions, extensive damage, emerging needs, and the expansion of affected areas beyond the initial operational scope, BDRCS has developed an enhanced response plan aimed at scaling up humanitarian assistance for the most vulnerable and severely affected households. Planned interventions include the mobilization of additional food parcels, mobile water treatment plants, emergency medical teams, essential household items, cash assistance, and agricultural recovery support. In light of increasing humanitarian needs and widening response gaps, BDRCS has requested IFRC to consider expanding the current DREF operation and has appealed to Participating National Societies to mobilize additional international resources to support a larger-scale response and early recovery effort across the flood-affected districts.

Chattogram, Cox's Bazar, Bandarban, Rangamati, Khagrachhari, Cumilla, Moulvibazar, Habiganj, Sunamganj, Sylhet
Current situation, 16 July 2026:
Heavy rainfall has caused flash floods and landslides in Chattogram Division, the Chattogram Hill Tracts, Cox's Bazar, and parts of Sylhet Division. The Bangladesh Meteorological Department forecasts that active monsoon precipitation will persist across the country through 20 July, bringing light to moderate rain and temporary gusty winds. Maritime ports at Chattogram, Cox's Bazar, Mongla, and Payra remain under Local Cautionary Signal No. 3. As of 16 July at 9:00 AM, hydrological conditions indicate that only one river (the Kushiyara) remains above the danger level at two stations: Markuli in Sunamganj and Fenchuganj in Sylhet. The Someshwari River at Kalmakanda has receded below the danger level. In the north-eastern region, the Surma river has decreased and flood conditions in the low-lying areas of Sylhet and Sunamganj are expected to improve over the next 24 hours. Additionally, water levels for the Teesta, Dharla, and Dudhkumar rivers in Rangpur Division have decreased and are projected to fall further over the next three days. BDRCS's Emergency Operations Centre remains open and active.
