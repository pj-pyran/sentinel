Crisis / situation: Bangladesh

--- Report 1: Oxfam (2026-07-15) ---
Since 4 July 2026, continuous heavy rainfall associated with an active monsoon and a persistent low-pressure system over the north-west Bay of Bengal, combined with upstream runoff, has triggered widespread flash floods, severe waterlogging, and landslides across southeastern, eastern, and northeastern Bangladesh.
The disaster has affected more than 1.11 million people across 10 districts: Chattogram, Cox's Bazar, Sunamganj, Khagrachhari, Rangamati, Bandarban, Cumilla, Moulvibazar, Sylhet and Feni that resulting in 53 fatalities and 39 injuries as of today.
While Chattogram District has experienced the highest level of impact in terms of the affected population. Exceptional rainfall has been recorded across the affected areas. Chattogram city received 412.3 mm of rainfall within 24 hours till date, marking the highest single-day July rainfall recorded in the past 42 years.
The major rivers, including the Matamuhuri, Sangu, Surma, and Kushiyara, exceeded their danger levels, leading to embankment breaches, extensive flooding, and prolonged waterlogging.
Several lowlying areas were completely inundated, including the entirety of Banshkhali Upazila in Chattogram District. In the hill districts of Chattogram Division, saturated slopes triggered multiple landslides, causing casualties, damaging homes and infrastructure, and disrupting road connectivity.
Bangladesh’s flood threat is shifting from the country’s southeastern districts to the north and northeast, with forecasters warning that continued heavy rainfall and upstream runoff could trigger fresh flooding in Sylhet,
Sunamganj and Rangpur over the coming days.
The Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD) has forecast moderate to very heavy rainfall across much of the country over the next several days, raising concerns that the existing flood situation could worsen before improving

--- Report 2: Bangladesh Red Crescent (2026-07-14) ---
Description
Bangladesh is experiencing widespread flooding, flash floods, riverine inundation, and landslides across the south-eastern and north-eastern regions of the country. Although the low-pressure system that triggered the initial period of extreme rainfall has weakened and moved towards Uttar Pradesh, India, the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD) continues to forecast intermittent heavy to very heavy rainfall through 13 July across the divisions of Chattogram, Sylhet, Rangpur, Rajshahi, Dhaka, Khulna, and Barishal. While rainfall intensity is expected to gradually decrease in the coming days, the risk of further flooding and landslides remains elevated.
Rainfall has remained exceptionally high. Chattogram recorded 298.8 mm on 9 July and 203 mm on 10 July, while Teknaf recorded 245 mm on 10 July. On 11 July, the highest rainfall was recorded in Jaflong, Sylhet (156 mm), followed by Lama, Bandarban (155 mm) and Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar (139 mm). Additional rainfall of 100–300 mm was forecast through 12 July, maintaining a high risk of flooding, waterlogging and landslides.
Flood conditions continue to evolve. As of 11 July, several river stations remained above danger level, including the Sangu, Kushiyara, Monu and Khowai rivers. By the evening of 11 July, seven stations on five rivers across six districts were flowing above danger level. The Sangu River in Bandarban exceeded danger level by more than two metres at its peak, while rising river levels continue to threaten low-lying areas in Chattogram, Feni, Khagrachhari, Sylhet, Sunamganj, Netrokona, Sherpur, Mymensingh and northern river basins.
The humanitarian impact remains significant. According to the Needs Assessment Working Group (NAWG), an estimated 1,074,534 people have been affected across 10 districts. The Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR) reports that approximately 38,500 people have been temporarily displaced, while more than 1,000 evacuation shelters have been opened.
Government reporting indicates that more than 267,918 households have been stranded or cut off by floodwaters across seven districts, while media reports citing the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief indicate that at least 51 people have died nationwide due to floods and landslides. Preliminary Government reports indicate that 15,881 houses were damaged in Bandarban and 13,860 in Chattogram. The full extent and severity of housing damage remain unclear, as standing water and access constraints continue to delay detailed assessments. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services have been disrupted across 27 upazilas in 10 districts. In several areas, infrastructure remains submerged, while water sources have been damaged or contaminated by floodwater carrying faecal matter, agricultural chemicals, industrial waste and other pollutants. As of 12 July, the Department of Public Health Engineering (DPHE) reported that more than 12,360 latrines and 3,583 waterpoints had been fully or partially damaged.
The most affected districts include Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Bandarban, Rangamati, Khagrachhari, Moulvibazar and Habiganj.
In Cox’s Bazar, the situation remains particularly concerning due to the combined impact on Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. Across the 33 camps affected more than 28,000 people, damaged over 3,100 shelters were damaged, more than 4,700 individuals were displaced, and caused multiple fatalities and injuries associated with landslides and flooding. UNHCR has reported 15 deaths among Rohingya refugees and thousands of people left homeless following landslides and shelter collapse.
The Government of Bangladesh, led by the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR), has activated a multi-agency response to support flood- and landslide-affected populations across the impacted districts. Emergency measures include the opening of more than 1,000 shelters, the allocation and distribution of cash assistance, rice, baby food, safe drinking water and other relief items, and the deployment of the Army, Navy, Coast Guard, Fire Service and local authorities to support rescue, evacuation and relief operations. The Government has deployed 516 medical teams across 50 affected upazilas. Government authorities continue to monitor the evolving situation through national and district-level coordination mechanisms, while prioritizing assistance to the most severely affected areas, including Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Bandarban, Rangamati, Khagrachhari, Moulvibazar and Habiganj. Efforts are ongoing to maintain access to essential services, support displaced populations, and address immediate humanitarian needs in affected communities.

The DREF operation is intended to complement ongoing response efforts by BDRCS and humanitarian partners while strengthening support to the most vulnerable and affected communities.

Actions taken by others
Following the heavy rainfall forecast issued on 5 July, the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR), the Department of Disaster Management (DDM), the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), and the Flood Forecasting and Warning Centre (FFWC) convened a series of coordination meetings to strengthen preparedness and response measures. The National Disaster Response Coordination Centre was activated on a 24-hour basis, and emergency contact information was disseminated to facilitate timely response. At the district level, disaster management committees were mobilised to coordinate early warning dissemination, evacuations, shelter management, provision of emergency meals, and rescue operations. These efforts were supported by the Bangladesh Army, Bangladesh Red Crescent Society (BDRCS), Border Guard Bangladesh, Fire Service and Civil Defence, Bangladesh Police, and local administrations. On 12 July, the Prime Minister’s Office convened a high-level virtual meeting to review the impacts of flooding and landslides, including waterlogging, infrastructure damage, shelter conditions, rescue operations, relief distribution, and health service provision. Relevant authorities were instructed to prioritise the protection of lives and property, expedite the delivery of food assistance, safe drinking water, medicines, and healthcare services, and ensure that evacuation shelters provide adequate safety, sanitation, electricity, communication facilities, and support for vulnerable groups. Government agencies were further directed to strengthen inter-agency coordination, maintain operational readiness, closely monitor weather forecasts and river levels, and ensure transparent and accountable delivery of relief assistance. Led by MoDMR, the Government of Bangladesh has activated a comprehensive multi-agency response across flood- and landslide-affected districts. Emergency measures have included the opening of more than 1,000 evacuation shelters, the distribution of cash assistance, rice, baby food, safe drinking water, and other relief supplies, and the deployment of the Bangladesh Army, Navy, Coast Guard, Fire Service and Civil Defence, and local authorities to support search and rescue, evacuation, and relief operations. To address urgent health needs and reduce public health risks, the Government has mobilised 516 medical teams across 50 affected upazilas. National and district-level coordination mechanisms remain active to monitor the evolving situation, support early warning dissemination, and facilitate targeted assistance in the hardest-hit districts, particularly Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Bandarban, Rangamati, Khagrachhari, Moulvibazar, and Habiganj. To reinforce response efforts, the Government allocated a cumulative BDT 17.5 million (approximately US$142,276) and 3,250 metric tons of rice between 7 and 12 July for seven flood- and rainfall-affected districts. Chattogram received the largest allocation, comprising BDT 6.5 million and 1,200 metric tons of rice, followed by Cox’s Bazar with BDT 3 million and 450 metric tons of rice. Bandarban and Khagrachhari each received BDT 2 million and 400 metric tons of rice, while Rangamati received BDT 2.5 million and 500 metric tons of rice. Additional allocations included BDT 1 million and 200 metric tons of rice for Moulvibazar, and BDT 500,000 and 100 metric tons of rice for Habiganj. These resources were released through successive general and flood-response allocations on 7, 9, 10, and 12 July, enabling the Government to progressively scale up assistance as the situation evolved. The humanitarian community has also significantly strengthened coordination and response efforts. On 11 July, the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) convened an ad hoc meeting to review the evolving flood situation, anticipatory action activations, and initial updates from clusters and operational partners. The meeting agreed to strengthen information management, consolidate reporting on response activities, identify priority needs and operational gaps, and produce the first ICCG Situation Report. Subsequently, the Displacement Management, Health, Logistics, Shelter, and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) Clusters convened emergency coordination meetings to review field-level information, identify operational constraints, and coordinate follow-up actions. The Needs Assessment and Information Management Working Group (NA/IMWG) issued Flash Updates on 9 and 12 July and continues to consolidate available data from affected areas. Preparations are underway for a multi-sectoral assessment once access conditions improve. Meanwhile, the Anticipatory Action Technical Working Group (AATWG) remains closely engaged with agencies implementing anticipatory action frameworks, monitoring trigger thresholds, sharing operational updates, and supporting coordinated preparedness actions. The ICCG Secretariat continues to liaise with cluster coordinators, BMD, DDM, MoDMR, the NGO Affairs Bureau, and district administrations across the affected areas to support a coordinated humanitarian response. In anticipation of peak flooding, the World Food Programme (WFP) activated both anticipatory action (AA) and shock-responsive social protection (SRSP) interventions in Bandarban on 10 July. Through these mechanisms, WFP reached 15,000 households under anticipatory action and an additional 859 households through SRSP, providing multipurpose cash assistance of BDT 5,000 per household alongside early warning messages. Interventions focused on Bandarban Sadar and Lama upazilas to support vulnerable households before conditions deteriorated further. On 13 July, the Start Fund Bangladesh Project Selection Committee approved GBP 799,890.60 under Alert B075 to support an emergency humanitarian response targeting approximately 55,554 people across six of the most severely affected districts: Bandarban, Khagrachhari, Rangamati, Cox’s Bazar, Chattogram, and Moulvibazar. The allocation will support rapid multi-sectoral assistance to flood- and landslide-affected populations. Several agencies also activated anticipatory actions under existing disaster preparedness frameworks. Under the Early Action Protocol for flash floods in the Haor region, CARE Bangladesh mobilised volunteers, assessed evacuation shelters, and pre-positioned four rescue boats in Derai and Gowainghat, targeting approximately 18,500 people. UN Women reached around 300 households in Kanaighat with preparedness support, while World Vision disseminated early warning messages to approximately 50,000 people, pre-positioned 1,000 hygiene kits, validated beneficiary lists for 700 households, and prepared evacuation support for up to 2,000 households. In response to heightened landslide risks, Caritas Bangladesh and Save the Children activated anticipatory actions under the draft National Landslide Early Action Protocol. Save the Children provided BDT 6,000 per household to 400 high-risk households, in line with the 2026 ICCG-endorsed transfer value, while also supporting early warning dissemination and evacuation preparedness activities in Bandarban, Chattogram, and Rangamati districts. Islamic Relief Bangladesh allocated GBP 150,000 to support flood-affected communities, including GBP 50,000 dedicated to emergency response activities in the Banshkhali and Satkania upazilas of Chattogram District. The intervention is expected to reach approximately 700 households through multipurpose cash grants, hygiene and dignity kits, and protection messaging designed to address immediate humanitarian needs and enhance community awareness. Similarly, Dhaka Ahsania Mission (DAM), with support from Start Fund Bangladesh, plans to provide multipurpose cash grants of BDT 8,000 per household to 1,000 households in Chattogram District. Assistance will target 600 households in Baharchara, Chambol, and Chanua unions of Banshkhali Upazila and 400 households in Roypur and Bairag unions of Anowara Upazila, helping affected families meet their most urgent recovery and basic needs.

--- Report 3: WFP (2026-07-14) ---
Key Highlights
• Flooding and landslides continue across southeastern Bangladesh following heavy monsoon rainfall, amid heightened risks associated with anticipated strong El Niño conditions.
• Preliminary estimate indicates that 1.1 million people are affected.
• The Government has called for urgent humanitarian assistance in affected areas.
• WFP emergency assistance is being provided to more than 211,000 people across flood-affected communities and Rohingya camps, while over 1 million people received early warning messages.
Situation Overview
Continuous heavy monsoon rainfall has triggered widespread flooding and landslides across southeastern Bangladesh, affecting more than 1.1 million people and displacing over 36,500 people. More than 1,700 shelters have been opened, while damage to over 10,700 water and sanitation facilities is increasing humanitarian needs and public health risks. The Chattogram Hill Tracts (CHT) remain among the hardest-hit areas, with damaged roads and landslides constraining humanitarian access to remote communities. Heavy rainfall has also affected Rohingya refugee camps and surrounding host communities in Cox’s Bazar, resulting in fatalities, including among Rohingya schoolchildren. Strong El Niño conditions are expected to further increase risks of continued flooding, landslides and displacement.
The emergency is affecting areas already facing high levels of food insecurity. Five of the affected districts - Bandarban, Khagrachhari, Rangamati, Sunamganj and Cox’s Bazar (including Rohingya refugees) - were identified in the latest IPC Acute Food Insecurity Analysis (30 June 2026) as having around 30–35 percent of their populations in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or above. Nationally, 15.3 million people are facing IPC Phase 3 or worse outcomes, including 483,000 people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), with the figure projected to rise to 18.1 million people between September and December 2026.

--- Report 6: AATWG (2026-07-13) ---
The Bangladesh Anticipatory Action Technical Working Group (AATWG), in collaboration with the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR), the Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP), the Department of Disaster Management (DDM), and the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), organized a full-scale, functional, scenario-based simulation exercise to test the National Early Action Protocol (NEAP) for cyclones. Following the simulation, a lessons-learned workshop was organized to capture key insights, identify gaps and challenges, and develop recommendations to further refine and strengthen the cyclone NEAP.

--- Report 7: AP GiHA WG (2026-07-13) ---
Gender Alert on the monsoon flood, flash flood and landslide situation affecting Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Chattogram Hill Tracts (CHT), Sylhet, Sunamganj, Netrokona and Kurigram highlights the gender-differentiated impacts of the current emergency, with women, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons, female-headed households, and Rohingya refugees facing heightened risks due to displacement, disrupted services, livelihood losses, protection concerns, and barriers to accessing assistance. The alert draws on situation reports shared by local Gender in Humanitarian Action (GiHA) chapters in Kurigram, Sylhet, Sunamganj and Chattogram, as well as the Inter-Sector Flash Flood Update by RCP and Need assessment working group Flash updates. The alert recommends immediate action to strengthen gender-responsive preparedness and response, including accessible early warning systems, targeted evacuations, protection services, GBV risk mitigation, maternal health support, and engagement of women-led organizations and local GiHA chapters. Urgent priorities for the next 72 hours are also outlined.

--- Report 8: Bangladesh Red Crescent (2026-07-13) ---
The emergency has now entered its second week and continues to widen in scope. While the low-pressure system that drove the initial spell of extreme rainfall has weakened and moved on over Uttar Pradesh, India, the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD) continues to forecast heavy to very heavy rainfall in spells through 13 July across Chattogram, Sylhet, Rangpur, Rajshahi, Dhaka, Khulna and Barisal divisions. Flooding has spread to new areas, including a breach of the Dhalai river embankment in Kamalganj upazila, Moulvibazar, and rising river levels in Habiganj, while conditions have deteriorated in Satkania, Lohagara, Banshkhali and Chakaria. According to the latest consolidated figures, over 1 million people are now affected nationally across 10 districts, with more than 40 casualties recorded, around 36,500 people temporarily displaced, and more than 1,700 shelters activated.
BDRCS continues to monitor the situation through its central Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) and remains in close coordination with its district units, the Population Movement Operation (PMO) in Cox’s Bazar, district administrations, BMD, NAWG and other humanitarian partners. Confirmed funding from the American Red Cross Quick Action Fund, Initial response Fund supported by Swedish Red Cross, Locally Led Disaster Response Fund supported by British Red cross, and a pending IFRC DREF request are supporting an expanded response, with cash allocations now released to Nine district units and Chattogram City Unit. Further rounds of emergency water jerrycans and search-and-rescue items dispatched to twelve units, including newly affected north-eastern districts. Response efforts currently include volunteer mobilization, search and rescue support, public awareness campaigns, evacuation assistance, first aid, emergency food and cash assistance, and preparedness measures in high-risk communities.

Disruptions to WASH services, shelter conditions, and humanitarian supply chains may further increase the vulnerability of affected populations.
