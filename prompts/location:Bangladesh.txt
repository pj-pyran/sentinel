--- Report 1: Bangladesh Red Crescent (2026-07-14) ---
Description
Impact in 33 camps:
Between 5 and 13 July 2026, a total of 518 weather-related incidents were reported, affecting 11,183 households (51,363 people) across the 33 camps. The incident caused damage to 4,656 shelters and displaced 13,038 individuals. To date, 15 fatalities and 22 injuries have been recorded. These figures underscore the significant humanitarian impact of the ongoing monsoon conditions and highlight the need for continued monitoring, preparedness, and response efforts, particularly in areas at heightened risk of flooding, waterlogging, and landslides.
Impact in Host community:
Based on available data, a total of 139,081 affected population across all the sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar. Out of this, 1,195 shelters have been damaged, 124 damaged bridges, with 15 deaths, 19 injured, and 1 missing person reported.
According also to preliminary information, about 2,566 individuals have been evacuated and temporarily accommodated in evacuation centers due to inundation of water from rivers and streams due to the continuous heavy rains in Cox’s Bazar.
At present, BDRCS is closely coordinating with relevant authorities and other humanitarian and development agencies as part of its monsoon monitoring process.

Actions taken by others
In the camp settlement, the Government of Bangladesh, through the Office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC), is monitoring the overall situation through camp-level Disaster Management Committees (DMCs) to support timely response and a standardized assistance approach. Fire Service and Civil Defense (FSCD), together with Armed Police Battalion (APBn), is engaged in emergency search and rescue operations, while the Bangladesh Armed Forces Division (AFD) remains on standby to provide support as needed. In host communities, the Deputy Commissioner's Office has activated the district-level EOC to monitor early actions by the Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP), BDRCS local branch and sub-district administrations. The Deputy Commissioner's Office has announced humanitarian assistance for affected populations, although the support remains inadequate compared with needs. More than 700 emergency shelters have also been opened to accommodate evacuees from at-risk areas and reduce loss of life from landslides and flooding. In addition, Government authorities including RRRC Office and Deputy Commissioner (DC) Office-Cox's Bazar have both kept provision of continuous monitoring of the situation through their EOC. In addition, there is provision of triggering contingency funds allocated for districts which will be very much specific to the host community only. For camp settlement, from the Government end they have their provision to monitor the situation and work closely with humanitarian partners in responding effectively and efficiently based on further escalation of the monsoon crisis in the near future. Led by IOM and UNHCR and implemented through a network of national and international NGOs including ACTED, BRAC, DRC, MBSS, NRC, CARE, Oxfam, BDRCS, IRC, World Vision, Mukti and others, the emergency response has focused on life-saving shelter, site management, WASH, protection, health, nutrition, food security and education interventions. Partners are conducting rapid damage assessments, relocating at-risk households, supporting rescue operations, providing temporary shelter and emergency food assistance, repairing critical WASH facilities, delivering psychosocial support and protection referrals, disseminating early warning messages, and maintaining essential services despite challenging access conditions. The Rohingya Coordination Platform, jointly with the RRRC Office, has also issued joint messaging to accelerate monsoon preparedness and scale up community readiness across all 33 camps. On the other hand, UNDP and other key partners are actively engaged in host community programming have triggered contingency funds (an amount of 300,000 BDT for Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts) to support sub-district administrations in providing humanitarian assistance to affected families.

--- Report 2: UN RC Bangladesh (2026-07-14) ---
Bangladesh Flash Floods and Landslides Situation Report #1 (13 July 2026) provides a comprehensive overview of the humanitarian impact of severe monsoon-induced floods and landslides affecting multiple districts across Bangladesh. Heavy rainfall, upstream runoff, and landslide incidents have affected more than 1.11 million people across 10 districts, resulting in 51 reported fatalities, widespread displacement, and significant damage to homes, water systems, roads, bridges, and public infrastructure.The report highlights the evolving emergency situation in Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Bandarban, Khagrachhari, Rangamati, Moulvibazar, Sunamganj, Habiganj, Sylhet, and Cumilla, where communities continue to face challenges related to displacement, access constraints, disrupted water and sanitation services, and damage to livelihoods. As of 12 July, more than 38,000 people were staying in over 1,000 evacuation shelters, while humanitarian partners and government authorities were scaling up food assistance, health services, shelter support, cash assistance, and WASH interventions.

--- Report 3: AATWG (2026-07-13) ---
The Bangladesh Anticipatory Action Technical Working Group (AATWG), in collaboration with the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR), the Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP), the Department of Disaster Management (DDM), and the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), organized a full-scale, functional, scenario-based simulation exercise to test the National Early Action Protocol (NEAP) for cyclones. Following the simulation, a lessons-learned workshop was organized to capture key insights, identify gaps and challenges, and develop recommendations to further refine and strengthen the cyclone NEAP.

--- Report 4: AP GiHA WG (2026-07-13) ---
Gender Alert on the monsoon flood, flash flood and landslide situation affecting Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Chattogram Hill Tracts (CHT), Sylhet, Sunamganj, Netrokona and Kurigram highlights the gender-differentiated impacts of the current emergency, with women, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons, female-headed households, and Rohingya refugees facing heightened risks due to displacement, disrupted services, livelihood losses, protection concerns, and barriers to accessing assistance. The alert draws on situation reports shared by local Gender in Humanitarian Action (GiHA) chapters in Kurigram, Sylhet, Sunamganj and Chattogram, as well as the Inter-Sector Flash Flood Update by RCP and Need assessment working group Flash updates. The alert recommends immediate action to strengthen gender-responsive preparedness and response, including accessible early warning systems, targeted evacuations, protection services, GBV risk mitigation, maternal health support, and engagement of women-led organizations and local GiHA chapters. Urgent priorities for the next 72 hours are also outlined.

--- Report 5: Bangladesh Red Crescent (2026-07-13) ---
The emergency has now entered its second week and continues to widen in scope. While the low-pressure system that drove the initial spell of extreme rainfall has weakened and moved on over Uttar Pradesh, India, the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD) continues to forecast heavy to very heavy rainfall in spells through 13 July across Chattogram, Sylhet, Rangpur, Rajshahi, Dhaka, Khulna and Barisal divisions. Flooding has spread to new areas, including a breach of the Dhalai river embankment in Kamalganj upazila, Moulvibazar, and rising river levels in Habiganj, while conditions have deteriorated in Satkania, Lohagara, Banshkhali and Chakaria. According to the latest consolidated figures, over 1 million people are now affected nationally across 10 districts, with more than 40 casualties recorded, around 36,500 people temporarily displaced, and more than 1,700 shelters activated.
BDRCS continues to monitor the situation through its central Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) and remains in close coordination with its district units, the Population Movement Operation (PMO) in Cox’s Bazar, district administrations, BMD, NAWG and other humanitarian partners. Confirmed funding from the American Red Cross Quick Action Fund, Initial response Fund supported by Swedish Red Cross, Locally Led Disaster Response Fund supported by British Red cross, and a pending IFRC DREF request are supporting an expanded response, with cash allocations now released to Nine district units and Chattogram City Unit. Further rounds of emergency water jerrycans and search-and-rescue items dispatched to twelve units, including newly affected north-eastern districts. Response efforts currently include volunteer mobilization, search and rescue support, public awareness campaigns, evacuation assistance, first aid, emergency food and cash assistance, and preparedness measures in high-risk communities.

Continued rainfall is expected to increase the risk of flooding, landslides, infrastructure damage, access constraints, and displacement. Although the overall rainfall intensity is projected to decrease, localized waterlogging and isolated flash flooding may still occur in lowlying areas, particularly on 13 July, warranting continued monitoring and preparedness.

SITUATION OVERVIEW
Following prolonged and heavy monsoon rainfall across the Rohingya refugee camps in Cox's Bazar, two significant landslide incidents were reported during the early hours of 6 July 2026 in Camp 11 and Camp 15. According to information available at the time of reporting, the incidents resulted in reported fatalities, injuries, shelter damage, and the displacement of affected households. Reported casualty figures remain subject to verification and confirmation by the relevant Government of Bangladesh (GoB) authorities.
The incidents occurred following several days of sustained rainfall, which resulted in widespread soil saturation and increased landslide risk across the camps. Initial field observations suggest that prolonged rainfall and the resulting soil saturation were the primary contributing factors in both reported incidents. Site-specific conditions, including terrain, drainage, slope loading, and settlement patterns, are also being assessed as part of ongoing technical review.
Immediately following the incidents, IOM, together with the Camp-in-Charge (CIC), Disaster Management Unit (DMU) volunteers, Site Management Support (SMS) teams, Site Development engineers, the Bangladesh Fire Service and Civil Defense, community volunteers, and humanitarian partners, initiated search and rescue operations, emergency evacuations, shelter damage assessments, and immediate assistance to affected households. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), emergency shelter assistance, and relocation support were also activated for affected and at-risk families.
Beyond the two reported landslide incidents, continuous rainfall has caused widespread impacts across IOM's Areas of Responsibility (AoR). As of the reporting period, 1,066 shelter damage cases were reported, of which 802 were verified through rapid damage assessments. In addition, flooding affected the IOM-PHALS Soap Production Centre in Camp 20, damaging packaging materials and raw materials. No landslide has been reported at the facility; however, the site remains vulnerable due to its proximity to an adjacent hillside.
These incidents highlight the continued vulnerability of refugee settlements to monsoon-related hazards and reinforce the importance of sustained risk reduction measures. As a priority, IOM met with the RRRC and agreed on the compulsory relocation of extremely vulnerable families in areas of imminent landslide risk, in order to prevent further loss of life. Other priorities include technical assessment and stabilization of high-risk slopes, timely relocation of households in hazardous locations, strengthened community risk communication, improved drainage, and coordinated implementation of risk mitigation measures across sectors.
