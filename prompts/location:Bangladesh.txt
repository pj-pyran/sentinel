Crisis / situation: Bangladesh

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-11-11) ---
KEY UPDATES: SEPTEMBER 2026
FOURTH PRESENCE CONFIRMATION EXER CISE (PCE) O N BHASAN CHAR: The September 2026 Presence Confirmation Exercise found that 26,775 refugees were present on Bhasan Char, reflecting an 8% decrease since December 2025. 94% of those confirmed present expressed an intention to remain, citing safety, better services, shelter and livelihood opportunities. Funding constraints and the absence of a clear national policy framework for Bhasan Char continue to hinder long-term planning and investment. Read the report.
NEW ARRIVALS &amp; BIOMETRIC ENROLLMENT: New arrivals continue to approach UNHCR in Cox’s Bazar camps to be biometrically identified, with 264 individuals enrolled in September, bringing the total to 155,560 people (40,903 families) since January 2025. The Government-approved process enables access to humanitarian assistance for new arrivals, except shelter allocation. However, on 15 September, the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) requested suspension of enrollment due to concerns over eligibility. UNHCR and the RRRC, who is seeking greater involvement in the process, will hold a technical meeting to refine admissibility criteria.
VIRTUAL COURT H EARING PROJ ECT: To strengthen access to justice, UNHCR and partners launched the virtual court hearing initiative on 1 September. Implemented with partner BLAST, and relevant government counterparts, the project allows refugees and host community victims and witnesses in selected cases to participate in court hearings remotely through secure video links in or near camps. By reducing travel requirements, costs and administrative barriers, the project makes access to justice safer and more efficient; it is expected to increase participation in legal proceedings and reduce withdrawals from cases. Watch the video.
STRATEGIC TRANSFORMATION OF ROHINGYA RESPONSE: As part of ongoing transformation efforts, UNHCR handed over five health facilities in Camps 2 West, 4, 5 and 12 to four Health Sectorendorsed NGOs. The transfers marked an important step in the further rationalization and localization of public health services while maintaining continuity of care. UNHCR continues to provide mental health and psychosocial support in the transferred facilities and manages primary healthcare centres in Camps 3 and 4-Extension.
LIVELIHOODS PRODUCTION: Refugee production centres completed the delivery of 68,060 menstrual hygiene management kits to UNICEF and delivered another 16,110 kits to UNHCR. During September, 893 volunteers, including 764 women, participated in production activities. The centres produced 354,725 sanitary pads, 113,500 items of female underwear, 57,558 kit-carrying bags and 67,805 environmentally friendly bags, linking humanitarian supplies with refugee skills and wage-earning opportunities. To facilitate sustainability, UNHCR’s livelihood partner CNRS is in the process of bringing the camp-based production centres under its social business registration while developing a long-term business plan.
EMERGEN CY PREPAR EDN ESS &amp; RESPONSE: As monsoon rains declined, reported incidents in UNHCR-managed camps fell from 144 in August to 55 in September (62% decrease). Still, the incidents affected 723 people, causing 28 injuries and three fatalities, and damaged or destroyed 76 shelters. Ahead of the post-monsoon cyclone season (October–November), more than 1,700 volunteers and community members participated in cyclone orientation, search-andrescue training, multi-hazard preparedness sessions and emergency drills.
SHELTER &amp; RELOCATIONS: UNHCR completed and handed over 205 Temporary Safer Shelters to families relocating from high-risk areas, while construction of additional shelters continued in Block E of Camp 4-Extension alongside shelter, WASH, site development and energy works. As of 27 September, 413 people (71 households) had relocated from high-risk areas in the camps. Meanwhile, regular shelter assistance reached 3,368 households, while 49 households received emergency support following damage by fire, heavy rain, wind and other incidents. Assistance included bamboo, rope, tarpaulin, wire and pre-cast footings provided following technical assessments and damage verification. UNHCR’s Global Distribution Tool — a biometricenabled digital system to manage distribution of humanitarian assistance — is now in use at all shelter distribution points, improving beneficiary verification, tracking, transparency and accountability.
LPG AND NFI DISTRIBUTIONS: UNHCR and partners continued distributing essential household and clean cooking items across the camps. Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) refills and pressure cookers reached 79,845 households (94% of yearly target), soap and tote bag distributions reached 81% of target households, female hygiene kits 91%, and solar lamps for single-person households 53%.
COMMUNITY-LED INITIATIVES: Nearly 2,000 community consultations involving more than 28,000 participants informed the second phase of Community-Led Initiatives in Cox’s Bazar camps. Planned projects will focus on rehabilitating community infrastructure affected during the monsoon. On Bhasan Char, all ten communityprioritized projects were completed, benefiting 12,587 people (2,641 households) through improvements including roof repairs, drainage, pipe networks, fencing and balcony construction while promoting community ownership and sustainability of the response.
NUTRITION CONCERNS: A September nutrition survey by ACF Canada, funded by UNICEF, WFP and Concern Worldwide, found worsening malnutrition among refugee children in the Cox’s Bazar camps alongside declining diet quality and diversity. Global acute malnutrition reached 14.6% — up from 13.9% in 2025 and 8.6% in 20241 . The survey also found increases in stunting and underweight children, with stunting rising to 42.6% from 41% in 2025 and 40.7% in 2024, and underweight children rising to 36.9% from 33.0% in 2025 and 29.3% in 2024. At the same time, infant and young children’s diets became less diverse and nutritious, with the proportion of young children receiving a minimum acceptable diet falling by half in two years — from 42.6% in 2024 to 21.2% in 2026. The findings underscore the need for sustained nutrition and food-security support amid funding cuts and a shift from blanket to targeted food assistance.
MENTAL HEALTH &amp; PSYCHOSOCIAL SUPPORT (MHPSS): UNHCR strengthened access to evidence-based MHPSS services through a five-day training in Teknaf for 19 community paracounsellors and one psychologist on Integrated ADAPT Therapy, enhancing support for refugees experiencing psychosocial distress. In addition, 16 para-counsellors and one psychologist completed training and supervised practice in Early Adolescent Skills for Emotions (EASE) in August, equipping them to support adolescents aged 10–15 experiencing emotional difficulties, and their caregivers. UNHCR and partners also marked World Suicide Prevention Day (10 September) through activities promoting supportive conversations, and awareness of available assistance and referral pathways.
FUNDING UPDATE: As of 30 September, the UNHCR Bangladesh operation was 46% funded, with USD 92.49 million available against an operating plan of USD 200.6 million. The hyper-prioritized 2026 Joint Response Plan, which appeals for USD 710.5 million to reach up to 1.56 million people, including refugees and Bangladeshi host communities, was 70% funded as of Q3, indicating an overall decrease in available funding compared to 2025.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-11-11) ---
Key highlights
• Q2’s surge in tickets was driven by the April rollout of WFP’s Targeting and Prioritization Exercise (TPE), which introduced needs-based food assistance and generated demand for information on food-assistance eligibility and registration updates.
• TPE generated 22,330 tickets, or 16% of the total, through joint WFP–UNHCR Coordinated Help Desks.
• The Helpline stayed open during the Eid Holiday (25–31 May): handling 1,943 tickets and more than 100 urgent protection requests.
• More than 100 partner staff across all 33 camps received training on family reunification processes and eligibility, to identify and support refugees with family members in third countries.
• Help-desk staff received CAIPP refresher training on recording and submitting cases, use of the intake form and navigation of the CAIPP dashboard.
• The overall closure rate rose to 95%, up 13% from Q2. Amid damage from monsoon weather, 98% of urgent shelter cases were closed.
