Crisis / situation: Bangladesh

--- Report 1: Bangladesh Red Crescent (2026-07-09) ---
Sources for data marked as Other
The well-marked low persists over Northwest Madhya Pradesh &amp; adjoining area. It is very likely to move north-northwestwards and recurve northeastwards thereafter. Under its influence steep pressure gradient persists over North Bay and adjoining area. The axis of monsoon trough runs through Rajasthan, the center of the wellmarked low, Bihar, West Bengal to Assam across southern part of Bangladesh. Monsoon is active over Bangladesh and strong over North Bay. According to the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), active monsoon conditions are expected to continue until at least 11 July, with heavy to very heavy rainfall forecast across Cox's Bazar and the Chattogram Division. The five-day rainfall forecast indicates that very heavy rainfall (≥88 mm/day) is expected across several sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar between 9–12 July, with Ukhiya forecast to receive the highest and most persistent rainfall. Teknaf, Maheshkhali, Kutubdia, Ramu, Cox’s Bazar Sadar, and Chakaria are also expected to experience very heavy rainfall on multiple days. Rainfall is expected to decrease significantly across all sub-districts on 13 July, reducing light to moderate levels. The prolonged period of intense rainfall during 9–12 July may increase the risk of flash flooding, waterlogging, landslides, and localized disruptions, particularly in the highly affected areas. A BMD landslide warning forecasts 200–500 mm of rainfall between 9 and 11 July, maintaining a high landslide risk in Teknaf, Ukhiya, and Cox's Bazar Sadar. The Needs Assessment Working Group (NAWG) Anticipatory Impact Analysis continues to identify Cox's Bazar as highly vulnerable to flash flooding. Continued rainfall is expected to increase the risk of flooding, landslides, infrastructure damage, access constraints, and displacement. Humanitarian partners should maintain heightened preparedness, monitor weather advisories and high-risk locations, disseminate early warning messages, support relocations where necessary, and exercise caution during field movements.
Risk Analysis
Across the 33 Camps: Between 5 and 9 July 2026, a total of 304 weather-related incidents were reported, affecting 5,863 households (28,091 people) across the 33 camps. The incident caused damage to 3,127 shelters and displaced 4,732 individuals. To date, *15 fatalities and 18 injuries have been recorded. These figures underscore the significant humanitarian impact of the ongoing monsoon conditions and highlight the need for continued monitoring, preparedness, and response efforts, particularly in areas at heightened risk of flooding, water logging, and landslides. Impact on BDRCS Priority Camps: In BDRCS Priority Camps (11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19), approximately 109 incidents (36%) with 1,896 Households or 9,241 Individuals (30%) are affected. These incidents have caused significant impacts to infrastructures inside the camp including 1,466 damaged shelters (47%), and 59 damaged WASH facilities (water points, latrines) which is 29% total. Additionally, 7 deaths (47%) have been reported within these camps due to landslide. Impact on BDRCS Priority Camps Remarks: The % reflected above are computed in reference to the total data reported per category (e.g. incident, population, shelter, etc.) across the 33 camps. Host Communities Persistent heavy rainfall has significantly exacerbated flooding across Cox's Bazar District, with Chakaria, Pekua, Matamuhuri, Maheshkhali, Ramu, and Teknaf among the worst-affected areas. Floodwaters have inundated 54 unions and 3 municipalities, affecting an estimated 80,000 people. During the past 24 hours, temporary flooding has also been reported across most parts of the district, disrupting mobility, communication, access to essential services, and daily livelihoods. To date, 6 fatalities have been reported in the host communities, including 4 deaths caused by landslides in Cox's Bazar Sadar and Chakaria, 1 death resulting from the collapse of a mud wall in Pekua Upazila, and 1 death caused by drowning in Maheshkhali. The evolving situation continues to place both refugee and host communities at heightened risk as heavy rainfall persists.

--- Report 2: Bangladesh Red Crescent (2026-07-09) ---
Sources for data marked as Other
The monsoon depression over south Jharkhand and adjoining Odisha has weakened into a well-marked low-pressure area over East Madhya Pradesh and adjoining regions and is expected to continue weakening as it moves west northwestward. However, a steep pressure gradient persists over the North Bay, while the monsoon trough extends across southern Bangladesh. Consequently, monsoon activity remains active over Bangladesh and strong over the North Bay. Local Cautionary Signal No. 3 remains in force for the seaports of Chattogram, Cox’s Bazar, Mongla, and Payra. Fishing boats and trawlers have been advised to remain close to the coast and operate with caution until further notice. Very heavy rainfall (≥88 mm/day) is forecast across Cox's Bazar District from 8 to 12 July, with the highest daily accumulation expected in Teknaf (up to 182 mm). The forecast indicates that very heavy rainfall will persist throughout the five-day period. In particular, Ukhiya, Teknaf, Ramu, Cox's Bazar Sadar, Maheshkhali, and Chakaria are expected to experience consistently very heavy rainfall, increasing the risk of flash flooding, waterlogging, landslides, and localized disruptions।. According to the latest forecast, above-normal rainfall is expected across parts of Sylhet, Mymensingh, and Chattogram divisions, with heavy upstream rainfall potentially contributing to rapid inundation and flash flooding between 5-12 July 2026. The situation is being aggravated by heavy rainfall in the adjoining upstream areas of Meghalaya, Assam, the Barak Basin and Tripura, in India. The analysis made by the Needs Assessment Working Group Bangladesh estimates that approximately 1.95 million people across 12 districts, 70 upazilas, and 585 unions potentially impacted to flood impacts (and probable landslides in some areas), including a significant number of children and older persons.Cox’s Bazar According to the flood risk analysis, Cox’s Bazar is one among the districts at the highest risk of flash flood incidents. An estimate of approximately 504,500 people across 8 upazilas and 74 unions in Cox’s Bazar could potentially be exposed to flashfoods, including 66,065 children under five, 187,476 children and adolescents (5-19 years), and 31,638 older persons. As of 1500 hours (BST) Cox’s Bazar has faced 506 mm of rainfall since 4 July 2026, and 150-380 mm rainfall has been forecasted for next 72 hours. This situation is reflecting potential risk for whole Cox’s Bazar. In the Camp settlement, where soils have already been saturated following heavy rainfalls and recent landslides, additional rainfall is likely to increase the probability of flash flooding, slope failures, and further landslides, particularly in low-lying and steep hill side areas. Humanitarian operations may face temporary access constraints due to inundated roads, damaged pathways and retaining walls, while disruptions to WASH services, shelter conditions, and humanitarian logistics may increase the vulnerability of affected populations.

Summary
BDRCS PMO Emergency Operation Centre (EOC) remains activated to coordinate the ongoing monsoon response. BDRCS Camp Focal Points and staff participated in Disaster Management Committee (DMC) meetings in camps and host communities to support coordinated response actions. Under DRM, BDRCS continued supporting camp DMUs and SMS volunteers in early warning dissemination, emergency relocation, and search and rescue through trained volunteer mobilization across 33 camps. Technical guidance was provided to Camp DMCs to strengthen emergency response, with close coordination maintained with SMS, BMD, CPP, RCP-EPR Working Group, and other sectors to identify needs and gaps. Shelter activities are ongoing, including joint verification with SMS for the remaining 86 affected households and distribution of emergency shelter kits. WASH volunteers were mobilized to assess WASH facilities, while coordination continued with WASH sector agencies in Camps 11, 18, and 19. Health teams continued field monitoring and community engagement through 10 volunteers. Follow-up support, needs identification, PFA, PSS, lay counselling, CFA, and awareness sessions are ongoing. The Mobile Medical Team remains on standby, with essential medicines, supplies, and emergency equipment available for rapid response and referral support. Livelihood volunteers and skills trainers continued sharing preparedness and safety messages with community members and monitored livelihood facilities to identify damages and provide timely support. PGI-CEA collected 74 community feedback items related to monsoon damage and needs from Camps 11, 12, 13, 15, 18, and 19, which will be referred to relevant sectors through CFRM. The BDRCS Unit Office also mobilized 200 Red Crescent Youth volunteers to disseminate early warning messages.

--- Report 3: Bangladesh Red Crescent (2026-07-08) ---
Overall Situation:
A monsoon depression persisting over south Jharkhand and adjoining Odisha, India, combined with a strong active monsoon current over the North Bay of Bengal, is bringing heavy to very heavy rainfall to Cox's Bazar, Bandarban and Chattogram districts from 6 to 9 July 2026, with cumulative rainfall forecast at 250–400 mm over this period an increase on the previous day's outlook1 . In the 24 hours to 6:00 AM on 7 July, BMD recorded 283 mm of rainfall in Chattogram, 195 mm in Kutubdia, 129 mm in Cox's Bazar and 128 mm in Bandarban, indicating that the heaviest rainfall band has shifted north into Chattogram while remaining severe across all three districts. Local Cautionary Signal No. 3 remains hoisted for the seaports of Chattogram, Cox's Bazar, Mongla and Payra and fishing boats have been advised to remain in shelter.
BMD's updated Special Warning Bulletin for Landslide (No. 05/2026, issued 7 July, 13:00 BST) has widened the high-risk classification. Teknaf, Ukhiya and Cox's Bazar Sadar upazilas in Cox's Bazar; Naikhongchhari, Lama and Bandarban Sadar upazilas in Bandarban; and Wards 7, 8, 9 and 14 of Chattogram City Corporation together with Banshkhali upazila in Chattogram are now all classified as high landslide risk with Bandarban Sadar and Banshkhali/Chattogram City Corporation upgraded from medium to high risk since the previous bulletin. Only Rangamati district remains under watch as a comparatively lower risk area1 . The warning has already translated into loss of life. In the early hours of 6 July, three separate landslides struck Rohingya refugee camps (FDMN) in Ukhiya upazila Jamtoli Camp-15, Kutupalong Camp-7 and Balukhali Camp-11 killing eight people, including five children, when hillsides collapsed onto shelters built on unstable slopes. A ninth person died in a separate landslide in Cox's Bazar municipality. Fire Service and Civil Defence personnel, the Armed Police Battalion (APBN) and Rohingya volunteers carried out overnight search-and-rescue operations3 . With rainfall intensifying and risk levels rising across all three districts, the cumulative toll has continued to climb: as of 7 July, 10 fatalities and 88 injuries have been recorded across 170 weather-related incidents since 5 July, and the potential for further casualties and damage remains high over the coming days as forcasted.

However, a steep pressure gradient persists over the North Bay, while the monsoon trough extends across southern Bangladesh. Conditions are likely to improve further on 10–11 July, although Teknaf and Ramu may continue to face elevated risks of localized flooding, waterlogging, and landslides. During the 24 hours ending at 1500 hours BST on 7 July, Cox’s Bazar recorded 78 mm of rainfall (Bangladesh Meteorological Department-BMD Cox’s Bazar Office), recorded cumulative rainfall 207 mm; reflecting the ongoing severity of the monsoon conditions. Given saturated soil conditions and continued heavy rainfall forecasts, the risk remains high for flash flooding in low-lying areas, waterlogging in urban and camp settings, landslides on vulnerable slopes particularly in Ramu, Ukhiya and Teknaf—damage to roads and access routes, and disruptions to WASH services and humanitarian operations
