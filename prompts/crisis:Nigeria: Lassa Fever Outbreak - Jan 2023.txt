Major issues and challenges include
Senegal: Diphtheria has re-emerged as a significant public health concern in Senegal after more than two decades without reported cases. Since the first confirmed case in July, transmission has spread across multiple regions and health districts, with the highest burden reported in Kédougou, Diourbel, Dakar,
Louga and Thiès. Cases in densely populated Dakar and Touba, a major religious centre with substantial population movement, increase the risk of further spread. Transmission appears to be driven by immunity gaps, with cases among schoolaged children, adolescents and adults suggesting waning or missed booster protection, while unvaccinated and children with unknown vaccination status indicate gaps in routine immunization and documentation. The high case fatality, particularly among unvaccinated young children, raises concerns about delayed care-seeking, referral, access to diphtheria antitoxin and quality clinical care.
Limited clinical familiarity may further delay recognition and treatment.

--- Report 2: Govt. Nigeria (2026-10-15) ---
Highlights
• In week 40, the number of new confirmed cases decreased from 13 reported in epi week 39 of 2026 to 3. These were reported in Bauchi and Benue States (Table 3).
• Cumulatively, 268 deaths have been reported with a Case Fatality Rate (CFR) of 23.6% which is higher than the CFR for the same period in 2025 (18.6%).
• In total for 2026, 24 States have recorded at least one confirmed case across 119 Local Government Areas (Figures 2 and 3).
• Eighty-eight percent (88%) of all confirmed Lassa fever cases were reported from 5 states (Ondo, Bauchi, Taraba, Edo and Benue) while twelve percent (12%) were reported from 19 states with confirmed Lassa fever cases. Of the 88% confirmed cases, Ondo reported 36%,
Bauchi 23%, Taraba 12%, Edo 11% and Benue 6%.
• The predominant age group affected is 21-30 years (Range: 1 to 93 years, Median Age: 30 years). The male-to-female ratio for confirmed cases is 1:0.9 (Figure 4).
• The number of suspected and confirmed cases increased compared to that reported for the same period in 2025.
• No new healthcare worker was affected in the reporting week 40.
• National Lassa fever multisectoral multi-partner Technical Working Group (TWG) continues supporting the coordination of activities at all levels.
