Crisis / situation: Mali

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-07-22) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• Security incidents and access constraints continue
to limit humanitarian access in Kidal, Gao, Mopti,
and Timbuktu.
• Despite access constraints, WFP continues
operations through adapted responses and
enhanced preparedness.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Mali’s humanitarian environment remains highly
challenging. Insecurity, access constraints, climate
shocks, rising prices, supply chain disruptions, and
population displacement continue to undermine
livelihoods, strain local markets, and limit
humanitarian assistance, reinforcing the need for
sustained support.
• WFP delivers food and nutrition assistance
through in-kind and cash-based transfers, based
on market conditions and access conditions.
UNHAS supports access to hard‑to-reach areas.
• WFP also supports national institutions in analysis,
planning, coordination, and implementation to
strengthen national capacity to respond to shocks
and help sustain improvements in food security
and nutrition over time.
• With needs increasing as the lean season
progresses, WFP is scaling up assistance and
preparedness, with plans to reach over 180,000
additional vulnerable people on top of the 350,000
initially targeted.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-07-20) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
05-06-2026
What happened, where and when?
On the night of April 24 to 25, 2026, Mali was confronted with a series of coordinated armed attacks on a scale not seen since the 2012 rebellion. These attacks were carried out simultaneously by the Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims (JNIM) and the Front for the Liberation of Azawad (FLA) against several strategic localities in the country.
The attacks mainly targeted military positions and strategic infrastructure. They have also had a direct impact on nearby residential areas, resulting in loss of life, destruction of property and a widespread climate of fear and uncertainty. Population movements have been disrupted, economic activities temporarily slowed down and access to some essential services made more difficult, thus increasing the vulnerability of communities already affected by years of crisis.
In addition, the security situation continues to deteriorate, with numerous reports of blockades of towns and villages by armed groups, burning of public transport vehicles on main roads, abductions of people and destruction of agricultural crops. At the same time, weather forecasts point to a high risk of flooding in several areas of the country, which could further increase humanitarian needs.
The attacks affected several regions of Mali, including:
- District of Bamako;
- Koulikoro region, in particular the city of Kati;
- Mopti region, in particular Sévaré and Bandiagara;
- Gao region;
- Kidal Region;
- as well as certain areas of central Mali.
The attacks took place on the night of April 24 to 25, 2026. However, the situation remains ongoing and highly volatile.
These events are part of a context of continuous deterioration of the security situation observed since 2024, marked in particular by the attack on Bamako airport in September 2024 and by the prolonged disruptions in fuel supply since September 2025.
Following the attacks of 25 April 2026, the Mali Red Cross (RCM), the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC) and the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) conducted a joint analysis of the security context and activated the Movement's coordination mechanisms within the framework of the Seville Agreement 2.0. This analysis indicates a high risk of further deterioration of the situation, with further displacement, increased humanitarian needs and increased difficulties in accessing essential services.
The current context is also marked by the implementation of restrictive measures recently adopted by the authorities, in particular the ban on the circulation and marketing of certain categories of motorcycles in several areas affected by insecurity through an interministerial order dated June 2, 2026. Although these measures are motivated by security imperatives, they have a significant impact on people's livelihoods, as motorcycles are the main means of transporting people, goods and basic services in many localities. Mobility restrictions limit access to markets, health facilities, schools and economic opportunities, leading to a decrease in the incomes of households already weakened by insecurity.
At the same time, restrictions on access to certain forest areas considered sensitive further reduce the livelihoods of communities. Many households depend on forest resources for firewood collection, charcoal production, non-timber forest product gathering, grazing and certain income-generating activities. Limiting these activities increases the economic vulnerability of populations, increases the risk of food insecurity and reduces households' coping mechanisms in the face of successive shocks.
In this context, an inter-headquarters meeting of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement was held on 5 June 2026 between the CRM, the IFRC and the ICRC. This meeting provided an opportunity to carry out an up-to-date joint analysis of the security, humanitarian and operational context as well as to assess the implications of recent developments on the affected populations and on the Movement's response capacities.
The conclusions of this meeting highlighted a continued deterioration of the context, characterized by the geographical spread of insecurity, the multiplication of restrictions on movement, the weakening of household livelihoods, the increased risks of population displacement and the gradual deterioration of access to essential services. The Movement partners also stressed the need to strengthen operational preparedness and coordination mechanisms in order to respond effectively to a possible escalation of the crisis.
The launch of this operation is based on the recommendations and analyses resulting from this inter-headquarters coordination meeting of the Movement held on 5 June 2026, organized within the framework of the coordination mechanisms provided for in the Seville Agreement 2.0. The joint analysis concludes that there is a high risk of further deterioration of the situation in the coming months, with a likely increase in humanitarian needs, additional population displacement and increasing access constraints for humanitarian actors. This is consistent with the findings of the UNHCR assessment of 2 June 2026.
The forward-looking scenarios developed by the Movement anticipate a protracted conflict in the short and medium term, marked by progressive fragmentation of territory, a reduction in the effective presence of the State in certain areas and increased pressure on civilian populations. In this context, the Mali Red Cross is currently operating under Code Orange, with its Emergency Operations Centre(EOC) activated and its Emergency Brigades (BU) mobilised to ensure a rapid response to emerging needs and to strengthen preparedness in the face of a possible deterioration of the situation.
