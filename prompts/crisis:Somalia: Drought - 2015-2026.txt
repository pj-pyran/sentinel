Crisis / situation: Somalia: Drought - 2015-2026

--- Report 1: FAO (2026-11-26) ---
Review Summary:

Very dry week across the country. During 17–23 November, rain was reported at only four stations: Banderbeyla (28.3 mm in 2 rainy days), Mogadishu (9.7 mm), Qansax Dheere (1.3 mm) and Cadale (0.8 mm). Satellite estimates (IMERG, 18–24 November) indicate 8–13 mm over parts of Lower Juba and less than 1 mm over the river catchments in Ethiopia.
Seasonal totals are almost unchanged after the dry week. By 23 November, 37 stations, 33 of them with a Deyr normal above 15 mm, had already received their full Deyr normal, including Baidoa (405.5 mm, 176% of the Deyr normal), Galkacyo (253.5 mm, 390%) and Banderbeyla (126.7 mm, 316%), which recorded the highest weekly total. Mogadishu rose to 35% of its Deyr normal, while Borama (16%), Burao (21%) and Luuq (25%) remain far below normal.
River levels on both rivers are falling or easing and remain well below flood-risk levels. Belet Weyne fell from 3.76 m on 18 November to 3.00 m today, 3.50 m below its moderate flood-risk level. Bulo Burti (2.16 m), Dollow (2.32 m) and Luuq (2.34 m) are also falling, and Jowhar (3.30 m) is easing after reaching 3.50 m on 23 November.
Flood water in Jowhar district continues to recede. Satellite images show about 46 km² of flood water in Jowhar district down from about 55 km² last week. Radio Ergo callers (12–18 November) reported river flooding on farms in Jowhar, while many callers in Galgaduud, Bay, the Juba valley and Gedo welcomed the rains

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-11-24) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Flood risk remains elevated across Somalia, with further humanitarian impacts expected through year-end. Deyr rainfall triggered flash floods, particularly in Galkacyo, Abudwaq and Baidoa, affecting more than 175,000 people and displacing over 51,000 in October. While Shabelle and Juba River levels have receded, above-average rainfall forecasts and potential El Niño conditions sustain risks through December. An estimated 874,000 people are projected to be displaced between October and December, largely due to flooding.
• In Luuq, some of the 58,500 people displaced by recent conflict are returning following the 29 October peace agreement, however, food insecurity, acute malnutrition and Juba River flood risks persist. Existing WFP programmes continue to be implemented with plans to provide additional cash assistance from December. In Baidoa, renewed violence has affected around 5,600 people amid severe food insecurity and critical acute malnutrition.
• According to WFP’s October market analysis, the cost of living remains high, with the national food and non-food basket reaching USD 210 in October, up 4.2 percent yearon-year. Market pressures persisted in Southwest State due to conflict-related disruptions and reduced Gu crop production, while diesel prices remained 26 percent above October 2025 levels, increasing food, water, transport and humanitarian operating costs.
