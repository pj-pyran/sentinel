Crisis / situation: Somalia: Drought - 2015-2026

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-09-29) ---

KEY HIGHLIGHTS

One-third of Somalia’s population (5.4 million people) is acutely food insecure, including nearly 1.5 million facing emergency-level hunger (IPC4) from October to December 2026.
Malnutrition remains alarmingly high, with nearly 2 million children projected to suffer acute malnutrition in 2026, including 433,000 children facing severe acute malnutrition and requiring urgent treatment.
Nearly 40 percent of children under five in Burhakaba are acutely malnourished, and the district faces a risk of famine. During a recent visit, WFP Acting Executive Director Carl Skau witnessed firsthand the scale of humanitarian needs, reinforcing the urgency of sustained nutrition and food assistance.
Funding shortfalls are sharply reducing WFP’s reach, amid a worsening humanitarian situation driven by drought and raising climate risks. From reaching over 4 million people per month during the 2022–2023 drought response, current resources allow WFP to support less than five percent of people facing emergency levels of hunger from October, with nutrition treatment running out of resources after September.

--- Report 2: NRC (2026-09-27) ---
Highlights


Somalia shifted towards a compound emergency driven by drought, hunger, conflict, displacement and imminent El-Nino flood risk.


PSMN recorded 774,923 displacements between January and 11 September, including 17,800 in August—42% caused by conflict and 36% by drought. Children represented 71% of those displaced and women and children nearly 87%.


About 6.5 million people faced acute food insecurity, while 1.9 million children were projected to suffer acute malnutrition, including nearly 500,000 severe cases. The national basket cost USD 213, up 8% annually, while wages fell 28% and livestock prices 16%.


Protection risks intensified, including fighting in Baidoa and the eviction of 310 households—1,860 people—from 10 Kismayo sites. Only 26% of specialized protection services remained operational; 54 facilities closed or scaled down and 295 staff were laid off.


Only 25% of health requirements were funded, leaving more than 380 facilities operating at reduced capacity. With risk of AWD/cholera outbreak due to Elnino projected.


NRC is implementing El Niño Anticipatory Action (AA) and preparedness initiatives through community-led Group Cash Transfers (GCTs), with 41 communities across five districts—Kismayo, Jowhar, Galkacyo, Dhusamareeb and Adado—submitting GCT applications totaling USD 258,000 following community engagement and contingency planning. Proposed actions reflect locally identified priorities, including drainage and flood-channel clearing, reinforcement of flood protection structures, rehabilitation of access routes, protection of communal infrastructure and preparation of evacuation areas, linked to localized early warning and contingency planning.
