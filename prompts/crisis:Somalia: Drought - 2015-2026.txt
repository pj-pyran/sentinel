Crisis / situation: Somalia: Drought - 2015-2026

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-10-06) ---
KEY FINDINGS

The national total food and non-food basket costs US$211, up 6.4 percent year-on-year, and has held above $200 since October 2025. Prices eased 1.3 percent month-on-month, led by sorghum and cowpeas as guharvest supplies reached markets.
The southern guharvest was the largest since 2018, an estimated 32 percent above average, while the northwest gu/karancrop is estimated 76 percent below average [IPC]. Sorghum prices fell 4 percent on average (Puntland −10.4%) as cereal reached deficit areas [FEWSNET], butremains 30 to 35 percent above last year in Banadir, Hirshabelleand Southwest.
Terms of trade improved month-on-month but remain well below last year: a goat now buys 96 kg of sorghum, up from 89 kg in August but below 109 kg a year ago (−11%), and a day's wage 7.7 kg against 10.1 kg (−24%). Goat prices rose 5.6 percent, led by Somaliland (+17%), Puntland (+14%, recovering from July/August lows) and Southwest (+12%, where grazing has improved [FEWSNET]); wages were flat, 10.6 percent below last year.
Diesel eased 2.3 percent to US$1.17 per litrebut remains 19.4 percent higher than a year agoand 26 percent above pre-Middle East crisis levels, keeping transport and water-trucking costs high, with renewed piracy expected to add pressure [FEWSNET]. In Mogadishu, cooking gas cylinders that sold for around US$16 reportedly reached up to US$30 in parts of the city in early September (+88 percent), which traders link to supply shortages.

--- Report 2: OCHA (2026-10-05) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

Hunger and malnutrition are persisting in Somalia, driven by climatic shocks, conflict, disease outbreaks, displacement and reduced assistance, according to new findings.
The analysis projects that 5.4 million people will face crisis levels of hunger or worse in October to December, including 1.5 million facing emergency levels of hunger or worse.
An estimated 1.92 million under-five children will require treatment for acute malnutrition between July 2026 and June 2027, including 433,000 with severe acute malnutrition.
Humanitarian partners, authorities and local actors are accelerating preparedness measures as forecasts indicate significant flooding in October to December.
An estimated 3.4 million people could be exposed to flood risks during October to December, especially in Hiraan, Middle Juba and Lower Juba regions.

--- Report 3: FAO (2026-10-05) ---
Forecast Highlight:
• Moderate to locally heavy rainfall is forecast over parts of Somaliland and Hiraan, with significant rainfall also expected over the Ethiopian headwaters of the Shabelle and Juba, sustaining rising river levels.
• Flood risk remains low this week, with all monitored stations expected to remain below moderate flood-risk levels; however, risk is expected to increase from mid-October as the MJO becomes more favourable for East African rainfall within an already wetter-than-normal Deyr outlook.
• Central Somalia will receive mostly patchy rainfall, while much of the Juba valley and interior Lower Shabelle will remain largely dry, providing little immediate drought relief.
• Very high daytime temperatures of 35–40°C will persist across much of the southern and central lowlands, further increasing heat and water stress in dry areas.
