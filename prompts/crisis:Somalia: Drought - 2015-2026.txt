Crisis / situation: Somalia: Drought - 2015-2026

--- Report 1: NRC (2026-10-29) ---
HIGHLIGHTS

Conflict, drought, food insecurity and flood risks intensified, with over 774,923 people displaced since January and 14,832 newly displaced in Baidoa.
Fighting in Baidoa, electoral disputes in Galmudug and Hirshabelle, non-state armed actors operations and AUSSOM/UNSOS uncertainty threatened humanitarian access. Yemen’s conflict increased regional displacement and potential arrivals in Somalia.
Gulf maritime disruptions increased import and fuel pressures. Maize and sorghum prices remained 88% and 68% above August 2025, respectively, further weakening household purchasing power.
The 2026 HRNP remained approximately 31% funded, limiting essential services. Priorities include drought assistance, anticipatory action, Group Cash Transfers, evacuation preparedness and integrated first-line response.
NRC reached 29,909 people in September, bringing its total reach since January 2026 to 666,340 people, including 412,023 through first-line emergency response. NRC has disbursed Group Cash Transfers to 45 communities in Kismayo, Jowhar, South Galkacyo and Adado for locally led El Niño preparedness, in coordination with SoDMA and state-level MoHADM. In response to conflict-induced displacement in Baidoa, NRC is providing MPCA, NFIs and protection monitoring, while promoting the Single Registration Form (SRF) and Referral Management System (RMS) to strengthen integrated response and referrals.

--- Report 2: FAO (2026-10-29) ---
Review Summary:
• Lighter rains in the south, moderate falls in the north. During 20–26 October, Fiqi Ayub recorded 103.0 mm, Caynabo 93.5 mm, Dararweyne 84.0 mm, Xaafuun 72.4 mm and Bandarbeyla 59.0 mm, while southern stations received 41 mm or less. Satellite estimates (IMERG, 21–27 October) indicate more than 40 mm over Diinsoor, Saakow and Afmadow districts, where no rain gauges reported.
• By 26 October, 42 stations with a long-term record had reached or exceeded their October normal, including Baidoa (381.0 mm, 282%), Mataban (333.4 mm, 394%), Galkacyo (253.5 mm, 532%) and Bandarbeyla (62.2 mm, 397%). Burao (26%), Borama (27%) and Qardho (16%) remain far below normal.
• Belet Weyne is above its moderate flood-risk level. Belet Weyne rose above 6.50 m on 26 October and stood at 6.70 m today, with the daily rise slowing to 0.06 m. Deefow peaked at 6.96 m on 27 October. Bulo Burti rose 0.56 m to 5.56 m, Jowhar has stayed at about 4.28 m since 24 October, and the Juba has started to rise again at Dollow and Luuq.
• Radio Ergo callers (15–21 October) reported floods and displacement in Jowhar, Cadale, Baidoa and Bardheere and flooded homes in Dabare and Cabudwaaq (Galgaduud). Drought continued around Tala-bur (Togdheer), Garasweyne (Bakool), Busley (Lower Shabelle) and Boholgaras (Gedo).
