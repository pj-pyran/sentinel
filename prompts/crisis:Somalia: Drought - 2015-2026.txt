Crisis / situation: Somalia: Drought - 2015-2026

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-11-12) ---
OPERATIONAL CONTEXT &amp; HIGHLIGHTS
Somalia’s humanitarian situation remained shaped by drought, conflict and insecurity, with over 804,000 internal displacement movements recorded by PSMN between January and September 2026. In September, political tensions over the electoral timetable and renewed violence in Baidoa and surrounding areas heightened protection risks and constrained humanitarian access and programme delivery. Regional instability, including conflict in Ethiopia and Yemen, continued to trigger limited refugee movements into Somalia, adding to preparedness and response needs. UNHCR declared a Level 1 Emergency for Djibouti, Somalia and Yemen in response to escalating conflict and humanitarian conditions in Yemen, strengthening preparedness for potential arrivals, including reception capacity in Berbera and Bossaso. By September, UNHCR, in coordination with NCRI, had facilitated the voluntary return of 470 Somali refugees from Yemen to Mogadishu through three Assisted Spontaneous Return (ASR) movements, supporting safe, dignified and sustainable return and reintegration. On 23 April, UNHCR, the Federal Government of Somalia and the African Development Bank launched the USD 20.5 million Strengthening Urban Resilience of Displaced and Host Communities in Dollow (SURDHT) project. Working with NCRDS and participating UN agencies, the project aims to advance durable solutions and resilience for displaced and host communities.

--- Report 2: FAO (2026-11-12) ---
Review Summary:

Little rain in the south, isolated falls in the north. During 3–9 November, rainfall decreased sharply over the south and central areas, where Awdheegle recorded 48.1 mm and Wanle Weyne 15.5 mm and most other stations recorded no rain. In the north, Las Dacawo recorded 73.5 mm, Bargaal 57.1 mm and Xaafuun 44.8 mm. Satellite estimates (IMERG, 4–11 November) indicate 10–22 mm over Badhaadhe, Saakow and Afmadow districts.
By 9 November, 30 stations had already received their full Deyr normal, including Baidoa (405.5 mm, 176% of the Deyr normal), Mataban (333.4 mm, 275%), Galkacyo (253.5 mm, 390%) and Awdheegle (176.8 mm, 113%), which passed its normal this week. Burao (21%), Borama (16%) and Qardho (39%) remain far below normal.
River levels are falling along both rivers and are well below flood-risk levels. Belet Weyne fell from 4.76 m on 4 November to 3.28 m today, 3.22 m below its moderate flood-risk level and below its long-term mean. Jowhar (3.88 m), Dollow (2.44 m) and Luuq (2.46 m) are also falling.
Flooding continues in some parts of Jowhar district and was reported in Bay. Satellite images show about 87 km² of flood water in Jowhar district and flood water in Qansax Dheere district. Radio Ergo callers (29 October–4 November) reported floods in Baidoa, near Buur Hakaba and at Gobanle in Lower Shabelle. Unverified field reports indicate flooding at Balcad; these have not been confirmed and the latest satellite pass over Balcad was on 5 November.
