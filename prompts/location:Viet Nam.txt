Crisis / situation: Viet Nam

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-07-20) ---
Description
Within minutes on the morning of 17 July, flash floods carrying large volumes of water, mud and debris swept across National Highway 32, inundating homes and posing an immediate threat to lives and property. The floods claimed five lives, while three people remain missing beneath mud and debris. Eight people were injured, and dozens of households were evacuated from high-risk areas as an emergency precaution.
The floods affected 53 houses, including 20 houses that were completely washed away. Approximately 300 hectares of rice fields, crops and aquaculture areas were buried or swept away. Several transport routes, irrigation facilities, schools and power systems were also damaged.
Although floodwaters have now receded, widespread destruction remains, with many affected households continuing to face the impacts of the disaster.
Immediately following the disaster, local authorities activated emergency response measures. Provincial and commune-level Civil Defence Command Committees were deployed to affected areas to coordinate search and rescue operations, restrict access to dangerous roads, and evacuate people from areas at risk of landslides. The province mobilised nearly 1,000 personnel, along with multiple vehicles and equipment, to support disaster response and recovery efforts.
The flash floods were triggered by persistent heavy to very heavy rainfall across the northern mountainous region, where cumulative rainfall over the past three days generally ranged from 150–300 mm. The highest accumulations were recorded at Chế Tạo (Lào Cai) with 474 mm, Nậm Păm (Sơn La) with 350 mm, Nà Tấu (Điện Biên) with 277 mm, and Pa Thắng (Lai Châu) with 250 mm.
Further rainfall is expected to hamper response and recovery efforts. On 20 July, moderate to heavy rain and thunderstorms are forecast across the northern mountainous region and Quảng Ninh Province, with widespread rainfall of 20–50 mm and isolated areas expected to receive more than 100 mm. Short-duration intense rainfall exceeding 70 mm within three hours is possible. Other parts of northern Viet Nam are forecast to experience scattered showers and thunderstorms, with rainfall of 10–20 mm and isolated areas receiving more than 50 mm.
According to reports from Lào Cai, Lai Châu, Điện Biên and Sơn La provinces, heavy rainfall, floods, inundation, flash floods and landslides from 15–19 July 2026 caused the following impacts:
Human impact: Five people were killed, three remain missing, and 10 people were injured, all reported in Lai Châu Province.
Housing: A total of 36 houses collapsed (Lai Châu: 24; Lào Cai: 1; Sơn La: 11); 391 houses were damaged (Lai Châu: 35; Lào Cai: 305; Điện Biên: 27; Sơn La: 24); 140 houses required emergency relocation (Lào Cai: 71; Lai Châu: 26; Điện Biên: 3; Sơn La: 40); and 194 houses were affected by flooding and landslides (Lai Châu: 51; Lào Cai: 65; Điện Biên: 20; Sơn La: 58).
Agriculture: A total of 707 hectares of rice and crops were affected (Lào Cai: 76.45 ha; Lai Châu: 409.51 ha; Điện Biên: 27.24 ha; Sơn La: 193.80 ha).
Livestock: Approximately 250 livestock and poultry were killed or washed away.
Transport infrastructure: Landslides affected multiple sections of roads, including national highways (QL4H, QL12, QL32 and QL279) and provincial roads in Lai Châu (ĐT127, ĐT128, ĐT129, ĐT133, ĐT133B, ĐT134, ĐT135, ĐT138 and ĐT138C); QL279, QL32 and ĐT175B in Lào Cai; 32 locations along ĐT109 in Sơn La; and QL12, QL279 and ĐT143 in Điện Biên, as well as various inter-village and inter-commune roads.
As of the latest update, traffic remains disrupted at Km139+300 on National Highway 279. Vehicles are currently being diverted through the new bypass section of QL279 in Lào Cai.
The total estimated economic loss is approximately VND 362.46 billion (Lai Châu: VND 190 billion; Lào Cai: VND 45.16 billion; Sơn La: VND 125 billion; Điện Biên: VND 2.3 billion).

CTP/Basic needs
Summary
The Lai Châu Red Cross Chapter, in coordination with local authorities and the Vietnam Fatherland Front, provided initial financial assistance to affected families, including VND 15 million (CHF 470) per deceased person and VND 1 million (CHF 31) per injured person. To date, eight injured people have received support, with a total assistance value of VND 8 million (CHF 250). The Red Cross continues to monitor the situation and coordinate further support based on identified needs.
Actions taken by others
In addition to search and rescue efforts for missing persons, response forces have been supporting local authorities in evacuating people and their belongings from hazardous areas, setting up temporary shelters, transporting essential supplies, and assisting affected communities to stabilise their living conditions amid the ongoing complex flood situation. On the morning of 19 July, a delegation from the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment visited and inspected areas affected by the recent floods in Mường Than. The delegation met with affected communities and response personnel involved in disaster recovery efforts. Deputy Minister acknowledged the proactive efforts of local authorities and response forces in mobilising resources for search, rescue and relief operations. He also requested local authorities to continue prioritising support for families who lost loved ones, accelerate the search for missing persons, ensure affected households have access to basic living conditions, and urgently restore damaged transport infrastructure, electricity and water supply. Authorities were also requested to conduct thorough assessments of affected areas and develop relocation plans for households whose homes were destroyed.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-07-20) ---
Description
Heavy rainfall from 15–17 July triggered flash floods and landslides across Lai Chau Province, with Muong Than Commune being the worst affected. In response to the severity of the disaster, the Lai Chau Provincial People's Committee declared a state of disaster emergency for Muong Than on 17 July (Decision No. 1501/QĐ-UBND).
Initial reports indicate that flash floods destroyed and buried houses in several villages, including Doi 9, Doi 11, Sap Ngua, Che Bo and Chit. National Highways 32 and 279, together with several local roads, have been cut off by landslides, significantly disrupting access and emergency response. Casualty figures continue to be updated as search and rescue operations progress. As of the morning of 18 July, authorities reported at least four fatalities, four people missing and seven injured, while approximately 80 households (358 people) have been evacuated from high-risk areas.
According to the initial rapid reports from Lao Cai, Lai Chau, Dien Bien and Son La provinces, heavy rainfall, flash floods and landslides occurring between 15 and 17 July 2026 caused the following impacts:

Human impact: Four person was killed, four people remain missing/unaccounted for, and seven people were injured (all in Lai Chau Province).
Housing: A total of 20 houses were destroyed (17 in Lai Chau and 3 in Son La); 113 houses were damaged (88 in Lao Cai, 2 in Lai Chau, 13 in Dien Bien and 10 in Son La); 42 households were urgently evacuated (26 in Lai Chau and 16 in Son La); and 46 houses were affected by flooding and landslides (29 in Lai Chau and 17 in Dien Bien).
Agriculture: Approximately 238.82 hectares of rice and other crops were damaged, including 65.51 ha in Lao Cai, 101.44 ha in Lai Chau, 31.67 ha in Dien Bien, and 40.2 ha in Son La.
Livestock: 25 livestock animals and 45 poultry were lost.
Transport: Multiple landslides caused significant disruption to transportation. In Lai Chau, several sections of National Highways 4H, 12, 32 and 279, as well as Provincial Roads 127, 128, 129, 133, 133B, 134 and 135, were blocked. In Son La, 32 landslide sites were recorded along Provincial Road 109. In Dien Bien, disruptions occurred on National Highways 12 and 279 and Provincial Road 143, while numerous commune and village roads across the affected provinces were also blocked.
Power supply: In Lai Chau Province, approximately 1,373 electricity customers remained without power.

Total estimated losses to housing, crops, vegetation, roads and power lines amount to more than VNĐ120 billion (US$4.6 million).

The Viet Nam Red Cross (VNRC) has mobilized its provincial chapter, district branches and volunteers to support the ongoing emergency response in Lai Chau Province. Actions taken by others
Immediately after the disaster, local forces in Mường Than Commune mobilised for rescue operations. Leadership of the Lai Châu Provincial People's Committee formed a working delegation to go directly to the scene and take command of search and rescue efforts. Provincial police and military units were deployed with equipment and support vehicles to focus on disaster relief, search and rescue operations. Lai Châu Provincial Police established a forward command post at Tân Uyên Commune Police station, under the direct command of Colonel Khoàng Văn Thương, Deputy Director of the Provincial Police, to co-ordinate rescue, relief and disaster recovery operations. The Chairman of the Lai Châu Provincial People's Committee issued an urgent directive on July 17, instructing departments, agencies, armed forces and local People's Committees to focus on responding to and recovering from the floods, flash floods and landslides in accordance with the &quot;four on-the-spot&quot; principle, with no room for passivity or unpreparedness. Emergency declarations have been issued for Mường Than Commune and for a landslide site that has severed National Highway 12 within Lê Lợi Commune. Rescue operations in Mường Than Commune are proceeding with urgency. Provincial People's Committee chairman travelled to the scene to inspect and direct recovery efforts and oversee disaster response measures. Provincial and local forces are deploying personnel and equipment to search and rescue operations and disaster relief work, with the aim of restoring stability to residents' lives as quickly as possible.
