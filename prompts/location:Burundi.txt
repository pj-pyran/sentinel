Crisis / situation: Burundi

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-10-28) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
21-05-2026
What happened, where and when?
On 15 May 2026, the Government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) officially declared an outbreak of Ebola Virus Disease (BVD) caused by the Bundibugyo strain in Ituri Province, in the northeastern part of the country. On the same day, the Republic of Uganda also declared an outbreak of the same Ebola strain, with the epicenter located in Ituri. In response to the evolving situation and the cross-border implications of the outbreak, the World Health Organization (WHO) subsequently recognized the event as a major public health concern due to its spread in border areas and the high risk of regional transmission.
The Bundibugyo strain currently has no approved vaccine, increasing concerns among health authorities and humanitarian partners. Ebola Virus Disease is a severe and often fatal illness transmitted through direct contact with infected bodily fluids, contaminated materials, or infected animals. The disease can cause large outbreaks with significant mortality if not rapidly contained.
As of the date the emergency plan of action was written (May 2026), the epidemiological situation is still critical.
As of 20 October 2026, no confirmed cases of Ebola virus disease have been reported in Burundi, according to the information provided. On 16 October 2026, the Ministry of Public Health refuted reports of a suspected case at Prince Régent Charles Hospital in Bujumbura. The individual tested negative for Ebola and other viral haemorrhagic fevers.
However, the regional epidemiological situation remains a concern. The Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), the epicentre of the ongoing outbreak involving the Bundibugyo virus, continues to report cases and deaths. According to the figures cited in the available information, the World Health Organization (WHO) reported 8,728 confirmed cases and 4,205 deaths as of 6 October 2026. The risk of cross-border transmission is heightened by reported cases in neighbouring countries. In Kenya, an imported case linked to the DRC was confirmed in early October, and the patient died on 5 October. Contact tracing and follow-up measures were initiated, with subsequent reports indicating additional positive cases among individuals identified through contact investigations. In Uganda, authorities reported that no active cases remained following the discharge of the last patient on 16 July 2026.
Burundi is considered a high-risk country due to its geographical proximity to the affected areas and its strong social and economic links with both the DRC and Uganda. Particular concern exists along major travel and trade corridors connecting Burundi to affected areas, including the Bujumbura–Goma, Bujumbura–Kivu, Bujumbura-Nairobi, and Bujumbura–Entebbe routes, through both land and air transport. The provinces bordering the DRC, as well as major entry points and urban centers receiving travelers, are considered areas at heightened risk of disease importation.
Recognizing the threat posed by the outbreak, the Government of Burundi, in collaboration with health partners including the Burundi Red Cross Society and the IFRC, finalized and validated a National Ebola Preparedness and Response Plan on 22 May 2026. Preparedness efforts remain ongoing as the outbreak continues to evolve in neighboring countries and the risk of importation into Burundi remains high.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-10-26) ---
WFP requires USD 60.6 million from October 2026 to March 2027. Although refugee assistance is secured through December 2026, USD 40 million is needed from January 2027 to restore full food rations for nearly 125,000 refugees through September 2027.
El Niño threatens more than one million people: A strong El Niño event is expected to increase food insecurity, flooding, landslides, crop losses, disease outbreaks, and market disruptions during the high-risk October–January period. WFP requires USD 26 million over the next nine months, USD 6.3 million for anticipatory action and USD 19.7 million for emergency response.
In September 2026, WFP assisted 199,786 people, including 118,874 Congolese refugees, while distributing 149 metric tons of food and transferring USD 2.2 million in cash. Greater reliance on cash-based transfers and livelihoods and social protection interventions enabled comparable beneficiary coverage with substantially lower food tonnage than in August.
