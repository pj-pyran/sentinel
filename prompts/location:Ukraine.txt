Crisis / situation: Ukraine

--- Report 1: Health Cluster (2026-08-07) ---
I. Rationale
Now in the fifth year of the full-scale war, the humanitarian
health response in Ukraine has evolved considerably, and
so have the expectations around collective reporting,
accountability, and evidence-based coordination. The
2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP)
introduced an issue-based approach around four strategic
priorities, new geographic targeting, and a joint reporting
platform managed by UN OCHA. Health partners must
now report against these four priorities while maintaining
focus on the most severely affected areas (severity levels
4 and 5).
Over five years, variations in how partners collect data, count
beneficiaries, and calculate results have accumulated,
making it harder for the Cluster to reflect the response
accurately. This prompted the Health Cluster to convene
75 partner representatives and reporting and MEAL
specialists for a one-day hybrid workshop to strengthen
shared understanding of reporting expectations, logic, and
beneficiary calculation.
The aim was not to impose uniformity, but to build enough
common ground for the collective humanitarian health
response to be reported coherently and credibly - to the
humanitarian community, donors, and the people the
response seeks to serve. As of June 2026, the Health
Cluster counts 154 partners delivering health activities
across priority war-affected oblasts. The Cluster looks
forward to continuing to guide these partners toward
alignment as part of this process.
Objectives
General Objective: To strengthen the capacity of Health
Cluster partners to collect, calculate, and report data in
line with HNRP requirements and Health Cluster reporting
standards — improving the coordination and visibility of
health interventions in Ukraine.
Specific Objectives:
1. Explain the rationale for accurate reporting and
alignment with Cluster requirements, alongside
bilateral donor interests and the funding partners
receive.
2. Identify and discuss the challenges partners face in
the reporting process in Ukraine.
3. Guide partners through an organization-specific
reflection on designing a counting method that fits
their reporting needs across emergency response and
early recovery.
4. Raise awareness of data sensitivity and the responsible
management of health-related data.
5. Surface the tensions in reporting under the OCHA-led
framework — regional targets versus the four strategic
priorities of the response.
6. Build a clear understanding of how reporting quality
shapes the Cluster’s visibility to donors, the wider
humanitarian community, and decision-makers
through its external products.

Key trends

Ukraine has been attacking warehouses of Russia’s largest online retailer across at least nine regions in Russia and in occupied Crimea since 17 July. While primarily a civilian store, the retailer sells dual-use products, including unarmed drones, drone payload-release components, and military clothing. Ukraine is targeting these warehouses in an attempt to affect Russia’s military logistics, but also to generally further worsen Russia’s economy. It is also likely in retaliation against Russia's long history of strikes on the Ukrainian postal service and shopping centers.
Russia launched a massive wave of missile strikes targeting Kyiv city on 19 July. It deployed 41 ballistic and Zircon hypersonic missiles in the attack, the largest number deployed in a single wave of attacks to date. Russia has been increasingly using ballistic and Zircon missiles to attack Ukraine’s population centers, mainly Kyiv city and the eponymous region, exploiting Ukraine’s shortage of Patriot missiles required for their interception. Between 1 and 19 July, Russia reportedly used more ballistic missiles than it produces in a month, as well as two-thirds of its planned annual Zircon missile production.
