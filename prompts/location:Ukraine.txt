Crisis / situation: Ukraine

--- Report 1: ACAPS (2026-06-11) ---
OVERVIEW
Between January–April 2026, humanitarian access in frontline oblasts remained challenging following shifts in the frontline. After frontline movement led to increased shelling, particularly in the east, raions in these areas have seen humanitarian access deteriorate. Access breakdown and service disruptions continue to develop in areas further beyond the frontline, with technological advancements expanding drone reach and placing an even wider region under threat of strikes.
This reporting period saw significant challenges in access to energy, heating, and other civilian services, as freezing weather conditions – the coldest in 15 years at −20°C – increased needs for electricity and heating sources. Persistent Russian strikes on energy infrastructure outpaced repairs, widening the gap between civilians’ service needs and the accessibility of those services and hampering humanitarian response capacity.
In Donetska oblast, Druzhkivka, Kramatorsk, and Sloviansk in Kramatorskyi raion became increasingly central to both military defence efforts and humanitarian action as the frontline moved as close as 15km away. These cities, which contain the largest remaining population centres in Ukrainian-controlled Donetska oblast, have experienced some of the most significant increases in aerial attacks and shifting frontlines from January–April 2026, increasing civilian evacuations and affecting humanitarians’ ability to consistently access these populations.
Kherson city and riverbank areas in Khersonskyi and Beryslavskyi raions continue to experience persistent strikes. Since January, the range of these strikes has slowly been increasing, with more attacks reaching as far as 25km and a very limited number reaching as far as 40km beyond the river. These attacks are raising the already high level of humanitarian action access severity within these regions. New Russian tactics, including the increased use of petal mines in Kherson city, are increasing the daily threat level throughout the city in both road and pedestrian areas, affecting civilian and humanitarian movements through the city.
Russian attacks on transport and logistics hubs persisted at elevated rates through the reporting period, reducing operational continuity for civilians, humanitarians, and military alike, particularly in the east and north. Because of this targeting, general insecurity in these areas also increased, hindering safe humanitarian access. Attacks have affected major roadways and transit points in places such as Barvinkove, Lozova, Pavlohrad, Romny, and Sloviansk, making movement more difficult and necessitating the use of alternative roads and routes for safer humanitarian passage.

--- Report 2: FAO (2026-06-10) ---
Through its Emergency and Early Recovery Response Plan for 2026–2028, the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) is targeting 239 844 rural families and small-scale farmers affected by the ongoing war. With a total funding requirement of USD 193 million, the plan combines urgent agricultural support with early recovery measures to help sustain food production and rural livelihoods. During the first half of 2026, FAO has supported over 11 000 rural families with seed potatoes, vegetable seeds, young poultry, cash and voucher assistance, including support for animal feed and fuelwood. In parallel, more than 600 farmers received agricultural input vouchers for sowing campaigns and mine action-related recovery, modular storage units, and grant support for farm development. This assistance helped them maintain production, safely store harvested grain, invest in value chains and resume agricultural activities in affected areas.
