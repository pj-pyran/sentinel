Key trends

Russia has expanded its targeting of Ukraine’s transport infrastructure beyond passenger trains and railway hubs to drone attacks on or near border crossings in an attempt to further degrade Ukraine's connection to the outside world. Following a series of disruptive strikes on three Ukraine-Moldova border crossings in August and a ferry crossing to Romania on 6 September, Russia launched a drone strike on the Starokozache border crossing on 8 September. This is significant as the border crossing connects Ukraine with Moldova and wider Europe. These kinds of attacks on border crossings risk hindering the movement of goods such as grain and military supplies and putting additional pressure on Ukraine’s economy.
In an attempt to further reduce Russia’s revenue sources and hinder its military production, Ukraine carried out its deepest strike inside Russia to date against two Arctic gas facilities on 9 September. Ukrainian Unmanned Systems Forces also resumed attacks on Russia’s shadow fleet in the Black Sea, targeting 13 vessels on 8 September alone, including the Malta-flagged Seaviolet tanker at the Caspian Pipeline Consortium’s mooring point near Novorossiysk while it was loading Kazakh oil. Despite United States President Donald Trump’s claim on 14 September that Kyiv and Moscow agreed to halt strikes on energy infrastructure, Ukraine is likely to maintain such strikes until a formal ceasefire is reached and Russia halts its attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure in turn

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-09-23) ---
OPERATIONAL CONTEXT &amp; HIGHLIGHTS:
• Since late August, the Russian Federation (RF) has significantly intensified frequency of attacks, damaging infrastructure and civilian sites, disrupting daily life and causing growing casualties. One of the deadliest attacks occurred on 28 August in Kyiv region, where a Russian strike triggered explosions that killed at least 37 people. According to the UN HRMMU, at least 372 civilians were killed and 2,349 injured in Ukraine in August 2026 - the second highest monthly figure in 2026, after July, with 448 killed and 2,675 injured.
• Strikes on Kherson on 27 August damaged the city’s combined heat and power plant, prompting the authorities to urge residents to evacuate. The situation reflects a broader trend across frontline areas, where escalating insecurity and damage to energy and water systems are driving new displacement, mostly within affected regions but also towards western Ukraine.
• Humanitarian operations faced growing security risks. RF strikes repeatedly hit warehouses contracted by various UN entities, including UNHCR’s, damaging stocks of critical relief supplies. The increased frequency of attacks also made emergency response efforts more complex and dangerous.
• Given security challenges, to ensure continuity of assistance, UNHCR is adapting its operational modalities, including through decentralizing humanitarian stock and exploring additional protective measures such as mobile bomb shelters for war-affected communities.
• In the fifth year since the start of the Russian full-scale invasion of Ukraine, and more than 12 years since the war began, UNHCR’s programme in Ukraine increasingly aims to help war-affected people and communities to recover, and displaced people to find durable solutions, particularly through housing and strategic protection advocacy.
