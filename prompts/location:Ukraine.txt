Crisis / situation: Ukraine

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-08-03) ---
UNHCR’s Emergency Shelter and Housing Programme in Ukraine: The programme aims to ensure that the most vulnerable among people affected by the war, whether displaced, remaining at home or having returned from displacement, have access to housing. In the seventh round of UNHCR’s Intention Survey, refugees and internally displaced people raised access to housing along with livelihoods opportunities as key enablers of sustainable and dignified return. UNHCR adopts approaches taking the local contexts into account and includes support that contributes to recovery and durable solutions.
Overview: The war in Ukraine has damaged or destroyed more than 14% of Ukraine’s total housing stock according to the fifth Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment, and consequently left millions of Ukrainians in need of various forms of housing support. Through its shelter and housing assistance, UNHCR supports:
▪ People in areas that continue to be directly affected by hostilities and need emergency support to cover damaged housing
▪ People who have either remained in or returned to damaged housing in war-affected areas and who need support with house repairs.
▪ People in situations of protracted internal displacement who need accommodation for the medium to longer-term.

--- Report 3: World Vision (2026-08-03) ---
As of June 2026, World Vision Ukraine Crisis Response has reached 2,413,068 people across Ukraine, Romania, Moldova and Georgia with essential humanitarian support.
Children are at the centre of our work: 44% of those reached, 1,061,542 children, have received aid designed to protect their well-being, restore learning, and process trauma.
Our response provides holistic support tailored to immediate and long-term needs:

Basic Needs: Food assistance, essential household items, and direct cash aid.
Child Well-Being: Safe education spaces and dedicated protection services.
Mental Health: Psychosocial support (MHPSS) to help families heal from trauma.
Livelihoods: Income-generating support to help families regain independence.

Where our aid reaches:

Ukraine: 1,509,734 people (662,740 children)
Moldova: 510,323 people (215,854 children)
Romania: 352,027 people (159,871 children)
Georgia: 40,984 people (23,077 children)

Together with local partners, World Vision remains committed to supporting displaced families and building a safer future for every child affected by this war crisis.

--- Report 4: ETC (2026-08-03) ---
Summary Points
• LTC Telecoms maintained critical security communications across 10 high-risk locations in Ukraine, restored VHF coverage at the Shakhtarske (ex. Pershotravensk) in Dnipropetrovsk region and the Sumy UN remote sites responding to conflict-related damage to communications infrastructure.
• LTC Telecoms supported maintenance activities at the UNDSS remote site in Lviv in line with recommendations from the recent {TESS+} mission.
• LTC Telecoms is relocating communications equipment from the Dnipro warehouse following a second incident of conflict-related damage to the facility, to ensure the security of telecommunications assets and continuity of services.
