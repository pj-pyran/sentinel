Crisis / situation: Ukraine

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-11-05) ---
OVERVIEW
In August 2026, the Accountability to Affected People Working Group with support of the UNHCR Ukraine conducted a consultation with IDP Councils to gather recommendations for the 2027 humanitarian needs and response planning process. The consultation brought together 22 representatives from different regions of Ukraine.
Based on the discussion, participants broadly agreed on the need to approach displacement as a cycle rather than a single event. Accordingly, they recommended strengthening assistance throughout all stages of displacement, as a significant share of humanitarian support is currently concentrated in frontline areas, while the needs of displaced people in other parts of Ukraine often remain unmet.
Participants noted, for example, that certain types of psychosocial support are available in frontline areas, although not all forms of such support are considered relevant or in demand. At the same time, host communities face shortages of psychological and psychiatric services, while the need and readiness to access such support may increase after evacuation, once people have had an opportunity to recover from their immediate experiences. The social adaptation of children was also identified as a priority, alongside long-term psychological support for both children and adults.

--- Report 2: ETC (2026-11-05) ---
Summary Points
• The 2026 LTC Telecoms User Satisfaction Survey recorded an 97 percent satisfaction rate across services delivered at 18 locations, with partner feedback informing future service priorities and improvements.
• As attacks on critical infrastructure intensified ahead of winter, LTC Telecoms strengthened communications resilience and contingency preparedness, including installing 4G backup connectivity at the remote VHF radio site in Shostka, Sumy region.
• LTC Telecoms continued to support resilient communications for humanitarian operations in frontline and high-risk areas, including RSoV installations in armoured vehicles, reprogramming 23 VHF radios, and testing enhanced mobile satellite connectivity along frontline routes in Dnipropetrovsk region.
