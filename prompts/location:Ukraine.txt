Ukraine conducted at least 35 attacks against energy infrastructure in occupied regions last week, close to the monthly average between January and May, and over half of them in Crimea. The strikes have targeted electrical substations, gas networks, and power plants, and have been on the rise since June. They have led to power outages, water supply issues, and price surges on the peninsula. As Ukraine aims to sever Russian communication lines with Crimea and complicate military operations there, the strikes are likely to continue to further affect the local population as temperatures start to drop in the coming months.
In Russia proper, Ukraine expanded its campaign against Russia’s economy. Since 21 August, Ukrainian forces have targeted the warehouses of Russia’s second-largest online retailer. Russia vowed to retaliate and not only attacked civilian warehouses across Ukraine, but also grain export facilities, steel plants, and shopping malls, including the deadly strike on a mall in Kryvyi Rih on 21 August. While framed as retaliation, Russian strikes on a broad range of civilian targets are not new and are set to continue regardless of the Ukrainian campaign, unless the struggling economy forces Russia to accept a limited ceasefire.

--- Report 2: Protection Cluster (2026-09-02) ---
Background and Rationale
Since the beginning of the full-scale invasion by Russian Federation on Ukraine, protection risks faced by affected populations have evolved in scale, severity, and complexity. Ensuring a coherent, evidence-based, and community-centered understanding of these risks is essential for effective protection response, advocacy, and strategic decision-making.
In 2022, the Global Protection Cluster (GPC) developed the Protection Analytical Framework (PAF) as a qualitative, risk-focused analytical approach to strengthen protection analysis across humanitarian contexts. Building on this, in 2023 the GPC, together with Protection Clusters globally, initiated the consolidation of Protection Monitoring Tools (PMT) and PAF into a unified Protection Risk Monitoring System (PRMS). This system is currently implemented in several operations (including Syria, oPt, Sudan, South Sudan, Haiti, and West Africa) and is now being introduced in Ukraine.
The PRMS transformation responds to the need to:
▪ Align protection monitoring with the 15 GPC Protection Risks, while ensuring alignment with Ukrainian context and focus on priority protection risks identified by partners.
▪ Harmonize protection analysis across protection actors including Child Protection and GBV areas of specialization, partners, and data providers.
▪ Strengthen the translation of protection risks into People in Need (PiN) and Severity estimates.
▪ Ensure more inclusive and consultative protection information collection and analysis, enabling participating organizations meaningful contributions, visibility and application of collected data as per established protocols
▪ Ensure that protection analysis systematically informs humanitarian planning, advocacy, and programming – both at operational and strategic level, including the HNRP and Protection Analysis Updates (PAUs), and linking up with humanitarian coordination system and wider information landscape in Ukraine, namely the HCT, ICCG, IMWG HOCGs, ABCs, national and sub-national clusters and partners, Data Coordination Group. This includes complementarity with UNHCR’s Protection Survey.
▪ Build in flexibility to provide protection analysis on specific thematic issues or for specific locations where protection risks are seen to become more acute.
▪ Enhance skill-building among protection partners in Ukraine to collect and analyze protection information for improved protection outcomes for IDPs and other war-affected populations.

--- Report 3: FAO (2026-09-02) ---
Ukraine Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster (FSLC) Winter Response 2026-2027 Strategic Plan
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The FSLC 2026-2027 Winter Response combines life-saving food assistance with emergency livelihoods support to address immediate food needs while protecting critical productive assets during the winter period.
The response will reach 136,888 people and requires an estimated USD 8.55 million, covering Ready-to-Eat Kits, hot meals, and emergency livestock and animal health assistance. Food assistance will support conflict-affected populations facing disruptions to food access and essential services, while livelihoods assistance will focus on livestock-owning households at risk of losing productive assets due to conflict, winter conditions, market disruption, and limited access to essential inputs.
