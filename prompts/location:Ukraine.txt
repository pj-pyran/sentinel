Key trends

Russian forces continued their advances in the Kharkiv region, claiming two settlements near Vovchansk and Kupiansk. In the Donetsk region, they advanced west of the Slovyansk-Kramatorsk agglomeration, occupying two villages. Despite local Ukrainian counteroffensives at the Dnipropetrovsk-Zaporizhia administrative line, Russia’s infiltration and flag-planting operations allowed it to claim advances and occupy a village northwest of Huliaipole.
Russian forces launched at least 65 long-range missile and drone attacks, including on Kyiv city and the surrounding region, but mostly on the Chernihiv and Dnipropetrovsk regions.
Russian strikes reportedly killed at least 96 civilians in the city of Kyiv and across the Sumy, Kharkiv, Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Zaporizhia, Kherson, Mykolaiv, and Odesa regions. Ukrainian strikes reportedly killed at least 20 civilians in the Russian-occupied parts of the Zaporizhia, Donetsk, Kherson, and Luhansk regions.

Spotlight: Russia resumes its air campaign on Kyiv to bend Ukraine’s will to fight
A combined attack of Russian drones, ballistic missiles, and cruise missiles launched during the night of 1 and 2 July at the city of Kyiv and its suburbs killed at least 31 civilians and injured over 100 others. The strikes also damaged over 100 residential buildings, as well as educational institutions, a Red Cross warehouse, and oil and energy infrastructure. Russia followed these attacks with a similar mass round of strikes on Kyiv and the Kyiv region on 6 July. Preliminary data point to at least 26 fatalities and tens of injured civilians. The strikes mark a new high of civilian targeting in population centers. ACLED records the third-highest fatality count in Kyiv following the 1 and 2 July overnight strikes since the start of Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, on par with the strikes it carried out on 31 July 2025.
During the week, Russian forces also struck the regional capitals of Sumy, Kharkiv, Dnipro, Zaporizhia, Kherson, Odesa, and Mykolaiv, killing at least 34 civilians and injuring over 250 others. They also targeted civilian infrastructure, including apartment buildings and gas stations. The urban centers in front line regions are particularly vulnerable to Russian strikes due to their proximity to Russian launchpads and importance in military logistics and humanitarian service provision. While these strikes highlight Ukraine’s critically low air defense missile stock, they also suggest Moscow’s intent to intensify targeting of civilians and civilian infrastructure behind the front lines, at the cost of breaking civilian casualty records. This intent relies on Vladimir Putin’s presumed theory that continuing deep strikes into Ukraine and their disastrous consequences will be sufficient to break the will of Ukrainian society to fight back. However, this has resulted in little change despite the Ukrainians’ openness to negotiations to end the war.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-07-15) ---
OPERATIONAL CONTEXT &amp; HIGHLIGHTS:
• The Russian Federation has significantly intensified aerial attacks against Ukraine, with Kyiv and other cities subjected to multiple large-scale strikes. The UN HRMMU reported that civilian casualties in Ukraine were higher in June 2026 than in any other month since April 2022, with at least 293 civilians killed and 1,990 injured. This concerning trend appears to be continuing into July. A massive attack on Kyiv overnight on 1-2 July killed 31 people. Only a few days later, another attack on 5-6 July killed 19 people in Kyiv city and ten people in the Kyiv region. So far this year, Russian Armed Forces launched over 520 ballistic missiles at Ukraine, more than twice as many as in the same period in 2025, according to Ukraine air force data.
• The Ukraine Recovery Conference (URC) took place in Gdańsk, Poland, on 25 and 26 June. UNHCR’s Representative Bernadette Castel-Hollingsworth attended, joining key discussions with partners, UN agencies and Government counterparts. The Representative also spoke at a side event on recovery and future returns of displaced Ukrainians – highlighting the need for people-centered investments in housing, jobs and services in Ukraine.
• Marking the 75th anniversary of the 1951 Refugee Convention and the 65th anniversary of the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness – and ahead of World Refugee Day in June – UNHCR together with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs launched a photo exhibition in Kyiv highlighting the stories of refugees and stateless people in Ukraine.
• In the fifth year since the start of the Russian full-scale invasion of Ukraine, and more than 12 years since the war began, UNHCR’s programme in Ukraine increasingly aims to help war-affected people and communities to recover, and displaced people to find durable solutions, particularly through housing and strategic protection advocacy.
