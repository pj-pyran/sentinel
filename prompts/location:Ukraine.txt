--- Report 1: WFP (2026-11-18) ---
HIGHLIGHTS:

WFP assisted an estimated 504,300 people across its activities in Ukraine in October, including 298,500 people provided with emergency food and cash assistance in frontline areas.
As Russian forces continued attacks across Ukrainian frontline and urban areas, October saw multiple incidents of hostilities impacting WFP and cooperating partner staff, assets, and infrastructure.
WFP and UNICEF have begun trialing integrated logistics and programme solutions in Kharkiv region, including shared warehousing and joint frontline deliveries

SITUATION OVERVIEW
Security Situation:

Russian attacks increase intensity in targeting Ukrainian energy infrastructure and focus on Kyiv city in multiple large-scale attacks, weakening the country and capital further as winter approaches.
In frontline and border areas, drone attacks against civilian vehicles, humanitarian movements and emergency services continued, including multiple instances of “double tap” strikes impacting emergency response, recovery and repair activities.
Repeated damage to critical infrastructure across central, eastern, southern and western regions of Ukraine is increasingly affecting the reliability of services supporting humanitarian operations beyond the immediate frontline.

WFP Impact:

The number of armed conflict incidents in Ukraine involving WFP or WFP cooperating partners this year (January – October) is already 81 percent higher than in 2025.
On 14 October, a WFP cooperating partner staff member of a was killed in an airstrike in the city of Sloviansk (Donetsk region).
The attack came just one day after a cooperating partner warehouse was attacked in Barvinokve (Kharkiv region) on 13 October, injuring two people and leading to the loss of 23 mt of WFP food.
In two separate incidents, two trucks belonging to WFP-supported bakeries were attacked by drones in Kherson region on 19 October.
Deteriorating security conditions in eastern Ukraine are increasingly limiting the willingness and ability of commercial transporters and humanitarian partners to access frontline areas. As such, WFP is increasingly leveraging its own delivery capacity to sustain humanitarian supply lines in frontline areas.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-11-18) ---
POST DISTRIBUTION MONITORING: CASH ASSISTANCE
As part of its accountability to affected people, UNHCR consistently conducts monitoring exercises during and after service delivery. The post-distribution monitoring (PDM) of UNHCR’s Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) programme in 2025 confirms the relevance, effectiveness and impact of cash assistance in helping conflict-affected households meet their most urgent needs. Key findings from the PDM report include:

Contributes to protection by improving overall living conditions &amp; wellbeing: 57% of recipients reported improved living conditions following receipt of assistance, while 62% reported reduced stress levels.
Preferred modality: Recipients overwhelmingly preferred cash assistance, with 83% preferring cash only and a further 15% preferring a combination of cash and in-kind support. Only 1% preferred in-kind assistance alone.
Meets identified needs: Cash assistance enabled households to cover essential needs that otherwise would have remained unmet, including utility bills, rent, clothing, shelter-related expenses, as well as food and healthcare costs.
Cash assistance delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable, &amp; participatory manner: 96% of recipients reported satisfaction with the assistance process, 96% experienced no difficulties during registration or enrolment, and 98% felt safe receiving, keeping and spending the assistance. • Market resilience: In almost all cases (96%), recipients reported that the goods and services they needed were available in local markets, while 88% reported access to quality goods and services.

--- Report 3: World Vision (2026-11-18) ---
World Vision started working in Ukraine in March 2022 through 6 and now 3 active offices in Ukraine (Kyiv, Dnipro, Mykolaiv), which currently support aid efforts reaching conflict-affected people. While also supported Ukrainian refugees and host communities in Moldova, Romania, and Georgia. WV UCR in Moldova concluded its operations in June 2025 and transitioned to WV Romania registered Moldova Foundation.
World Vision’s Ukraine Crisis Response and its local partners have reached over 2.4 million people, including more than 1 million children, providing essential support through food, non-food items, cash assistance, protection and education interventions, livelihood, and mental health &amp; psychosocial support programming.
In 2025, World Vision intentionally incorporated early recovery and economic development into its programming, expanding livelihood opportunities for affected families and creating new jobs.
Its microfinance arm, VisionFund, is operating in Ukraine in the field of medium- to long-term recovery and development via financing small and medium-sized businesses for further growth, opportunities to generate income and create new jobs.

--- Report 5: UNHCR (2026-11-18) ---
OPERATIONAL CONTEXT &amp; HIGHLIGHTS:
• Throughout October, the Russian Federation (RF) continued large-scale aerial attacks across Ukraine, increasingly targeting energy and other critical infrastructure as temperatures dropped and the winter season approached. Repeated attacks damaged energy facilities and disrupted electricity and water provision and other essential services, increasing risks for civilians during the cold season, particularly older people and persons with reduced mobility. According to the UN HRMMU, at least 372 civilians were killed and 1,981 injured in October 2026.
• Humanitarian operations are increasingly facing security challenges, with at least seven incidents affecting UNHCR partners while implementing UNHCR’s programmes in October. These included strikes impacting humanitarian personnel and operations, damage to humanitarian vehicles and partners’ premises, and repeated damage to interim evacuation points. While no fatalities were reported among UNHCR or partner personnel, the incidents underscore the volatile operational environment and continued risks to humanitarian activities.
• UNHCR supported the working visit of the delegation of the Ministry of Social Policy, Family and Unity of Ukraine (MoSP) to participate in the 77th session of the UNHCR Executive Committee (ExCom). Speaking at the ExCom session, Minister Uliutin underscored the importance of international humanitarian support to Ukraine, protection of internally displaced people (IDP) and investment in long-term solutions.
• In the fifth year since the start of the Russian full-scale invasion of Ukraine, and more than 12 years since the war began, UNHCR’s pro gramme in Ukraine increasingly aims to help war-affected people and displaced people to find durable solutions, particularly through housing and strategic protection advocacy.

--- Report 6: Logistics Cluster (2026-11-17) ---
Background
As the war continues and the 5th year of the full-scale invasion is approaching, 10.8 million people in Ukraine continue to be in need of humanitarian assistance, with those living the near the frontline facing the most acute and lifethreating risks, as outlined in the 2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP). Since that day on 24 February 2022, the population is experiencing a steady escalation in the intensity of attacks, resulting in continued destruction and damage to the civilian infrastructure, interrupting critical services and supply chains. Recent frontline shifts prompted new displacements and evacuations. While the number of people in need dropped by 15% compared to last year, reduced funding and limited resources are challenging organisations’ capacity to respond to this evolving humanitarian crisis.
To support responding humanitarian organisations in their delivery of assistance to affected populations, the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Cluster Approach was activated in Ukraine for Logistics on 3 March 2022.

--- Report 7: Ukraine CWG (2026-11-17) ---
The Winter Group Cash Transfers (GCT) AAP Package provides practical guidance and tools for humanitarian organizations and implementing partners to integrate Accountability to Affected People (AAP) into the design and implementation of Winter Group Cash Transfers
The plan is structured around three complementary pillars:

Information provision: Ensure communities receive timely, clear, accessible, and consistent information about the purpose of Winter GCT, eligibility and selection processes, available assistance, implementation arrangements, and how to raise questions or concerns.
Two-way communication: Establish and maintain accessible channels for communities to ask questions, share feedback, raise complaints, and report concerns throughout the program cycle. A Winter GCT Frequently Asked Questions document is provided to support hotline operators and frontline workers in addressing people's questions and concerns related to the program.
Participation and inclusion: Enable communities to meaningfully contribute to identifying solutions to the winter-related needs, and how interventions are implemented and monitored. Particular attention should be given to ensuring that older people, people with disabilities, displaced people, women and other groups who may face barriers to participation are included in decision-making processes.

Partners are encouraged to use this AAP Package alongside the Winter GCT Operational Guidelines and accompanying community engagement tools to strengthen transparency, participation, inclusion and responsiveness throughout the program cycle.
