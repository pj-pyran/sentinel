--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-08-19) ---
OPERATIONAL CONTEXT &amp; HIGHLIGHTS:
• The Russian Federation has continued to carry out massive attacks across Ukraine, including repeated large-scale attacks on Kyiv. The increasing use of ballistic missiles and other long-range weapons in populated areas is contributing to rising civilian casualties and widespread damage to civilian homes and infrastructure. According to the UN HRMMU , at least 437 civilians were killed and 2,610 injured in Ukraine in July 2026, a 30% increase compared to June 2026, and a 70% increase compared to July 2025. The number of those killed and injured is the highest recorded since March 2022, while the number of those killed is the highest since May 2022. August 2026 has already started with more Russian attacks which have killed and injured civilians.
• Overnight on 31 July-1 August, a Russian attack destroyed the warehouse and office of UNHCR’s local NGO partner Proliska in Pavlohrad, Dnipropetrovska oblast, impacting stocks of humanitarian supplies and emergency shelter materials. Fortunately, no staff members were injured.
• The humanitarian community launched the inter-agency 2026-2027 Winter Response Plan in July. As part of the inter-agency response, and as millions of people in Ukraine brace themselves for the fifth winter since the Russian fullscale invasion, UNHCR plans to deliver critical assistance to help war-affected people stay safe and warm in their homes. UNHCR’s winter assistance will complement the response by the Government of Ukraine and will be delivered in close coordination with partners, prioritizing war-affected people with specific vulnerabilities. See UNHCR’s winter response plan here .
• In the fifth year since the start of the Russian full-scale invasion of Ukraine, and more than 12 years since the war began, UNHCR’s programme in Ukraine increasingly aims to help war-affected people and communities to recover, and displaced people to find durable solutions, particularly through housing and strategic protection advocacy.
