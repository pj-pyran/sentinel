Crisis / situation: Colombia

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-06-11) ---
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
• In March, WFP transitioned from a predominantly migration‑focused response to a nationally aligned strategy prioritizing emergency assistance to conflict‑ and climate‑related shocks, peacebuilding, territorial development, climate action, and community resilience—positioning itself as a key long‑term partner to the host government while maintaining humanitarian response capacity.
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Despite being an upper-middle-income country, Colombia faces a complex humanitarian and food security crisis. WFP´s joint food security assessment in the 15 most vulnerable departments found that 37% of households, roughly 7 million people, face moderate or severe food insecurity, with historically conflict‑affected areas showing particularly high levels of severe food insecurity. When extrapolated nationally, overall food insecurity remains unchanged at about 25%, with departments such as Atlántico, Sucre, and La Guajira presenting the highest prevalence of severe food insecurity.
• The situation in Colombia is also marked by internal violence, forced displacements, widespread presence of illegal armed groups, ongoing mixed-migration flows, severe climate-related emergencies, and economic shocks. From January to April, 633,000 people have been affected by violence and more than 528,000 have been impacted by weather-related events.
• The country entered the national elections season with congressional voting and presidential consultations (primaries) held on Sunday, 8 March 2026 - a period that further heightened political tensions and security risks. As the country will go into polls for the presidential elections on May 31st, increasing violence could affect the electoral process, especially in rural areas with limited state presence.
• According to the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan, 6.9 million needed Food Security and Nutrition response. Likewise, Colombia’s decades-long armed conflict resulted in 10.1 million victims of which 90 percent are internally displaced persons. In 2025, alone more than 1.6 million people were affected by violence, adding about 150,000 new victims to the country´s unique registry.
• About 2.8 million migrants are currently reported living in the country, representing one of the largest figures across Latin America. In the first two months of 2026, about 4,000 migrants have transited the country irregularly, a 43% reduction in comparison to the same period of 2025.
• WFP operates in Colombia since 1969, and its Country Strategic Plan aligns with the Government’s priorities on food security, humanitarian response, recovery, development, and capacity-strengthening to achieve Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) 2 (Zero Hunger) and SDG 17 (Partnerships for Goals).

--- Report 3: IFRC (2026-06-10) ---
EARLY ACTION PROTOCOL ACTIVATION
Activation Overview
As part of the monthly monitoring process of the sEAP triggers—specifically, the three-month forecast of the percentage change in precipitation and the forecast of the Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI), both provided by the Institute of Hydrology, Meteorology, and Environmental Studies (IDEAM) — it was found that for the month of April, the first trigger was met, but not the second; however, according to bulletins issued by IDEAM on May 20, there was evidence of the possibility that both triggers would be met. Therefore, IDEAM was asked to share the shapefiles containing the data associated with both triggers, which made it possible on Monday, June 1, to confirm that both triggers had been met through the cartographic analysis conducted (Figure 1).
According to an analysis based on data provided by IDEAM, three-month precipitation forecast indicates a more than 50% probability that precipitation will be below normal, and the SPI forecast, based on a moderate drought, shows values equal to or less than –1.4 for August 2026, in large areas of the departments of Tolima and Cesar. Early measures listed below are therefore expected to be implemented during the June–August 2026 quarter in Tolima and Cesar.
The early action will target:

Individuals and families are susceptible to being affected by the shortage of water resources, limited access to water for human consumption.
Individuals and families producing (agriculture and livestock) on a medium and small scale, who:
• Are carrying out agricultural activity as their main source of income and subsistence and with a maximum of 5 productive hectares.
• Havea livestock activity as their main source of income and subsistence and with a maximum of 5 heads of livestock.
Individuals and families in areas susceptible to the proliferation of disease-transmitting vectors.
