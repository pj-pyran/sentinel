Crisis / situation: Côte d'Ivoire

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-08-18) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Despite strong economic growth, with GDP projected to grow by around 6 percent in 2026, Côte d’Ivoire continues to face food security and nutrition challenges, with over 1.3 million acutely food insecure people and an additional 3.9 million at risk. Among children under five, 21.4 percent are stunted, while anaemia affects 68 percent of children. Despite a relatively low national global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of 3.7 percent, significant regional disparities persist, with higher rates recorded in the Savanes (6.7 percent), Abidjan (6.3 percent) and Vallée du Bandama (4 percent) districts. The country hosts nearly 83,000 refugees and asylum seekers who have fled insecurity in Burkina Faso and Mali.
• WFP supports national efforts to address food insecurity, malnutrition and humanitarian needs, particularly in northern regions hosting refugees and vulnerable communities.
• Beyond direct implementation, WFP strengthens national systems and capacities to advance sustainable, government-led solutions.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-08-18) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Despite strong economic growth (GDP projected to grow by around 6 percent in 2026), Côte d’Ivoire continues to face food security and nutrition challenges.
• According to the October 2025 Cadre Harmonisé projections, over 1.3 million people face acute food insecurity (Phases 3-4) between June and August 2026, with an additional 3.9 million at risk (Phase 2), representing a 43 percent increase compared to 2025. In Tchologo and Bounkani regions, over 150,000 people (13 percent of the population) are acutely food insecure, a 36 percent increase from 2025.
• Among children under five, 21.4 percent are stunted and 68 percent suffer from anaemia. Despite a low national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 3.7 percent, significant regional disparities persist, particularly in the Savanes (6.7 percent), Abidjan (6.3 percent) and Vallée du Bandama (4 percent) districts.
• According to UNHCR’s June 2026 statistics, the country hosts 93,266 forcibly displaced people, primarily from Burkina Faso, Mali and other neighbouring countries with 82 percent of refugees living in host communities and 18 percent residing in two transit sites. This includes 80,162 refugees, 3,443 asylum seekers and 9,661 people not yet registered. Women and children represent 79 percent of this population.
