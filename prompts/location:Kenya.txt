Crisis / situation: Kenya

--- Report 1: WFP (2026-11-23) ---
SITUATION OVERVIEW
• Ongoing conflict on the Ethiopian side of Moyale has reportedly displaced approximately 60,000 people into Kenya, with registration continuing as new arrivals are reported. Although a ceasefire has reportedly held since 17 November, the security situation remains fragile, with cross-border humanitarian movements disrupted.
• Displaced populations are sheltering in communal reception centres, including schools, and with host families. Disrupted cross-border trade and livelihoods, coupled with increased pressure on host households' food resources, are heightening food security concerns. Nutrition screening is being prioritized to establish needs among new arrivals and host communities.
• The Government is prioritizing the relocation of displaced populations from school-based reception centres before schools reopen. Qilta is currently the only authorized relocation site, with plot demarcation underway under IOM guidance. Government direction on additional sites is awaited.

--- Report 2: WHO (2026-11-23) ---
Since 18 October 2026, there has been active conflict within the Oromia and Somali regions of Ethiopia.
The conflict on the Ethiopian side of Moyale, has reportedly displaced approximately 60 000 people into Kenya, with registration continuing as new arrivals are reported. Although a ceasefire has reportedly held since 17 November, the security situation remains fragile, with cross-border humanitarian movements disrupted.
A Government led assessment published 3 November 2026 found limited access to health care with 53% of all households (398/755) reporting that they cannot access nearby health centre. Households who reported inaccessible healthcare cited higher costs (49%) and drug stockouts (44%) as the drivers of inaccessibility.
Marsabit is one of Kenya’s 23 Arid and Semi-Arid Lands (ASAL) counties and was already facing food insecurity before the influx, which compounds pressure on local resources. Humanitarian access to some border areas, including Somare, remains constrained.
On 9 November, Kenya’s Prime Cabinet Secretary said Ethiopia had assured Kenya it is working to restore normalcy. Reports indicate that the root cause of clashes between rival forces are long-standing local disputes related to land ownership, governance, ethnic differences and political disagreements. Successful peace talks remain essential for people to return home safely.
The United Nations is appealing for USD 8.9 million to address the most critical lifesaving needs for the next three months.
