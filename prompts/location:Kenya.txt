Crisis / situation: Kenya

--- Report 1: IFRC (2026-11-04) ---
Scenario analysis
When and where is the hazard expected to happen?
Since the onset of the October-November-December (OND) rainy season, forecasts from the Kenya Meteorological Department have consistently indicated an increased likelihood of above-average rainfall across much of Kenya, with the highest probabilities concentrated in eastern, northeastern, coastal, and several central counties. The monthly outlook highlights enhanced rainfall conditions throughout October and anticipates heavy rainfall events in several parts of the country. The weekly forecast valid 20-26 October similarly indicates widespread rainfall activity across much of the country. These forecasts suggest a continued strengthening of seasonal rainfall towards the end of October. (KMSA Monthly Forecast; KMSA Weekly Forecast 20-26 October).
Sub-seasonal forecast guidance from NOAA GFS further supports this outlook. The Week-1 accumulated rainfall forecast (NOAA GFS Week 1) valid through approximately 2 November indicates widespread moderate to heavy rainfall across central, eastern, northeastern and coastal Kenya, with many areas expected to receive between approximately 60-150 mm of rainfall during the week. The subsequent Week-2 forecast (NOAA GFS Week 2) valid through approximately 9 November continues to show above-normal rainfall across many of the same areas, suggesting persistence of wet conditions beyond the initial rainfall episode. The consecutive weekly forecasts therefore indicate that rainfall is expected to remain elevated into early November rather than being confined to a short-duration event. Taken together, the monthly, weekly and sub-seasonal forecasts point to late October through the first week of November as the period of greatest hydrological concern, with the highest likelihood of peak impacts occurring during the first week of November (1-9 November) when cumulative rainfall from successive wet periods is likely to result in increased soil saturation, elevated river levels, localized flooding, flash flooding and access disruptions, particularly in eastern, northeastern and coastal counties.
The trigger date has been set at 1 November 2026, based on forecast evidence that rainfall is expected to become both more widespread and sustained across Kenya by the beginning of November. While NOAA forecasts indicate localized heavy rainfall developing over parts of western and northern Kenya around 28 October, the NOAA daily accumulated precipitation forecast (NOAA 24h precipitation model) valid 1 November 2026 shows rainfall extending across much of the country, including western, central, eastern and northeastern Kenya.
This outlook is further supported by global forecasting products, which indicate an increased likelihood of heavy rainfall over western, central, northeastern, and southeastern Kenya in the next two weeks. In western Kenya, persistent rainfall since early October, combined with the forecast for continued rainfall, is expected to significantly increase soil moisture levels and lead to widespread soil saturation. Consequently, river and stream levels are likely to rise, increasing the risk of riverine flooding, particularly in flood-prone, low-lying, and poorly drained areas. In contrast, much of northern Kenya has remained relatively dry in recent weeks. Therefore, intense rainfall events are likely to generate substantial surface runoff and increase the risk of flash flooding, especially in arid and semi-arid areas where infiltration rates are low.
Furthermore, heavy rainfall has persisted over southern Ethiopia for more than a week, increasing river levels. This raises concerns about increased river flows into northeastern Kenya through the transboundary Juba-Shebelle river basin, potentially exacerbating flood risks in downstream areas.
In central Kenya, the flood risk is primarily driven by high levels of urbanization. Persistent or intense short-duration rainfall frequently overwhelms drainage systems, resulting in surface flooding and disruption to transportation and critical infrastructure. Given the forecast for heavy rainfall in the coming weeks, urban flood risks are expected to increase significantly across several major population centres.

--- Report 2: IOM (2026-11-02) ---
Overview
The security situation in and around Moyale remains volatile following armed clashes that erupted on 22 October. The use of heavy weaponry has been reported, with clashes continuing on a daily basis. New arrivals are reporting that homes, buildings and neighbourhoods have been damaged and destroyed. Initial estimates from sources in Ethiopia estimated some 80,000 people may be internally displaced in Ethiopia.
There are reports that the armed forces have regular pauses to allow for humanitarian passage for civilians to leave. Displacement is ongoing within Ethiopia and across the border into Kenya. The Kenyan Defense Forces are securing the border. Based on the latest estimates from local authorities, the number of new arrivals has now reached approximately 50,000 individuals, underscoring the scale of the crisis and the rapidly growing pressure on local infrastructure, public services, and host communities. Most new arrivals are reported to be women and children, alongside older people and persons with disabilities who may face heightened risks.
