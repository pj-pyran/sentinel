Crisis / situation: Kenya

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-11-11) ---
Overview
The security situation in and around Moyale remains volatile, since armed clashes erupted on 22 October.
Heavy weaponry has been reported, and new arrivals describe homes, buildings and neighbourhoods being damaged or destroyed. Fighting has been intermittent with gunfire being heard in Moyale, Kenya as of 9 November, and clashes are reported to occur mostly at night. Regional authorities in Ethiopia report large-scale internal displacement across eight districts in Oromia and Somali Region with a growing risk of violence spreading to neighbouring districts.
Stray fire and explosives continues to cross into Kenya displacing Kenyan residents. The situation is complex, with a mixed population emerging: internally displaced people from the border area seeking safety further inland, Kenyans returning from Ethiopia, and displaced Ethiopians from different parts of Ethiopia wishing to return home, and a small number of third-country nationals. Successful peace talks remain essential for people to return home safely.
The UNHCR Representative visited with Government officials on 4 November and appealed for a ceasefire so that families can return home. Humanitarian access to some border areas, including Somare, remains constrained. On 9 November, Kenya’s Prime Cabinet Secretary said Ethiopia had assured Kenya it is working to restore normalcy.
Local authorities and partners estimate that approximately 50,000 people had crossed into Kenya as of 3 November, up from a 36,000 headcount on 24 October. The Department of Refugee Services is conducting registration and profiling of the new arrivals to verify the population. Most arrivals are women and children, alongside older people and persons with disabilities. Most are staying with host families in Moyale town, while others are in six temporary sites, public buildings and open spaces.
Residents continue to show considerable solidarity despite limited resources. The continued influx is placing increasing pressure on host communities and already limited services in Moyale County. However, congestion and crowded sites heighten protection and public-health risks, and reported measles cases are a concern. These public buildings, now serving as displacement sites, were not built to accommodate these numbers of people.

--- Report 2: IFRC (2026-11-10) ---
Description of the Event
Date of event
22-10-2026
What happened, where and when?
Armed conflict between the Oromia and Somali communities residing in Ethiopia has resulted from a dispute over administrative control of a contested area between the Oromia Region and the Somali Region of Ethiopia. The conflict has led to insecurity, population displacement, and humanitarian needs among affected communities.
Reports indicate that tensions intensified after authorities from the Somali Region opened an administrative office and raised the regional flag in the disputed area, triggering armed clashes between Oromia-affiliated militia and Somali regional security forces. The death of a 13-year-old girl during the violence reportedly fuelled further unrest.
Clashes intensified from Wednesday night, 21 October 2026, with active exchange of gunshots continuing in Moyale, Ethiopia. As tensions escalated across the border, Kenya began experiencing an influx of people into Moyale Town on the Kenyan side on 22nd October 2026.
The sudden displacement, exposure to violence and insecurity, separation from family and community networks, and uncertainty about safety and return may have significant psychosocial consequences for affected individuals and families. These considerations should be included in ongoing needs assessments and response planning.
As of 25 October, Kenya had received 15,240 people fleeing the conflict in Ethiopia, including elderly persons, women, and children. Of these, 10,950 people are accommodated across six displacement sites: Somare (503 people), Sessi (799), Taqwa (700), Manyatta Burji Madarasa (6,321), St. Mary’s Catholic (317), and Arosa Mixed School (2,310). The remaining 4,290 people are being hosted by local communities in Moyale and surrounding areas. Population movement into these areas remains ongoing, with additional arrivals expected as the situation continues to evolve.
The conflict has resulted in a localized attack on the Oramia community in Mandera town. 176 people have moved to the Mandera Police Station for safety, fearing retaliatory attacks. This indicates a possible population movement situation within Mandera.
Sporadic gunfire continues to be reported on the Ethiopian side of Moyale, disrupting cross-border movement, trade, transport, customs operations and other public services. Continued insecurity raises concerns over further displacement into Kenya should the situation persist.
On 22nd October 2026, the County Government of Marsabit had written to the Kenya Red Cross seeking support in the Humanitarian response.
Population movements into Kenya remain ongoing, with a large number of new arrivals continuing to cross the border. The current figures reflect only those individuals registered by KRCS in the established displacement sites, while others remain unregistered and are sheltering in public places and other community facilities. As a result, the actual number of people on the move will be much higher than the figures currently recorded, with arrivals continuing as the conflict persists.
