Crisis / situation: Greece

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-06-22) ---
CONTEXT
People fleeing war or persecution often undertake dangerous journeys, but those with specific vulnerabilities—such as children, older persons, single women, people with disabilities, survivors of violence against women and children, or those with serious health and mental health conditions—face disproportionately higher risks. They may experience violence, exploitation, and abuse, often leaving them traumatized, especially when protection systems may not be able to address immediate needs and humanitarian resources are overstretched.
Vulnerability is not static; it evolves over time and is shaped by factors such as social and environmental barriers, personal characteristics, and individual resilience. EU and national legislation recognize these compounding challenges by providing for specific safeguards for vulnerable asylum‑seekers during both reception and legal procedures.
UNHCR’s added value lies in its field presence and partnerships with authorities, civil society, and refugee communities, enabling early identification of vulnerabilities and tailored, rights‑based responses. UNHCR ensures timely identification during reception procedures and adapts assistance to individual needs to safeguard well‑being and uphold fair and dignified treatment that prevents further harm.
Severe funding constraints from mid‑2025 resulted in the suspension of critical protection activities, disproportionately affecting the most vulnerable. Case management and emergency accommodation for survivors of violence against women and children, youth protection and self‑reliance interventions were scaled back, and a vital mental health and psychosocial support programme ended.
A total of $8.3 million is needed in 2026 to sustain priority responses and scale up critical interventions across all UNHCR programmatic areas.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-06-22) ---
CONTEXT
Early legal support is essential to ensure fair asylum procedures and effective access to rights for refugees and asylum-seekers. As of 12 June 2026, the implementation of the EU Pact on Migration and Asylum introduced new procedural demands, requiring early and effective legal assistance and representation for the most vulnerable ones. UNHCR and its partners’ work is essential to sustain minimum safeguards and address emerging gaps through the provision of targeted legal assistance and strategic litigation.
UNHCR brings extensive experience in delivering free, high-quality legal assistance, covering key stages of the asylum process and access to fundamental rights, such as right to family reunification. Partners have secured landmark decisions at national and international level, shaping jurisprudence and strengthening protection for refugees and asylum-seekers. This impact is driven by a coordinated approach combining legal action with monitoring, referrals, evidence-based advocacy, and judicial engagement. UNHCR has also played a critical role in bridging systemic gaps, including in the provision of compulsory legal assistance at the appeal stage.
However, severe funding reductions since mid‑2025 have significantly undermined this capacity. By end‑2025, legal assistance coverage was withdrawn from northern Greece, and services on the islands ended in April 2026. Specialised support for family reunification, statelessness, and detainees ceased between mid and late-2025, leaving only limited resources available for targeted cases. Cuts to social assistance and interpretation services further undermined access to rights.
A total of $8.3 million is needed in 2026 to sustain priority responses and scale up critical interventions across all UNHCR programmatic areas.

--- Report 3: UNHCR (2026-06-22) ---
CONTEXT
Arrivals to Greece by sea and land routes continue, though at reduced levels compared to previous years. In 2025, Greece recorded 48,800 arrivals (21% less than in 2024), with the sea route from North Africa to the Crete region gaining prominence.
Since 2013, UNHCR has supported the functioning of the State’s first-reception mechanism. Pursuant to Article 45 of Law 4939/2022, UNHCR monitors reception and identification procedures, provides information to persons subject to these procedures, and offers any form of assistance, in accordance with its mandate and responsibilities. Through its long-standing presence in key locations, UNHCR enables direct engagement with affected populations and authorities, real-time identification of protection risks, and evidence-based advocacy to uphold access to territory and asylum.
Ensuring refugees’ access to territory and asylum remains a priority. UNHCR will remain engaged with the State to support with any activities under the Screening Regulation of the EU Pact on Migration and Asylum, which sets out streamlined procedures for identifying, registering, and conducting security checks on thirdcountry nationals at the EU’s external borders.
In 2025, due to reduced resources, UNHCR’s presence in field locations was scaled back. The premises of Samos, Chios, Kos, and Thessaloniki closed, and a limited staff presence was retained in all field locations primarily through camp-based modalities, while the Country Office remains in Athens covering southern Greece. In-person interpretation services were shifted to remote modalities, and interpretation hours were reduced, affecting communication with refugees.
A total of $8.3 million is needed in 2026 to sustain priority responses and scale up critical interventions across all UNHCR programmatic areas.
