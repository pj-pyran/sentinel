Crisis / situation: World

--- Report 1: FAO (2026-11-20) ---
Synopsis (short abstract)
Global wheat and maize export prices declined in October 2026. Wheat prices fell amid subdued import demand and improving global production prospects, while maize prices declined from their September three‑year high amid seasonal harvest pressure in the United States of America and expectations of ample supplies from South America. Expectations of easing constraints on Black Sea export flows also weighed on wheat prices, although continued logistical disruptions in the region partly offset the downward pressure. By contrast, international rice prices increased, supported by harvest delays in some exporting countries, adverse weather conditions and rising production costs. In most domestic markets monitored by FAO, staple food prices remained relatively elevated in September and October 2026. In parts of East Africa and the Near East, conflict‑related market disruptions, currency depreciation and tight supplies continued to exert upward pressure on prices. In Far East Asia, weather-related production shortfalls and high production costs contributed to the increases in staple food prices, while in Eastern Europe, the Caucasus and Central Asia, high transport and import costs continued to exert upward pressure on domestic food prices. By contrast, the arrival of new harvests improved market supplies and contributed to seasonal price declines in parts of West Africa and Central and South America. However, strong demand, delayed harvests and unfavourable production prospects continued to drive price increases in some markets. In Southern Africa, ample domestic supplies and continued intra-regional trade helped contain seasonal price increases.

--- Report 2: FAO (2026-11-20) ---
The extremely strong El Niño reaches its peak. Dry conditions are expected across most regions until the end of November, except for parts along the Red Sea coasts and in Mauritania. In the Western Region, above-normal rainfall is likely in December in the northern Sahel, with light rainfall in Mauritania and Western Sahara, which may favour continuation and expansion of ongoing breeding. Wetter-than-average conditions are forecast from February in Mauritania and Western Sahara, and may continue into spring and extend to southern Algeria, supporting locust breeding. In the Central Region, rains are likely around the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden from early December, becoming much heavier in February and persisting through March, thereby supporting breeding along the coasts in winter and in interior areas during spring. In the Eastern Region, near- to above-normal rainfall from December to May may allow small-scale spring breeding in Iran and Pakistan.
