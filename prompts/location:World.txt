--- Report 1: Insecurity Insight (2026-08-18) ---
This News Brief bears testimony to the brave survivors who speak about sexual violence by state bodies or conflict actors. Most events of violence are never reported. This compilation is neither complete nor representative of the extent or nature of sexual violence in general. It brings together dispersed accounts about survivors from around the world who broke the silence.
Developments in July 2026
Central African Republic: In July 2026, reported incidents in Ouham-Fafa and Vakaga highlight the exposure of women and girls to sexual violence amid insecurity and armed-group activity, particularly in remote and rural communities. The incidents, involving unidentified armed men and LRA fighters, reflect recurring trends of sexual violence by multiple armed actors, heightened risks for women and girls while carrying out everyday livelihood activities, and limited access to protection and essential services in isolated, conflict-affected areas. See the Central African Republic section for more details.
Democratic Republic of the Congo: In July 2026, reported incidents in Kwilu and South Kivu provinces highlight the continued risk of sexual violence faced by women amid insecurity and armed activity, including attacks by both state forces and unidentified armed men. The incidents reflect recurring trends of sexual violence occurring in the context of everyday activities, armed attacks and insecurity along key transport routes. See Democratic Republic of the Congo section for more details.

--- Report 2: Insecurity Insight (2026-08-17) ---
Attacks on health care were relentless in 2025. Using data collected by Insecurity Insight, the Safeguarding Health in Conflict Coalition (SHCC) recorded 2,546 incidents in 33 countries in which damage to or the destruction of health care facilities was reported on at least 790 occasions, 455 health workers were killed, 218 kidnapped and 263 arrested.
In 2025, violence by state actors was reported in 26 countries and territories, an increase of nine compared to 2024. Over 80% of these incidents involved state actors attacking health care outside their own territory, including through cross-border operations or attacks in contested areas. The vast majority of such incidents were attributed to the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) in Iran, Lebanon, the oPt, Syria, and Yemen and to Russian forces in Ukraine. Other state actors linked to attacks on health care outside their own territories included the Bolivarian National Intelligence Service (in Venezuela), the Burundi National Defence Force (in the DRC), the Indian Armed Forces (in Pakistan), Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (in Israel), the Turkish Armed Forces (in Syria), the US Armed Forces (in Yemen), and the Ukrainian Armed Forces (in Russia).
The report includes detailed profiles of 21 countries and territories where many acts of violence against health care by state parties and non-state armed groups, including organized criminal groups, took place. These include:

Africa: Burkina Faso, Cameroon, the CAR, the DRC, Ethiopia, Mali, Nigeria, Somalia, South Sudan and Sudan
Asia: Myanmar and Pakistan
Middle East and Europe: Lebanon, Iran, the occupied Palestinian territory; Syria, Ukraine and Yemen
The Americas: Colombia, Haiti and Mexico
Year in Review: 2025; 2024; 2023

Publicly reported incidents affecting Ebola response measures in the DRC
25 July 2026: In Bunia city, Ituri province, doctors, nurses, and security staff at Elikya Ebola Treatment Centre went on strike over two months of unpaid performance bonuses, bringing operations at the facility to a standstill and disrupting Ebola patient care. At around 100 health workers demonstrated outside the treatment centre and suspended work until concrete solutions were provided to resolve the payment dispute. Sources: CBC and Sky News
27 July 2026: In Bulengera commune, Butembo city, North Kivu province, Zawadi Mapendo Health Post was set on fire by unidentified men following rumours that a health worker was deliberately infecting patients or falsely diagnosing them with Ebola before transferring them to Katwa Ebola Treatment Centre. Sources: Actualite, Radio Okapi and The Telegraph
28-29 July 2026: In Kavikene locality, Congo ya Sika district, Butembo city, North Kivu province, Crinovic Hospital Centre was set on fire and destroyed by unidentified perpetrators overnight after its director was accused by local residents of killing patients under the pretext of Ebola. Sources: Actualite, Moto TV UAC and The Telegraph
31 July 2026: In Kyaghala neighbourhood, Butembo city, North Kivu province, a team conducting a safe Ebola burial was obstructed by residents, who demanded that the coffin be opened to verify the identity of the deceased. Several young people subsequently handled the body, disrupting established Ebola safe burial procedures. Source: Congo Quotidien
01 August 2026: In Cité Belge neighbourhood, Bungulu commune, Beni city, North Kivu province, an ambulance sent to evacuate an Ebola patient was stoned by demonstrators, who broke its windshield. Police fired warning shots to disperse the demonstrators. Sources: Congo Quotidien, MSN and Reuters
03–04 August 2026: In Bunia city, Ituri province, Ebola response workers staged a protest outside an Ebola treatment centre, calling for the government to pay salaries and allowances that they said were nearly three months overdue. Source: US News
03–04 August 2026: In Rwampara locality, Irumu territory, Ituri province, Ebola response workers staged a protest outside an Ebola treatment centre, calling for the government to pay salaries and allowances that they said were nearly three months overdue. Source: US News
03–04 August 2026: In Mongbwalu town, Djugu territory, Ituri province, Ebola response workers staged a protest outside an Ebola treatment centre, calling for the government to pay salaries and allowances that they said were nearly three months overdue. On 04 August, police dispersed the demonstrators after they disrupted activities outside a hospital. Source: US News
04 August 2026: In Nia Nia village, Mambasa territory, Ituri province, an Ebola treatment centre was pelted with projectiles by a group of young men, prompting police to fire warning shots. Source: Reuters
Data from Insecurity Insight shows that since the outbreak on 21 May and 04 August 2026, 35 attacks on Ebola response measures have been reported in the DRC. Ebola treatment centres have been attacked and damaged 11 times, including five cases of facilities being deliberately set on fire. In addition, nine response workers have been injured and another 12 kidnapped. Data available on HDX (see dataset Ebola Bundibugyo Virus Disease Crisis Attacks on Health Incident Data).

Using data collected by Insecurity Insight, the Safeguarding Health in Conflict Coalition (SHCC) identified 613 incidents of violence against or obstruction of health care in Ukraine in 2025. Hospitals, including maternity and children’s facilities, pharmacies, and warehouses, were damaged or destroyed by explosive weapons use on 382 separate occasions in 2025. There is strong evidence of cluster munitions having damaged hospitals in at least two cases in Sumska oblast in May and June 2025.4 For example, on May 12, at around 2 a.m., four Russian Tornado missiles armed with cluster munitions struck and damaged a paramedic and obstetrics center, a school, a cultural center, a gas pipeline, and power lines in Mykolaiv settlement.
