--- Report 1: Insecurity Insight (2026-05-14) ---
This News Brief bears testimony to the brave survivors who speak about sexual violence by state bodies or conflict actors. Most events of violence are never reported. This compilation is neither complete nor representative of the extent or nature of sexual violence in general. It brings together dispersed accounts about survivors from around the world who broke the silence.

Africa
Burundi
04 April 2026: In Rusuguti hill, Ngozi zone and commune, Butanyerera province, a ten-year-old girl was raped by a member of the National Council for the Defense of Democracy–Forces for the Defense of Democracy (CNDD-FDD). The girl was taken to hospital whilst the perpetrator was arrested. He escaped from jail the same day. Source: Ligue Iteka

The incident was reported by her father; however, another officer attempted to settle the matter in exchange for money. The father refused and insisted on pursuing the complaint. On 18 April 2026, the father was detained by a colonel in an apparent attempt to intimidate him. He was released later the same day. The officer who attempted to bribe the family was arrested for complicity in concealing the perpetrator, who remains at large. Source: Ligue Iteka

--- Report 2: WHO (2026-05-14) ---
Although ceasefire arrangements involving Iran and Lebanon continue to hold, the situation remains fragile, with continued hostilities in southern Lebanon resulting in civilian casualties and repeated displacement.
Closure of essential and maternal health services continues to have severe consequences on women and children's health, particularly in Lebanon. Environmental and CBRN risks from strikes on petrochemical, nuclear facility-adjacent, and desalination facilities remain under close monitoring, with no increased off-site radiation detected to date.
There are persistent constraints in global fuel supply chains, and electricity rationing has become increasingly prolonged, leading to persistent challenges for health service delivery. Examples of countries most affected include Cuba, South Sudan, the occupied Palestinian territory, Somalia and Malawi. Increasing food prices pose a further risk to health due to the risk of malnutrition globally and especially in settings already impacted by food insecurity.
WHO continues to monitor the global situation, in particular the global constraints in health commodity supply chains, and to leverage alternative modalities to ensure continued provision of essential health services.

--- Report 3: WHO (2026-05-13) ---
Major issues and challenges include Mpox in Guinea: The mpox outbreak in Guinea remains persistent with ongoing low-level transmission across urban districts, reflecting incomplete interruption of transmission chains despite sustained response efforts. The response is constrained by suboptimal contact tracing effectiveness, centralized case management capacity, and continued transmission among identified contacts, limiting early detection and timely isolation of cases. These challenges are compounded by population movement within densely populated areas of Conakry and surrounding districts, increasing the risk of continued spread and prolonging the outbreak. Addressing these gaps will require strengthening decentralized case management, improving the timeliness and completeness of contact follow-up, and intensifying targeted interventions in high-transmission urban settings.
Humanitarian Crisis in Burundi: The response to the humanitarian crisis in Burundi remains constrained by limited capacity in overcrowded reception and refugee sites, resource gaps, and increasing pressure on host communities, limiting the effective delivery of essential services. These challenges are compounded by rising food insecurity and population movements, increasing the risk of disease outbreaks and worsening nutritional outcomes. Addressing these constraints will require scaling up resource mobilization, expanding service capacity in high-burden areas, and strengthening integrated humanitarian and public health response.

--- Report 4: Insecurity Insight (2026-05-13) ---
Insecurity Insight’s Protection in Danger open-source monitoring identified 8,527 incidents of threats and violence that took place at IDP/refugee camps, including temporary/makeshift shelters between 01 January 2020 and March 2026. In these incidents, camps were damaged or destroyed 5,120 times, 6,320 camp residents were killed, 13,153 injured, 473 kidnapped and 5,144 arrested. Almost 55% of incidents reported Israeli aircraft and drone strikes, artillery shelling and sniper attacks on refugee and makeshift camps in Gaza between October 2023 and March 2026.
