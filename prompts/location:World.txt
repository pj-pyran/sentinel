--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-10-13) ---
Highlights
1. Recorded movement indicators declined in August, particularly by sea. Compared with July, total departures fell by 26% and arrivals by 44%; however, the decline in arrivals should be interpreted cautiously because August data for Greece are not yet available. Maritime disembarkations decreased more moderately, by 3%, to 4,352. Between January and August, reported departures and arrivals were both 44% lower than during the same period in 2025, while maritime disembarkations were 41% lower. Despite the lower number of recorded movements, the protection toll remained severe: 2,050 people were reported dead or missing at sea between January and August 2026, only 6% fewer than during the same period in 2025.
2. The cumulative geographic composition continued to evolve. The Central Mediterranean and Western Africa Atlantic Routes represented smaller shares of reported arrivals, while the South and South-East Asia and Western Mediterranean Routes accounted for a larger share of movements recorded between January and August 2026. As August data for Greece are not yet available, findings related to the Eastern Mediterranean Route should be treated as preliminary.
3. Nationality patterns shifted on the Central Mediterranean Route in August. Bangladeshi nationals became the largest reported group, while arrivals of Somali, Sudanese, Tunisian and Pakistani nationals also increased. Algerian arrivals declined and accounted for a substantially smaller share than in July, resulting in a more diversified nationality profile. Nationality changes on the Eastern Mediterranean Route cannot be assessed reliably because August data currently include Cyprus but not Greece.
(*) Figures reflect data available at the time of publication and may be subject to subsequent updates or revisions. For the latest available data, please consult the Routes Monitor platform.

--- Report 2: Insecurity Insight (2026-10-13) ---
Insecurity Insight identified 2,064 incidents of violence against or obstruction of health care in 42 countries between 01 January and August 2026. On average, health care was attacked four times a day. Health facilities were damaged or destroyed over 800 times, 418 health workers were killed, 96 kidnapped and 221 arrested. Nearly 70% of reported incidents were attributed to state actors, with the Russian Armed Forces and Israeli Defense Forces accounting for over half of the 1,202 incidents recorded.

--- Report 3: Insecurity Insight (2026-10-13) ---
Aid in Danger News Brief
The impact of drones on aid operations
In 2025, incidents where drone-delivered explosives impacted aid operations increased by 235%, from 23 recorded incidents in 2024 to 77. In these incidents, aid workers were killed or injured while aid assets and humanitarian supplies were damaged or destroyed, including in attacks on distribution sites and convoys transporting supplies. These incidents occurred amid over 1,800 recorded drone-related incidents affecting aid operations and critical civilian services such as healthcare, education, food and water systems and IDP or refugee settlements areas - a 131% increase compared to 2024.
In 2025 a growing number of aid workers killed by drones
In 2025, at least 28 aid workers were killed by drone strikes, a 65% increase from 2024 and the highest annual toll since 2021, when Insecurity Insight documented an aid worker killed by a drone. Almost a third of aid workers killed or injured were employed by local non-governmental organisations (LNGOs), which deliver frontline assistance despite having more limited financial resources than UN agencies and larger international organisations. Aid workers were killed by drones whilst carrying out their duties, particularly when travelling in convoys delivering critical aid supplies or moving between project sites. Aid workers were also killed in drone strikes at aid compounds or residences, and at aid distribution points.

--- Report 4: World Vision (2026-10-12) ---
Cambodia experienced significant flooding throughout September 2026 due to prolonged heavy rainfall and tropical weather disturbances, affecting 10 provinces, 53 districts and cities, 255 communes/sangkats, and approximately 73,922 households nationwide. The floods damaged homes, agricultural land, and public infrastructure, while continued rainfall poses ongoing risks to vulnerable communities, particularly in Battambang and Banteay Meanchey provinces.
In response, World Vision Cambodia launched a 90-day Flood Emergency Response Plan (October–December 2026) to address urgent humanitarian needs through an integrated multi-sectoral approach, including food assistance, nutrition, WASH, shelter and non-food items, protection, and education support. The response targets more than 20,000 people across the most affected areas.
To date, World Vision Cambodia has reached 3,169 people, including 1,798 children, and supported 898 households with emergency food and hygiene assistance valued at USD 35,922.73. The response has prioritized vulnerable groups such as young children, pregnant and lactating women, and persons with disabilities. Child protection awareness and community feedback mechanisms have also been integrated to ensure safe, accountable, and people-centered assistance.
Working closely with government authorities, local disaster management committees, humanitarian partners, volunteers, and youth, World Vision Cambodia continues to coordinate relief efforts while appealing for USD 800,000 to meet the growing needs of flood-affected communities across eight districts in Battambang and Banteay Meanchey.
