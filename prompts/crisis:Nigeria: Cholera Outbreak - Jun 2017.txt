Crisis / situation: Nigeria: Cholera Outbreak - Jun 2017

--- Report 1: UNICEF (2026-08-25) ---
Highlights

Armed conflict, banditry, communal violence, food insecurity and persistent malnutrition, disease outbreaks, including earlier than usual cholera outbreaks and floods deepened needs during the first half of 2026, with 5.9 million people facing severe humanitarian needs in the Northeast, 1.4 million displaced in the Northwest, and 464,000 in Benue State.
UNICEF and partners treated over 453,500 children with severe acute malnutrition across the Northeast and Northwest, achieving cure rates of 98 per cent in the Northeast and above 95 per cent in the Northwest.
UNICEF-supported polio vaccination reached 4.5 million children in the Northwest; emergency primary health care reached 157,201 women, children and adolescents; and improved sanitation benefited over 308,000 people across 103 IDP camps in Borno.
By Midyear, UNICEF had received US$58.2 million with a funding gap of 72%. As needs rise and funding declines, flexible, timely resources are required to sustain services for affected children.

--- Report 2: Call to Action on GBV (2026-08-24) ---
In July 2026, Gender-Based Violence (GBV) risks across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States were driven by conflict, food insecurity, camp closures, and the ongoing cholera outbreak. During the June–August lean season, 6.4 million people BAY States were projected to face severe food insecurity, including more than 1.1 million people facing emergency levels (OCHA). Insecurity, restricted access to farmland and markets, high food prices, and reduced purchasing power further heightened pressure on vulnerable households and exacerbated GBV risks for women and girls.
During the period under review, population movements linked to internally displaced persons (IDPs) camp closures in Bama Local Government Area (LGA) remained an important protection concern, with potential implications for the continuity of GBV services and referral pathways. Announced camp closures in Gwoza and Konduga LGAs require continued planning and advocacy by GBV partners to ensure the protection of IDPs during and after relocation.
The ongoing cholera outbreak also constrained service delivery. During the second week of July, partners responding in cholera-affected LGAs reported that fear of infection among community members and frontline workers reduced participation in GBV activities and home visits. Furthermore, overstretched health facilities due to the outbreak, disrupted referral pathways. GBV partners reported protection concerns linked to the cholera outbreak, including unsafe water and sanitation, stigma and discrimination, sexual exploitation, early/forced marriage, intimate partner violence (IPV) and limited access to referral services.
