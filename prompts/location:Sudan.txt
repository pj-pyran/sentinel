--- Report 3: UNHCR (2026-10-13) ---
Key developments

During September, the operational environment in the conflict affected regions of Sudan was highly complex and volatile, marked by localized ground clashes, intensified military activity, drone strikes, and severe weather conditions that placed increased pressure on forcibly displaced and host communities. In Blue Nile State, an escalation of violence triggered renewed internal displacement, heightened protection risks, and UN personnel had to adopt alternate and remote working modalities in line with common UN security guidance. In parts of Kordofan and the Darfur States, persistent ground fighting, drone attacks, and intercommunal tensions are consistently affecting civilians, humanitarian service delivery and damaging basic civilian infrastructure. Despite growing access and security challenges, UNHCR continues to adapt operational models to sustain critical protection and assistance services.
Attacks on humanitarian operations and civilians continued to pose severe challenges. On October 3, 2026, an airstrike struck two WFP-contracted aid trucks in South Kordofan, just one day after an explosive device incident occurred in Dilling. The WFP noted that these events significantly hindered the delivery of essential food assistance to vulnerable populations. Further compounding the violence, an attack on a displacement site in Blue Nile State on October 10 resulted in a reported 30 civilian casualties.
Seasonal strong winds, heavy rainfall, and flooding caused extensive structural damage to shelter, sanitation, and educational facilities in Kassala and Gedaref States. In Northern State, adverse weather in Merowe resulted in localized structural damage and one fatality, while a fire at the Al-Afad internally displaced persons (IDP) gathering site some 100 family shelters.
