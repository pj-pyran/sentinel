Crisis / situation: Sudan

--- Report 1: CCCM Cluster (2026-11-12) ---
Site Overview:
Ardamata Site, located in Ag Geneina, West Darfur, was established in 2002 and is divided into two sections: East Ardamata (new site) and West Ardamata (Ardamata 1, 2, 3 and 4). Originally hosting approximately 37,000 individuals, the site has experienced multiple shocks in recent years, including severe flooding in 2022 and 2024 and a cholera outbreak in 2025, highlighting persistent vulnerabilities and pressure on basic services and infrastructure.
Displacements continue to shape the site, with 65 newly arrived households recorded in July 2026 from Khartoum, Al Jazera, Nyala, Al Fasher and Kebkabiya. Community representatives reported that approximately 60% of former residents have moved to Chad, reflecting continued displacement and cross-border movement trends.

--- Report 2: CCCM Cluster (2026-11-12) ---
Site Overview:
As Salam Site, Nertiti, Central Darfur, was established in May 2026 to relocate displaced households sheltering in schools across the locality and allow schools to reopen. Community consultations were reportedly conducted prior to relocation, and households agreed to move to the site.
The relocation process was planned in two phases, with 986 households relocated from 12 schools during Phase I and approximately 3,000 additional households expected during Phase II. The site is located approximately 1 km from Nertiti town and is divided into North and South blocks. Local authorities secured landowner approval for the South Block in July 2026.
The site faces several environmental and humanitarian concerns. The South Block is reportedly prone to flooding during the rainy season, potentially affecting shelter conditions. The nearest health facility, Nertiti Rural Hospital, is located approximately 5 km away and remains overstretched. The area also experienced confirmed cholera cases in 2025, highlighting the need for strengthened WASH and disease surveillance measures. Fire hazards remain significant due to the widespread use of makeshift shelters constructed from local materials (Shargan mats)

--- Report 3: CCCM Cluster (2026-11-12) ---
Site Overview:
Kalma IDP Site, located in Beliel Locality, South Darfur, was established in 2004 and remains one of the oldest and largest displacement sites in Darfur. The site covers approximately 9 km² and is located 17 km from Nyala. Recent arrivals originate from Nyala, surrounding villages, and Al Fasher in North Darfur. While many HHs have transitioned to more durable mud-brick shelters after years of displacement, vulnerable groups including female-headed households, elderly persons, and newly displaced families continue to live in makeshift shelters constructed from sorghum stalks, grass thatch, and palm-leaf mats.
Healthcare access remains a major challenge, with the nearest hospital located in Nyala and no ambulance services available, particularly during night-time emergencies. Seasonal flooding affects Centres 1, 3, 5, and 8, while poor drainage continues to affect Centre 4 and other low-lying areas.

--- Report 4: CCCM Cluster (2026-11-12) ---
Site Overview:
Zink Al Khudar Site (also known as Souk Al Khudar) is the largest displacement site in Golo in terms of both population and geographic coverage. The site was established during early 2025 after collective centres within Golo reached maximum capacity due to the continued arrival of IDPs from across Darfur. The first displaced households were accommodated in a former vegetable market originally constructed by UNAMID.
As insecurity intensified across Darfur, particularly following the deterioration of the situation in Zamzam in April 2025 and subsequent events in Al Fasher, the site experienced successive waves of displacement. Between April and October 2025, large numbers of HHs, primarily from North Darfur, settled in the site. Zink Al Khudar subsequently expanded into two sections, the New Block and the Extension Block, with the last major influx recorded in October 2025.

--- Report 5: Insecurity Insight (2026-11-10) ---
Data by Insecurity Insight shows that since fighting started between the SAF and RSF in April 2023 and 30 September 2026, 807 attacks on health care took place across Sudan, with over 55% of attacks attributed to the RSF. Health facilities were attacked and damaged at least 232 times while 222 health workers were killed, 144 arrested and 16 kidnapped.
Since January 2026, reported incidents in the Kordofan states accounted for 51% of all incidents, reflecting the intensification of fighting between conflict parties and the threats to health care delivery, health workers and patients. In the first nine months of 2026, 45 incidents affecting health care were reported across the three Kordofan states, exceeding the annual totals recorded in 2025 (35), 2024 (7) and 2023 (24). Of the 45 incidents in 2026, 51% recorded aircraft or drone strikes which damaged health facilities and killed and injured health workers. Most of these incidents were attributed to the RSF, particularly in North Kordofan, and to RSF-allied groups, including the Sudan People's Liberation Movement-North, in South Kordofan. Incidents attributed to the SAF were less frequent and occurred predominantly in West Kordofan. Health facilities were also looted and on at least one occasion, forcibly repurposed, further undermining access to essential health care for civilians. In one such incident in West Kordofan, the RSF reportedly converted a hospital into a field hospital to treat its own members, forcibly evacuating patients in the process, depriving civilians of access to critical medical care at a time when conflict-related needs are acute and health services are already under severe strain.
