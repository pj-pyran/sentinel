Crisis / situation: Sudan

--- Report 1: UNFPA (2026-07-08) ---
Drone attacks on El Obeid in North Kordofan have intensified markedly in recent weeks, now occurring on a near-daily basis as part of a sustained campaign to impose siege-like conditions on the city. The strikes have systematically targeted critical infrastructure, including power substations, fuel stations, water facilities and healthcare infrastructure, disrupting essential services and affecting sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and gender-based violence (GBV) service delivery across the city.
Essential services remain critically fragile in light of repeated strikes, and continued degradation of these systems is expected to further constrain humanitarian access and fresh water availability, disrupt healthcare delivery, increase protection risks for women and girls who require health services, and reduce resilience of the civilian population should the situation on the ground deteriorate further.
In response to this emerging crisis, UNFPA has expanded its operational presence in the central zone of Sudan by deploying an inter-agency SRH coordinator for the SRH Working Group along with two humanitarian programme coordinators, currently based in White Nile. They are supported by existing SRH and GBV technical staff to preserve life-saving services, strengthen coordination, and facilitate effective service delivery and supply chain management in view of the evolving humanitarian situation.
In the face of continuously growing humanitarian needs and new emergencies, existing resources are becoming increasingly overstretched, placing the continuity and quality of life-saving SRH and GBV services at risk across the country. Immediate additional funding is required to enable UNFPA to scale up life-saving SRH and GBV interventions in newly affected and high-risk areas while sustaining essential services for women and girls and other vulnerable groups affected by the crisis.

--- Report 2: ETC (2026-07-08) ---
The WFP-led Logistics and Telecommunications Cluster (LTC) was activated as the Emergency Telecommunications Cluster (ETC) on 25 May 2023 in response to the conflict crisis in Sudan. This Situation Report provides a monthly update on LTC telecoms activities.
Summary Points

LTC Telecoms maintained critical connectivity services for approximately 1,700 humanitarian responders across 67 sites in seven hubs despite funding shortfalls, regulatory constraints, and a highly volatile operating environment. Network expansion planning progressed in Darfur and Kordofan, with equipment pre-positioned and partner agreements established to support future service delivery.
LTC Telecoms continued to adapt to severe telecommunications disruptions across Sudan, including restrictions on satellite services and delays in regulatory approvals. In coordination with the United Nations Department of Safety and Security (UNDSS), HF radio services and refresher trainings were sustained to ensure humanitarian responders could maintain critical communications in constrained operating environments.
Funding and regulatory challenges continue to limit the scale of telecommunications support. With the USD 5.5 million 2026 LTC Telecoms requirement remaining unfunded, LTC Telecoms is pursuing cost-sharing service models, engaging donors, and advancing community connectivity initiatives in Khartoum, Geneina, and Kordofan to support both humanitarian responders and conflict-affected populations.
