Crisis / situation: Sudan

--- Report 1: IOM (2026-11-17) ---
OVERVIEW
DTM Sudan defines a returnee as a person who was previously displaced from their habitual residence within Sudan due to an event dating from 2003 onwards and has now voluntarily returned to the location of their habitual residence, irrespective of whether they have returned to their former residence or to another shelter type. DTM Sudan also collects data on individuals who returned to their locality or state of origin, but not their exact location of origin.
This map is for illustration purposes only. Names and boundaries do not imply official endorsement or acceptance by IOM.
By the end of October 2026, DTM recorded an estimated 8,594,507 internally displaced persons (IDPs) and 5,395,491 returned individuals across Sudan. IDPs were originally displaced from all 18 states in Sudan, and were recorded across 185 localities, across all 18 states. The total IDP population decreased by 26 per cent compared to the highest-ever recorded population of 11,585,384 IDPs, recorded in January 2025.
Compared to the previous month, the number of IDPs decreased across 6 states and increased across the other 12 states.
During October 2026, the number of IDPs in Blue Nile increased by six per cent from 417,714 IDPs to 443,494 IDPs, primarily in Ar Rusayris and Wad Al Mahi localities due to continued insecurity across the state. The number of IDPs also increased across Khartoum (4% increase, primarily in Karrari and Sharg An Neel localities), South Kordofan (1% increase),
Kassala (3% increase), Central Darfur (&lt;1% increase), East Darfur (&lt;1% increase), and West Darfur (&lt;1% increase).
In terms of specific events triggering displacement in October 2026, field teams reported nine incidents in Blue Nile (5), North Darfur (3), and South Darfur (1). Of the nine incidents, seven were conflict-related incidents, while two events were reportedly related to fires that displaced an estimated 185 individuals. DTM field teams also reported increased movement reportedly linked to water shortages, poor rainfall, crop failures, and limited livelihood opportunities, particularly across North Darfur and South Darfur.
In addition, returned households were recorded across 84 localities across ten states in October 2026. The vast majority of returned individuals moved back to their habitual residence (93%). A minority returned to other locations near their locations of origin.4 The majority of returns were recorded in Khartoum (2,884,725 returned individuals) and Aj Jazirah (1,312,110 returned individuals). The majority (81%) of returnees moved back from internal displacement, while 19 per cent reportedly returned from abroad. The number of returns increased by two per cent compared to the previous month. This report represents data collected between 30 September and 27 October 2026. It includes extensive data on both IDP and returnee populations, including sections for displaced and returned individuals across each state in Sudan.
Corresponding datasets, with data on state, locality, and location levels, are available on the DTM-IOM website. For further information, see the DTM Sudan Interactive Displacement Dashboard.

--- Report 2: WHO (2026-11-16) ---
Monthly highlights

WHO supported the first phase of the polio outbreak-response vaccination campaign targeting more than 6 million children under 5 years of age across 12 states, out of the 11 million targeted in 2 phases across all 18 states.
WHO and partners conducted a reactive oral cholera vaccination campaign in Gharb Bara, North Kordofan, targeting more than 271 000 people amid ongoing cholera transmission and disruptions to essential water and sanitation services.
WHO strengthened Ebola preparedness in collaboration with national health authorities, including assessments of isolation capacity in Red Sea State that identified critical gaps in infrastructure, equipment and staffing to guide further readiness measures.
