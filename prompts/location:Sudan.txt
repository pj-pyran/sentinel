Crisis / situation: Sudan

--- Report 1: CP AoR (2026-10-27) ---
The report provides an overview of the evolving protection situation in El Obeid, highlighting the deteriorating protection environment, increasing displacement-related vulnerabilities, and persistent gaps in access to essential services and humanitarian assistance.
Key findings highlight:

Heightened protection risks, including insecurity, gender-based violence, child protection concerns, psychosocial distress, and barriers to civil documentation.
Critical gaps in basic services, particularly water, sanitation, food assistance, and adequate shelter, which further exacerbate protection risks.
Significant disparities across displacement sites, including limited humanitarian presence, weak registration systems, and inadequate identification and referral mechanisms for persons with specific needs.
Increasing pressure on host communities, whose coping capacities continue to deteriorate amid prolonged displacement and limited resources.

The September follow-up mission reaffirmed the findings of the May assessment and underscored the urgent need for strengthened protection monitoring, coordinated multisectoral interventions, improved referral pathways, and targeted assistance to the most vulnerable populations.
We hope the findings and recommendations will inform ongoing humanitarian response planning, operational prioritization, advocacy, and resource mobilization efforts to address critical protection needs in El Obeid.
We encourage partners and relevant stakeholders to review the report and consider its recommendations in their respective response planning and interventions.

--- Report 2: WFP (2026-10-26) ---
In September, WFP assisted 3.1 million people, including 880,000 people in areas classified as at Risk of Famine (RoF).


Due to lack of funding, the number of people reached in September declined significantly from the 4.1 million people reached in August.


WFP provided 9,600 mt of in-kind general food assistance (GFA), reaching 1.2 million people.


GFA cash-based transfers (CBT) reached 1.4 million people with USD 8.8 million in assistance. However, the exchange rate depreciated sharply. The rate reached its lowest at SDG 7,500 per US dollar, compared to SDG 5,600 in July and SDG 6,150 in August, reducing households purchasing power and creating pressure to renegotiate retailer prices, which in turn delayed implementation in some locations.


WFP's nutrition programme reached 349,000 children under five and pregnant and breastfeeding women across all 18 states, providing both preventive and treatment services to address acute malnutrition.


School feeding activities continued to expand towards the 2026 plan of reaching 450,000 students. Since schools reopened in June, the programme has reached 264,000 students, equivalent to 60 percent of the annual plan. In September alone, 175,000 children received school meals.
