Crisis / situation: Sudan

--- Report 1: UNHCR (2026-06-17) ---
Highlights
▪ Sudan: The security environment in Sudan remains marked by sustained drone strikes and armed clashes across Khartoum, Darfur, White Nile, Kordofan, and Blue Nile States. In South Darfur, intercommunal tensions escalated in Um Zaifa (Ed Al Fursan locality) and Buram locality, resulting in civilian casualties and numerous injuries across multiple incidents linked to localised disputes and armed group retaliation. In North Darfur, a drone strike on a UNHCR truck transporting emergency shelter assistance destroyed relief items intended for over 1,300 displaced families. Key border crossing points between Chad and Sudan remain partially closed, restricting commercial movement and contributing to shortages and rising prices.
▪ Central African Republic (CAR): A U.S. diplomatic delegation visited Birao, in the remote Vakaga Prefecture near the Sudan border, to assess humanitarian needs amid a rapidly deteriorating situation driven by the influx of Sudanese refugees, persistent insecurity, and limited access to basic services. The mission reviewed ongoing response efforts by UN agencies and partners and engaged with local authorities and humanitarian actors to identify priority gaps and operational constraints
▪ Chad: Ouaddaï Province continued to face a fragile and deteriorating public health situation, with a deadly meningitis outbreak, ongoing measles transmission, and heightened epidemic risks placing significant strain on already fragile health systems. These converging threats posed serious risks to population health and had the potential to affect planned large-scale refugee relocations if not urgently contained.
▪ Egypt: On 28 April, UNHCR hosted a high‑level delegation from the Embassy of Japan, for discussions on refugee protection and displacement trends in Egypt. UNHCR briefed the delegation on the impact of the Sudan crisis, including return dynamics under adverse circumstances and Egypt’s role as a key refugee‑hosting country, and reiterated appreciation for Japan’s continued engagement and support. A field visit to a community-based organization was conducted, where social cohesion activities bringing together host communities and Sudanese refugees were showcased, highlighting the importance of community‑level interventions in promoting inclusion and resilience.
▪ Ethiopia: In Benishangul Gumuz region, the overall security situation across refugee sites remained largely calm during the reporting period. UNHCR, GIZ, and BMZ conducted joint appraisal and highlevel missions to Ura refugee site, generating key insights such as Ura’s potential as an inclusionfocused settlement, that will inform the design of a forthcoming multi-year programme focused on socio-economic inclusion, protection, and solutions.
▪ Libya: In April, UNHCR Chief of Mission met with Ambassadors in Tunis from Argentina, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Finland, Sweden, Switzerland, and the United States to highlight the humanitarian and protection situation in Libya, UNHCR’s operational priorities, and seek their continued support. On 14 April, UNHCR welcomed Peter Bundy, Political Counsellor at the Canadian Embassy, during his first visit to UNHCR Libya. He visited the registration centre and met with the Deputy Chief of Mission. Discussions highlighted the growing needs of Sudanese refugees amid the global funding crisis and ongoing challenges in refugees’ access to public services.
▪ South Sudan: From 13 to 16 April 2026, Maban County hosted a delegation from the European Civil Protection and Humanitarian Aid Operations (ECHO). The mission assessed EU-funded humanitarian interventions, with a focus on cash assistance under the general food distribution programme and provided the delegation with firsthand insights into operational progress and emerging challenges in the context of continued refugee arrivals. The delegation engaged with refugee and host community leaders, humanitarian partners, and local authorities, observing how interventions contribute to lifesaving support, household resilience, and improved access to basic needs. Discussions highlighted both achievements in service delivery and the urgent need for sustained and expanded funding to address growing humanitarian needs.
▪ Uganda: The security situation in Kiryandongo Settlement, home to the largest population of Sudanese refugees, continued to deteriorate, driven by economic hardship and underlying ethnic tensions, with UNHCR and partners scaling up community-based protection, conflict mitigation initiatives, and livelihood support to ease tensions and enhance social cohesion.

--- Report 2: UNHCR (2026-06-15) ---
Highlights
▪ Sudan: Armed clashes and near-daily drone strikes continued across Darfur and Kordofan States, affecting multiple towns including Dilling, Kadugli, Bara, El Rahad, and Abu Zabad. In North Kordofan, commercial vehicles and telecommunications infrastructure sustained damage in Tendalti village. Road insecurity along key routes including the Um Rawaba–El Rahad corridor is increasingly disrupting humanitarian access and supply lines in Central Darfur. In late February, an attack on Misteriya in Kebkabiya locality (North Darfur) triggered significant new displacement of over 3,000 people within Kebkabiya locality and caused damage to residential buildings.
▪ Central African Republic (CAR): On 20 February, a high-level government delegation led by the Ministers of Humanitarian Action and Territorial Administration visited Birao to strengthen cooperation with humanitarian partners and reaffirm the Government's commitment to durable solutions for forcibly displaced people and host communities. The delegation engaged with host communities, Sudanese refugees, and Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs to discuss challenges related to reception, protection, and access to basic services amid continued arrivals from Sudan and visited UNHCRsupported infrastructure, including a health post, water points, and livelihood projects. The visit underscored the need to support local authorities in developing inclusive local development plans that strengthen resilience and foster sustainable refugee integration. Identified by the Government as a development hub, Birao will require stronger partnerships with development actors and predictable, multi-year funding to ensure a comprehensive and sustainable response for both refugees and host communities.
▪ Chad: On 23 February, the Chadian government announced the complete closure of its border with Sudan in response to repeated and reported armed incursions into Chadian territory, citing the need to prevent the expansion of the conflict, protect the population, and safeguard national integrity. Humanitarian exemptions were granted subject to prior authorization. This decision followed a Rapid Support Forces (RSF) offensive on 22 February against the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) in Tina, Sudan, during which the RSF also attacked a post of the Chadian Defense and Security Forces (FDS) across the border in Tiné, Chad. During the clashes, stray bullets struck the premises of the UNHCR’s &quot;Guichet Unique,&quot; which is one-stop service center for refugees, including the meeting room, which was unoccupied at the timeThe closure of the Chad–Sudan border has significant implications for UN agencies and partners, restricting cross-border humanitarian access and supply routes, delaying aid delivery and staff movement, while also limiting refugee inflows, complicating protection monitoring, and increasing pressure on existing sites and already overstretched services in eastern Chad.
▪ Egypt: On 8 February, UNHCR engaged with the Sudanese Embassy to identify areas for strengthened coordination. Discussions with the Sudanese Ambassador covered access to public services, voluntary return initiatives, and ongoing support programmes, including medical assistance delivered with partners and efforts to streamline documentation processes. On 11 and 24 February, in response to issues raised by the Emergency Fund for Refugee Response (EFRR) and the Refugee Advocacy Group in their respective meetings, the UNHCR Representative outlined ongoing efforts to expand registration capacity, including through digital tools such as the MyUNHCR platform and reiterated continued engagement with the Government of Egypt as part of the transition to the new asylum law. Both engagements underscored the importance of improved information-sharing, regular coordination, and timely follow-up for individuals with specific protection needs.
▪ Ethiopia: On 27 February, the UNHCR Representative conducted a high-level mission to Assosa, reviewing protection, service delivery, and solutions programming at the Ura refugee site which currently hosts 14,000 refugees. The site follows an integrated settlement approach, whereby services such as schools and health facilities are designed to serve both refugees and host communities, promoting inclusion, strengthening local systems, and supporting early self-reliance.
▪ Libya: On 11 February, UN agencies and representatives from relevant Libyan government ministries held technical consultations on the Libya chapter of the 2026 Sudan Regional Refugee Response Plan (RRP) at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Tripoli. The meeting fostered an open and collaborative discussion focused on critical issues such as data management, the impact of the Sudan crisis on Libya, and areas for strengthened UN–government collaboration. Following the consultations, UNHCR received formal feedback from the government and the Sudan RRP was virtually launched on 18 February.
▪ South Sudan: UNHCR South Sudan concluded a joint mission featuring Goodwill Ambassador Kristin Davis, High-Profile Supporter Ann Curry and private donor Rachelle Lopp, focused on the Sudan response, with particular attention to the experiences of women and girls. The visit to Renk and to several projects in Juba aimed to raise the visibility of the crisis and included a PBS NewsHour interview conducted by Ann Curry. A press release featuring Kristin Davis was also published and is available to read here.
▪ Uganda: The security situation in Kiryandongo Settlement, home to the largest population of Sudanese refugees, continued to deteriorate, driven by economic hardship and underlying ethnic tensions, with UNHCR and partners scaling up community-based protection, conflict mitigation initiatives, and livelihood support to ease tensions and enhance social cohesion.

--- Report 3: UNHCR (2026-06-15) ---
Highlights
▪ Sudan: Armed clashes intensified in the Darfur and Kordofan States, marked by ground fighting, artillery fire, and drone strike and reports of criminal activity, intercommunal violence, and increased tensions near the Tina crossing along the Chad–Sudan border. In the Kordofan States, fighting escalated further along the Dilling, Abu Jubaiha, and Al Rahad towns, with attacks on vehicles along key routes disrupting movement and supply lines, heightening risks for civilians. On 31 March, the Government of Sudan announced an extension of the Adre humanitarian corridor from 1 April to 30 June 2026, a cross-border route facilitating the delivery of humanitarian assistance from Chad into Darfur and supporting limited movement of aid supplies and personnel into affected areas.
▪ Central African Republic (CAR): Despite relative calm in Birao, the security situation across Vakaga remains fragile and volatile. Robbery, extortion, and looting were reported along the Ouanda-Djallé, Sikikédé–Mangagne, and Tissi–Sarayevo roads, underscoring the continued exposure of civilians to armed actors on key transit routes. Incidents of intercommunal violence were also recorded, reflecting a persistently concerning security and protection environment across the prefecture.
▪ Chad: Ouaddaï Province continued to face a fragile and deteriorating public health situation. A deadly meningitis outbreak, ongoing measles transmission, and heightened outbreak risks posed serious threats to population health and had the potential to constrain planned large-scale refugee relocations if not urgently contained.
▪ Egypt: On 11 March, representatives from the embassies of France, the European Union, Poland, and Switzerland visited UNHCR’s Reception Centre where they observed how refugees access registration and protection services. The delegation received an overview of UNHCR’s processes and witnessed frontline reception activities aimed at ensuring refugees and asylum‑seekers receive timely documentation and support.
▪ Libya: Following the technical consultations held in February 2026 between UN agencies and relevant Libyan government ministry counterparts, the virtual launch of the 2026 Sudan Refugee Response Plan (RRP) – Libya Chapter took place 16 March 2026. The launch featured high-level interventions from the Deputy Special Representative of the Secretary-General (DSRSG), the UNHCR Regional Refugee Coordinator for the Sudan Situation, the UNHCR Representative in Sudan, and the Head of the Department for International Organizations at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The Response Plan, involving 14 UN agencies and international NGOs, aims to assist 667,000 people comprising 596,000 refugees, 1,000 third country nationals, and 70,000 host community members, with a projected budget of US$115.5 million. Multi-sectoral assistance, focusing on protection, basic services, and resilience support, seeks to provide nearly 200,000 people with protection services, over 221,000 people with food assistance, 180,000 people with essential items and support 398,000 individual health consultations.
▪ South Sudan: The Norwegian Minister of Foreign Affairs visited Renk, with engagements spanning from the Joda border crossing, the Abukadra returnee settlement site, and to the Renk Transit Centre. The Minister engaged directly with newly arrived families in Abukadra, as well as refugees, returnees, and third-country nationals at the transit centre, commending the strong coordination and efforts of partners in addressing the needs of new arrivals. The delegation expressed appreciation to partners at every stage of the visit and requested that gratitude be extended to the County Commissioner for his efforts in facilitating returnee settlement in Abukadra. During one-on-one briefings, the delegation raised questions on the security situation, funding challenges, protection risks, priority needs, and ongoing collaboration with government authorities.
▪ Uganda: The security situation in Kiryandongo Settlement, home to the largest population of Sudanese refugees, continued to deteriorate, driven by economic hardship and underlying ethnic tensions, with UNHCR and partners scaling up community-based protection, conflict mitigation initiatives, and livelihood support to ease tensions and enhance social cohesion.
